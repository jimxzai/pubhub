# The Heart of the Gospel — Complete 8-Sermon Series
### In the Style of Billy Graham

---

# Sermon 1: "The Cross — Where God Met Man"

---

### Opening (5 minutes)

**[Start with a story — Billy Graham always opened with something people could feel]**

> "A few years ago, a father was driving home from work when he got the phone call every parent dreads. His teenage son had been in an accident. He broke every speed limit to get to that hospital. When he arrived, the doctors told him his son needed a blood transfusion — rare blood type. As it turned out, the father was a match. Without hesitation, he rolled up his sleeve and said, 'Take whatever he needs.'
>
> Friends, that is a small picture — a tiny, imperfect picture — of what God did for you and me. Except God didn't just roll up His sleeve. He sent His only Son to die."

**Transition**: "Tonight I want to talk to you about the most important event in human history. Not the moon landing. Not the signing of any constitution. I'm talking about a hill outside Jerusalem called Calvary — where God met man."

---

### Point 1: The Problem — We Are Separated from God (10 minutes)

**The Bible says** in Romans 3:23 — *"For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God."*

> "Now I know what some of you are thinking. 'Billy, I'm a good person. I pay my taxes. I love my family. I've never killed anyone.' And I believe you. But the Bible doesn't say *some* have sinned. It says **all**. That includes me. That includes you. That includes the person sitting next to you tonight.
>
> Sin is not just what you *do*. Sin is what you *are* without God. It's the condition of the human heart. The Bible says in Jeremiah 17:9 — *'The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked.'*
>
> You see, we have a tendency to compare ourselves to each other. 'Well, I'm better than that person.' But God doesn't grade on a curve. His standard is perfection. And none of us — not one — meets that standard."

**Illustration:**

> "Imagine three people standing on the cliffs of California, trying to jump to Hawaii. One jumps 5 feet. Another jumps 15 feet. An Olympic athlete jumps 30 feet. Who makes it? **Nobody.** It doesn't matter how far you jump — the distance is too great. That's the gap between man and God. And no amount of human effort can bridge it."

**Transition**: "So if we can't reach God — what hope do we have? That brings me to my second point."

---

### Point 2: The Plan — God Reached Down to Us (12 minutes)

**The Bible says** in Romans 5:8 — *"But God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us."*

> "Did you catch that? **While we were still sinners.** God didn't wait for us to clean ourselves up. He didn't wait for us to become worthy. He came to us in our mess, in our rebellion, in our worst moment — and He said, 'I love you anyway, and I'm going to prove it.'
>
> This was not an accident, friends. This was not Plan B. Before the foundation of the world — before Adam and Eve, before the Garden, before the first star was hung in the sky — God knew. He knew man would fall. And He already had a plan."

**Walk through the Bible's storyline:**

> "In Genesis 3, when Adam and Eve sinned, what did God do? He killed an animal and covered them with its skin. The first blood was shed to cover sin. That was a picture.
>
> In Exodus, the Passover — a lamb was slain, and its blood was put on the doorpost, and the angel of death passed over. That was a picture.
>
> In Leviticus, year after year, the high priest would enter the Holy of Holies with the blood of a sacrifice for the sins of the people. That was a picture.
>
> In Isaiah 53, written 700 years before Jesus was born — *'He was wounded for our transgressions, He was bruised for our iniquities; the chastisement for our peace was upon Him, and by His stripes we are healed.'* That was a prophecy.
>
> And then — in the fullness of time — God sent His Son. Not to a palace. To a manger. Not with an army. With shepherds. The God of the universe wrapped Himself in human flesh and walked among us."

**The Crucifixion:**

> "And then came Calvary. They drove nails through His hands — hands that had healed the blind, touched the leper, blessed the children. They drove nails through His feet — feet that had walked on water. They put a crown of thorns on His head — the head that contained the mind of God Almighty.
>
> And as He hung there between heaven and earth, between two thieves, He looked down at the very people who had put Him there — and He said, *'Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do.'*
>
> Friends, He wasn't just dying for *them*. He was dying for *you*. He had **your** face in His mind. **Your** name on His heart. He went to that cross *voluntarily* — because there was no other way to bridge the gap between a holy God and sinful man."

**The Bible says** in John 3:16 — *"For God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son, that whoever believes in Him should not perish but have everlasting life."*

**Transition**: "But the cross is not the end of the story. If it were, we would have no hope. And that brings me to my third point."

---

### Point 3: The Power — He Rose Again (8 minutes)

**The Bible says** in 1 Corinthians 15:3-4 — *"Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures, and that He was buried, and that He rose again the third day according to the Scriptures."*

> "On Friday, they buried Him. On Saturday, the disciples hid in fear. But early Sunday morning — **the tomb was empty.**
>
> The stone wasn't rolled away to let Jesus out. He didn't need anyone to open the door. The stone was rolled away so that you and I could look in and see — **He's not here. He is risen.**
>
> And that changes everything. Because of the resurrection, death has lost its sting. The grave has lost its victory. Sin has lost its power. The enemy has lost his hold."

**Illustration:**

> "During World War II, there was a moment when the Allied forces had clearly won — but the fighting continued for months. Soldiers were still dying in battle even though the outcome was already decided. D-Day had happened. Victory was certain. But V-Day — Victory Day — hadn't arrived yet.
>
> Friends, that's where we are. The cross was D-Day. Satan was defeated. The price was paid. But we're still living between D-Day and V-Day. We're waiting for the King to come back and finish what He started. And **He will.** The book of Revelation tells us — He wins. And if you're on His side, you win too."

**Transition**: "So here's the question. And this is where it gets personal."

---

### Point 4: The Response — What Will You Do? (10 minutes)

**The Bible says** in Ephesians 2:8-9 — *"For by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God, not of works, lest anyone should boast."*

> "Salvation is a **gift**. You cannot earn it. You cannot buy it. You cannot work for it. It is freely offered to every man, woman, and child on the face of this earth.
>
> But a gift that is never received is a gift that does you no good. If I held out a gift to you tonight and you refused to take it — whose fault is that? Not mine. I offered it. You rejected it.
>
> God is holding out the gift of eternal life to you **right now**. The question is — will you receive it?"

**Address the excuses:**

> "'I'll do it later.' — The Bible says, *'Now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation.'* You are not promised tomorrow.
>
> 'I'm not good enough.' — That's the whole point. None of us are. That's why He died.
>
> 'I've done too much.' — The thief on the cross was a criminal. And Jesus said to him, *'Today you will be with Me in paradise.'* It's never too late — but you must come while there's still breath in your lungs.
>
> 'I don't understand it all.' — You don't have to understand electricity to turn on a light. You don't have to understand God fully to trust Him."

---

### The Altar Call (5 minutes)

> "I'm going to ask you to do something. I'm going to ask you to get up out of your seat — right now — and come forward. I'm going to ask you to make the most important decision of your life.
>
> You might be here tonight and you've been running from God for years. He's been chasing you. He's been calling you. And tonight, He brought you here for a reason.
>
> You might be here and you've sat in church your whole life, but you've never had a personal relationship with Jesus Christ. Religion won't save you. Only Jesus can.
>
> I'm going to ask you to come. Come as you are. You don't have to clean up first. Come with your doubts. Come with your failures. Come with your shame. Come with your questions. Just come.
>
> The Bible says, *'Whoever calls on the name of the Lord shall be saved.'*
>
> Let's bow our heads."

**Closing Prayer:**

> "Lord Jesus, I know that I am a sinner. I know that I cannot save myself. But I believe that You died on the cross for my sins and that You rose again on the third day. I turn from my sin and I put my trust in You alone. Come into my heart. Come into my life. I receive You as my Lord and my Savior. In Jesus' name. Amen."

---

# Sermon 2: "The Empty Tomb Changed Everything"

## Series: The Heart of the Gospel (Part 2 of 8)

**Theme:** The resurrection as proof of who Jesus is
**Key Scriptures:** 1 Corinthians 15:3–8, Romans 1:4, Matthew 28
**Target Length:** 35–40 minutes

---

## OPENING ILLUSTRATION (5 minutes)

I want to begin tonight with a story.

Some years ago, I was visiting a major university — I won't say which one — and after I had spoken, a young philosophy student came up to me. He was bright. You could see it in his eyes. He had his arms folded and he said, "Reverend Graham, I respect you. But I want you to know — I cannot believe in a man who came back from the dead. It's scientifically impossible. Dead men don't rise."

I looked at that young man, and I said, "You're absolutely right. Dead men *don't* rise." He looked surprised. I said, "That's exactly the point. Dead men don't rise — but *Jesus Christ was no ordinary man.* And the fact that He walked out of that tomb is what separates Christianity from every other religion, every other philosophy, every other idea the human mind has ever produced."

You see, my friends, tonight we come to the *hinge* of history. Last week we talked about the cross — the love of God poured out for you and me. But if the story ended at the cross, we would be the most pitiful people on the face of the earth. A dead Savior cannot save anybody. A dead teacher is just another dead teacher.

But I have news for you tonight. *The tomb is empty.* The grave could not hold Him. Death could not keep Him. And that single fact — that one, explosive, earth-shaking fact — *changed everything.*

The Bible says in 1 Corinthians 15, verse 17: *"If Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile; you are still in your sins."* But then, in verse 20, the apostle Paul shouts it out: *"But Christ has indeed been raised from the dead!"*

That word *"but"* is the most important word in the entire Bible. "But Christ has indeed been raised!" And tonight, I want to talk to you about what the empty tomb means — for history, for eternity, and for *you*, right where you are sitting.

---

## POINT 1: THE REALITY — It Really Happened (8–10 minutes)

Now, the first thing I want you to understand is this: *the resurrection of Jesus Christ is not a fairy tale.* It is not a legend that grew up over centuries. It is not a myth invented by superstitious fishermen. It is a fact of history — and the evidence is overwhelming.

### The Eyewitnesses

The Bible says in 1 Corinthians 15, verses 3 through 8 — and I want you to listen carefully:

> *"For what I received I passed on to you as of first importance: that Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures, that he was buried, that he was raised on the third day according to the Scriptures, and that he appeared to Cephas, and then to the Twelve. After that, he appeared to more than five hundred of the brothers and sisters at the same time, most of whom are still living… Then he appeared to James, then to all the apostles, and last of all he appeared to me also."*

Did you catch that? More than *five hundred people* saw Jesus alive after His death. Five hundred! Now, you might fool one person. You might fool ten. But you cannot fool five hundred people at the same time. And Paul says, "Most of them are still alive" — in other words, "Go ask them! Check my facts! They'll tell you what they saw."

My friends, this is not blind faith. This is *evidence.* In any courtroom in this country, if you brought five hundred eyewitnesses to the stand, the case would be closed before lunch.

### The Empty Tomb

And then there is the tomb itself. The enemies of Jesus — the Roman government and the religious authorities — they *knew* where He was buried. They had placed a Roman seal on the stone. They had stationed trained soldiers to guard it. They did everything they could to make sure that body stayed in that grave.

But on the third day — just as He said — *the stone was rolled away.* Not to let Jesus out — He didn't need the stone moved. It was rolled away to let the world *look in* and see that the tomb was empty.

Now think about this. If the religious leaders could have produced the body of Jesus, they would have paraded it through the streets of Jerusalem and Christianity would have been finished before it started. *But they couldn't produce the body.* They couldn't, because there was no body to produce. He had risen.

### The Transformed Disciples

And look at what happened to the disciples. Before the resurrection, these men were hiding behind locked doors, trembling with fear. Peter — the big fisherman, the bold one — Peter denied three times that he even *knew* Jesus. They were defeated. They were broken.

But something happened. *Something* transformed those cowards into the boldest preachers the world has ever known. Peter stood up in the streets of Jerusalem — the very city where Jesus had been crucified — and he preached so powerfully that three thousand people gave their lives to Christ in a single day. What changed him? He had *seen the risen Lord.*

And every one of those apostles, except John, went on to die a martyr's death. They were beaten. They were stoned. They were crucified. They were sawed in two. Now, my friends, people will die for something they *believe* is true. But no one dies for something they *know* is a lie. Those men had seen Jesus alive, and no amount of persecution could make them deny it.

The resurrection is a fact. It really happened. You can stake your life on it — and millions have.

---

### TRANSITION

But now I want to go further. It's not enough to know that the resurrection *happened*. You must understand what it *means.*

---

## POINT 2: THE MEANING — What the Resurrection Proves (8–10 minutes)

### He Is Who He Said He Was

The Bible says in Romans 1, verse 4, that Jesus was *"declared to be the Son of God with power… by the resurrection from the dead."*

You see, during His life on earth, Jesus made the most staggering claims any person has ever made. He said, "I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me." He said, "Before Abraham was born, I am!" — taking upon Himself the very name of God. He said, "I and the Father are one."

Now, when a man makes claims like that, there are only three possibilities. Either He was a *liar* — He knew it wasn't true and He deceived people deliberately. Or He was a *lunatic* — He genuinely believed He was God but was out of His mind. Or He was *Lord* — He was telling the truth.

The resurrection settles the question. God raised Him from the dead. The Father put His stamp of approval on everything Jesus ever said and did. The resurrection is God's great "Amen!" to the life of His Son. Jesus is not *a* way — He is *the* way. He is not *a* truth — He is *the* truth. The tomb is empty, and that means *Jesus Christ is exactly who He claimed to be: the Son of the living God.*

### Sin Is Defeated

But there's more. The resurrection means that the *payment for sin has been accepted.* You see, on the cross, Jesus said, "It is finished." He paid the debt we owed. And the resurrection is God's receipt — it is the proof that the payment was sufficient.

I heard about a man who owed a great debt — thousands and thousands of dollars — and he could not pay. He was going to lose everything. But a friend came and paid the debt for him. When the man received the stamped receipt in the mail — "Paid in full" — he wept. He held that piece of paper in his hands and he said, "I'm free. I'm finally free."

My friends, the empty tomb is God's receipt stamped across history: *Paid in full.* Your sin, my sin — the guilt, the shame, the weight of it — it has been paid for. And the resurrection is the proof.

### Death Is Conquered

And this is the third thing the resurrection proves: *death has been conquered.*

The Bible says in 1 Corinthians 15:55–57: *"Where, O death, is your victory? Where, O death, is your sting? The sting of death is sin, and the power of sin is the law. But thanks be to God! He gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ."*

You know, death is the one enemy no human being has ever been able to defeat. The greatest kings, the mightiest armies, the most brilliant scientists — every one of them has fallen before death. But on that first Easter morning, Jesus Christ walked out of that tomb and said to death, *"You have no power over me."* And because He defeated death, He promises that everyone who trusts in Him will defeat death too.

---

### TRANSITION

Now I want to bring it very close, my friends. I want to talk about what the resurrection means — not just for history, not just for theology — but for *you*, tonight, right where you are.

---

## POINT 3: THE HOPE — Because He Lives, We Can Face Tomorrow (8 minutes)

### Eternal Life

The Bible says in John 11:25–26 — and these are the words of Jesus Himself: *"I am the resurrection and the life. The one who believes in me will live, even though they die; and whoever lives by believing in me will never die."*

My friends, because of the empty tomb, death is not the end. It is a doorway. For the Christian, the moment you take your last breath on this earth, the next thing you see is the face of Jesus. You step out of this life and into eternity — not because of anything you have done, but because of what *He* has done.

I have stood at the bedsides of many people who were dying. And I have seen a remarkable thing. Those who know Christ — those who have placed their faith in the risen Savior — they face death differently. I remember a dear woman, a grandmother, who was in her final hours. Her family was weeping around her bed. She opened her eyes and she smiled — she *smiled* — and she said, "Don't cry for me. I'm going home. I'm going to see my Jesus." And then she was gone. That is the hope of the resurrection. Death had lost its sting for that woman, because she knew the One who had conquered the grave.

### No Fear of Death

Because Jesus lives, you don't have to be afraid of death. I know that fear grips many of you tonight. You lie awake at night and you think about it. "What happens when I die? Is there anything on the other side? Will I just… cease to exist?" The resurrection answers every one of those questions. Jesus has been to the other side — and He came back. And He says to you tonight, "Because I live, you also will live."

### New Life Now

But the resurrection doesn't just promise you life *after* death — it offers you new life *right now.* The Bible says in Romans 6:4: *"Just as Christ was raised from the dead through the glory of the Father, we too may live a new life."*

Some of you are sitting here tonight and you are carrying burdens that are crushing you. Guilt. Addiction. A broken marriage. Loneliness so deep you can hardly breathe. Fear that you can't shake. I want you to know — the same power that raised Jesus from the dead is available to you *tonight.* God can take your broken life and make it new. He can take your emptiness and fill it with purpose. He can take your despair and replace it with a hope that will never, ever disappoint you.

That's the power of the resurrection. It's not just something that happened two thousand years ago. It is a living reality that can transform your life *today.*

---

### TRANSITION

And that brings me to my final point. And I want you to listen carefully, because everything I have said tonight leads to this.

---

## POINT 4: THE RESPONSE — Do You Believe? (5 minutes)

The resurrection demands a response. You cannot hear the evidence and simply walk away unchanged. Either Jesus Christ rose from the dead, or He didn't. And if He did — and I am telling you tonight on the authority of God's Word that *He did* — then everything changes.

The Bible says in Romans 10:9: *"If you declare with your mouth, 'Jesus is Lord,' and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved."*

Did you see what that verse says? It says if you *believe in your heart that God raised Him from the dead.* The resurrection is at the very center of the gospel. It is the thing you must believe.

Now, I know some of you are here tonight and you have doubts. You're like that young university student I told you about at the beginning. You have questions. You're not sure. Let me say something to you: *God is not afraid of your questions.* But I want to challenge you — have you honestly examined the evidence? Have you read the accounts with an open heart? Or have you simply decided ahead of time that it can't be true?

Thomas, one of Jesus' own disciples, doubted. He said, "Unless I see the nail marks in His hands and put my finger where the nails were, I will not believe." And do you know what Jesus did? He didn't reject Thomas. He came to him. He showed him His hands. He showed him His side. And He said, "Stop doubting and believe." And Thomas fell on his knees and cried out, *"My Lord and my God!"*

Jesus is here tonight, and He is saying the same thing to you: *"Stop doubting and believe."*

---

## ALTAR CALL (5 minutes)

And now I'm going to ask you to do something. I'm going to ask you to do what Thomas did. I'm going to ask you to come to the risen Christ tonight and say, "My Lord and my God."

I'm going to ask you to get up out of your seat — wherever you are in this place — and come and stand here at the front. I'm going to ask you to come as an act of faith. You come and say, "Tonight, I believe. I believe that Jesus died for me. I believe that God raised Him from the dead. And I want Him to be my Lord and my Savior."

You may be here tonight and you've been coming to church your whole life, but you've never had a personal encounter with the living Christ. You know *about* Him, but you don't *know* Him. This is your night. Come.

You may be here tonight and you've been running from God for years. You've been filling your life with everything except the one thing that can satisfy your soul. The empty tomb tells you that Jesus is alive — and He's been pursuing you. He's been calling your name. Tonight, stop running. Come home.

You may be here tonight and you're carrying a grief so heavy you don't think you can take another step. You've lost someone you love. You're afraid. You need the hope that only a risen Savior can give. Come.

I'm going to ask the choir to sing softly. And as they sing, you come. From up in the balcony, from the back, from the sides — you come. It's a long way, but Christ went a longer way for you. He went all the way from heaven to a cross to a grave — and then He walked out of that grave *for you.* The least you can do is walk these few steps to say, "Yes, Lord, I believe."

Come now. Don't put it off. Don't wait for another day. *The Bible says, "Today is the day of salvation."* Come while the Spirit of God is speaking to your heart. Come now.

---

## CLOSING PRAYER

Let us pray.

Our Father, we thank You tonight for the empty tomb. We thank You that on that glorious morning, when the stone was rolled away and the angels declared, "He is not here — He is risen" — the world was changed forever. Lord, we thank You that because Jesus lives, we can be forgiven. Because He lives, we need not fear death. Because He lives, we can have new life — tonight.

And now, Father, I pray for every man, woman, and young person who has come forward tonight. They are making the most important decision of their lives. Receive them, Lord. Forgive their sins. Write their names in the Lamb's Book of Life. Fill them with Your Holy Spirit. Give them the assurance — the deep, unshakable assurance — that because Jesus rose from the dead, they too will live forever in Your presence.

And for those who are still in their seats, still struggling, still unsure — I pray that Your Spirit would continue to work in their hearts. Do not let them rest until they rest in You.

We ask it all in the mighty, matchless, risen name of Jesus Christ our Lord.

*And all God's people said — Amen.*

# Sermon 3: "The Age of Grace — The Clock Is Ticking"

## Series: *The Heart of the Gospel* (Part 3 of 8)

**Theme:** We live in a unique window — the Church Age, the dispensation of grace
**Key Scriptures:** Ephesians 2:8-9 | 2 Corinthians 6:2 | Romans 11:25

---

## OPENING ILLUSTRATION (5 minutes)

I want to tell you a story tonight.

Some years ago, a young man in Charlotte, North Carolina — not far from where I grew up on a dairy farm — received a full scholarship offer to the university he had dreamed about his whole life. The letter came in the mail. It said, *"You have been selected. Full tuition. Full room and board. But you must respond by March 15th."*

He set the letter on his kitchen table. He said, "I've got time." He went about his business. March 1st came. He said, "I'll get to it next week." March 10th came. He thought, "I still have a few days." On the morning of March 16th, he sat down, filled out every form, drove to the post office — and the woman behind the counter looked at the envelope, looked at the date, and said, "Sir, I'm sorry. The deadline was yesterday."

He begged. He called the university. He wrote letters. But the door had closed.

Now, friends, that is a sad story about a college scholarship. But tonight I want to talk to you about something infinitely more important than any scholarship, any career, any opportunity this world has to offer. I want to talk to you about the most extraordinary window of opportunity in the history of the human race — and the fact that *it will not stay open forever.*

The Bible says we are living right now — tonight — in what Scripture calls **the Age of Grace.** God Almighty has flung open the door of salvation to every man, woman, boy, and girl on the face of this earth. The door is open wide. But the Bible also says — and I want you to hear me clearly — *God will not hold that door open forever.*

And so I want to ask you a question tonight, and I want you to think about it very carefully: **What are you waiting for?**

---

## POINT 1: THE DOOR IS OPEN — What the Age of Grace Means (8 minutes)

Let me begin by explaining something that many people — even many churchgoing people — have never fully understood.

The Bible says in **Ephesians 2:8-9**: *"For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: not of works, lest any man should boast."*

Now, what does that mean? It means that right now — in this present age — God is offering you something you could never earn, never buy, and never deserve. He is offering you **a free pardon.** He is offering you **complete forgiveness.** He is offering you **eternal life** — not because of anything you have done, but because of what Jesus Christ did for you on that cross two thousand years ago.

You see, throughout the Bible, God has dealt with humanity in different ways at different times. There was a time when He dealt with mankind through the law — the Ten Commandments, the sacrificial system, the priesthood. And do you know what the law proved? It proved that *not one single person could keep it.* The Bible says, "All have sinned and come short of the glory of God" (Romans 3:23). The law was like a mirror — it showed us how dirty we were, but it couldn't wash us clean.

But then God did something extraordinary. He sent His only Son, Jesus Christ, into this world. Jesus lived the perfect life that you and I could never live. He went to that old rugged cross and He died the death that you and I deserved to die. And when He rose from that grave on the third morning, He ushered in a brand-new era — **the Age of Grace.**

And here is what the Age of Grace means in plain language: **God is not counting your sins against you tonight.** He is holding out His hands to you. He is saying, "Come. Come as you are. Come with all your failures, all your guilt, all your shame. I will forgive you. I will cleanse you. I will make you new."

The Bible says in **2 Peter 3:9** that the Lord is *"not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance."* Did you hear that? God does not want you to perish. God does not want you to go to hell. God wants you to be saved. And *right now,* in this very hour, the door is wide open.

I have traveled to every continent on this earth. I have preached in stadiums and mud huts and palaces and prisons. And everywhere I go, I tell people the same thing: **You are living in the most gracious period in all of human history.** God's grace is available to the rich and the poor, the educated and the uneducated, the moral and the immoral. It doesn't matter what color your skin is. It doesn't matter what country you come from. It doesn't matter what you have done. The door is open — *tonight.*

---

### TRANSITION

But now I must tell you something else. And I would not be faithful to the Word of God if I did not say it. The door is open — *but it will not stay open forever.*

---

## POINT 2: THE DOOR WON'T STAY OPEN FOREVER — The Bible Warns Us (10 minutes)

Turn with me, if you will, to **Romans 11:25.** The Apostle Paul writes: *"For I would not, brethren, that ye should be ignorant of this mystery, lest ye should be wise in your own conceits; that blindness in part is happened to Israel, until the fulness of the Gentiles be come in."*

Now, I want you to notice that little word — **"until."** The Bible does not say the Age of Grace will last forever. It says it will last *until* something happens — until the full number of the Gentiles comes in. God has a number. God has a plan. God has a timetable. And when that last person walks through the door of grace, the Bible says that door is going to close.

Let me give you an illustration from the Old Testament. You remember the story of **Noah and the ark.** God told Noah that judgment was coming — a great flood that would cover the whole earth. And God told Noah to build an ark. For 120 years, Noah preached to the people. For 120 years, he warned them. For 120 years, the door of that ark stood open, and anyone — *anyone* — could have walked in and been saved.

But the people laughed. They mocked. They went on with their buying and selling, their eating and drinking, their marrying and giving in marriage. They said, "Old Noah is a fool. There's no flood coming."

And then one day — and I want you to see this — **Genesis 7:16** says: *"And the Lord shut him in."* Did you catch that? Noah didn't shut the door. *God shut the door.* And when God shut that door, it didn't matter how hard you knocked. It didn't matter how loud you cried. It didn't matter how sorry you were. The door was shut. And the rain began to fall.

My friends, the Bible says that what happened in Noah's day is a *picture* of what is going to happen again. Jesus Himself said in **Matthew 24:37-39**: *"But as the days of Noah were, so shall also the coming of the Son of man be. For as in the days that were before the flood they were eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage, until the day that Noe entered into the ark, and knew not until the flood came, and took them all away; so shall also the coming of the Son of man be."*

The Bible teaches that Jesus Christ is coming back. It may be today. It may be tomorrow. It may be before I finish this sermon tonight. And when He comes, the Age of Grace will be over. The Bible says in **1 Thessalonians 4:16-17** that the Lord Himself shall descend from heaven with a shout, and the dead in Christ shall rise first, and then we who are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them in the clouds to meet the Lord in the air. Theologians call it the Rapture. I call it the *closing of the door.*

And let me say something else that I think many people overlook. You do not have to wait for the Second Coming for your door to close. I have stood at the bedsides of men and women who waited too long. I have conducted funerals for people who said, "I'll get around to it next year." The Bible says, *"It is appointed unto men once to die, but after this the judgment"* (Hebrews 9:27). When you die, your opportunity is over. There are no second chances on the other side of the grave.

The door is open tonight. But I cannot promise you it will be open tomorrow morning.

---

### TRANSITION

And that brings me to my third point — and I pray that the Holy Spirit will drive this deep into your heart tonight.

---

## POINT 3: TODAY IS THE DAY — Why You Cannot Wait (8 minutes)

The Bible says in **2 Corinthians 6:2**: *"Behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation."*

The Bible does not say *tomorrow* is the day of salvation. It does not say *next week* is the day of salvation. It does not say *when you get your life together* is the day of salvation. It says **now.** It says **today.**

I want to tell you why you cannot afford to wait.

**First, you are not promised tomorrow.** I don't care how young you are tonight. I don't care how healthy you are. The Bible says your life is *"even a vapour, that appeareth for a little time, and then vanisheth away"* (James 4:14). I have known teenagers who went to bed healthy and never woke up. I have known businessmen who dropped dead at their desks at forty years old. You do not know — you *cannot* know — whether you will be alive one hour from now. And if you die without Christ, you will be lost forever.

**Second, the longer you wait, the harder your heart becomes.** The Bible warns about this again and again. **Hebrews 3:15** says: *"Today if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts."* Every time you hear the gospel and say "not yet," something happens inside of you. Your heart gets a little harder. Your conscience gets a little quieter. The voice of God gets a little fainter. I have met people who told me, "Billy, I used to feel something when I heard preaching. But I don't feel anything anymore." And that, my friend, is one of the most dangerous conditions a human soul can be in.

I remember hearing about a man in London — a successful man, a wealthy man — who attended one of our crusades in Harringay Arena in 1954. On the first night, his wife said he gripped the armrest of his seat so hard his knuckles turned white. He was under conviction. She whispered, "Why don't you go forward?" He said, "Not tonight. Maybe tomorrow night." They came back the next night. Again, he was moved. Again, he said, "Not tonight." They came back a third night. She looked over at him — and he was reading the program. His face was calm. He felt nothing. He never came back. Three months later, he died of a heart attack — without Christ.

Oh, my friend, do not gamble with your eternal soul! Do not play Russian roulette with eternity! The Bible says **today.** Not tomorrow. Not next week. Not next year. *Today.*

**Third, the devil is a liar, and his favorite lie is "there's always tomorrow."** Satan does not need you to become an atheist. He does not need you to become a criminal. He just needs you to *wait.* He just needs you to procrastinate. He whispers in your ear, "You're young. You have plenty of time." Or he says, "Wait until you clean up your life first." Or he says, "You can't give up your lifestyle." And year after year, people listen to that lie — until one day, time runs out.

The Bible says, *"How shall we escape, if we neglect so great salvation?"* (Hebrews 2:3). Notice it does not say *reject.* It says **neglect.** You don't have to stand up and shake your fist at God to be lost. You just have to do *nothing.* You just have to sit there. You just have to wait. Neglect will damn your soul just as surely as rebellion.

---

### TRANSITION

And so now I come to the most important part of this message. I have told you the door is open. I have told you it won't stay open forever. I have told you that today is the day. And now I must ask you the question that matters more than any question you will ever be asked in your life.

---

## POINT 4: THE RESPONSE — Will You Walk Through the Door? (5 minutes)

In **Revelation 3:20**, Jesus says: *"Behold, I stand at the door, and knock: if any man hear my voice, and open the door, I will come in to him, and will sup with him, and he with me."*

Did you know that Jesus Christ — the Son of the living God, the King of Kings and Lord of Lords — is knocking at the door of your heart *right now?* He is not breaking the door down. He is not forcing His way in. He is knocking. He is waiting. He is asking you to open the door.

But here is what you must understand: **He will not stand there forever.**

Some of you tonight are very close to the kingdom of God. You know you need Christ. You have felt the Holy Spirit tugging at your heart. Maybe it's been happening for weeks. Maybe for years. You know that something is missing in your life. You know that if you died tonight, you would not be ready to meet God. And the Spirit of God is saying to you right now, at this very moment: *"Come. This is your time. This is your moment. Don't let it pass."*

What does it mean to walk through the door? It is very simple. The Bible makes it so simple that a child can understand it.

**First, you must admit that you are a sinner.** The Bible says, "All have sinned and come short of the glory of God" (Romans 3:23). You cannot be saved until you admit you need saving.

**Second, you must believe that Jesus Christ died for your sins and rose again.** The Bible says, "Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and thou shalt be saved" (Acts 16:31). Not just believe in your head — but trust Him with your heart, the way you would trust a parachute to hold you when you jump from a plane.

**Third, you must receive Him — personally, deliberately, right now.** The Bible says, "As many as received him, to them gave he power to become the sons of God" (John 1:12). You must open the door of your heart and invite Jesus Christ to come in as your Lord and your Savior.

Will you do that tonight? Will you walk through the door while it is still open?

---

## ALTAR CALL (5 minutes)

I'm going to ask you to do something now. I'm going to ask you to get up out of your seat — from all over this arena — and come and stand here in front of this platform. I'm going to ask you to come and say, by your coming, *"Tonight, I am walking through the door. Tonight, I am giving my life to Jesus Christ."*

You say, "Billy, why do I have to come forward? Can't I just accept Christ where I am?" Yes, you can. But Jesus called people publicly. He said, *"Whosoever shall confess me before men, him shall the Son of man also confess before the angels of God"* (Luke 12:8). There is something about that act of getting up and coming forward that helps to settle it in your heart.

You may be here tonight and you've been a church member all your life, but you have never had a personal relationship with Jesus Christ. I want you to come.

You may be here tonight and you've wandered far from God. You knew Him once, but you've drifted away. I want you to come home tonight.

You may be here tonight and you've never set foot in a church before. You don't know the Bible. You don't know the hymns. But something inside you is telling you that this is real. I want you to come.

Don't let this moment pass. Don't walk out of here tonight and say, "I'll do it next time." There may not *be* a next time. The door is open **now.** Come now. Come while the choir sings. Come while the Spirit is calling. Come just as you are.

*"Just as I am, without one plea, but that Thy blood was shed for me, and that Thou bidd'st me come to Thee, O Lamb of God, I come, I come..."*

---

## CLOSING PRAYER

Let us pray.

Our Heavenly Father, we thank You tonight for Your grace. We do not deserve it. We cannot earn it. But You offer it freely, through the blood of Your Son, Jesus Christ.

Lord, I pray for every person in this place tonight who is hearing Your voice. I pray that You would break through every wall of pride. Break through every excuse. Break through every lie of the enemy that says, "Wait. There's always tomorrow."

Father, for those who are coming forward right now, I pray that You would receive them. Forgive their sins. Write their names in the Lamb's Book of Life. Fill them with Your Holy Spirit. Make them new creations in Christ Jesus.

And Lord, for anyone who is hesitating — anyone who is sitting in their seat right now, gripping the armrest, feeling the pull of the Holy Spirit — I pray that You would give them the courage to stand up and come. Before it is too late. Before the door closes.

For we pray all of this in the mighty, saving, matchless name of Jesus Christ our Lord.

And all God's people said — **Amen.**

# Sermon 4: "Sin — The Disease Nobody Wants to Talk About"

### *The Heart of the Gospel* Series (Sermon 4 of 8)

**Theme:** Why We Need a Savior in the First Place
**Key Scriptures:** Romans 3:23 | Romans 6:23 | Jeremiah 17:9 | Isaiah 64:6 | Romans 5:8

---

## OPENING ILLUSTRATION (5 minutes)

A number of years ago, a man went to see his doctor. He had been feeling tired for months. He had no energy. He was losing weight he couldn't afford to lose. But he kept telling himself, "I'm fine. I just need a little more rest." His wife begged him to go get checked. His children pleaded with him. But he refused. He said, "I don't want to hear bad news."

Finally — finally — he went. And the doctor sat him down and said, "Sir, I have to be honest with you. You have a serious illness. If we had caught it six months ago, treatment would have been simple. But you waited. And now this is going to be the fight of your life."

And that man looked at the doctor with tears running down his face, and he said, "I knew something was wrong. I just didn't want anyone to *say* it."

My friends, I want to talk to you tonight about a diagnosis that nobody wants to hear. I want to talk to you about a disease that has infected every man, every woman, every boy and every girl who has ever lived on the face of this earth. It is the most ancient disease. It is the most deadly disease. And it is the disease that nobody — *nobody* — wants to talk about.

I'm talking about **sin**.

Now, I know that's not a popular word. You won't hear it much on television. You won't read it in the magazines. The world has tried to rename it. They call it "a mistake." They call it "a poor choice." They call it "just being human." But the Bible doesn't call it any of those things. **The Bible calls it sin.** And until you and I are willing to face that diagnosis honestly, we will never understand why we need the cure.

You see, you cannot treat what you will not diagnose. A doctor who tells you you're healthy when you're dying is not a kind doctor — he's a *dangerous* doctor. And a preacher who stands in a pulpit and refuses to talk about sin is not a loving preacher. He is leaving you to die without a remedy.

So tonight, I want us to open the Word of God together. And I want us to look honestly — maybe more honestly than we ever have — at what the Bible says about this disease called sin. And then — *and then* — I want to show you the cure. Because, my friend, there *is* a cure. And His name is Jesus Christ.

---

## POINT 1: THE DIAGNOSIS — What Sin Really Is (10 minutes)

Now, when most people hear the word *sin*, they think of the big things. They think of murder. They think of robbery. They think of the man sitting in a prison cell. And they look at their own lives and they say, "Well, I'm not *that* bad."

And I want to say to you tonight — gently, but honestly — **that is the most dangerous thought you can think.**

Let me tell you what the Bible says. Turn with me to **Romans chapter 3, verse 23**. The Apostle Paul writes these words: *"For ALL have sinned and fall short of the glory of God."*

Did you hear that word? **ALL.** Not some. Not most. Not the really bad ones. ALL. That includes me. That includes you. That includes the finest, most moral, most upstanding person in this arena tonight.

Now, what *is* sin? Let me give you a definition that I think you'll understand. Sin, at its root, is not primarily about what you *do*. Sin is about the condition of your *heart*. The prophet Jeremiah wrote in **chapter 17, verse 9**: *"The heart is deceitful above all things and desperately wicked. Who can know it?"*

Sin is **rebellion against God.** It is the creature shaking his fist at the Creator and saying, "I will run my own life. I don't need You. I will be my own god." That is what happened in the Garden of Eden. The serpent came to Eve and said, "You will be *like* God." And that has been the temptation of every human heart ever since.

Sin is **pride.** It is the quiet, respectable, well-dressed belief that I am the center of my own universe. You don't have to rob a bank to be full of pride. You just have to live as though God doesn't matter.

Sin is **self-worship.** We bow before the mirror every morning and we don't even know we're doing it. My plans. My ambitions. My desires. My comfort. My reputation. And God is an afterthought — if He's a thought at all.

Sin is **indifference to God.** And I want you to hear me carefully: some of the most sinful people in the world are not in prisons tonight. They're in nice homes, with nice families, living nice lives — and they have *never once* surrendered their hearts to Almighty God. They are moral. They are decent. And they are **lost.**

The prophet Isaiah put it this way, in **chapter 64, verse 6**: *"All our righteous acts are like filthy rags."* Not our *sins* — our *righteous acts*! The very best things you and I can do, when they are done apart from God, are stained with the infection of self. We do good things for selfish reasons. We give so others will think well of us. We serve so we can feel good about ourselves. Even our goodness is contaminated.

That is the diagnosis. And it applies to every one of us.

---

## TRANSITION

Now, I've told you what sin *is*. But I want you to see what sin *does*. Because this disease doesn't stay hidden. It shows itself. It has symptoms. And if you're honest tonight — really honest — you'll recognize them in your own life.

---

## POINT 2: THE SYMPTOMS — How Sin Shows Up in Our Lives (10 minutes)

**The first symptom is broken relationships.** Have you noticed how hard it is for human beings to get along? Husbands and wives who once loved each other can barely stand to be in the same room. Parents and children are estranged. Friendships shatter over petty offenses. Nations go to war. And we wonder, "What is *wrong* with us?" I'll tell you what's wrong. Sin. Sin makes us selfish. Sin makes us defensive. Sin makes us hold grudges and refuse to forgive. Every broken relationship on this earth is a *symptom* of the disease.

**The second symptom is emptiness.** I have talked to some of the most famous, most wealthy, most successful people in the world. And do you know what many of them have told me, in private, when the cameras were off? They said, "Billy, I have everything — and I have *nothing*. There is a hole inside of me that nothing can fill." King Solomon, the richest man who ever lived, tried everything — wealth, pleasure, knowledge, power — and at the end of it all he said in **Ecclesiastes 1:2**, *"Vanity of vanities! All is vanity."* Meaningless. Empty. That emptiness you feel at three o'clock in the morning — that is a symptom of sin. It is your soul crying out for the God it was made to know.

**The third symptom is guilt.** Real guilt. Not the kind a therapist can talk you out of. I mean the deep, abiding sense that something is *wrong*. That you have offended a holy God. That you are not the person you were meant to be. People spend billions of dollars and millions of hours trying to silence that guilt — through therapy, through alcohol, through entertainment, through staying so busy they never have to sit alone with their own thoughts. But it doesn't go away. It *can't* go away. Because it is the alarm bell of the soul. It is telling you the truth.

**The fourth symptom is addiction.** Not just addiction to drugs or alcohol — though those are devastating. I'm talking about addiction to approval. Addiction to control. Addiction to pleasure. Addiction to your phone, your social media, your work. Anything that you *cannot stop doing* even though it's destroying you — that is a symptom of sin's power. The Apostle Paul described it perfectly in **Romans 7:19**: *"For the good that I want to do, I do not do, but the evil I do not want to do — this I keep on doing."* That is the experience of every honest human being. We are enslaved.

**The fifth symptom is the endless chase — chasing things that never satisfy.** More money. More success. More relationships. More experiences. We get what we wanted and the satisfaction lasts — what? A week? A day? An hour? And then we're chasing again. Because *things* were never meant to satisfy the deepest hunger of the human heart. Only God can do that. And sin keeps us running in every direction *except* toward Him.

---

## TRANSITION

But now I have to tell you something that is very serious. And I ask you to listen carefully. Because the Bible is honest about where this disease leads if it is not treated.

---

## POINT 3: THE PROGNOSIS — Where Sin Leads (8 minutes)

**Romans chapter 6, verse 23** says it as plainly as it can be said: *"For the wages of sin is death."*

The **wages** of sin. That means it's what sin *earns*. It's what sin *pays*. And the Bible says that what sin earns — what it pays — is **death.**

Now, the Bible speaks of death in three ways.

**First, there is physical death.** The Bible says that death entered the world through sin. Every funeral. Every hospital. Every grave in every cemetery is a reminder that something has gone terribly, terribly wrong with the human race. We were not *made* to die. Death is an intruder. Death is an enemy. And it came because of sin.

**Second, there is spiritual death.** This is the separation of the human soul from God — and this can happen while you are still breathing. You can be alive physically and dead spiritually. You can sit in a church pew and have no living connection to the God who made you. **Ephesians 2:1** says, *"You were dead in your trespasses and sins."* Dead. Not sick. Not weak. *Dead.*

**Third — and this is the hardest thing I ever have to say — there is eternal death.** The Bible calls it the second death. It is eternal separation from God. It is what Jesus described more than any other person in all of Scripture. He spoke of a place of outer darkness. A place of weeping and gnashing of teeth. A place where the soul is separated from God forever and ever and ever.

Now, I don't say that to frighten you. I say that because it is the *truth*. And because I believe with all my heart that God sent me here tonight to warn you — the way a doctor warns a patient — that this disease is *fatal* if it is not treated.

That is the prognosis. And it is devastating.

**But** — and I want you to hear this word — **BUT...**

That is *not* the end of the story.

---

## POINT 4: THE CURE — "But God..." (10 minutes)

There are two of the most powerful words in all the Bible. You'll find them in **Ephesians 2:4**. After describing our hopeless, helpless, dead-in-sin condition, the Apostle Paul writes two words that change *everything*:

**"But God..."**

But God. We were dead — *but God.* We were lost — *but God.* We were condemned — *but God.* We were without hope and without help and without remedy — **BUT GOD.**

My friend, listen to me. Turn with me to **Romans chapter 5, verse 8**. This is the heartbeat of the Gospel: *"But God demonstrates His own love for us in this: while we were still sinners, Christ died for us."*

Did you hear that? While we were **still sinners.** Not after we cleaned ourselves up. Not after we got our act together. Not after we became worthy. While we were still in our rebellion — **God ran toward us.**

And He ran to a cross.

Two thousand years ago, on a hill outside the city of Jerusalem, they took the Son of God — the spotless, sinless, perfect Son of God — and they nailed Him to a Roman cross. And on that cross, something happened that the human mind can barely comprehend. The Bible says that God took *our* sin — your sin and mine — and He placed it on Jesus. **Second Corinthians 5:21**: *"God made Him who had no sin to be sin for us, so that in Him we might become the righteousness of God."*

He took *our* disease. He took *our* diagnosis. He took *our* death sentence. And He paid it. In full. With His own blood.

And now, look at the rest of **Romans 6:23**. We read the first half: *"The wages of sin is death."* But listen to the rest: *"BUT the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord."*

Wages and gift. Sin *pays* you death. You've earned it. But God *gives* you life. You haven't earned it. It's a gift. All you have to do — all you *can* do — is reach out and receive it.

---

## ALTAR CALL (7 minutes)

And now I'm going to ask you to do something. I'm going to ask you to do the most important thing you've ever done in your life.

I'm going to ask you to get up out of your seat — right now, wherever you are in this arena — and come forward. Come stand here in front of this platform. I'm going to ask you to come to Christ.

Some of you tonight came in here knowing something was wrong. You've felt the symptoms. The emptiness. The guilt. The restlessness. The broken places in your life that nothing has been able to fix. You've tried everything. And tonight, for the first time, you understand *why* nothing has worked. You have a disease that only Jesus can cure.

Some of you are moral, decent, good people. You've never been in serious trouble. But deep down, you know that you have never given your life to Christ. You've been running your own show. And the Holy Spirit is speaking to you right now, saying, "Tonight is the night."

Some of you are far from God. You've wandered. You've run. You've done things you're ashamed of. And you think, "God could never forgive *me*." My friend, listen: *while we were still sinners, Christ died for us.* There is nothing you have done that the blood of Jesus cannot cover. **Nothing.**

I'm going to ask you to come. Get up out of your seat. It may be a long walk. But it's the most important walk you'll ever take. Come right now.

---

## CLOSING PRAYER

*Our Heavenly Father, I thank You tonight for Your Word, which is truth. I thank You that You did not leave us in our sin. You sent Your only Son, the Lord Jesus Christ, to take our disease upon Himself — to die our death — so that we might live.*

*And for those who are giving their lives to Christ for the very first time, I ask them to pray this prayer with me — right now, silently, in their hearts:*

**"Dear God, I know I am a sinner. I am sorry for my sins. I am willing to turn from my sins. I receive Jesus Christ as my Savior. I confess Him as my Lord. From this day forward, I want to follow Him and serve Him in the fellowship of His Church. In Jesus' name, Amen."**

*If you prayed that prayer and meant it from your heart, the Bible says you have been forgiven. You have been made new. You have passed from death to life. Old things have passed away — behold, ALL things have become new.*

*Welcome to the family of God.*

*In the name of the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit. Amen.*

# Sermon 5: "Born Again — What Jesus Really Meant"

### Series: *The Heart of the Gospel* (Part 5 of 8)

**Theme:** Salvation is not religion — it's regeneration
**Key Scriptures:** John 3:1–7 | 2 Corinthians 5:17 | Titus 3:5 | Ezekiel 36:26

---

## OPENING STORY (5–6 minutes)

Some years ago, I read about a man in New York City — a very successful man. He was a surgeon. One of the best in the country. He lived in a beautiful penthouse apartment overlooking Central Park. He had a wife who loved him, three children in the finest schools, and more money than he could spend in two lifetimes. If you had passed him on the street, you would have said, "Now *there* is a man who has it all together."

But one night, this surgeon sat alone at his kitchen table at two o'clock in the morning, and he wrote a letter to his wife. And in that letter he said, "I have spent my whole life fixing other people's hearts — but nobody has been able to fix mine. I have everything a man could want, and I have *nothing* that a man truly needs."

Friends, that letter was found the next morning. That man had tried to take his own life. By the grace of God, he survived. And do you know what brought him back from the edge? It was not another degree. It was not a vacation. It was not a new car or a promotion. A hospital chaplain sat by his bed and opened a Bible — a simple Bible — and read him the words of Jesus from the third chapter of John: *"You must be born again."*

That surgeon gave his life to Jesus Christ in that hospital room, and he later said — and I'll never forget this — he said, "For fifty-two years, I was alive on the outside and dead on the inside. Then Jesus made me alive for the first time."

Tonight, I want to talk to you about what it means to be *born again*. Because there is someone here — someone listening to my voice right now — and you are just like that surgeon. You look fine on the outside. You've got the right clothes. You've got the right smile. You say the right things. But deep down inside, in a place no one else can see, there is an emptiness. There is a restlessness. There is a hunger that nothing in this world has been able to fill.

And Jesus Christ has something to say to you tonight. He said it two thousand years ago to a very important, very religious man. And He says it to you now: **"You must be born again."**

---

## POINT 1: The Religious Man Who Wasn't Saved — Nicodemus (10–12 minutes)

Turn with me in your Bibles to the Gospel of John, chapter 3.

The Bible says in John 3, verse 1: *"There was a man of the Pharisees, named Nicodemus, a ruler of the Jews."*

Now I want you to understand who this man was. Nicodemus was not some criminal. He was not some drunkard. He was not some man living in open, scandalous sin. No. Nicodemus was one of the most respected men in all of Israel.

He was a Pharisee — and that means he kept the Law of Moses down to the smallest detail. He tithed. He fasted. He prayed three times a day. He knew the Scriptures by memory — not just a few verses, but great portions of what we call the Old Testament.

He was a *ruler* of the Jews — a member of the Sanhedrin, the Jewish Supreme Court. There were only seventy men in the entire nation who held that position.

And if you had taken a survey of the people in Jerusalem and asked, "Who is going to heaven?" I guarantee you that every single person would have put Nicodemus at the top of the list.

But look at what happens. The Bible says Nicodemus came to Jesus *by night*. He came under the cover of darkness. Why? I believe it was because something was missing, and he knew it. All of his religion, all of his education, all of his moral living — it had left him empty. He had a hunger that all his righteousness could not satisfy.

Now watch what Jesus does. Jesus does not say, "Well, Nicodemus, you're doing a fine job. Keep up the good work." No. **The Bible says** in John 3, verse 3: *"Jesus answered and said unto him, Verily, verily, I say unto thee, Except a man be born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God."*

Do you see what Jesus did? He looked at the most religious man in the nation and said, "Nicodemus — *it's not enough.*"

Your religion is not enough. Your morality is not enough. Your knowledge of the Bible is not enough. Your position in the church is not enough. **You must be born again.**

That word "must" — I want you to circle it if you've got a pen. Jesus did not say you *should* be born again. He did not say it would be *nice* if you were born again. He said you **MUST** be born again. It is a divine necessity. There is no other way.

I've had people come to me after a Crusade and say, "But Billy, I've been a church member for forty years!" And I say to them gently, kindly, but honestly — "Friend, you can sit in a garage for forty years, and that does not make you an automobile." Church membership is a wonderful thing. But church membership without the new birth is like having a beautiful car with no engine. It *looks* good in the driveway, but it is not going anywhere.

---

### Transition

Now, Nicodemus was confused. He said, "How can a man be born when he is old?" He was thinking in physical terms. So let's look at what Jesus *really* meant.

---

## POINT 2: What Born Again Means — A New Creation (8–10 minutes)

When Jesus said, "You must be born again," He was not talking about physical birth. He was talking about a *spiritual* transformation — a miracle that only God can perform in the human heart.

**The Bible says** in 2 Corinthians 5:17: *"Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new."*

Did you hear that? Not a *reformed* creature. Not an *improved* creature. A **new** creature.

There is a tremendous difference between *reformation* and *regeneration*. Let me explain it so simply that a child could understand it.

Reformation says, "I'm going to turn over a new leaf." Regeneration says, "God has given me a *new life*."

Reformation says, "I'm going to try harder." Regeneration says, "God has made me *different*."

Reformation is like taking a pig out of the mud, cleaning him up, putting a pink bow on his head, and spraying him with perfume. He looks good. He smells good. But the first chance he gets — he's going right back into the mud. Why? Because his *nature* has not changed. He is still a pig.

But regeneration — being born again — that's when God changes the pig into a lamb. A lamb doesn't *want* the mud. A lamb has a new nature.

And that is what God does when you are born again. He doesn't just clean up your outside. He changes your *inside*. He gives you new desires, new affections, new longings. Things you used to love, you begin to hate. Things you used to ignore — like prayer, like the Bible, like fellowship with God's people — you begin to hunger for them the way a starving man hungers for bread.

**The Bible says** in Ezekiel 36:26: *"A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh."*

**The Bible says** in Titus 3:5: *"Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to His mercy He saved us, by the washing of regeneration, and renewing of the Holy Ghost."*

That word *regeneration* means "a new beginning." When you receive Jesus Christ, the Holy Spirit of God comes to live inside of you, and He makes you alive spiritually. You were dead — and now you are alive. You were blind — and now you can see. You were lost — and now you are found.

---

### Transition

Now, because there is so much confusion about this, I want to take a moment and tell you what being born again is *not*.

---

## POINT 3: What Born Again Is NOT (6–8 minutes)

There are many people who think they have been born again, but they are deceived. They are resting on something that *cannot save them*. And I want to be very clear tonight, because eternity is at stake.

**First: Being born again is NOT the same as being a church member.** I love the church. I believe in the church. But friend, you can have your name on every church roll in this city and still be lost. Church membership does not save you. *Jesus* saves you.

**Second: Being born again is NOT the same as being baptized.** Baptism is important. Baptism is an act of obedience. Every believer should be baptized. But baptism is an outward sign of an inward change — it is not the change itself. You can be dunked in every river from here to the Jordan, and if your heart has not been changed by the power of God, you will come up out of that water just as lost as you went in — only wetter.

**Third: Being born again is NOT the same as being a good person.** The Bible says *all* our righteousnesses are as filthy rags before a holy God (Isaiah 64:6). You cannot be good *enough*. The standard is not *good*. The standard is *perfect*. And only Jesus Christ has met that standard.

**Fourth — and this is one of the most important things I will say tonight: Being born again is NOT something you inherit from your parents.** I want you to listen to me very carefully. **God has no grandchildren. He only has children.** Your mother's faith cannot save you. Your father's prayers, as precious as they are, cannot take your place before God. Every person must make their *own* decision. Every person must be born again for themselves.

I think of a young man who came to one of our Crusades years ago in London. He was the son of a pastor. He had grown up in the church. He had been baptized, confirmed, and had attended Sunday School since before he could walk. And when I gave the invitation that night, he came forward with tears streaming down his face. The counselor asked him, "What brings you forward tonight?" And he said, "All my life, I've been living off my father's faith. Tonight, I need a faith of my own."

Friend, do you have a faith of your own? Or are you borrowing someone else's?

---

### Transition

You may be saying, "All right, Billy, I understand. I need to be born again. But *how*? What do I do?"

---

## POINT 4: How to Be Born Again — Simple Steps of Faith (8–10 minutes)

The new birth is a work of God. You cannot *make* yourself be born again any more than you could make yourself be born the first time. But there is something you must do. You must *respond* to what God is offering.

And I want to give you three simple steps tonight.

### Step 1: Acknowledge your sin.

**The Bible says** in Romans 3:23: *"For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God."*

You must be honest with God. You must come to the place where you say, "Lord, I am a sinner. I have broken Your laws. I have gone my own way. I cannot save myself."

### Step 2: Believe in Jesus Christ.

**The Bible says** in Romans 10:9: *"If thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised Him from the dead, thou shalt be saved."*

This is not just intellectual belief. The devil believes that Jesus existed — and he trembles. No, this is *heart* belief. This is trusting. This is staking your whole life, your whole eternity, on the fact that Jesus Christ is the Son of God, that He died on the cross for your sins, that He was buried, and that He rose again on the third day.

### Step 3: Receive Him.

**The Bible says** in John 1:12: *"But as many as received Him, to them gave He power to become the sons of God, even to them that believe on His name."*

You must *receive* Him. Jesus is standing at the door of your heart right now. **The Bible says** in Revelation 3:20: *"Behold, I stand at the door, and knock: if any man hear My voice, and open the door, I will come in."*

He will not force His way in. He is a gentleman. He knocks. He calls. He waits. But *you* must open the door. *You* must say, "Come in, Lord Jesus. Come into my life. Forgive my sins. Make me new. I receive You as my Lord and my Savior."

And when you do that — in that very moment — the Holy Spirit of God enters your life, and you are *born again*. You become a child of God. Not because you earned it. Not because you deserved it. But because God, in His great love and mercy, *gave* it to you as a free gift.

---

## ALTAR CALL (5–6 minutes)

And now I'm going to ask you to do something. I'm going to ask you to get up out of your seat — right now — and come and stand here in front of this platform. I'm going to ask you to come and say by your coming, "Tonight, I want to be born again. Tonight, I open the door of my heart to Jesus Christ."

You may be here with friends. You may be here with family. They'll wait for you. I promise.

You may have come from a long distance tonight. But I tell you, the most important distance you will ever travel is the distance from your seat to this platform. It is the distance between your old life and your new life.

I'm going to ask you to come now. From the upper stands, from the lower stands, from every section of this arena. Young and old. Rich and poor. Whatever your background, whatever you've done, wherever you've been — Jesus Christ will receive you tonight.

You come. While the choir sings softly, you come.

*[Choir sings "Just As I Am"]*

That's it. Just get up. Step into the aisle. Others are coming. You come too. Don't let this moment pass. **The Bible says**, *"Now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation"* (2 Corinthians 6:2).

---

## CLOSING PRAYER

> *"Dear Lord Jesus, I know that I am a sinner. I have lived my life my own way, and I am sorry. I believe that You are the Son of God. I believe that You died on the cross for my sins and that You rose again from the dead. Right now, I open the door of my heart, and I receive You as my Savior and my Lord. Come into my life. Forgive me. Cleanse me. Make me new. Make me the person You created me to be. I turn from my old life, and I choose to follow You — from this day forward, forever. In the name of Jesus Christ, I pray. Amen."*

If you prayed that prayer and you meant it from your heart — I want to tell you on the authority of God's Word: you have been born again. You are a new creation. The old has passed away. The new has come. Welcome to the family of God.

Now, I want you to do three things starting tonight. **First**, get a Bible and read it every day. Start with the Gospel of John. **Second**, pray. Talk to God like you would talk to your best friend — because that is what He is now. **Third**, find a church where the Bible is preached and where Christ is honored, and get planted there.

God bless you. And may the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all.

# Sermon 6: "Revelation — The King Is Coming Back"

### Series: *The Heart of the Gospel* (Part 6 of 8)

**Theme:** Jesus's return and why it matters today
**Key Scriptures:** Revelation 1:7, Revelation 19:11-16, 1 Thessalonians 4:16-17, Acts 1:11, Matthew 24:44

---

## OPENING STORY (4-5 minutes)

In June of 1953, the entire nation of England came to a standstill. Queen Elizabeth II was about to be crowned at Westminster Abbey. For months — *months* — the people prepared. They cleaned the streets of London until they shone. They hung banners from every lamppost. They sewed new clothes. Families who had never owned a television set went out and bought one just so they could watch the coronation. Schoolchildren practiced their flag-waving. Soldiers polished their boots and pressed their uniforms until there was not a single wrinkle.

And do you know why they did all of that? Because royalty was coming. And when royalty comes, *you get ready*.

My friends, I want to tell you something tonight that is far more important than any earthly coronation. *A King is coming.* Not a king who inherits a throne from a parent. Not a king who rules over one nation. But the King of Kings and the Lord of Lords — Jesus Christ Himself — is coming back to this earth.

And most of the world is not ready.

The Bible says in Revelation 1:7, *"Behold, He is coming with clouds, and every eye will see Him."*

Every eye. Not some eyes. Not just the eyes of believers. *Every eye will see Him.*

And the question I want to put to you tonight, as simply and as directly as I know how, is this: *Are you ready?*

---

## POINT 1: The Promise — He Said He Would Return (5-6 minutes)

Turn with me to the book of Acts, chapter 1, verse 11. After Jesus rose from the dead, He spent forty days with His disciples. He taught them. He ate with them. He showed them the scars in His hands. And then, while they were watching, He ascended into heaven. A cloud received Him out of their sight.

And those disciples stood there, staring up into the sky with their mouths open. And then two angels appeared. And the Bible records their words: *"Men of Galilee, why do you stand gazing up into heaven? This same Jesus, who was taken up from you into heaven, will so come in like manner as you saw Him go into heaven."*

This *same* Jesus. Not a different one. Not a symbolic Jesus. Not an idea. *This same Jesus.* The one who was born in Bethlehem. The one who walked on water. The one who healed the blind. The one who died on a Roman cross and rose from the grave on the third day. *That* Jesus is coming back.

In John 14:3, Jesus said with His own lips, *"If I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again and receive you to Myself; that where I am, there you may be also."*

"I *will* come again." That is not a suggestion. That is not a maybe. That is a promise from the Son of the Living God.

Did you know there are over three hundred prophecies in the Bible about the second coming of Christ? There were about three hundred prophecies concerning His first coming — and every single one of them was fulfilled. If God kept every promise about the *first* coming, you can be absolutely certain He will keep every promise about the *second*.

Jesus is coming back. That is not my opinion. That is the promise of God, and God does not lie.

---

## POINT 2: The Picture — What His Return Looks Like (5-6 minutes)

Now I want you to turn with me to Revelation chapter 19, beginning at verse 11, because I want you to *see* something tonight.

The Bible says:

*"Now I saw heaven opened, and behold, a white horse. And He who sat on it was called Faithful and True, and in righteousness He judges and makes war. His eyes were like a flame of fire, and on His head were many crowns. He had a name written that no one knew except Himself. He was clothed with a robe dipped in blood, and His name is called The Word of God. And the armies in heaven, clothed in fine linen, white and clean, followed Him on white horses. Now out of His mouth goes a sharp sword, that with it He should strike the nations. And He Himself will rule them with a rod of iron. He Himself treads the winepress of the fierceness and wrath of Almighty God. And He has on His robe and on His thigh a name written: KING OF KINGS AND LORD OF LORDS."*

My friend, do you see that picture? That is not a baby in a manger. That is a *King on a white horse.* His eyes are blazing with fire. On His head are *many* crowns. His robe is dipped in blood because He has already fought the battle at Calvary and won. And written on His robe is the name that settles every question: KING OF KINGS AND LORD OF LORDS.

The same Jesus who came the first time as a Lamb — meek, humble, willing to be led to the slaughter — is coming back as the *Lion of Judah.*

The Bible says in Philippians 2:10-11 that at the name of Jesus, *"every knee should bow, of those in heaven, and of those on earth, and of those under the earth, and every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is Lord."*

The question is not *whether* you will bow before Jesus. The question is *when*. Will you bow before Him tonight, willingly, in love and repentance? Or will you bow before Him on that day, when it is too late?

---

## POINT 3: The Signs — We Are Living in the Last Days (5-6 minutes)

In Matthew chapter 24, the disciples asked Jesus, "What will be the sign of Your coming, and of the end of the age?"

And Jesus answered them. He said in Matthew 24:6-7, *"You will hear of wars and rumors of wars... For nation will rise against nation, and kingdom against kingdom. And there will be famines, pestilences, and earthquakes in various places."*

He said in Matthew 24:12, *"Because lawlessness will abound, the love of many will grow cold."*

He said in Matthew 24:14, *"And this gospel of the kingdom will be preached in all the world as a witness to all the nations, and then the end will come."*

Now I am not a date-setter. The Bible says no man knows the day or the hour — not even the angels in heaven. Anyone who tells you they know the exact date of Christ's return is not reading the same Bible I am reading.

But Jesus told us to *watch*. He told us to be *aware*. He told us to read the signs of the times.

And my friends, I look at the world tonight, and I see those signs everywhere. Wars and conflicts on nearly every continent. The love of many growing cold. And the gospel being preached to the ends of the earth in ways that would have been unimaginable even fifty years ago.

The Bible says in Romans 13:11, *"Now it is high time to awake out of sleep; for now our salvation is nearer than when we first believed."*

We are closer tonight than we have ever been. Every tick of the clock brings us nearer to the return of the King.

---

## POINT 4: The Question — Are You Ready? (5-6 minutes)

And that brings me to the most important part of my message tonight.

*Are you ready?*

Jesus said in Matthew 24:44, *"Therefore you also be ready, for the Son of Man is coming at an hour you do not expect."*

Jesus told a parable about ten young women — ten virgins — who were waiting for a bridegroom. Five of them were wise. They brought oil for their lamps. Five of them were foolish. They did not prepare.

And when the bridegroom came at midnight — at an unexpected hour — the five who were ready went in to the wedding feast. And the five who were not ready were shut out. And when they knocked on the door and cried out, "Lord, Lord, open to us!" — do you know what He said? The Bible says He answered, *"I do not know you."*

Those are the most terrifying words in all of Scripture. "I do not know you."

My friend, let me speak to you personally tonight. If you have never personally surrendered your life to Jesus Christ — if you have never repented of your sin and put your trust in Him as your Lord and Savior — then you are not ready.

And He could come tonight.

---

## ALTAR CALL (4-5 minutes)

I am going to ask you to do something. I am going to ask you to get up out of your seat, right now, and come forward. Come and stand here at the front. I am going to ask you to make the most important decision of your life.

You may be here tonight and you have never received Christ. Come. This is your moment.

You may be here tonight and you once walked with God, but you have wandered far from Him. Come back. He is waiting for you.

You may be here tonight and there is something in your heart — a restlessness, a conviction, a quiet voice saying, "This is for you." That is the Holy Spirit speaking to you. Do not ignore Him.

I want you to come. Get up out of your seat. It will only take a moment. You come now, while the choir sings softly. You come, while there is still time.

---

## CLOSING PRAYER

Our Father in Heaven, we thank You tonight for the promise of Your Son's return. We thank You that He is faithful and true. We thank You that what You have promised, You will perform.

Lord, there are men and women and young people standing here tonight who have made the decision to give their lives to You. Seal this decision in their hearts. Forgive them of their sins. Wash them clean by the blood of Jesus Christ. Write their names in the Lamb's Book of Life.

And for all of us here tonight, Lord, help us to live as those who are watching and waiting. Help us to be faithful. Help us to be ready. Help us to live every day as though it could be the day Your Son breaks through the clouds.

We ask this in the precious and mighty name of Jesus Christ, our coming King.

*Amen.*

# Sermon 7: "The Bible — God's Love Letter to You"

### *The Heart of the Gospel Series — Part 7 of 8*

**Theme:** The whole Bible tells one story — redemption through Jesus Christ
**Key Scriptures:** Luke 24:27 | 2 Timothy 3:16 | Hebrews 1:1-2 | John 5:39 | Psalm 119:105

---

## OPENING ILLUSTRATION (5 minutes)

A few years ago, I heard a story that I have never been able to forget.

A young woman named Catherine lost her mother when she was only nineteen years old. It was sudden — a heart attack — and Catherine never got to say goodbye. For years she carried that grief like a stone in her chest. She would tell people, "I never got to hear the things I needed to hear from her."

Ten years went by. Catherine was cleaning out her childhood home, getting it ready to sell, when she found a shoebox in the back of her mother's closet. Inside that box were letters. *Dozens* of letters. Each one had a date on the envelope — her twentieth birthday, her wedding day, the birth of her first child, her thirtieth birthday. Her mother, who had been battling heart trouble for years and knew she might not live long, had written letters ahead of time for all the milestones she was afraid she would miss.

Catherine sat on that bedroom floor and *wept*. Not tears of sorrow — tears of wonder. She said, "My mother had been speaking to me for ten years, and I didn't know it. The letters were there the whole time. I just hadn't opened them."

My friend, tonight I want to tell you something. There is a letter that has been waiting for you. Some of you have had it on your nightstand for years. Some of you have one in a drawer somewhere, still in the plastic wrapping. Some of you have never picked it up at all.

I am talking about *this Book*. The Bible.

**The Bible says** in Psalm 119, verse 105: *"Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path."*

This is not just an old religious book. This is not a collection of ancient myths. This is a *love letter* — written by the God of the universe — addressed to *you*. And many of you have never opened it.

Tonight I want to show you four things about this Book that I believe will change your life.

---

## POINT 1: THE AUTHOR — God Himself Spoke (10 minutes)

The first thing I want you to see is *who wrote this Book*.

**The Bible says** in 2 Timothy chapter 3, verse 16: *"All Scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness."*

Now that word "inspiration" — in the original language it means "God-breathed." God *breathed out* these words. He didn't merely suggest ideas to men. He didn't give them a general outline and say, "Write whatever you feel like." No — the Holy Spirit moved upon human authors and guided them so that what they wrote was exactly what God intended.

Here is what is remarkable. This Book was written over a period of approximately *fifteen hundred years*. It was written by more than *forty different authors*. And those authors could not have been more different from one another. Moses was a prince raised in a palace. David was a shepherd boy. Amos was a fig picker. Daniel was a government official. Peter was a fisherman. Luke was a medical doctor. Paul was a scholar and a theologian. Matthew was a tax collector.

They wrote on three different continents — Asia, Africa, and Europe. They wrote in three different languages — Hebrew, Aramaic, and Greek. They wrote in deserts, in dungeons, in palaces, in prisons, on hillsides, and on islands.

And yet — and I want you to hear this — when you put all sixty-six books together, they tell *one unified story* without a single contradiction in theme or purpose.

Now let me ask you something. If I took forty people from *this audience tonight* — people from different backgrounds, different education levels, different political views — and I asked each of you to write independently on the subjects of God, morality, suffering, death, and the meaning of life — do you know what I would get? I would get *chaos*. I would get forty completely different opinions that contradict each other on every page.

But this Book doesn't do that. Forty authors, fifteen hundred years, and they all agree — because they all had the same Editor. God Himself was superintending every word.

**The Bible says** in 2 Peter 1:21: *"For the prophecy came not in old time by the will of man: but holy men of God spake as they were moved by the Holy Ghost."*

My friend, this is a *supernatural* Book. No human being could have produced it. The unity of this Book is one of the greatest proofs that God exists and that He has spoken.

And He has spoken to *you*.

---

### TRANSITION

Now, if God is the Author — then what is the story He is telling? That brings me to my second point.

---

## POINT 2: THE STORY — One Thread from Genesis to Revelation (10 minutes)

The second thing I want you to see is this: the Bible is not sixty-six separate books. **It is one story. And the hero of that story is Jesus Christ.**

**The Bible says** in Luke chapter 24, verse 27, that after His resurrection, Jesus walked with two disciples on the road to Emmaus, and it says: *"And beginning at Moses and all the prophets, He expounded unto them in all the Scriptures the things concerning Himself."*

Did you catch that? Jesus said, "The whole Book is about *Me*." And tonight I want to take you on a journey — a walk through the greatest story ever told.

**In Genesis**, Christ is the Creator who spoke the worlds into existence, and He is the *Promised Seed* of the woman who would crush the serpent's head. The very first promise of a Savior is right there in Genesis chapter 3, verse 15.

**In Exodus**, Christ is the *Passover Lamb*. When the death angel swept through Egypt, the only thing that saved a household was the blood of a lamb on the doorpost. My friend, that was a picture — a preview — of the blood of Jesus Christ that would be shed on the cross for *your* sins and *mine*.

**In Leviticus**, Christ is the *great High Priest* — the one who stands between a holy God and sinful humanity and makes a way for us to come into God's presence.

**In the Psalms**, Christ is the *Good Shepherd*. "The Lord is my shepherd; I shall not want." David wrote those words a thousand years before Jesus was born, and Jesus Himself said, "I am the Good Shepherd. The Good Shepherd giveth His life for the sheep."

**In Isaiah**, Christ is the *Suffering Servant*. Seven hundred years before Calvary, Isaiah wrote: "He was wounded for our transgressions, He was bruised for our iniquities; the chastisement of our peace was upon Him, and with His stripes we are healed." If you read Isaiah 53, you would think the man was standing at the foot of the cross. But he wrote it seven centuries before it happened. How? Because God was the Author.

**In the Gospels** — Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John — the Promised One *arrives*. The Word becomes flesh and dwells among us. Jesus Christ, the Son of the living God, walks on this earth, heals the sick, raises the dead, teaches with authority, and then lays down His life on a Roman cross for the sins of the whole world.

**In Acts**, Christ is the *risen Lord*, and the Holy Spirit is poured out, and the Church explodes across the Roman Empire because a dead man *walked out of a tomb*.

**In the Epistles** — Romans, Corinthians, Galatians, Ephesians, and all the rest — Christ is the *Head of the Church*, and those letters explain what His death and resurrection mean for every man, woman, and child who will believe.

And then, my friend, you turn to the last book of the Bible — **Revelation** — and Christ is revealed as the *King of Kings and Lord of Lords*. He is coming again! And every knee shall bow and every tongue shall confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.

Do you see it? One story. One scarlet thread of redemption running from the first page to the last. It all points to Jesus.

**The Bible says** in John 5:39, Jesus told the religious leaders: *"Search the scriptures; for in them ye think ye have eternal life: and they are they which testify of Me."*

Every page of this Book is pointing you to the Savior.

---

### TRANSITION

Now, some of you might be thinking, "That's interesting history, Billy. But what does it have to do with my life right now?"

---

## POINT 3: THE POWER — This Book Changes Lives (8 minutes)

The third thing I want you to see tonight is that this Book has *power*.

**The Bible says** in Hebrews chapter 4, verse 12: *"For the word of God is living, and active, and sharper than any two-edged sword, piercing even to the dividing asunder of soul and spirit, and of the joints and marrow, and is a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart."*

This Book is *alive*. I have held many books in my hands. I have read literature and philosophy and history. But I have never held any book that does what *this* Book does. When you read Shakespeare, Shakespeare does not read *you*. But when you read the Bible — the Bible reads you. It knows your thoughts. It exposes your secrets. It reaches down into the deepest corners of your heart and it shines a light there.

It *convicts* you — and then it *comforts* you. It shows you your sin — and then it shows you your Savior.

I think of a man I met years ago at one of our crusades in London. He was a hardened, bitter man — an alcoholic who had lost his family, his career, everything. He told me he had wandered into the meeting that night only because it was raining and he wanted to get out of the cold.

But someone had left a Gideon Bible on the seat next to him. And while the choir was singing, he opened it — almost out of boredom — to the book of Isaiah, and his eyes fell on chapter 1, verse 18: *"Come now, and let us reason together, saith the Lord: though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow; though they be red like crimson, they shall be as wool."*

He told me that when he read those words, something broke inside of him. He said, "It was as if God Himself sat down next to me on that bench and said, 'I'm talking to *you*.'"

That man gave his life to Jesus Christ that night. And I saw him three years later — completely transformed. Reunited with his wife. Sober. Working. Serving in his local church. And he carried that same Bible with him everywhere he went, the pages worn thin from reading.

That is the *power* of this Book.

**The Bible says** in Isaiah 55:11: *"So shall My word be that goeth forth out of My mouth: it shall not return unto Me void, but it shall accomplish that which I please."*

God's Word *never* comes back empty. It always does its work.

---

### TRANSITION

And that brings me to my final point — and this is the most important one of all.

---

## POINT 4: THE INVITATION — Will You Read His Letter? (5 minutes)

Remember the story I told you at the beginning? About the young woman who found her mother's letters?

My friend, God has written you a letter. It has been waiting for you. Maybe it has been waiting for you for twenty years. Maybe fifty. Maybe your whole life.

**The Bible says** in Hebrews chapter 1, verses 1 and 2: *"God, who at sundry times and in divers manners spake in time past unto the fathers by the prophets, hath in these last days spoken unto us by His Son."*

God has been *speaking*. He spoke through creation. He spoke through prophets. He spoke through Scripture. And finally, He spoke through *Jesus* — the Living Word. The question tonight is not whether God has spoken. He has. The question is: *are you listening?*

**The Bible says** in John 5:39-40: *"You search the Scriptures because you think that in them you have eternal life; and it is they that bear witness about Me, yet you refuse to come to Me that you may have life."*

You see, it is possible to *know about* the Bible and still not know the One the Bible is about. The Bible is God saying to you tonight: *"I love you. I made you. You have wandered away from Me. But I sent My Son to bring you home."*

That is the message of this Book. From Genesis to Revelation. Every page. Every chapter. Every verse. *Come home.*

---

## ALTAR CALL (5 minutes)

And so tonight, I am going to ask you to respond to what God has been saying to you — not just through this sermon, but through His Word, through His Spirit, through the still small voice that has been speaking to your heart perhaps for years.

I am going to ask you to get up out of your seat and come forward. Come and stand here in front of this platform. And by coming, you are saying, "Tonight, I want to receive Jesus Christ. I want to open God's letter and let it change my life."

Come now. While there is still time. While the door is still open.

**The Bible says** in 2 Corinthians 6:2: *"Now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation."*

Come.

---

## CLOSING PRAYER

Our Father in heaven, I thank You tonight for Your Word. I thank You that it is true, that it is living, and that it is powerful. I thank You that You did not leave us in silence — that You spoke through prophets, through Scripture, and finally through Your own dear Son, the Lord Jesus Christ.

And I pray tonight for every man, woman, and young person who has come forward. Lord, they are responding to *Your* Word. Seal this decision in their hearts. Forgive their sins. Write their names in the Lamb's Book of Life. Give them the Holy Spirit to guide them, to teach them, to open their eyes as they read Your Word.

And for those who did not come tonight — Lord, don't let them rest until they do. Pursue them. Speak to them. Let Your Word follow them out of this place and never let them go.

We ask it all in the precious and mighty name of Jesus Christ our Lord.

And all God's people said — *Amen*.

# Sermon 8: "What Will You Do With Jesus?"

## The Grand Finale of *The Heart of the Gospel* Series

**Theme:** The Unavoidable Decision — Series Finale
**Key Scriptures:** Matthew 27:22, Acts 4:12, Joshua 24:15, John 14:6, Revelation 3:20, Romans 10:9-10

---

## OPENING: THE MOST TRAGIC SCENE IN HUMAN HISTORY (7-8 minutes)

I want you to come with me tonight to a courtroom. Not a modern courtroom with fluorescent lights and a jury box. I want to take you back two thousand years to a stone courtyard in Jerusalem, early in the morning, where a Roman governor named Pontius Pilate is about to make the most consequential decision any human being has ever made.

The Bible tells us in Matthew chapter 27 that Jesus was brought before Pilate. Now I want you to picture this scene. It is early morning. The sun is just beginning to rise over the walls of the city. There is a crowd — an angry, restless, shouting crowd. The religious leaders are there, and they are furious. They have dragged Jesus through an illegal trial in the middle of the night, and now they want Rome to do their dirty work.

And there stands Pilate. He is the governor. He has the power of the Roman Empire behind him. He has soldiers at his command. He wears the authority of Caesar himself. And standing before him, with His hands bound, is a carpenter from Nazareth. His face is already bruised. He has been slapped, spit upon, mocked. He says almost nothing.

And Pilate — the Bible says Pilate *knew* Jesus was innocent. His own wife had sent him a message that very morning: "Have nothing to do with that righteous man, for I have suffered greatly in a dream because of Him." Pilate examined Jesus and found no fault in Him. He said it publicly — *"I find no guilt in this man."*

Pilate knew the truth. He was looking at the truth. The Truth was standing right in front of him.

And then he turned to the crowd and asked a question that echoes across twenty centuries and lands right here in this room tonight. Matthew 27, verse 22 — Pilate said to them:

**"What then shall I do with Jesus who is called Christ?"**

My friend, that is the question of the ages. That is the question of your life. What will you do with Jesus?

And you know what Pilate did? The Bible says he took water and washed his hands before the crowd, saying, "I am innocent of this man's blood." He thought he could stay neutral. He thought he could wash his hands of the decision. He thought he could send Jesus away and go back to his breakfast and his politics and his comfortable life.

But you cannot wash your hands of Jesus Christ. Pilate made a decision that morning. By trying not to decide, he decided. He handed the Son of God over to be crucified. And history has never forgotten it. Every time the church gathers anywhere in the world and recites the Apostles' Creed, they say it — *"suffered under Pontius Pilate."* His name is forever linked to the most tragic decision in human history.

Tonight, I want to tell you — the same Jesus who stood before Pilate is standing before *you*. Not with His hands bound. No — He is risen. He is alive. He is the King of Kings and the Lord of Lords. And He is asking you the same question Pilate faced: **What will you do with Me?**

You can accept Him. You can reject Him. But I tell you on the authority of the Word of God — you *cannot* ignore Him. Not tonight. Not after what you have heard in this series. Not after the Holy Spirit has been speaking to your heart.

---

## POINT 1: THE UNAVOIDABLE QUESTION — Everyone Must Answer (8-10 minutes)

The first thing I want you to see tonight is this: **this question cannot be avoided.** Every person who has ever lived must answer it. Every person in this room tonight must answer it. What will you do with Jesus?

You say, "Well, Billy, I just haven't made up my mind yet. I'm not for Him, and I'm not against Him. I'm just — neutral." My friend, there is no neutral ground when it comes to Jesus Christ. Jesus Himself said in Matthew chapter 12, verse 30 — *"He who is not with Me is against Me."* There is no middle ground. There is no fence to sit on. You are either for Christ or you are against Him.

Not to decide *is* to decide. If a doctor tells you that you have a disease and offers you the cure, and you say, "Well, I'll think about it," and you walk out the door and never come back — you have made a decision. You have chosen to remain sick. You have chosen to die. Not because you said "no" with your mouth, but because you never said "yes."

Now, you cannot be neutral about Jesus because of *who He is*. The great writer C.S. Lewis — and he was a man who had been an atheist, a skeptic, a man who did not want to believe — C.S. Lewis said you really only have three options when it comes to Jesus Christ.

Jesus claimed to be God. He said, *"I and the Father are one."* He said, *"Before Abraham was, I AM."* He accepted worship. He forgave sins — something only God can do. Now, Lewis said, a man who claimed these things is either a *liar* — He knew He was not God and said it anyway — or He was a *lunatic* — He genuinely believed He was God but was out of His mind — or He was telling the truth, and He is *Lord.*

Those are your only options. What you *cannot* say — what is not intellectually honest — is, "Well, I think Jesus was a great moral teacher, but I don't believe He was God." A man who said the things Jesus said and was not God would not be a great moral teacher. He would either be a liar of monstrous proportions or He would be insane. But you cannot call Him merely a good man and dismiss His claims.

Everyone will answer this question. You will answer it tonight, or you will answer it on the Day of Judgment. But you *will* answer it. The question is not *whether* you will face Jesus — the question is *when,* and on what terms.

---

## POINT 2: THE WRONG ANSWERS — What People Do With Jesus (8-10 minutes)

Now, over the centuries — and right here tonight — people have given many answers to this question. And most of them are the wrong answer.

**Some reject Him outright.** They say, "I don't need God. I don't want a Savior. I'll live my life my own way." My friend, that is a person's right. God will not force Himself on anyone. But the Bible says there is a way that seems right to a man, but its end is the way of death. You can reject Jesus Christ, but you cannot escape the consequences of that rejection.

**Some postpone the decision.** And this may be the most dangerous response of all. Turn with me to Acts chapter 24. The Apostle Paul was brought before a Roman governor named Felix. And Paul preached. The Bible says Paul reasoned with Felix about righteousness, self-control, and the judgment to come. And the Bible says Felix was *afraid.* The Word of God got to him. The Holy Spirit was working on his heart.

And then Felix said these tragic words — Acts 24, verse 25: **"Go away for now; when I find a convenient time I will call for you."**

When I find a convenient time. My friend, the Bible never records that Felix ever found that convenient time. As far as we know, he died without Christ. He heard the gospel from the lips of the greatest preacher who ever lived aside from Jesus Himself, and he said, "Not now. Later. Some other time."

I want to tell you — "later" is the most dangerous word in the English language when it comes to your soul.

**Some try to add Jesus as an accessory to their life.** They want Jesus *plus* their old life. They want fire insurance from hell, but they do not want a new Lord. They want Him in the backseat, maybe giving suggestions, but they want to keep their hands on the steering wheel.

My friend, Jesus Christ did not die on that cross so that He could be your add-on. He is not an accessory. He is either Lord of *all* or He is not Lord at *all.*

**And some people are "almost persuaded."** Turn with me to Acts chapter 26. Paul is now before King Agrippa, and Agrippa says to Paul in verse 28 — **"You almost persuade me to become a Christian."**

Almost. My friend, "almost" is one of the saddest words in the Bible. Almost persuaded — but lost. Almost saved — but still in sin. Almost home — but still outside the door. Almost is not enough.

I am pleading with you tonight — do not leave this place "almost persuaded." Cross the line. Come all the way to Jesus.

---

## POINT 3: THE ONLY RIGHT ANSWER — Surrender and Believe (8-10 minutes)

Now I want to give you the right answer. And the right answer is not complicated. A child can understand it. The right answer is: **surrender your life completely to Jesus Christ as your Lord and Savior.**

The Bible says in Acts chapter 4, verse 12: **"There is no other name under heaven given among men by which we must be saved."** No other name. Not Buddha. Not Muhammad. Not any philosopher or guru or religious leader. There is one name and one name only — and that name is Jesus.

Jesus Himself said it in John chapter 14, verse 6 — **"I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through Me."** Now, I did not say that. Your pastor did not say that. *Jesus* said that. And He is either telling the truth or He is not. And if He rose from the dead — and He did — then He has the credentials to back up that claim.

Romans chapter 10, verses 9 and 10: **"If you confess with your mouth the Lord Jesus and believe in your heart that God has raised Him from the dead, you will be saved. For with the heart one believes unto righteousness, and with the mouth confession is made unto salvation."**

Believe in your heart. Confess with your mouth. It is not a twelve-step program. It is not a PhD in theology. It is a simple act of faith and surrender.

But I want to say something very important. The Bible does not just say, "Accept Jesus as your Savior." It says, *"Confess with your mouth the LORD Jesus."* Lord. He must be Lord. Not just Savior — Lord. A Savior rescues you. A Lord *rules* you. And Jesus Christ must be both.

You cannot say, "Save me from my sin, but let me keep running my own life." That is not the gospel. The gospel is total surrender. *"All to Jesus I surrender, all to Him I freely give."* All. Not some. Not most. All.

The Bible says in 2 Corinthians 5:17, *"If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away; behold, the new has come."* That is what He wants to do for you tonight. Not repair the old life. Not patch it up. Make you *new.*

---

## POINT 4: THE MOMENT — Tonight Is Your Night (8-10 minutes)

And now I come to the most important part of this message, and the most important moment of this entire series.

For seven nights we have walked through the heart of the gospel together. And I want to bring it all back to you right now.

In **Sermon 1**, we talked about **the cross** — that place called Calvary where the sinless Son of God bled and died in your place. He took your punishment. He paid your debt. And that cross still stands tonight.

In **Sermon 2**, we stood before **the empty tomb** — because Jesus did not stay dead. On the third day He rose again. He conquered death, hell, and the grave. And because He lives, you can live also.

In **Sermon 3**, we talked about **the Age of Grace** — that God is holding the door open right now, but He won't hold it forever. The clock is ticking.

In **Sermon 4**, we faced **the reality of sin** — that every one of us has sinned and fallen short, that we are sick with a disease only Jesus can cure.

In **Sermon 5**, we talked about what it means to be **born again** — not religious reformation, not turning over a new leaf, but a supernatural work of God's Spirit that transforms you from the inside out. Jesus said you *must* be born again.

In **Sermon 6**, we talked about **the King coming back** — that this same Jesus who ascended into heaven is returning. And when He comes, it will be too late to make your decision.

In **Sermon 7**, we talked about **the Bible — God's love letter to you** — one story from Genesis to Revelation, and the hero is Jesus Christ.

And now here we are. **Sermon 8.** The final message. And I am asking you the same question Pilate asked two thousand years ago: *What will you do with Jesus?*

The Bible says in Revelation chapter 3, verse 20 — Jesus said, **"Behold, I stand at the door and knock. If anyone hears My voice and opens the door, I will come in to him and dine with him, and he with Me."**

He is knocking tonight. Right now. On the door of your heart. He will not break the door down. He is a gentleman. He will not force His way in. But He is knocking. Can you hear Him?

The Bible says in Joshua chapter 24, verse 15 — Joshua gathered the people and he said, **"Choose for yourselves this day whom you will serve... but as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD."** Choose this day. Not next year. Not when you retire. Not on your deathbed. *This day.* Tonight. Right now.

---

## THE GRAND ALTAR CALL (10-12 minutes)

I am going to ask you to do something now. And I am going to ask you to do it publicly, because Jesus said, *"Whoever confesses Me before men, him I will also confess before My Father who is in heaven."*

I am going to ask you to get up out of your seat — from every section of this place, from the balcony, from the back row, from the very front — and I want you to come and stand here. I am going to ask you to come forward as an act of surrender to Jesus Christ.

Some of you — this is the night you give your life to Christ for the very first time. You have never been born again. You have been religious, maybe. You have gone to church. You have tried to be a good person. But you have never personally surrendered your life to Jesus. Tonight is your night. He has been waiting for you. He has been pursuing you. Every sermon in this series has been leading to this moment.

Some of you have been running from God. You have been running for years. You are tired. You are empty. You have tried everything the world has to offer and it has left you hollow. My friend, stop running. Turn around. He is right there. His arms are open. The Bible says the father saw the prodigal son coming from a long way off, and he ran to him and embraced him. Your heavenly Father will do the same for you tonight.

Some of you are carrying a weight of guilt and shame so heavy you can barely breathe. You think, "If people knew what I have done... if God knew..." My friend, God already knows. He knows everything. And He loves you anyway. The Bible says, *"Though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow."* There is no sin too big for the cross.

Some of you are here tonight and you are afraid. You are afraid of what people will think. You are afraid of what you will have to give up. You are afraid of change. I want to tell you something — the only thing you should be afraid of is leaving this place tonight without Christ.

I am going to ask the choir to sing softly. And while they sing, I want you to come. You come from up there in the balcony. You come from the back. It is a long walk, but Christ walked a longer road to Calvary for you. You come.

Don't let this moment pass. You may never feel this pull again. The Bible warns — *"My Spirit shall not strive with man forever."* God is striving with you tonight. He is calling you. He is drawing you. But He will not force you.

You come now. While the choir sings. Hundreds of you. Get up out of your seat right now and come. This is the most important step you will ever take. Your eternal destiny hangs in the balance. Not tomorrow. *Tonight.* You come.

That is it. You come right now. God bless you. I see you coming from over there. God bless you, young man. God bless you, ma'am, coming from the back. You come right now. You bring your sin, your brokenness, your fear, your doubt — you bring it all, and you lay it at the foot of the cross. He will take it. He will take it all. You come.

---

## CLOSING PRAYER

Now, I want you to pray with me. I am going to pray a simple prayer, and I am going to ask you to pray it from your heart. Not just the words — from your *heart.* God does not listen to words alone. He listens to the cry of a surrendered heart. Let us pray:

*"Dear God... I come to You tonight just as I am. I am a sinner. I know that. I cannot save myself. I have tried, and I have failed. But tonight I believe — I believe that Jesus Christ is Your Son. I believe He died on the cross for my sins. I believe He was buried and rose again on the third day. And tonight, I turn from my sin, and I turn to You. I open the door of my heart, and I invite Jesus Christ to come in — as my Savior and as my LORD. I surrender everything to Him. Take my life and make it new. I receive Your forgiveness. I receive Your gift of eternal life. And I will follow You from this day forward, by Your grace and by Your power. In the name of Jesus I pray. Amen."*

If you prayed that prayer tonight and meant it from your heart — the Bible says you are a new creation. Old things have passed away. All things have become new. Welcome to the family of God. Angels in heaven are rejoicing over you right now.

Now — do not walk out of here tonight and try to live this new life on your own. Get into a Bible-believing church. Get into the Word of God daily. Learn to pray. Tell someone what you did tonight. And know this — He who began a good work in you will be faithful to complete it.

God bless you. And may the peace of Jesus Christ — the One who loved you enough to die for you and powerful enough to rise again — may His peace go with you now and forever. Amen.
