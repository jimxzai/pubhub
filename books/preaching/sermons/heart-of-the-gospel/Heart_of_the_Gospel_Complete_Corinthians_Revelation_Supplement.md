# Sermon 1: "The Cross — Where God Met Man"

## Supplementary Preaching Content from 1 & 2 Corinthians and Revelation

---

### I. The Message of the Cross: God's Power Hidden in Foolishness (1 Corinthians 1:18-25)

The Bible says in 1 Corinthians 1:18: *"For the message of the cross is foolishness to those who are perishing, but to us who are being saved it is the power of God."*

My friends, I want you to listen carefully tonight. The Apostle Paul wrote these words to a city that thought it knew everything. Corinth was the New York City and the Las Vegas of the ancient world rolled into one. It was a city of philosophy, a city of commerce, a city of pleasure. The Greeks in Corinth loved wisdom. They loved debate. They loved eloquent speeches and grand ideas. And into that proud, sophisticated city, Paul came with a message so simple, so scandalous, so offensive to their ears that they laughed at him.

He came preaching a crucified Savior.

Now you have to understand what the cross meant in the first century. The cross was not a piece of jewelry. The cross was not a decoration on a steeple. The cross was the most shameful, most degrading, most horrifying instrument of execution the Roman Empire had ever devised. It was reserved for the lowest of the low — for slaves, for rebels, for the worst criminals society could produce. The great Roman statesman Cicero said that the very word "cross" should be far removed from the thoughts and eyes and ears of a Roman citizen. It was too disgusting to even talk about in polite company.

And Paul said, "THIS is the power of God."

The Bible says the Greeks sought after wisdom. They wanted philosophy. They wanted grand systems of thought. They wanted Plato and Aristotle and Socrates. And Paul said, "I am not giving you philosophy. I am giving you a cross."

The Bible says the Jews demanded signs. They wanted miracles. They wanted fire from heaven. They wanted a conquering Messiah riding on a white horse to overthrow Rome. And Paul said, "I am not giving you a military victory. I am giving you a man bleeding and dying on a Roman cross."

Look at verse 23: *"But we preach Christ crucified, to the Jews a stumbling block and to the Greeks foolishness."*

A stumbling block! The Greek word there is *skandalon* — it is the word from which we get our English word "scandal." The cross was a scandal to the religious mind. And to the intellectual mind, it was *moria* — moronic, foolish, absurd.

But then Paul drops the thunderbolt. Verse 24-25: *"But to those who are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God. Because the foolishness of God is wiser than men, and the weakness of God is stronger than men."*

Oh, my friends, do you see what the Spirit of God is saying here? What looks like foolishness to the world is actually the deepest wisdom in the universe! What looks like weakness — a man hanging helpless on a cross — is actually the mightiest act of power that has ever been performed in all of history!

I have preached in universities where brilliant professors shook their heads and said, "This is too simple. This cannot be the answer to humanity's problems." I have preached in places where the intellectual elite looked down their noses at this message of a crucified carpenter from Nazareth. And I say to them what Paul said to Corinth: You can have all the wisdom of the world, but it cannot save a single soul. You can have all the philosophy, all the education, all the sophistication — but when you stand before Almighty God, none of it will matter. The only thing that will matter is what you did with the cross of Jesus Christ.

The cross looks foolish. But it is the wisdom of God.

The cross looks weak. But it is the power of God.

Every other religion in the world says, "Do this, achieve this, earn this, climb this ladder." The cross says, "It is finished. It is done. Receive it." And that offends human pride. That is why the world calls it foolishness. Because the cross tells you that you cannot save yourself. The cross tells the richest man and the poorest man, the most educated and the most uneducated, that they all stand on level ground before God — helpless, hopeless, and in desperate need of a Savior.

### II. Paul's Singular Resolve: Nothing but Christ Crucified (1 Corinthians 2:2)

The Bible says in 1 Corinthians 2:2: *"For I resolved to know nothing while I was with you except Jesus Christ and him crucified."*

Now think about who is writing this. This is not some uneducated fisherman. This is Paul — a man trained at the feet of Gamaliel, one of the greatest scholars in all of Israel. Paul could speak multiple languages. Paul could quote Greek poets. Paul could debate with the finest philosophical minds of his day. Paul had credentials that would fill a wall.

And Paul said, "When I came to Corinth, I made a decision. I determined. I resolved. I set my mind. I would know NOTHING among you — nothing — except Jesus Christ and Him crucified."

Not Jesus Christ the great teacher. Not Jesus Christ the moral example. Not Jesus Christ the social reformer. Jesus Christ and Him CRUCIFIED.

My friends, Paul could have impressed Corinth with his education. He could have dazzled them with his rhetoric. He could have built a following with his intellect. But Paul said, "I will not do it." He stripped everything away until there was nothing left but the cross.

And I say to every preacher, every teacher, every church tonight: when you move away from the cross, you have moved away from the power. You can have the most beautiful music, the most eloquent preaching, the most impressive programs — but if the cross of Jesus Christ is not at the center, you have nothing. You are a noisy gong and a clanging cymbal.

Paul made the cross the center of everything. Not one thing among many. THE thing. The only thing. And wherever that message was preached — in Corinth, in Ephesus, in Philippi, in Rome — lives were transformed, chains were broken, and the dead were raised to life.

### III. The Great Exchange (2 Corinthians 5:21)

Now I want you to turn with me to one of the most staggering verses in all of Holy Scripture. The Bible says in 2 Corinthians 5:21: *"God made him who had no sin to be sin for us, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God."*

My friends, this verse contains the deepest mystery of the Gospel. This is what happened at the cross. This is WHY the cross is the power of God.

Let me try to explain it simply.

Jesus Christ, the Son of God, never committed one sin. Not one. In all of His thirty-three years on this earth, He never had one sinful thought. He never spoke one sinful word. He never performed one sinful act. The Bible says He was tempted in every way that we are, yet was without sin. He was the spotless Lamb of God — pure, holy, perfect, without blemish.

And on the cross, God the Father took all of the sin of the world — my sin, your sin, the sin of every person who has ever lived or ever will live — and He placed it on His sinless Son. He MADE Him to be sin. Not a sinner — sin itself. Everything ugly, everything vile, everything corrupt and wicked and evil that humanity has ever produced was laid upon the shoulders of Jesus.

And in exchange — oh, glory! — in exchange, He took the perfect righteousness of Jesus Christ and offered it to us. So that when God looks at me — a sinner, a rebel, a man who deserved hell — He does not see my sin. He sees the righteousness of His Son.

This is the great exchange. This is what Martin Luther called "the joyful exchange." My sin for His righteousness. My death for His life. My condemnation for His freedom.

I remember years ago talking with a man after a crusade. He said, "Billy, I have done terrible things. Things I am ashamed to even speak about. How could God ever accept me?" And I pointed him to this verse. I said, "My friend, God is not asking you to clean yourself up. God is not asking you to make yourself good enough. God took your sin — all of it — and He put it on Jesus. And Jesus paid for it. All of it. Every last bit. And now He offers you His righteousness as a free gift. Will you take it?"

That man wept. And that night, he gave his life to Jesus Christ. Because he understood the exchange. He understood what happened at the cross.

### IV. The Warning of Ephesus: Losing Your First Love (Revelation 2:1-7)

Now I want to take you from Corinthians to the book of Revelation, because these threads are woven together by the Holy Spirit throughout the whole Bible.

In Revelation chapter 2, the risen Lord Jesus Christ speaks to the church at Ephesus. And what He says should make every one of us tremble.

He begins with commendation. He says, "I know your works, your labor, your patience. I know you cannot bear those who are evil. You have tested those who say they are apostles and are not, and have found them liars. You have persevered and have labored for My name's sake and have not become weary."

My friends, this was a GOOD church! By every outward measure, Ephesus was a model congregation. They worked hard. They had sound doctrine. They could spot a false teacher a mile away. They did not tolerate heresy. They were busy, committed, dedicated, orthodox.

And then Jesus says — verse 4: *"Nevertheless I have this against you, that you have left your first love."*

Left your first love!

Do you know what your first love is? It is the cross. It is Calvary. It is that moment when you first understood what Jesus did for you and your heart broke with gratitude and wonder and love. It is that first love — not just love for doctrine, not just love for service, not just love for the church — but love for HIM. Love for the One who bled and died in your place.

And Jesus says to Ephesus: "You have everything else right. But you have forgotten WHY you do what you do. You have forgotten the cross. You have become so busy working FOR Me that you have forgotten to love Me."

My friends, this is a warning for our churches today. You can have the right theology and the wrong heart. You can have the right programs and the wrong passion. You can be busy, busy, busy for God and be utterly loveless. And Jesus says, "This I have against you."

Look at verse 5: *"Remember therefore from where you have fallen; repent and do the first works, or else I will come to you quickly and remove your lampstand from its place."*

Remove your lampstand! Do you know what that means? The lampstand is the church's witness, its testimony, its very existence as a functioning body of Christ. Jesus is saying, "If you do not return to your first love — if you do not return to the wonder of the cross — I will shut you down. Your church will become nothing more than a religious social club. The light will go out."

And I ask you tonight: Have YOU left your first love? Has the cross become routine to you? Has Calvary become just a doctrine you believe instead of a reality that breaks your heart? When was the last time you wept at the thought of what Jesus did for you?

Go back to Calvary, my friend. Go back to the cross. Go back to your first love. Before it is too late.

### V. The Lamb Who Was Slain: The Cross Echoes in Eternity (Revelation 5:6-12)

And lest you think the cross is merely a historical event — something that happened two thousand years ago and is now past — let me take you to the throne room of heaven itself.

The Bible says in Revelation 5:6: *"And I looked, and behold, in the midst of the throne... stood a Lamb as though it had been slain."*

Do you see that? John is caught up into heaven. He is standing before the throne of Almighty God. He is surrounded by angels — millions upon millions of them. And there, in the center of it all, in the place of highest honor, stands a Lamb.

Not a lion. Not a conquering king on a horse. A Lamb. And not just any Lamb — a Lamb that bears the marks of slaughter. "As though it had been slain."

My friends, Jesus Christ is in glory tonight. He is seated at the right hand of the Father. He is robed in majesty and splendor and power. But He still bears the marks of Calvary! The nail prints are still in His hands. The wound is still in His side. The scars of the cross are eternal.

Why? Because the cross is not just a moment in time. The cross is the center of all eternity. The cross is the event around which all of heaven revolves. The cross is the reason the angels sing.

And what do they sing? Verse 12: *"Worthy is the Lamb who was slain to receive power and riches and wisdom, and strength and honor and glory and blessing!"*

They do not sing, "Worthy is the great teacher!" They do not sing, "Worthy is the moral example!" They sing, "Worthy is the Lamb who was SLAIN!" The cross! It is always the cross! Ten thousand times ten thousand angels, and the elders, and the living creatures, and every creature in heaven and on earth and under the earth — they are all singing about the cross!

And one day, my friend, if you have trusted in Jesus Christ, you will join that song. You will stand before that Lamb, and you will see the nail prints with your own eyes, and you will fall on your face and worship, and you will sing with a voice you did not know you had: "Worthy! Worthy! Worthy is the Lamb who was slain!"

The cross is not foolishness. The cross is the wisdom of God. It is the power of God. It is the love of God made visible, tangible, and eternal.

Will you come to that cross tonight? Will you come just as you are — with all your sin, all your shame, all your brokenness — and lay it down at the foot of Calvary? Jesus Christ is waiting for you there. He paid the price. He made the exchange. He offers you His righteousness.

The Bible says, *"Whoever calls on the name of the Lord shall be saved."*

Come to the cross tonight. Come home.

# Sermon 2: "The Empty Tomb Changed Everything"

## Supplementary Preaching Content from 1 Corinthians 15, 2 Corinthians, and Revelation

---

### I. The Gospel Defined: The Bedrock Facts (1 Corinthians 15:1-4)

The Bible says in 1 Corinthians 15:1-4: *"Moreover, brethren, I declare to you the gospel which I preached to you, which also you received and in which you stand, by which also you are saved, if you hold fast that word which I preached to you — unless you believed in vain. For I delivered to you first of all that which I also received: that Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures, and that He was buried, and that He rose again the third day according to the Scriptures."*

My friends, I want you to notice something tonight. When Paul wanted to define the Gospel — when he wanted to boil down the Christian faith to its absolute irreducible core — he gave us not a philosophy, not a code of ethics, not a system of theology, but three historical facts:

Christ DIED. Christ was BURIED. Christ ROSE AGAIN.

That is the Gospel. Everything else flows from those three facts. Take away any one of them, and the whole thing collapses like a house built on sand.

He died — that means the cross was real. He was not playacting. He did not swoon and revive. He died. The Roman soldiers, who were experts in death, certified it. The spear pierced His side. Blood and water flowed out — a medical sign of death by cardiac rupture. He was dead.

He was buried — that means His death was confirmed and final. They wrapped His body in burial cloths. They laid it in a stone tomb. They rolled a massive stone across the entrance. The Roman governor sealed it with the authority of the Empire. Guards were posted. It was over. Finished. Done. The disciples went home in despair.

He rose again the third day — and THAT, my friends, changed everything. That is the earthquake at the center of human history. That is the hinge on which all of eternity swings. That is the fact that turned a band of frightened, scattered, hopeless men into a force that conquered the Roman Empire without a single sword.

And notice: Paul says this happened "according to the Scriptures." The resurrection was not an afterthought. It was not Plan B. From Genesis to Malachi, the Old Testament pointed forward to this moment. When God made coats of skin for Adam and Eve, an animal had to die — but the story did not end there. When Abraham raised the knife over Isaac, God provided a ram — but the story did not end there. When the Passover lamb was slain in Egypt, the blood was applied — but the story did not end there. Every sacrifice, every offering, every type and shadow in the Old Testament whispered: "Something greater is coming. Death will not have the last word."

And on the third day, death did not have the last word.

### II. The Devastating "If" — What If Christ Did Not Rise? (1 Corinthians 15:12-19)

Now Paul does something remarkable. He takes the unthinkable position — just for a moment — and says, "What if it is not true? What if Christ did not rise from the dead? What then?"

The Bible says in verse 14: *"And if Christ is not risen, then our preaching is empty and your faith is also empty."*

Empty! The Greek word is *kenos* — void, hollow, without content. If there is no resurrection, then every sermon I have ever preached in my life is hot air. Every hymn you have ever sung is a lie. Every prayer you have ever prayed went nowhere.

Verse 15: *"Yes, and we are found false witnesses of God, because we have testified of God that He raised up Christ."*

If Christ is not risen, then the apostles were not just mistaken — they were LIARS. They were bearing false witness about God Himself. Peter was a fraud. John was a deceiver. Paul was a con man. Every martyr who died rather than deny the resurrection died for a fairy tale.

Verse 17: *"And if Christ is not risen, your faith is futile; you are still in your sins!"*

Futile! If Jesus is still dead, then you are still guilty. Every sin you have ever committed is still on your record. The cross accomplished nothing. You have no forgiveness, no cleansing, no hope. You will stand before God with the full weight of your sin upon you, and there will be no advocate, no mediator, no one to plead your case.

Verse 18: *"Then also those who have fallen asleep in Christ have perished."*

If there is no resurrection, then your mother who died trusting Jesus — she is gone. Your father, your husband, your wife, your child who died in the faith — they have perished. They are not in heaven. They are nowhere. Death swallowed them up and that is the end of the story.

Verse 19: *"If in this life only we have hope in Christ, we are of all men the most pitiable."*

Most pitiable! Most to be pitied! If this life is all there is, then Christians are the biggest fools on the planet. We gave up pleasures, we endured persecution, we sacrificed comfort, we lived by faith — all for nothing. We are pathetic.

My friends, Paul is saying: The stakes could not be higher. If the resurrection is a lie, then Christianity is not just wrong — it is the cruelest hoax ever perpetrated on the human race. There is no middle ground. You cannot say, "Well, Jesus was a good teacher but He did not really rise from the dead." If He did not rise, He is not a good teacher — He is a false prophet, because He SAID He would rise. And His followers are not good people — they are liars, because they SAID He did rise.

It is all or nothing. The resurrection is either the greatest fact in history or the greatest fraud in history. There is no in between.

### III. The Glorious "But Now" — Christ IS Risen! (1 Corinthians 15:20-23)

And then Paul pivots. Having painted the bleakest picture imaginable, he drops two words that change everything.

The Bible says in verse 20: *"But now Christ IS risen from the dead, and has become the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep."*

BUT NOW! Oh, those two words ring like a bell in the darkness! But now! After all the "what ifs," after all the devastating possibilities, after staring into the abyss of a world without resurrection — BUT NOW!

Christ IS risen. Not "might be." Not "we hope." Not "perhaps." IS. It is a fact. It is a settled, established, unshakeable, immovable, eternal FACT. Christ is risen from the dead!

And He is the "firstfruits." Now that is a word that takes us straight back to the Old Testament. In the Law of Moses, when the harvest came in, the people were to bring the very first sheaf of grain — the firstfruits — to the Lord as an offering. And that firstfruit was a PROMISE. It was a guarantee. It said: "There is more coming. This is just the beginning. The full harvest is on its way."

Jesus Christ rose from the dead — and He is the firstfruit! He is the promise! He is the guarantee! His resurrection is not the end of the story — it is the BEGINNING. Because He rose, WE will rise. Because He conquered the grave, WE will conquer the grave. The harvest is coming, my friends. One day, every person who has trusted in Jesus Christ will be raised from the dead, just as He was raised.

Verse 22-23: *"For as in Adam all die, even so in Christ all shall be made alive. But each one in his own order: Christ the firstfruits, afterward those who are Christ's at His coming."*

In Adam — death. In Christ — life. The first man brought a funeral. The second Man brought a resurrection. And if you are in Christ tonight, your future is not the grave. Your future is glory!

### IV. The Resurrection Body: From Corruption to Glory (1 Corinthians 15:35-44)

Now someone might ask — and Paul anticipates this question — "How are the dead raised up? And with what body do they come?"

The Bible says in verse 42-44: *"So also is the resurrection of the dead. The body is sown in corruption, it is raised in incorruption. It is sown in dishonor, it is raised in glory. It is sown in weakness, it is raised in power. It is sown a natural body, it is raised a spiritual body."*

Sown in corruption — raised in incorruption! My friends, this body we live in is wasting away. I know it. You know it. The aches, the pains, the diseases, the aging — this body is perishing day by day. But the resurrection body will never decay. Never grow old. Never get sick. Never wear out.

Sown in dishonor — raised in glory! When they put a body in the ground, there is nothing glorious about it. It is a humbling, heartbreaking thing. But the body that comes out of that ground on resurrection morning will shine like the stars. It will be glorious beyond anything we have ever imagined.

Sown in weakness — raised in power! We are so weak now. A virus can lay us flat. A fall can break our bones. But the resurrection body will be clothed in power — the power of Almighty God Himself.

I have stood at more gravesides than I can count. I have watched caskets lowered into the earth. And every single time, I have thought of these words: "Sown in corruption — raised in incorruption." This is not goodbye forever. This is a seed being planted. And what comes up will be far more glorious than what went down.

### V. The Great Shout of Triumph (1 Corinthians 15:50-58)

And now Paul reaches the crescendo. The climax. The great shout that has echoed through twenty centuries of Christian hope.

The Bible says in verses 51-53: *"Behold, I tell you a mystery: We shall not all sleep, but we shall all be changed — in a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trumpet. For the trumpet will sound, and the dead will be raised incorruptible, and we shall be changed."*

In a moment! In the twinkling of an eye! Faster than you can blink! The trumpet sounds. The graves burst open. The dead in Christ come forth. And those who are alive are transformed — instantly, completely, gloriously!

And then verse 54-55: *"So when this corruptible has put on incorruption, and this mortal has put on immortality, then shall be brought to pass the saying that is written: 'Death is swallowed up in victory.' 'O Death, where is your sting? O Grave, where is your victory?'"*

This is the taunt! This is the victory cry! This is the redeemed of the Lord standing on the other side of the grave, looking back at death and LAUGHING. "Where is your sting, death? You stung my Savior on Calvary, and He pulled out your stinger! Where is your victory, grave? You held my Lord for three days, and He blew the doors off! You have no power over me! You have no claim on me! I belong to the risen Christ!"

And verse 57: *"But thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ."*

Not through our good works. Not through our religion. Not through our efforts. Through our Lord Jesus Christ. HE won the victory. HE conquered death. HE walked out of that tomb. And He gives that victory to every person who puts their trust in Him.

### VI. The Confidence of the Resurrection (2 Corinthians 4:14 and 5:1-8)

The Bible says in 2 Corinthians 4:14: *"Knowing that He who raised the Lord Jesus will also raise us with Jesus, and will present us with you."*

Paul says "knowing." Not hoping. Not wishing. KNOWING. The same power that raised Jesus from the dead will raise us. This is not a maybe. This is a certainty. It is as certain as the character of God Himself.

And because of this certainty, look at what Paul says in 2 Corinthians 5:1: *"For we know that if our earthly house, this tent, is destroyed, we have a building from God, a house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens."*

This body is a tent. A tent! It is temporary. It is fragile. You can poke a hole in a tent with your finger. But when this tent comes down, we have a building — a permanent, eternal, indestructible dwelling made by God Himself.

And then the great declaration in verse 8: *"We are confident, yes, well pleased rather to be absent from the body and to be present with the Lord."*

Absent from the body — present with the Lord! There is no gap. There is no soul sleep. There is no wandering in some gray in-between. The moment a Christian closes their eyes in death, they open them in the presence of Jesus Christ. That is the confidence of the resurrection. That is why the martyrs could sing as the flames licked at their feet. That is why the saints of God have faced death with a smile on their lips and a song in their hearts for two thousand years.

### VII. The Church at Smyrna: Courage in the Face of Death (Revelation 2:8-11)

Now let me connect this to the book of Revelation, because the risen Christ speaks directly to a church that needed the resurrection more than any other.

The Bible says in Revelation 2:8: *"And to the angel of the church in Smyrna write, 'These things says the First and the Last, who was dead, and came to life.'"*

Stop right there. Look at how Jesus introduces Himself to this particular church. He does not say, "I am the Almighty." He does not say, "I am the Creator." He says, "I am the One who was DEAD and CAME TO LIFE."

Why? Because Smyrna was a suffering church. Smyrna was a persecuted church. The Christians in Smyrna were being arrested, imprisoned, tortured, and killed for their faith. They were facing death every single day. And Jesus says to them, in effect: "I know what death is. I have been there. I have tasted it. And I came back. I am alive. And because I am alive, you have nothing to fear."

Verse 10: *"Do not fear any of those things which you are about to suffer. Indeed, the devil is about to throw some of you into prison, that you may be tested, and you will have tribulation ten days. Be faithful until death, and I will give you the crown of life."*

Be faithful UNTIL DEATH. Jesus does not promise them escape from death. He does not say, "I will make sure nothing bad happens to you." He says, "Some of you WILL die. But be faithful anyway. Because death is not the end. I have the crown of life waiting for you on the other side."

My friends, this is the courage that the resurrection gives. It does not remove suffering. It does not eliminate death. But it takes away death's POWER. It takes away death's TERROR. It takes away death's FINALITY.

The great church father Polycarp was the bishop of Smyrna. Tradition tells us he was a disciple of the Apostle John himself. When he was an old man — eighty-six years old — the Roman authorities arrested him and demanded that he curse Christ. And Polycarp said those immortal words: "Eighty and six years have I served Him, and He has done me no wrong. How then can I blaspheme my King and my Savior?" They burned him at the stake. And he went to his death singing hymns.

Why? Because of the empty tomb. Because he served a risen Savior. Because he knew that to be absent from the body is to be present with the Lord.

### VIII. The Living One Who Holds the Keys (Revelation 1:17-18)

And this connects to the most magnificent self-declaration of the risen Christ in all of Scripture.

The Bible says in Revelation 1:17-18: *"Do not be afraid. I am the First and the Last. I am He who lives, and was dead, and behold, I am alive forevermore. Amen. And I have the keys of Hades and of Death."*

I am alive FOREVERMORE. Not temporarily. Not for a while. Forevermore! He will never die again. Death had its chance with Jesus Christ, and death LOST. And He will never, ever, ever die again.

And He has the KEYS of Hades and of Death. Keys represent authority. Keys represent control. Jesus Christ holds the keys! Death cannot take one single person without His permission. The grave cannot hold one single soul that He commands to rise. He is the master of death and the conqueror of the grave, and He holds the keys in His nail-scarred hands!

My friends, I do not know what you are facing tonight. Some of you are facing illness. Some of you are facing the death of someone you love. Some of you are facing your own mortality, and you are afraid.

But the Bible says, "Do not be afraid." The One who speaks those words is not some distant deity who does not understand. He is the One who DIED. He has been where you are going. And He came back. And He says, "I am alive forevermore. And because I live, you will live also."

The empty tomb changed everything. It changed everything for Peter, who went from denial to boldness. It changed everything for Thomas, who went from doubt to worship. It changed everything for Paul, who went from persecutor to apostle. It changed everything for Smyrna, who went from fear to faithfulness. And it will change everything for you tonight, if you will put your trust in the risen Christ.

He is not in the tomb. He is not in the grave. He is alive. He is here. And He is calling you.

Will you come?

# Sermon 3: "The Age of Grace — The Clock Is Ticking"

## Supplementary Preaching Content from 2 Corinthians, 1 Corinthians, and Revelation

---

### I. The Key Verse of the Age of Grace (2 Corinthians 6:1-2)

The Bible says in 2 Corinthians 6:1-2: *"We then, as workers together with Him also plead with you not to receive the grace of God in vain. For He says: 'In an acceptable time I have heard you, And in the day of salvation I have helped you.' Behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation."*

My friends, I believe this may be one of the most urgent passages in all of the Word of God. And I want you to listen to every word as though your eternal soul depends on it — because it does.

Paul says, "We PLEAD with you." That word "plead" is not a casual suggestion. It is not a polite invitation. It is a desperate, earnest, passionate appeal. Paul is on his knees, figuratively speaking, begging the Corinthians: Do not waste this! Do not let the grace of God pass you by! Do not receive it in vain!

What does it mean to receive the grace of God in vain? It means to hear the Gospel and do nothing about it. It means to sit in church week after week, year after year, hearing about the love of God and the sacrifice of Christ and the offer of salvation — and never responding. It means to know the truth in your head but never let it reach your heart. It means to be close to the Kingdom of God and yet never enter it.

And Paul says: Do not do this! For the love of God, do not do this!

And then he quotes from Isaiah 49:8 — a prophecy that spans the centuries. God said through the prophet Isaiah: "In an acceptable time I have heard you, and in the day of salvation I have helped you." There is an acceptable time. There is a day of salvation. God has appointed it. God has ordained it. And we are living in it.

And then Paul brings it crashing into the present tense: "BEHOLD — look! pay attention! wake up! — NOW is the accepted time! BEHOLD — NOW is the day of salvation!"

Not tomorrow. Not next week. Not when you get your life together. Not when you retire. Not on your deathbed. NOW. This very moment. This breath you are breathing. This heartbeat pulsing in your chest. NOW.

My friends, the Age of Grace is the period of human history in which God is holding the door open. It began at the cross and the empty tomb, and it will end when Christ returns. During this age, God is not executing judgment on the world — He is offering mercy. He is not pouring out wrath — He is extending an invitation. The door of the ark is open, just as it was in Noah's day, and God is saying, "Come in. Come in before it is too late."

But the door will not stay open forever. In Noah's day, there came a moment when God Himself shut the door of the ark. And when God shuts a door, no one can open it. The rains came, and those who were outside perished.

We are living in the Age of Grace. But the clock is ticking. And every sermon you hear, every invitation you refuse, every Sunday you walk out of church without Christ — the clock ticks a little closer to midnight.

"NOW is the accepted time. NOW is the day of salvation."

I beg you — do not waste this day.

### II. The Ministry of Reconciliation: God Is Pleading (2 Corinthians 5:18-20)

The Bible says in 2 Corinthians 5:18-20: *"Now all things are of God, who has reconciled us to Himself through Jesus Christ, and has given us the ministry of reconciliation, that is, that God was in Christ reconciling the world to Himself, not imputing their trespasses to them, and has committed to us the word of reconciliation. Now then, we are ambassadors for Christ, as though God were pleading through us: we implore you on Christ's behalf, be reconciled to God."*

I want you to see something staggering in this passage. The Bible says that God is PLEADING. The Almighty, sovereign, all-powerful Creator of heaven and earth — the One who spoke the galaxies into existence, who holds the oceans in the hollow of His hand — is PLEADING with you.

He is not demanding. He is not threatening. He is not forcing. He is pleading.

"Be reconciled to God." That is the message of the Age of Grace. Something is broken between you and God. Sin has driven a wedge between the creature and the Creator. There is a separation, a distance, a wall. And God says, "I have done everything necessary to tear down that wall. My Son died on the cross to remove that barrier. The way is open. Will you come?"

And He has committed this message to us — to the church, to His people. We are AMBASSADORS. Do you know what an ambassador is? An ambassador is a representative of a king in a foreign land. An ambassador speaks with the authority of the one who sent him. And we are ambassadors for Christ in this world, carrying the message of reconciliation.

This is what the Age of Grace is about. God is reaching out. God is making the first move. God is crossing the distance. God is not waiting for you to clean up your act and come to Him. He is coming to YOU, through the preaching of the Gospel, through the witness of His people, through the drawing of His Holy Spirit — and He is saying, "Please. Be reconciled. Come home. I love you."

I have traveled to every continent on this earth. I have preached in stadiums and arenas and open fields. I have preached to presidents and to prisoners, to the rich and to the poor. And everywhere I go, the message is the same: God loves you. Christ died for you. Be reconciled to God.

This is the Age of Grace. God is pleading. Will you listen?

### III. The Time Is Short (1 Corinthians 7:29-31)

The Bible says in 1 Corinthians 7:29-31: *"But this I say, brethren, the time is short... For the form of this world is passing away."*

The time is short. Paul wrote those words nearly two thousand years ago, and they are MORE true today than they were then. If the time was short in Paul's day, what is it now? The clock has been ticking for twenty centuries. The sands in the hourglass are running out.

"The form of this world is passing away." Everything you see around you — the buildings, the cities, the governments, the economies, the entertainment, the technology — all of it is passing away. It is temporary. It is fading. It is like the morning fog that looks so substantial but vanishes when the sun comes up.

My friends, we live as though this world will go on forever. We make our plans for five years, ten years, twenty years from now. We accumulate possessions. We build careers. We chase after things that are passing away. And all the while, eternity is rushing toward us at the speed of every ticking second.

The time is short. That is not a scare tactic. That is reality. Your life is, as James says, a vapor — it appears for a little while and then vanishes away. And when it vanishes, you will step into eternity. And the question that will matter then — the ONLY question that will matter then — is this: What did you do with Jesus Christ?

### IV. The Warning of Laodicea: The Lukewarm Church (Revelation 3:14-22)

Now I want to connect this urgency to one of the most devastating warnings in the book of Revelation — the letter to the church at Laodicea.

The Bible says in Revelation 3:15-16: *"I know your works, that you are neither cold nor hot. I could wish you were cold or hot. So then, because you are lukewarm, and neither cold nor hot, I will vomit you out of My mouth."*

My friends, these are the words of the Lord Jesus Christ, and they should make us tremble. He says, "I would rather you were cold than lukewarm." That is an astonishing statement. Jesus is saying that a person who is openly opposed to the Gospel, a person who is cold and knows they are cold, is in a better position than someone who is lukewarm — someone who has just enough religion to make them comfortable but not enough faith to make them saved.

The lukewarm person is the most dangerous person in any church. They come every Sunday. They know the hymns. They can recite the creeds. They put money in the offering plate. But their heart is far from God. They have a form of godliness but deny its power. And they are so comfortable in their lukewarmness that they do not even know they are in danger.

Look at verse 17: *"Because you say, 'I am rich, have become wealthy, and have need of nothing' — and do not know that you are wretched, miserable, poor, blind, and naked."*

"I have need of nothing." That is the motto of the lukewarm church. "I am doing fine. I do not need revival. I do not need repentance. I do not need to change. I have my religion, I have my routine, I have my respectability. I need nothing."

And Jesus says: You do not even know what you are. You think you are rich — you are poor. You think you can see — you are blind. You think you are clothed in righteousness — you are naked. You are wretched, and you do not even know it!

My friends, this is what happens when people take the grace of God for granted. This is what happens when the Age of Grace becomes an excuse for complacency. "God is gracious, so I can live however I want. God is merciful, so I do not need to be urgent about my soul. God is patient, so I have plenty of time."

NO! A thousand times, NO! The Bible says grace is not a license to be lukewarm. Grace is not permission to be half-hearted. Grace is the most costly, most precious, most urgent gift in the universe — it cost the blood of the Son of God — and to treat it casually is to trample underfoot the very sacrifice of Christ.

But even here, even to lukewarm Laodicea, Jesus offers grace. Look at verse 20: *"Behold, I stand at the door and knock. If anyone hears My voice and opens the door, I will come in to him and dine with him, and he with Me."*

Jesus is standing at the door of the church — OUTSIDE the church — knocking. He has been shut out of His own church! The church is so self-sufficient, so self-satisfied, so lukewarm that Jesus is on the outside, knocking, asking to be let in.

But He is still knocking. He has not walked away. He has not given up. He is still there, and He is still calling: "If anyone — ANYONE — hears My voice and opens the door..." It is personal. It is individual. Even if the whole church is lukewarm, if ONE person responds, Jesus will come in.

Will you be that one person tonight?

### V. The Warning of Sardis: Alive in Name Only (Revelation 3:1-6)

And there is one more church that speaks directly to this message about the Age of Grace, and that is the church at Sardis.

The Bible says in Revelation 3:1: *"And to the angel of the church in Sardis write, 'These things says He who has the seven Spirits of God and the seven stars: I know your works, that you have a name that you are alive, but you are dead.'"*

"You have a name that you are alive, but you are dead." What a terrifying diagnosis. This was a church with a reputation. People in the surrounding cities probably said, "Oh, the church at Sardis — that is a wonderful church. Great history. Great tradition. They have done marvelous things."

And Jesus says, "You are dead."

How can a church be dead and not know it? How can a church have a reputation for being alive while the reality is death? Because they were living on the memory of past grace. They were coasting on yesterday's revival. They had a name, a reputation, a history — but the fire had gone out. The Spirit had departed. They were going through the motions, performing the rituals, maintaining the institution — but there was no life.

My friends, this is what happens when the Age of Grace is treated as something that happened in the past rather than something that is happening NOW. The grace of God is not a museum piece to be admired behind glass. It is a living, active, present-tense reality. "NOW is the accepted time. NOW is the day of salvation." Not yesterday. Not in the glory days. NOW.

And Jesus gives them the remedy. Verse 2-3: *"Be watchful, and strengthen the things which remain, that are ready to die, for I have not found your works perfect before God. Remember therefore how you have received and heard; hold fast and repent. Therefore if you will not watch, I will come upon you as a thief, and you will not know what hour I will come upon you."*

Be watchful! Wake up! Strengthen what is left before it dies completely! Remember the grace you received! Hold fast! And REPENT!

The Age of Grace demands watchfulness. It demands alertness. It demands urgency. The thief does not announce his arrival. The Lord's return will come suddenly, unexpectedly, without warning. And if you are asleep — if you are dead while having a reputation for being alive — you will be caught unprepared.

The clock is ticking, my friends. The Age of Grace will not last forever. The door that is open today may be closed tomorrow. The voice that is calling today may fall silent tomorrow. The heart that is soft today may be hard tomorrow. Every day that you delay, you run the risk that the day of grace will end — either because Christ returns or because your own life comes to its close.

I have had people tell me after a crusade, "Billy, I almost came forward tonight, but I decided to wait. I will come next time." And some of those people never had a next time. A car accident. A heart attack. A sudden illness. They waited one day too long.

Do not wait. Do not gamble with your eternal soul. Do not treat the grace of God as though it is something you can pick up whenever it is convenient.

NOW is the accepted time. NOW is the day of salvation.

The Bible says, *"Today, if you will hear His voice, do not harden your hearts."*

Come now. Come today. Come while the door is open. Come while the voice is calling. Come while grace is still being offered. Because the clock is ticking, my friend, and no one knows when the time will be up.

# Sermon 4: "Sin — The Disease Nobody Wants to Talk About"

## Supplementary Preaching Content from 1 & 2 Corinthians and Revelation

---

### I. Sin in the Church: The Corinthian Scandal (1 Corinthians 5:1-8)

The Bible says in 1 Corinthians 5:1-2: *"It is actually reported that there is sexual immorality among you, and such sexual immorality as is not even named among the Gentiles — that a man has his father's wife! And you are puffed up, and have not rather mourned, that he who has done this deed might be taken away from among you."*

My friends, I want you to listen carefully, because what I am about to say is not popular in our modern world. But I did not come here to be popular. I came here to tell you the truth. And the truth is this: sin is real, sin is deadly, and sin in the church is the most dangerous sin of all.

The church at Corinth had a problem. A man in their congregation was living in open, flagrant sexual immorality — a kind so scandalous that even the pagans in that notoriously immoral city were shocked by it. He was sleeping with his father's wife — his stepmother. This was not a secret sin. This was not a momentary weakness. This was open, ongoing, defiant rebellion against the law of God.

And what was the church's response? Were they grieved? Were they brokenhearted? Were they on their faces before God, weeping and repenting? No! The Bible says they were "puffed up." They were PROUD. Perhaps they were proud of their "tolerance." Perhaps they were proud of their "grace." Perhaps they told themselves, "We are not judgmental like those other churches. We accept everyone just as they are."

My friends, there is a difference between accepting sinners and accepting sin. Jesus accepted sinners — He ate with tax collectors and prostitutes. But He never accepted sin. To the woman caught in adultery He said, "Neither do I condemn you" — that is grace. But then He said, "Go and sin no more" — that is truth. Grace and truth came together in Jesus Christ, and they must come together in His church.

And then Paul uses an illustration that every one of them would have understood. Verse 6-7: *"Your glorying is not good. Do you not know that a little leaven leavens the whole lump? Therefore purge out the old leaven, that you may be a new lump."*

A little leaven — a little yeast — works its way through the entire batch of dough. You cannot contain it. You cannot quarantine it. It spreads. And sin is the same way. You tolerate a little sin in the church, and before long, it has spread everywhere. What was once shocking becomes acceptable. What was once acceptable becomes celebrated. What was once celebrated becomes mandatory. And before you know it, the church looks no different from the world.

I have watched this happen in my lifetime. Behaviors that would have been unthinkable in the church fifty years ago are now not only tolerated but championed. And we call it progress. And we call it love. And we call it grace. But God calls it leaven. And He says, "Purge it out."

Sin tolerated becomes sin accepted. Sin accepted becomes sin celebrated. And sin celebrated becomes a church that has lost its witness, its power, and its Lord.

### II. The List and the Pivot: From "You Are" to "You Were" (1 Corinthians 6:9-11)

Now, someone might say, "Billy, are you saying that sinners cannot come to church? Are you saying the church is only for perfect people?" Absolutely not! And the next passage I want to show you proves it.

The Bible says in 1 Corinthians 6:9-10: *"Do you not know that the unrighteous will not inherit the kingdom of God? Do not be deceived. Neither fornicators, nor idolaters, nor adulterers, nor homosexuals, nor sodomites, nor thieves, nor covetous, nor drunkards, nor revilers, nor extortioners will inherit the kingdom of God."*

That is a hard list. That is an uncomfortable list. That is a list that covers sins of the body and sins of the heart, sins of passion and sins of greed, sins that are visible and sins that are hidden. And Paul says plainly: those who practice these things will not inherit the Kingdom of God. Do not be deceived about this.

Now, the world does not want to hear this. The world says, "How dare you say that? Who are you to judge?" But I am not the one saying it — GOD is saying it. The Bible is saying it. And I would rather hurt your feelings now than watch you spend eternity separated from God because no one loved you enough to tell you the truth.

Sin is a disease. And the first step in treating any disease is diagnosis. A doctor who tells you that you have cancer is not being cruel — he is being kind. Because you cannot treat what you refuse to acknowledge. And Paul is giving the Corinthians a diagnosis: This is sin. This is what separates you from God. This is what will keep you out of the Kingdom.

BUT THEN — oh, glory to God — BUT THEN comes verse 11. And this is the most beautiful pivot in all of Scripture.

*"And such WERE some of you. But you were washed, but you were sanctified, but you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and by the Spirit of our God."*

WERE! Past tense! You WERE fornicators — but not anymore! You WERE idolaters — but not anymore! You WERE adulterers, thieves, drunkards — but not anymore! Something happened to you! Someone reached into the gutter and pulled you out! Someone washed the filth away! Someone changed you from the inside out!

You were WASHED — the blood of Jesus Christ cleansed you from every stain of sin. You were SANCTIFIED — set apart, made holy, dedicated to God. You were JUSTIFIED — declared righteous, acquitted, the verdict of "guilty" overturned and "not guilty" written in its place.

My friends, the church at Corinth was not a country club for respectable people. It was a hospital for sinners who had been healed by grace. It was full of former prostitutes, former thieves, former drunkards, former cheats — people who had been dragged out of the deepest pit of sin and set on the solid rock of Jesus Christ.

And that is what the church is supposed to be today! Not a museum for saints, but a hospital for sinners! Not a place where we pretend we have never sinned, but a place where we declare that we have been forgiven! We do not hide the diagnosis — we proclaim the CURE!

I have looked into the eyes of men and women bound by every kind of sin imaginable — addiction, immorality, hatred, greed — and I have told them: Christ can change you. He changed the Corinthians, and He can change you. You do not have to stay the way you are. "Such WERE some of you." Past tense. Done. Finished. New creation.

### III. Godly Sorrow vs. Worldly Regret (2 Corinthians 7:10)

The Bible says in 2 Corinthians 7:10: *"For godly sorrow produces repentance leading to salvation, not to be regretted; but the sorrow of the world produces death."*

Now here is something that I think many people misunderstand. Not all sorrow over sin is the same. There is a sorrow that saves, and there is a sorrow that destroys. And you must know the difference.

Worldly sorrow is the sorrow of getting caught. It is the sorrow of consequences. It is the drunk driver who weeps in the courtroom — not because he is grieved over his sin, but because he is grieved over his sentence. It is the unfaithful husband who cries when his wife finds out — not because he hates what he did, but because he hates that he got caught. Worldly sorrow says, "I am sorry I got caught." But it does not change the heart. And the Bible says it produces death.

Godly sorrow is different. Godly sorrow says, "I have sinned against a holy God. I have broken His law. I have grieved His heart. I have trampled on His grace. And I am sick over it — not because of the consequences, but because of what my sin cost my Savior."

Godly sorrow looks at the cross and says, "My sin did THAT. My rebellion drove those nails. My selfishness put those thorns on His brow. And I am broken over it."

And that brokenness — that godly sorrow — produces genuine repentance. Not just regret. REPENTANCE. The Greek word is *metanoia* — a complete change of mind, a turning around, a reversal of direction. You were walking away from God, and now you turn and walk toward Him. You were loving your sin, and now you hate it. You were running from the light, and now you run to it.

I remember a man who came to one of our crusades in London. He was a businessman — successful, wealthy, respected. And he sat in his seat with tears streaming down his face. After the service, he came to me and said, "Mr. Graham, I have lived my entire life for myself. I have cheated in business. I have lied to my wife. I have ignored God. And tonight, when you preached about the cross, I saw for the first time what my sin cost. Not what it cost me — what it cost HIM. And I am broken."

That is godly sorrow. And that night, that man repented — truly repented — and gave his life to Jesus Christ. And I watched God transform him over the years that followed. His marriage was restored. His integrity was rebuilt. His life was changed from the inside out.

That is what repentance does. Not worldly regret that fades by morning. Godly sorrow that produces a lasting, real, revolutionary change.

### IV. Standing and Falling: The Warning and the Promise (1 Corinthians 10:12-13)

The Bible says in 1 Corinthians 10:12-13: *"Therefore let him who thinks he stands take heed lest he fall. No temptation has overtaken you except such as is common to man; but God is faithful, who will not allow you to be tempted beyond what you are able, but with the temptation will also make the way of escape, that you may be able to bear it."*

Here is a warning and a promise in the same breath.

The warning: "Let him who thinks he stands take heed lest he fall." My friends, the most dangerous moment in the Christian life is the moment you think you are safe from sin. The moment you say, "I would NEVER do that" — that is the moment you are most vulnerable. Peter said, "Lord, even if all the others deny You, I never will!" And before the rooster crowed, Peter had denied Jesus three times.

Pride goes before a fall. The person who is most susceptible to temptation is the person who thinks they are immune to it. And so Paul says: Take heed. Be careful. Be humble. Watch yourself. Because sin is crouching at the door, and it desires to have you.

But then comes the promise — and it is one of the most comforting promises in all of Scripture: "God is faithful." Not you — you are not faithful. You are weak. You are prone to wander. But GOD is faithful. And He will not allow you to be tempted beyond what you are able to bear.

Did you hear that? There is a limit on your temptation. God has set a boundary. The devil cannot throw anything at you that is too much for you to handle — because God will not allow it. He is watching over you. He is monitoring the pressure. And when it reaches the limit, He opens a door — a way of escape.

My friends, you do not have to give in to sin. I do not care what the temptation is. I do not care how strong it feels. I do not care how many times you have failed before. God is faithful. He has promised to provide a way out. And His promises never fail.

But you have to TAKE the escape. You have to walk through the door He opens. You have to choose, in that moment of temptation, to flee — to run in the other direction. Joseph ran from Potiphar's wife. He literally left his coat and ran. And sometimes that is what you have to do. Do not negotiate with temptation. Do not flirt with it. Do not see how close you can get to the edge without falling. RUN.

### V. The Church at Pergamos: Compromising with Sin (Revelation 2:12-17)

Now let me take you to the book of Revelation, because the risen Christ had something to say about sin in the churches — and what He said should put the fear of God in every one of us.

The Bible says in Revelation 2:12-14: *"And to the angel of the church in Pergamos write, 'These things says He who has the sharp two-edged sword: I know your works, and where you dwell, where Satan's throne is. And you hold fast to My name, and did not deny My faith... But I have a few things against you, because you have there those who hold the doctrine of Balaam, who taught Balak to put a stumbling block before the children of Israel, to eat things sacrificed to idols, and to commit sexual immorality.'"*

Now notice how Jesus introduces Himself to this church: "He who has the sharp two-edged sword." That is not a greeting of comfort. That is a warning. The sword is the Word of God, and it cuts. It separates truth from error, righteousness from sin. And Jesus is coming to Pergamos with His sword drawn.

Pergamos dwelt "where Satan's throne is." This was a city steeped in pagan worship, idolatry, and emperor worship. The Christians there were surrounded by darkness on every side. And to their credit, they had held fast. They had not denied the faith. Some of them had even been martyred — Antipas, whom Jesus calls "My faithful martyr."

But they had a problem. They had begun to COMPROMISE with the sin around them. They tolerated teachers who held the "doctrine of Balaam."

Now who was Balaam? Go back to the book of Numbers in the Old Testament. Balaam was a prophet who was hired by King Balak of Moab to curse Israel. When God would not let him curse them directly, Balaam came up with a more cunning plan: he taught Balak to send Moabite women into the Israelite camp to seduce the men into sexual immorality and idol worship. He could not defeat Israel from the outside, so he corrupted them from the inside.

That is the doctrine of Balaam: You cannot beat God's people, so infiltrate them. Get them to compromise. Get them to blend in with the world. Get them to think that a little sin is no big deal. Tell them they can worship God on Sunday and live like the world on Monday. Tell them grace means they can sin freely. Tell them that holiness is old-fashioned and purity is unrealistic.

And Jesus says to Pergamos: "REPENT, or else I will come to you quickly and will fight against them with the sword of My mouth."

Repent! That is the word. Not "adjust." Not "moderate." Not "have a conversation about it." REPENT. Turn from it. Reject it. Cast it out. Or I am coming with My sword.

My friends, there are churches today that have adopted the doctrine of Balaam. They have decided that the world's standards are more relevant than God's standards. They have decided that acceptance means affirming everything and confronting nothing. They have decided that love means never telling anyone they are wrong.

But the Jesus of Revelation is not the meek-and-mild Jesus of popular imagination. He is the one with eyes like a flame of fire and a sharp two-edged sword coming out of His mouth. And He says, "Repent, or I will fight against you."

Sin is not something to be negotiated with. It is something to be repented of.

### VI. The Church at Thyatira: Tolerating Jezebel (Revelation 2:18-29)

And if the warning to Pergamos is not sobering enough, listen to what Jesus says to the church at Thyatira.

The Bible says in Revelation 2:20: *"Nevertheless I have a few things against you, because you allow that woman Jezebel, who calls herself a prophetess, to teach and seduce My servants to commit sexual immorality and eat things sacrificed to idols."*

"You ALLOW." You TOLERATE. You PERMIT.

My friends, the sin of Thyatira was not that they were committing the sin themselves — at least, not all of them. The sin was that they ALLOWED it. They tolerated it. They let it happen on their watch. They had a woman in their church — Jesus calls her "Jezebel" — who was teaching and seducing God's servants into immorality and idolatry. And the church did nothing about it.

Now why does Jesus call her Jezebel? Because He wants us to remember the original Jezebel — the wicked queen in 1 Kings who married King Ahab of Israel and introduced Baal worship into the nation. She did not destroy Israel from the outside. She corrupted it from the inside. She brought the idols into the palace, into the courts, into the very heart of God's people. And a whole nation was led astray.

And Thyatira had its own Jezebel. Someone who was leading God's people into sin — and the church was allowing it.

But look at what Jesus says next. Verse 21: *"And I gave her time to repent of her sexual immorality, and she did not repent."*

"I gave her TIME." Do you see the grace of God even here? God gave this woman an opportunity to repent. He did not strike her down immediately. He gave her time — days, weeks, months, perhaps years. He waited. He was patient. He gave her chance after chance after chance.

And she did not repent.

My friends, there comes a point when time runs out. There comes a point when grace, having been refused again and again and again, gives way to judgment. God is patient — but God is not passive. God is longsuffering — but God is not indifferent. And when repentance is refused, judgment comes.

Verse 22-23: *"Indeed I will cast her into a sickbed, and those who commit adultery with her into great tribulation, unless they repent of their deeds. I will kill her children with death, and all the churches shall know that I am He who searches the minds and hearts. And I will give to each one of you according to your works."*

These are fearsome words. But they are necessary words. Because they tell us that God takes sin seriously. Deadly seriously. He is not winking at it. He is not looking the other way. He searches the minds and hearts. Nothing is hidden from Him.

But even here — even in the midst of this terrible warning — there is an open door. "Unless they repent." The door of repentance is still open. The invitation still stands. Even for those who have fallen into the deepest sin, God says, "Repent. Turn around. Come back to Me. And I will receive you."

My friends, sin is the disease nobody wants to talk about. We would rather talk about self-esteem. We would rather talk about personal fulfillment. We would rather talk about anything other than the ugly, devastating, soul-destroying reality of sin.

But the Bible talks about it. The Bible diagnoses it. The Bible calls it what it is. And the Bible offers the cure.

The cure is not self-improvement. The cure is not therapy. The cure is not positive thinking. The cure is the blood of Jesus Christ, shed on Calvary's cross, applied to the human heart through repentance and faith.

"Such WERE some of you. But you were washed. You were sanctified. You were justified." Past tense. Done. Finished. New creation. Old things have passed away. All things have become new.

I do not care what sin has its grip on you tonight. I do not care how long you have been in its chains. I do not care how many times you have tried to break free and failed. The blood of Jesus Christ, God's Son, cleanses us from ALL sin. Not some sin. Not most sin. ALL sin.

But you must come. You must repent. Not with worldly sorrow that produces death, but with godly sorrow that produces repentance leading to salvation. You must turn from your sin and turn to Jesus Christ. You must do it NOW — while the door is open, while grace is being offered, while the Holy Spirit is drawing you.

The Bible says, *"If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness."*

Will you confess tonight? Will you bring your sin to the cross? Will you let the Great Physician heal the disease that is destroying your soul?

Come. Come now. Come just as you are. The Savior is waiting.

## SERMON 5: "Born Again — What Jesus Really Meant"

### Supplementary Preaching Material from 1 & 2 Corinthians and the Book of Revelation

---

My friends, I want to talk to you tonight about the most important experience any human being can ever have. It is not graduating from a university. It is not getting married. It is not landing your dream job or buying your first home. The most important experience you will ever have — the experience that determines where you will spend eternity — is being *born again*.

Now, Jesus said those words to a man named Nicodemus in John chapter 3. Nicodemus was a religious leader, a Pharisee, a ruler of the Jews. He had religion. He had education. He had morality. He had respectability. And Jesus looked at this fine, upstanding, religious man and said, "Unless you are born again, you cannot see the kingdom of God."

But I want you to see tonight that this teaching did not stop with Jesus in the Gospel of John. The Apostle Paul picked up that same truth, and under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit, he gave us one of the most explosive verses in all the Bible. Turn with me to **2 Corinthians chapter 5, verse 17**. The Bible says:

> *"Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation; old things have passed away; behold, all things have become new."*

---

### I. THE NEW CREATION — What It Really Means

Now I want you to look at those words very carefully. Paul does not say, "If anyone is in Christ, he is an *improved* person." He does not say, "He is a *reformed* person." He does not say, "He is a *religious* person." He says he is a **new creation**.

Do you understand the weight of that word? A new *creation*. That word takes you all the way back to Genesis chapter 1, when God spoke the heavens and the earth into existence out of nothing. "In the beginning, God *created* the heavens and the earth." That was creation — God bringing something into being that had never existed before. Something entirely new. Something that bore His fingerprint, His power, His design.

And Paul says, my friends, that when you come to Jesus Christ — when you repent of your sin and put your faith in Him — God performs an act of creation *inside you* that is just as real, just as powerful, and just as miraculous as when He spoke the universe into existence. You become a *new* creation. Not a repaired creation. Not a patched-up creation. Not an upgraded creation. A **new** creation.

I have been to many countries around the world, and everywhere I go I see people trying to change themselves. They try education, and education is a wonderful thing, but education cannot change the human heart. They try psychology, and there are helpful things in psychology, but psychology cannot make you a new person inside. They try religion — attending services, performing rituals, following rules — and religion has its place, but religion alone cannot recreate your soul.

Only God can create. And when Paul says "new creation," he is telling you that the new birth is nothing less than a *supernatural act of God*. It is God doing for you what you could never do for yourself. It is God reaching down into the wreckage of your fallen human nature and making something *brand new*.

The Bible says in Ezekiel 36:26, "I will give you a new heart and put a new spirit within you; I will take the heart of stone out of your flesh and give you a heart of flesh." That is what happens when you are born again. God takes out the old heart — the heart that was hard, rebellious, indifferent to God — and He puts in a *new* heart. A heart that loves what God loves. A heart that grieves over sin. A heart that hungers for righteousness. A heart that longs for His presence.

---

### II. OLD THINGS PASSED AWAY — What Dies When You Are Born Again

Now look at what the verse says next: *"Old things have passed away."*

When you are born again, something *dies*. Oh, I know that sounds strange. We are talking about birth, and yet there is a death. But that is exactly how God works. Jesus said in John 12:24, "Unless a grain of wheat falls into the ground and dies, it remains alone; but if it dies, it produces much grain." There must be a death before there can be a new life.

What are the "old things" that pass away? Let me tell you, my friends.

The old *guilt* passes away. Before you come to Christ, you carry the weight of every sin you have ever committed. It sits on your soul like a millstone. You may try to forget it. You may try to excuse it. You may try to drink it away or work it away. But it is there. It haunts you in the quiet hours of the night. But when you are born again — when you come to Christ — the Bible says your sins are forgiven. They are removed "as far as the east is from the west" (Psalm 103:12). The old guilt passes away.

The old *bondage* passes away. Before Christ, you are a slave to sin. You do things you do not want to do. You go back to the same habits, the same addictions, the same patterns of destruction, again and again. You promise yourself, "Never again," and then you do it again. That is the bondage of the old nature. But when you are born again, the chains break. Paul writes in Romans 6:6, "Our old man was crucified with Him, that the body of sin might be done away with, that we should no longer be slaves of sin." The old bondage passes away.

The old *way of seeing* passes away. Before Christ, you saw the world one way. You lived for yourself. Your goals were your own comfort, your own pleasure, your own advancement. But when you are born again, everything looks different. You see people differently — they are not obstacles or tools, they are souls for whom Christ died. You see money differently — it is not yours to hoard but God's to steward. You see suffering differently — it is not pointless, it is purposeful. The old way of seeing the world passes away.

And then Paul says — and I love this — *"Behold, all things have become new."*

Did you notice that word *"behold"*? Paul puts that word in there because he wants you to stop and *look*. He wants you to marvel. He wants you to be astonished. "Behold!" Look at this! All things — not some things, not most things — *all things* have become new. New desires. New affections. New purposes. New power. New hope. New destiny. You are not the person you used to be. You are a new creation in Christ Jesus.

I remember hearing about a man who had been a terrible alcoholic for twenty-five years. He was cruel to his wife, neglectful of his children, and miserable in his own skin. One night, at a simple gospel meeting in a small church, he heard the message of Christ, and he gave his life to the Lord. The next morning, his little daughter came downstairs and saw her father sitting at the kitchen table reading the Bible. She ran back upstairs to her mother and said, "Mommy, Mommy, come quick — God gave us a new daddy!" That is what the new creation looks like, my friends. God gives you a new daddy. God gives you a new mommy. God gives you a new husband, a new wife, a new son, a new daughter. He makes all things new.

---

### III. WHY YOU MUST BE BORN AGAIN — The Natural Man Cannot See

But now I want to show you *why* the new birth is absolutely necessary. Turn back to **1 Corinthians chapter 2, verse 14**. The Bible says:

> *"But the natural man does not receive the things of the Spirit of God, for they are foolishness to him; nor can he know them, because they are spiritually discerned."*

Now that is one of the most sobering verses in the entire Bible. Paul is telling us that before the new birth, there are things about God that you *cannot* understand. Not that you *will not* understand them — you *cannot*. You are spiritually incapable. It is not a matter of intelligence. Some of the most brilliant people in the world look at the gospel and say, "That is foolishness." And Paul says, *of course* they do. Because spiritual things must be *spiritually* discerned, and the natural man — the man who has not been born again — does not have the spiritual equipment to see them.

Think of it this way. Imagine a man who has been blind from birth. You take him out into a garden full of beautiful flowers — roses, lilies, sunflowers, all the colors of creation — and you say, "Look at these beautiful colors!" And he says, "What are you talking about? I do not see anything. There are no colors." Now, is the problem with the flowers? No. The flowers are real and they are beautiful. The problem is with his *eyes*. He does not have the capacity to see what is right in front of him.

That is the condition of every man and woman before the new birth. The glory of God is all around you. The truth of the gospel is shining like the noonday sun. The love of Christ is as real as the air you breathe. But you cannot see it, you cannot feel it, you cannot understand it, because you are spiritually blind. Your spirit is dead in trespasses and sins, as Paul says in Ephesians 2:1. And a dead man cannot see anything.

That is *why* Jesus said, "You *must* be born again." It is not a suggestion. It is not a recommendation. It is not one option among many. It is a divine necessity. Without the new birth, you cannot see the kingdom of God. Without the new birth, you cannot enter the kingdom of God. Without the new birth, the things of God will always be foolishness to you.

But oh, my friends, when the Holy Spirit opens your eyes — when you are born again — suddenly you can see! The gospel that once seemed like nonsense now seems like the most glorious truth in the universe. The cross that once seemed like weakness now seems like the power of God. The Bible that once seemed like an old, dusty book now comes alive in your hands. You are *seeing* for the first time. The lights have come on. You have been born again.

---

### IV. THE VEIL REMOVED — Progressive Transformation

And here is something beautiful about the born-again life. It does not stop at the moment of conversion. Turn with me to **2 Corinthians chapter 3, verses 16 through 18**. The Bible says:

> *"Nevertheless when one turns to the Lord, the veil is taken away. Now the Lord is the Spirit; and where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is liberty. But we all, with unveiled face, beholding as in a mirror the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from glory to glory, just as by the Spirit of the Lord."*

Paul uses a magnificent picture here. He refers back to Moses, who would come down from the mountain of God with his face shining so brightly from being in God's presence that the people could not look at him. Moses had to put a veil over his face (Exodus 34:33). But Paul says, when you turn to Christ, *the veil is taken away*. There is nothing between you and God anymore. No barrier. No curtain. No wall of sin. The veil is gone.

And then he says something astonishing. He says that as we behold the glory of the Lord — as we look at Him, worship Him, walk with Him, read His Word, spend time in His presence — we are being *transformed* into His image. Changed. Slowly, steadily, surely, being made more and more like Jesus Christ. "From glory to glory," Paul says. From one degree of glory to the next. From one level of Christlikeness to a deeper level.

The new birth is not just a one-time event, my friends. It is the *beginning* of a lifelong transformation. The moment you are born again, you receive a new nature. But then, day by day, week by week, year by year, the Holy Spirit is working in you, conforming you to the image of Jesus Christ. You are being changed from the inside out. And the more you behold Him — the more you fix your eyes on Jesus — the more you become like Him.

I have known people who walked with God for fifty years, and I can tell you, there is something about them. There is a kindness, a gentleness, a depth of love, a quiet strength that you do not find in the world. It is the glory of the Lord shining through an unveiled face. They have been beholding Him so long that they are beginning to look like Him. That is the born-again life in full bloom.

---

### V. BAPTIZED INTO ONE BODY — The New Birth Connects You

There is one more aspect of the new birth I want to show you from Paul's letters. Turn to **1 Corinthians chapter 12, verse 13**. The Bible says:

> *"For by one Spirit we were all baptized into one body — whether Jews or Greeks, whether slaves or free — and have all been made to drink into one Spirit."*

When you are born again, you are not born into isolation. You are born into a *family*. You are baptized by the Holy Spirit into the body of Christ — the Church. You are connected to every other believer on the face of the earth. The man sitting next to you in the pew, the woman across the aisle, the brother in Christ on the other side of the world whom you have never met — you are one body. One Spirit. One family.

The new birth makes you a member of the most extraordinary community in all of human history. It crosses every boundary that human beings have erected — racial boundaries, social boundaries, economic boundaries, national boundaries. "Whether Jews or Greeks, whether slaves or free" — in Christ, those distinctions do not determine your worth or your place. You are one.

This is why the church matters, my friends. I know there are people who say, "I can be a Christian without going to church." And I want to say to them, gently but firmly, that is like saying, "I can be a hand without being connected to a body." It does not work. When you are born again, you are baptized into the body, and you need the body, and the body needs you. You are not meant to walk this road alone.

---

### VI. THE WARNING FROM REVELATION — Religion Without Regeneration

Now I want to take you to the book of Revelation, because our Lord Jesus Christ had something to say about the new birth that should make every one of us sit up straight in our seats. Turn to **Revelation chapter 3, verses 1 through 6** — the letter to the church at Sardis. Listen to what Jesus says:

> *"I know your works, that you have a name that you are alive, but you are dead."*

My friends, there is nothing more terrifying in all the Bible than those words. "You have a name that you are alive, but you are *dead*."

Sardis was a church. They had a building. They had a congregation. They had services on the Lord's Day. They had a name — they called themselves Christians. They had a *reputation*. They were known as a living church. People in the surrounding area probably said, "Oh yes, the church at Sardis — wonderful church, very active." They had a name that they were alive.

But Jesus, who sees not the outward appearance but the heart, looked at that church and said, "You are dead."

This is the danger of religion without regeneration. This is the peril of churchgoing without conversion. This is what happens when you have the *form* of godliness but deny its *power* (2 Timothy 3:5). You can sit in a church pew every Sunday for fifty years and still be dead in your sins. You can sing the hymns, put money in the offering plate, serve on a committee, teach a Sunday School class, and still be dead. Because you have never been born again.

Jesus goes on to say to Sardis, *"I have not found your works perfect before God"* (Revelation 3:2). Their works were there — they were busy, they were active — but their works were incomplete, unfulfilled, not measuring up. Why? Because works done without the new birth are dead works. They may look impressive to the eye, but they are wood, hay, and stubble in the sight of God.

But then Jesus gives them hope. He says in verse 4, *"You have a few names even in Sardis who have not defiled their garments; and they shall walk with Me in white, for they are worthy."* Even in that dead church, there were a few — just a *few* — who were truly born again. A few who were genuinely alive. A few who had not just the name but the *reality*. And Jesus says, "They shall walk with Me."

I want to ask you tonight: Are you among the few? Or are you part of the crowd that has a name — "Christian," "church member," "religious person" — but is spiritually dead? Do not be deceived. The most dangerous place in the world is a church pew occupied by someone who has never been born again, because you are so *close* to the truth and yet so *far* from it. You are like a man dying of thirst while sitting next to a well.

---

### VII. THE OVERCOMERS — You Cannot Overcome Without the New Birth

Now let me show you something that runs through all seven of the churches in Revelation. In every single letter — to Ephesus, to Smyrna, to Pergamos, to Thyatira, to Sardis, to Philadelphia, to Laodicea — Jesus ends with a promise "to him who overcomes."

To the overcomer in Ephesus: "I will give to eat from the tree of life" (Revelation 2:7).
To the overcomer in Smyrna: "He shall not be hurt by the second death" (Revelation 2:11).
To the overcomer in Pergamos: "I will give some of the hidden manna" and "a white stone with a new name" (Revelation 2:17).
To the overcomer in Thyatira: "I will give power over the nations" (Revelation 2:26).
To the overcomer in Sardis: "He shall be clothed in white garments, and I will not blot out his name from the Book of Life" (Revelation 3:5).
To the overcomer in Philadelphia: "I will make him a pillar in the temple of My God" (Revelation 3:12).
To the overcomer in Laodicea: "I will grant to sit with Me on My throne" (Revelation 3:21).

Seven churches. Seven promises. All to the overcomer.

But here is my question, my friends: Who *can* overcome? Can the natural man overcome the world, the flesh, and the devil? Can the unregenerate person overcome temptation, overcome sin, overcome the seductions of this age?

The Bible answers that question plainly. **1 John 5:4** says, *"This is the victory that has overcome the world — our faith."* And verse 5: *"Who is he who overcomes the world, but he who believes that Jesus is the Son of God?"*

Only the born-again believer can overcome. The natural man, the man Paul describes in 1 Corinthians 2:14, cannot overcome because he does not even understand the battle. He does not see the spiritual realities. He does not have the Holy Spirit empowering him. He is fighting without armor, without weapons, without a commanding officer. He is a dead soldier on a battlefield.

But the new creation — the man or woman who has been born again by the Spirit of God — has the power to overcome. Because "greater is He who is in you than he who is in the world" (1 John 4:4). Because you are a new creation. Because old things have passed away and all things have become new. Because the same Spirit who raised Jesus from the dead now lives inside you (Romans 8:11).

---

### THE INVITATION

So I ask you tonight, plainly and simply: Have you been born again?

I am not asking if you go to church. I am not asking if you were baptized as a baby. I am not asking if you come from a Christian family. I am not asking if you consider yourself a good person.

I am asking: Has there been a moment in your life when you realized you were a sinner, separated from God, unable to save yourself — and you turned to Jesus Christ in repentance and faith and said, "Lord, I need You. Save me. Make me new"?

If you have never done that, you can do it tonight. Right now. Right where you are sitting. The Bible says in 2 Corinthians 5:17 that *if anyone* is in Christ — anyone, that means *you* — you become a new creation. The old passes away. Everything becomes new.

Jesus is not asking you to clean yourself up. He is not asking you to become religious. He is asking you to be *born again*. To receive a new life. A new heart. A new spirit. A new beginning. And He will do it, my friends, if you will come to Him tonight.

Do not be like the church at Sardis — alive in name, dead in reality. Come to Christ, and He will make you truly, eternally, gloriously *alive*.

Let us pray.

## SERMON 6: "Revelation — The King Is Coming Back"

### Supplementary Preaching Material from 1 & 2 Corinthians and the Book of Revelation

---

My friends, I want to speak to you tonight about the most anticipated event in all of human history. It is not the next election. It is not the next technological breakthrough. It is not the next peace treaty or economic recovery. The most anticipated event in all of human history is the *return of Jesus Christ*.

The whole Bible points toward this moment. From Genesis to Revelation, the scarlet thread of Scripture pulses with this one magnificent hope: *He is coming back.* The prophets foretold it. The angels proclaimed it. Jesus Himself promised it. And the Apostle Paul, writing to the church at Corinth, gave us one of the most breathtaking descriptions of that coming day anywhere in the Word of God.

Turn with me to **1 Corinthians chapter 15, verses 51 and 52**. The Bible says:

> *"Behold, I tell you a mystery: We shall not all sleep, but we shall all be changed — in a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trumpet. For the trumpet will sound, and the dead will be raised incorruptible, and we shall be changed."*

---

### I. THE MYSTERY REVEALED — "We Shall All Be Changed"

Paul begins with that word *"mystery."* In the New Testament, a mystery is not a whodunit novel. A mystery is a truth that was hidden in the past but has now been revealed by God. And Paul says, "I am going to tell you something that God has revealed to me — something the Old Testament prophets glimpsed in shadows but could not fully see."

And here is the mystery: *"We shall not all sleep."* Not every believer will die. Some will still be alive when Jesus returns. And those who are alive — along with those who have already died in Christ — will be *changed*. Transformed. In a single, instantaneous, breathtaking act of divine power, our mortal bodies will be changed into immortal, glorified, resurrection bodies.

Now look at the speed of it. Paul says, *"In a moment."* The Greek word is *atomos* — we get our English word *atom* from it. It means an indivisible unit of time. The smallest possible fraction of a second. You cannot split it any further. That is how fast it will happen.

And then Paul uses a phrase that I love: *"In the twinkling of an eye."* Now, how fast is the twinkling of an eye? Scientists tell us that the blink of a human eye takes about 300 to 400 milliseconds. But Paul does not say the *blink* of an eye. He says the *twinkling* of an eye. That is not the blink — that is the glint of light that reflects off the surface of the eye. It is faster than the blink itself. It is almost instantaneous.

My friends, that is how fast the return of Christ will change everything. One moment you are sitting in your living room, or driving your car, or working in your office, or lying in your bed. The next moment — faster than you can blink, faster than light can dance across the surface of your eye — the trumpet sounds, the heavens open, and *everything changes*.

The dead in Christ rise first. The graves open. Every believer who has ever died — from the first martyr to the last saint who drew their final breath moments before — rises with a new, glorified, incorruptible body. And then we who are alive are caught up together with them in the air, and so we shall always be with the Lord.

I have preached in many parts of the world where believers are suffering. I have been to places where Christians meet in secret, where they face imprisonment and death for their faith. And I have seen the light in their eyes when you speak of the return of Christ. Because for them, it is not a theological abstraction. It is their *hope*. It is the moment when all their suffering ends, when every tear is wiped away, when every wrong is made right. The King is coming back, and when He comes, *we shall all be changed*.

---

### II. EVERYTHING EXPOSED — Nothing Hidden Anymore

But the return of Christ is not only a day of glory for the believer. It is also a day of *exposure*. Turn to **1 Corinthians chapter 4, verse 5**. The Bible says:

> *"Therefore judge nothing before the time, until the Lord comes, who will both bring to light the hidden things of darkness and reveal the counsels of the hearts. Then each one's praise will come from God."*

When Jesus returns, He will bring to light the *hidden things of darkness*. My friends, every person in this room tonight has secrets. Things you have done that nobody else knows about. Words you have spoken in private. Thoughts you have entertained in the dark chambers of your mind. Actions you have taken when you believed no one was watching.

But Someone *was* watching. God sees everything. And when Christ returns, the hidden things will be brought to light. Not to embarrass you — if you are in Christ, your sins have been forgiven and covered by the blood — but to reveal the truth. Nothing will remain concealed. The masks we wear will be removed. The pretenses we maintain will be stripped away.

This is a solemn truth, and it ought to make every one of us search our hearts. Are you living a double life? Are you one person on Sunday morning and another person the rest of the week? Are there hidden things in your life that you would be ashamed of if they were projected on a screen for the whole world to see? My friend, the day is coming when Christ will reveal the counsels of every heart. Make sure your heart is clean.

---

### III. THE JUDGMENT SEAT OF CHRIST — Every Believer Will Give Account

Now I want to take you to a passage that many Christians overlook, and it is one of the most important passages in the New Testament regarding the return of Christ. Turn to **2 Corinthians chapter 5, verse 10**. The Bible says:

> *"For we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ, that each one may receive the things done in the body, according to what he has done, whether good or bad."*

Now pay very close attention. Paul is writing to *believers*. He is not talking about the Great White Throne Judgment of Revelation 20, where the unsaved are judged. He is talking about the *Bema seat* — the judgment seat of Christ — where *believers* give account.

The word *Bema* comes from the ancient Greek athletic competitions. After the races were run and the contests were completed, the athletes would stand before the *Bema* — the judge's platform — and receive their rewards. The crowns. The prizes. The honors. It was not a criminal court. It was a reward ceremony. But it was also a moment of evaluation, because not every athlete ran well.

Paul is telling us that every Christian will stand before Christ and give an account of their life. Not to determine whether they go to heaven — that was settled at the cross. But to determine their *rewards*. How did you live after you were saved? How did you use your time, your talents, your treasure, your opportunities? Were you faithful? Were you obedient? Did you serve? Did you love? Did you share the gospel? Did you care for the poor, the widow, the orphan?

And Paul says each one will receive according to what he has done — *"whether good or bad."* That word "bad" in the Greek is *phaulos*, and it does not mean "evil" in the sense of wicked wickedness. It means *worthless*. *Trivial.* Empty. Some believers will stand before Christ and realize that they spent their lives on things that did not matter. They were saved, yes — but their works amounted to nothing of eternal value.

That is a sober thought, my friends. And it ought to drive every one of us to live with purpose, with intentionality, with an eye on eternity. The King is coming back, and when He comes, He is not only coming to *save* — He is coming to *evaluate*.

---

### IV. GOLD, SILVER, PRECIOUS STONES — The Fire Test

And that brings me to one of the most vivid pictures in all of Paul's letters. Turn back to **1 Corinthians chapter 3, verses 12 through 15**. The Bible says:

> *"Now if anyone builds on this foundation with gold, silver, precious stones, wood, hay, straw, each one's work will become clear; for the Day will declare it, because it will be revealed by fire; and the fire will test each one's work, of what sort it is. If anyone's work which he has built on it endures, he will receive a reward. If anyone's work is burned, he will suffer loss; but he himself will be saved, yet so as through fire."*

What a picture! Paul says we are all building on the foundation, which is Jesus Christ (verse 11). But what are we building *with*? Some people build with gold, silver, and precious stones — enduring materials, valuable materials, materials that can withstand fire. These represent works done for the glory of God, in the power of the Spirit, motivated by love, aligned with His will. Ministry that cost you something. Sacrifice that nobody saw. Faithfulness in the small things. Kindness shown to the least of these.

But other people build with wood, hay, and straw. These look impressive for a while. They might even be large structures — big ministries, grand programs, elaborate religious enterprises. But they are flammable. They cannot survive the fire of God's testing. These represent works done for the wrong motives — for applause, for recognition, for selfish gain. Religion performed to be seen by men. Service rendered with impure hearts.

And Paul says the Day — the day of Christ's return — will reveal it all. The fire will test every work. And when the fire is done, some will have a pile of gleaming gold, refining silver, and glittering precious stones. They will receive a reward. But others will have nothing left but ash. Their works will be burned up completely. They themselves will be saved — thank God for that! — "yet so as through fire." They will smell like smoke. They will arrive in heaven with nothing to show for their lives on earth.

My friends, I do not want to arrive at the judgment seat of Christ empty-handed. I do not want to smell like smoke. I want to hear my Lord say, "Well done, good and faithful servant." And I believe you want to hear that, too.

So let me ask you: What are you building with? Is your life being invested in things that will last forever — souls, Scripture, service, sacrifice? Or are you pouring your energy into things that will burn up in a moment — possessions, pleasures, prestige, the praise of men?

The King is coming back. And He is bringing fire with Him.

---

### V. THE SEVEN CHURCHES OF REVELATION — A Prophetic Mirror

Now let me take you into the very heart of the book of Revelation. In chapters 2 and 3, the risen, glorified Lord Jesus Christ dictates seven letters to seven churches in Asia Minor. These were real churches in real cities in the first century. But they are also much more than that. They are a prophetic picture of the entire history of the Church across the centuries. And they are a *mirror* for every church and every individual believer today.

I want to walk through all seven, because when Jesus comes back, He is coming back to *these* churches — and He has something to say to each one.

---

**1. EPHESUS — The Church That Lost Its First Love (Revelation 2:1-7)**

Ephesus was the *apostolic* church. It was founded by Paul himself. It had sound doctrine. It tested false apostles and found them liars. It labored hard and did not grow weary. By every outward measure, Ephesus was a model church.

But Jesus says, *"I have this against you, that you have left your first love"* (Revelation 2:4).

My friends, you can have perfect theology and a cold heart. You can be doctrinally precise and spiritually lifeless. Ephesus had truth without tenderness, orthodoxy without passion, labor without love. And Jesus said, "Repent — or I will remove your lampstand." A church without love is a church without light.

In the story of church history, Ephesus represents the first-century church — the church of the apostles. Doctrinally rich. But even before the apostles had all passed from the scene, the love was beginning to cool.

Let me ask you: Do you remember when you first came to Christ? Do you remember the fire, the passion, the overwhelming love you felt for Him? Is it still burning? Or has it grown cold? Jesus is coming back, and He wants your *love*, not just your labor.

---

**2. SMYRNA — The Persecuted Church (Revelation 2:8-11)**

Smyrna was the *suffering* church. Jesus says to them, *"I know your works, tribulation, and poverty (but you are rich)"* (Revelation 2:9). They were poor in the world's eyes but rich in God's. They were oppressed, persecuted, some of them thrown into prison.

And what does Jesus say to this suffering church? He does not rebuke them. Not one word of criticism. He says, *"Be faithful until death, and I will give you the crown of life"* (Revelation 2:10).

In church history, Smyrna represents the era of the Roman persecutions — the second and third centuries, when Christians were thrown to the lions, burned at the stake, and tortured for their faith. And still they overcame.

There are believers around the world today who are living in Smyrna. They are suffering for Christ. And the King who is coming back sees their tears, knows their pain, and will reward their faithfulness with a crown.

---

**3. PERGAMOS — The Compromising Church (Revelation 2:12-17)**

Pergamos was the church that *married the world*. Jesus says they dwelt "where Satan's throne is" (Revelation 2:13) — right in the heart of pagan power. And some in Pergamos held to the doctrine of Balaam, who taught God's people to compromise with idolatry and immorality. They also held to the doctrine of the Nicolaitans, which many scholars believe was an early form of clericalism — a priestly class that lorded it over God's people.

In church history, Pergamos represents the era when the church became institutionalized under Constantine in the fourth century. The church married the state. Christianity became popular, and with popularity came compromise. The world came into the church, and the church began to look like the world.

My friends, we are living in a Pergamos age today. The pressure to compromise is enormous. The culture says, "Tone down your message. Soften your stance. Do not be so narrow." But Jesus says to the compromising church, *"Repent, or else I will come to you quickly and will fight against them with the sword of My mouth"* (Revelation 2:16). The King is coming, and He is not coming with a handshake for compromise — He is coming with a *sword*.

---

**4. THYATIRA — The Corrupt Church (Revelation 2:18-29)**

Thyatira was the church that *tolerated false teaching*. Jesus says, *"You allow that woman Jezebel, who calls herself a prophetess, to teach and seduce My servants to commit sexual immorality and eat things sacrificed to idols"* (Revelation 2:20). False prophecy. False teaching. Immorality tolerated in the name of tolerance.

In church history, Thyatira represents the medieval church — the long period when corruption, superstition, and unbiblical teaching dominated. The church of the Dark Ages, when the Word of God was kept from the common people, and human tradition was elevated above divine revelation.

Jesus says to Thyatira, *"I am He who searches the minds and hearts"* (Revelation 2:23). He *sees* the corruption. He is not fooled. And He is coming to set it right.

---

**5. SARDIS — The Dead Church (Revelation 3:1-6)**

We spoke of Sardis in our last message. "You have a name that you are alive, but you are dead." Religion without reality. Activity without life. The church of the Reformation era — in history, a time when the great truths were recovered but soon calcified into dead orthodoxy.

The warning is clear: You can have the right name on the door and the right creed on the wall and still be dead inside. The King is coming back, and He sees through the facade.

---

**6. PHILADELPHIA — The Faithful Church (Revelation 3:7-13)**

Philadelphia — the church of brotherly love. This is the church I want to be part of, my friends. Jesus says to Philadelphia, *"I have set before you an open door, and no one can shut it; for you have a little strength, have kept My word, and have not denied My name"* (Revelation 3:8).

Philadelphia was not a mega-church. They had "a little strength." They were not impressive by worldly standards. But they had two things that made them precious to Christ: they *kept His Word* and they *did not deny His name*. In a world of compromise, they held fast.

And Jesus makes them a staggering promise: *"Because you have kept My command to persevere, I also will keep you from the hour of trial which shall come upon the whole world"* (Revelation 3:10). Many scholars believe this is a reference to the Great Tribulation — and that the faithful church will be kept from it. Taken out. Raptured. Changed in the twinkling of an eye before the wrath falls.

In church history, Philadelphia represents the great missionary era — the 18th and 19th centuries — when believers took the gospel to the ends of the earth through an open door that no one could shut.

Are you a Philadelphia Christian? Do you keep God's Word? Do you refuse to deny His name, even when it costs you? The King is coming, and He has an open door for the faithful.

---

**7. LAODICEA — The Lukewarm Church (Revelation 3:14-22)**

And finally, Laodicea. The lukewarm church. The self-sufficient church. Listen to what Jesus says: *"I know your works, that you are neither cold nor hot. I could wish you were cold or hot. So then, because you are lukewarm, and neither cold nor hot, I will vomit you out of My mouth"* (Revelation 3:15-16).

My friends, those are some of the most frightening words in the Bible. Jesus says lukewarmness makes Him *nauseous*. He would rather you be ice cold than halfhearted. At least a cold person knows they are cold. A lukewarm person thinks they are fine.

And listen to Laodicea's self-assessment: *"I am rich, have become wealthy, and have need of nothing"* (Revelation 3:17). They thought they had it all together. They were comfortable, prosperous, self-satisfied.

But Jesus says, *"You do not know that you are wretched, miserable, poor, blind, and naked."*

In church history, Laodicea represents the church of the last days — the church of our present age. Affluent. Comfortable. Entertained. Lukewarm. "We do not need anything," we say. But Jesus says we are blind and do not even know it.

This is the church Jesus is coming back to, my friends. And I ask you tonight — I ask you with a broken heart — which church are *you*? Are you Ephesus, having lost your first love? Are you Smyrna, suffering but faithful? Are you Pergamos, compromising with the world? Are you Thyatira, tolerating what should not be tolerated? Are you Sardis, dead but pretending to be alive? Are you Philadelphia, keeping God's Word and holding fast to His name? Or are you Laodicea, lukewarm, comfortable, and nauseating to the Lord?

---

### THE INVITATION

The King is coming back. The Bible says in 1 Corinthians 15:52, *"The trumpet will sound."* It could sound tonight. It could sound before the sun rises tomorrow morning. It could sound before you finish reading this page.

Are you ready?

Paul says in 2 Corinthians 5:10 that we must *all* appear before the judgment seat of Christ. That includes you. That includes me. There are no exemptions, no excuses, no continuances.

What will you have to show for your life? Gold, silver, precious stones? Or wood, hay, and straw?

If you have never given your life to Christ, I urge you — come to Him tonight. Come before the trumpet sounds. Come while there is still time. The King who is coming back in glory is *also* the Savior who stands with open arms tonight, saying, "Come unto Me."

And if you *are* a believer, but your life has grown cold, your fire has dimmed, your love has faded — come back. Repent. Return to your first love. The King is coming, and He wants to find you faithful.

Let us pray.

## SERMON 7: "The Bible — God's Love Letter to You"

### Supplementary Preaching Material from 1 & 2 Corinthians and the Book of Revelation

---

My friends, I hold in my hand tonight a book. It does not look like much from the outside — leather cover, thin pages, small print. You can buy one for a few dollars in almost any bookstore in the world. It sits on nightstands and coffee tables and church pews, sometimes gathering dust. Many homes have one. Many people own one. But far too few have ever truly *opened* one and let its words sink deep into their souls.

This book is the Bible. And I want to tell you tonight that this is no ordinary book. This is God's Word. This is God's love letter to you. This is the living, breathing, supernatural revelation of the God who made you, who loves you, and who wants you to know Him.

Now, there are many things I could say about the Bible tonight, and we do not have time for all of them. But I want to show you what the Apostle Paul — writing under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit — tells us about this remarkable book and how it works in the life of a believer.

Turn with me to **1 Corinthians chapter 2, verses 9 and 10**. The Bible says:

> *"Eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor have entered into the heart of man the things which God has prepared for those who love Him. But God has revealed them to us through His Spirit. For the Spirit searches all things, yes, the deep things of God."*

---

### I. WHAT THE HUMAN MIND COULD NEVER DISCOVER — Supernatural Revelation

Now that is an extraordinary passage. Paul quotes from Isaiah 64:4 and says that there are things — magnificent, glorious, wonderful things — that God has prepared for those who love Him, and those things are so far beyond human comprehension that no eye has seen them, no ear has heard them, and no human heart has ever imagined them.

Let me put it simply. If God had remained silent — if He had never spoken, never revealed Himself, never given us this Book — you and I would know almost *nothing* about the most important questions of life. Where did we come from? Why are we here? Where are we going when we die? What is the meaning of life? What is the purpose of suffering? Is there a God? Does He care? Can we know Him?

The greatest philosophers in human history have wrestled with these questions for thousands of years and have come to no consensus. The human mind, left to itself — no matter how brilliant — cannot penetrate the mysteries of God. The eye cannot see them. The ear cannot hear them. The heart cannot imagine them.

But then Paul says the most wonderful thing: *"But God has revealed them to us through His Spirit."*

God did not leave us in the dark. He *spoke*. He revealed Himself. He pulled back the curtain and showed us what we could never have discovered on our own. And the means by which He did it — the vehicle, the instrument — is this Book. The Bible.

The Bible is not a human book that contains some nice ideas about God. The Bible is a *divine* book — a supernatural revelation from the mind of God to the hearts of men. The Apostle Peter says it this way: *"Holy men of God spoke as they were moved by the Holy Spirit"* (2 Peter 1:21). God *moved* them. God breathed His truth through human authors — through their personalities, their experiences, their vocabularies — and the result is a book that is both fully human and fully divine. Every word of it is God-breathed (2 Timothy 3:16).

That is why this Book is different from every other book ever written. It is why it has survived every attempt to destroy it — and there have been many. Emperors have tried to burn it. Governments have tried to ban it. Critics have tried to discredit it. Skeptics have tried to disprove it. And yet here it stands, after thousands of years, the best-selling book in the history of the world, still changing lives, still breaking hearts, still opening blind eyes, still transforming sinners into saints.

As I have traveled the world, I have seen the Bible's power in every culture, every language, every continent. I have seen a tribal chief in Africa weep over the Gospel of John. I have seen a university professor in Europe fall to his knees after reading Romans. I have seen a prisoner in a cold cell find freedom through the Psalms. This Book has a power that no human book possesses, because it is not merely the word of man — it is the Word of *God*.

---

### II. THE LETTER KILLS, BUT THE SPIRIT GIVES LIFE

Now, there is a warning I must give you about the Bible, and it comes from **2 Corinthians chapter 3, verse 6**. The Bible says:

> *"The letter kills, but the Spirit gives life."*

Now what does that mean? Does Paul mean the Bible is dangerous? Does he mean we should not read it? No, not at all. What Paul means is that the Bible, when read apart from the Holy Spirit, becomes merely a *letter* — a collection of rules, laws, commands, and doctrines that condemn you. The Law of God, taken by itself, shows you how far short you fall. It holds up a perfect standard and says, "You have failed." The letter kills.

But when the Holy Spirit illuminates the Scriptures — when you read the Bible with an open, believing, Spirit-filled heart — the same words that condemn now *liberate*. The same commands that seemed impossible now become empowering. The law that showed you your sin now points you to the Savior who forgives your sin. The Spirit gives *life*.

This is why you can have two people reading the same Bible and having completely different experiences. One reads it and finds nothing but dead religion — rules, restrictions, judgments. The other reads it and encounters the *living God*. The difference is not in the Book. The difference is in the reader. One is reading with the Spirit; the other is reading without Him.

I have heard people say, "I tried reading the Bible, but I could not understand it." My friend, I am not surprised. If you try to read the Bible in your own human understanding, without asking the Spirit of God to open your eyes, it will be confusing and frustrating. But if you will get on your knees before you open this Book and say, "Lord, open my eyes to see wonderful things from Your Word" (Psalm 119:18), I promise you — the pages will come alive.

The Bible is not meant to be read like a textbook. It is meant to be read like a love letter. And when you read a love letter, you do not just analyze the grammar and the syntax — you *feel* the heart of the one who wrote it. You read it slowly, savoring every word, hearing the voice of the beloved behind the pen.

That is how God wants you to read His Word. With the Spirit giving life to every page.

---

### III. THE BLINDING — Satan's War Against Scripture

But I must tell you, my friends, that there is an enemy who does not want you to read this Book. Turn to **2 Corinthians chapter 4, verses 3 and 4**. The Bible says:

> *"But even if our gospel is veiled, it is veiled to those who are perishing, whose minds the god of this age has blinded, who do not believe, lest the light of the gospel of the glory of Christ, who is the image of God, should shine on them."*

Paul tells us here that Satan — the god of this age — is actively, intentionally, relentlessly *blinding* the minds of unbelievers so that they cannot see the light of the gospel shining through Scripture. This is not a passive blindness. This is an active, demonic blinding.

Do you wonder why some people can hear the gospel preached with power and clarity and walk away completely unmoved? Do you wonder why someone can sit in church for years and never be changed? Do you wonder why you can hand someone a Bible and they treat it like it is nothing more than an old history book?

Paul tells you why: the god of this age has blinded their minds. It is *spiritual warfare*, my friends. Every time you open the Bible, every time the gospel is preached, every time the truth of God goes forth, there is a battle raging in the invisible realm. Satan knows the power of this Book. He knows that if the light of the gospel shines into a darkened heart, that heart will be transformed. So he does everything in his power to keep people from seeing it.

He blinds through distraction — "I am too busy to read the Bible." He blinds through doubt — "Can we really trust a book that was written thousands of years ago?" He blinds through pride — "I am too intelligent to believe in fairy tales." He blinds through indifference — "It does not really matter; all religions are basically the same."

Every one of those thoughts is a veil placed by the enemy over the eyes of the human soul. And the only thing that can tear that veil away is the power of God.

---

### IV. "LET THERE BE LIGHT" — When God Opens Your Eyes

And that brings me to the next verse, one of the most beautiful verses in all of Paul's letters. **2 Corinthians chapter 4, verse 6**:

> *"For it is the God who commanded light to shine out of darkness, who has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ."*

Do you see what Paul is doing here? He takes you all the way back to Genesis 1. *"In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth. The earth was without form, and void; and darkness was on the face of the deep."* Total darkness. Absolute blackness. Nothing but chaos and emptiness and dark.

And then God spoke. Four words that changed everything: *"Let there be light."* And there was light. The darkness was shattered. The void was filled. Creation began.

Paul says that the *same* God who spoke light into the dark void of creation has *shone in our hearts* to give us the light of the knowledge of His glory in the face of Jesus Christ.

My friends, when God opens the Bible to your heart — when the Spirit illuminates the Word — it is like Genesis 1 all over again. Your soul has been in darkness — dark, formless, chaotic, empty. And God speaks into that darkness, and He says, "Let there be light!" And suddenly there *is* light. Suddenly you can see. Suddenly the Bible is no longer a dead book — it is a *living* Word. Suddenly you see the glory of God shining in the face of Jesus Christ.

That is what happens when you read the Bible with the eyes of faith. It is not just information transferred from a page to your mind. It is *illumination*. It is light from heaven breaking into the darkness of your soul. It is God Himself revealing Himself to you through His written Word.

I remember Dr. Billy Sunday — the great evangelist of an earlier generation — once said that he held the Bible up and he could see *through* it. Not physically, of course. But spiritually. He saw God through its pages. He saw Christ in every chapter. He saw the Holy Spirit breathing through every verse.

That is what the Bible is meant to be. Not just read, but *seen through* — a window into the heart of God.

---

### V. WRITTEN FOR US — The Old Testament Is Our Book Too

Now there is one more verse I want to show you, and it is vitally important for understanding how the Bible works. Turn to **1 Corinthians chapter 10, verse 11**. The Bible says:

> *"Now all these things happened to them as examples, and they were written for our admonition, upon whom the ends of the ages have come."*

Paul is talking about the Old Testament. He has just recounted the stories of Israel — the Exodus, the wilderness wandering, the golden calf, the rebellion, the judgment. And he says these things happened as *examples* — literally, *types*, patterns — and they were written down for *our* benefit. For us, who live at the end of the ages.

Do you understand what that means, my friends? It means the Old Testament is not just ancient history. It is *your* book. It was written for *you*. When you read about Abraham leaving everything to follow God's call, that is for you — because God may be calling you to leave your comfort zone and step out in faith. When you read about Moses standing before Pharaoh and saying, "Let my people go," that is for you — because God wants to set you free from every form of bondage. When you read about David facing Goliath with nothing but a sling and a stone and the name of the Lord, that is for you — because you have giants in your life, and the same God who gave David the victory will give you the victory.

The whole Bible — from Genesis to Revelation, from the creation of the world to the creation of the new heavens and new earth — is God's love letter to you. Every page is written with you in mind. Every story is told for your instruction. Every promise is given for your encouragement. Every warning is sounded for your protection. Every prophecy is declared for your hope.

As the great preacher Charles Spurgeon once said, "The Bible is a letter from God with your personal address on it." He wrote it for you, my friend. He wrote it because He loves you and wants you to know Him.

---

### VI. THE CHURCH AT PHILADELPHIA — "You Have Kept My Word"

Now let me take you to the book of Revelation, because our Lord Jesus Christ has something remarkable to say about people who value His Word. Turn to **Revelation chapter 3, verses 7 through 13** — the letter to the church at Philadelphia.

Jesus says to this church, *"I know your works. See, I have set before you an open door, and no one can shut it; for you have a little strength, have kept My word, and have not denied My name"* (Revelation 3:8).

Do you see the commendation? Two things. First, *"You have kept My Word."* Second, *"You have not denied My name."* That is it. That is what made Philadelphia the faithful church. Not great resources. Not impressive numbers. Not worldly influence. They had "a little strength" — they were small, they were weak by human standards.

But they held on to the Bible. They kept God's Word. They did not water it down, did not twist it, did not accommodate it to the culture, did not apologize for it, did not ignore the parts that made people uncomfortable. They kept it. All of it. They treasured it and obeyed it and lived by it.

And because they kept His Word, Jesus set before them an *open door* — a door of opportunity, a door of ministry, a door of advance — and no one could shut it. My friends, when you hold fast to Scripture, God opens doors that no person, no government, no force of hell can close. The open door is for those who keep the Word.

The name *Philadelphia* means "brotherly love." And I want you to see the connection. When you truly love God's Word, you will love God's people. The two go together. You cannot love the Bible and hate your brother. You cannot treasure Scripture and despise the church. The Word of God creates a community of love — brotherly love, Philadelphia love — because the God who wrote the Book is the God who *is* love.

Jesus also promises Philadelphia something extraordinary: *"Because you have kept My command to persevere, I also will keep you from the hour of trial which shall come upon the whole world, to test those who dwell on the earth"* (Revelation 3:10). The faithful church — the church that keeps God's Word — will be *kept* by God in the day of trial. What a promise! What a motivation to hold fast to Scripture in these last days!

---

### VII. THE CHURCH AT EPHESUS — Testing All Things by the Word

Now turn back to **Revelation chapter 2, verses 1 through 7** — the letter to the church at Ephesus. Jesus gives this church a remarkable commendation. He says, *"You have tested those who say they are apostles and are not, and have found them liars"* (Revelation 2:2).

Ephesus was a *discerning* church. When someone showed up claiming to be a teacher, claiming to have a word from God, claiming apostolic authority, the Ephesians did not just accept it at face value. They *tested* it. They measured it against the standard of God's revealed truth. And when the teaching did not line up with Scripture, they called it what it was — a *lie*.

My friends, we need that kind of discernment today more than ever. We live in an age of deception. False teachers abound. People come in the name of Christ and teach things that Christ never taught. They twist Scripture. They invent doctrines. They tell people what they want to hear instead of what they need to hear. Paul warned Timothy about this: *"The time will come when they will not endure sound doctrine, but according to their own desires, because they have itching ears, they will heap up for themselves teachers; and they will turn their ears away from the truth, and be turned aside to fables"* (2 Timothy 4:3-4).

How do you protect yourself from deception? The Bible is your standard. The Bible is your measuring stick. The Bible is your plumb line. When someone tells you something about God, you measure it against Scripture. If it lines up, you receive it. If it does not, you reject it — no matter how famous the person is who said it, no matter how good it sounds, no matter how many people believe it.

The Bible says in 1 Thessalonians 5:21, *"Test all things; hold fast what is good."* Test it! Test every sermon, every book, every teaching, every prophetic utterance, every spiritual claim — test it by the Word of God. That is what the Ephesians did, and Jesus commended them for it.

You see, the Bible is not just a devotional book for quiet moments. It is a *weapon* against falsehood. It is a *shield* against deception. It is the "sword of the Spirit" (Ephesians 6:17), and every believer needs to know how to wield it.

---

### THE INVITATION

My friends, God has given you a love letter. It is sitting on your shelf. It is in your drawer. It may be on an app on your phone. But it is there, waiting for you.

The Bible says in 1 Corinthians 2:9 that God has prepared things for those who love Him that are beyond anything you could imagine. And He has *revealed* those things through His Spirit in His Word.

But Satan is working to keep you from it. He is blinding minds, sowing doubt, fueling indifference. And the question tonight is this: Will you let him win?

Or will you open the Book?

Open it, my friends. Read it. Study it. Memorize it. Treasure it. Let the Spirit breathe life through its pages into your soul. Let God speak "Let there be light!" into the darkness of your heart.

The Bible says in Psalm 119:105, *"Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path."* In a world of darkness and confusion, this Book is your light. Do not neglect it. Do not ignore it. Do not leave it gathering dust while your soul starves for truth.

God wrote you a love letter. Open it. And hear Him say, "I love you. I have always loved you. And I have so much to show you."

Let us pray.

## SERMON 8: "What Will You Do With Jesus?" (Grand Finale)

### Supplementary Preaching Material from 1 & 2 Corinthians and the Book of Revelation

---

My friends, we have come to the final sermon in this series, and I want to speak to you tonight from the deepest place in my heart. I have preached the gospel for many years, in many places, to many people. I have preached in stadiums and in small churches, in great cities and in tiny villages, on every continent and in many languages. And every time I stand to preach, there is one question — one unavoidable, inescapable, eternal question — that presses upon my soul. It is the question that Pontius Pilate asked two thousand years ago, and it is the question I ask *you* tonight:

**What will you do with Jesus?**

In Matthew 27:22, Pilate stood before the crowd with Jesus bound before him and said, *"What then shall I do with Jesus who is called Christ?"* And that question has echoed across twenty centuries, and it echoes in this room tonight, and it demands an answer from every one of you.

You cannot avoid this question. You cannot table it for later. You cannot refer it to a committee. You cannot abstain from voting. Every human being who has ever heard the name of Jesus Christ must decide: What will I do with Him?

Tonight, I want to bring everything together. I want to bring the fullness of Paul's teaching in 1 and 2 Corinthians to bear on this one decisive moment. And I want to connect it to the final words of Jesus in the book of Revelation — and to the very last invitation in all of Holy Scripture.

---

### I. GOD IS PLEADING WITH YOU — The Ambassador's Cry

Turn with me to **2 Corinthians chapter 5, verse 20**. The Bible says:

> *"Now then, we are ambassadors for Christ, as though God were pleading through us: we implore you on Christ's behalf, be reconciled to God."*

My friends, I want you to feel the weight of what Paul is saying here. He says God is *pleading*. Do you understand that word? The God of the universe — the God who flung the stars into space, who set the sun on fire, who scooped out the oceans with the palm of His hand, who breathed the breath of life into the dust of the ground and made a man — that God is *pleading* with you tonight.

He is not demanding. He is not threatening. He is not twisting your arm or holding a sword over your head. He is *pleading*. He is *imploring*. He is, as it were, on His knees before you, saying, "Please. Come home. Be reconciled to Me."

Now why would God plead? Because He loves you. Because He made you. Because He knows what you were designed for, and He knows that apart from Him, you will never find it. Because He sent His Son to die for you, and the price that was paid was infinite, and He does not want that price to have been paid in vain for you. Because He sees where you are headed without Him, and it breaks His heart.

When I stand before an audience and preach the gospel, I am an ambassador. Paul says we are *ambassadors for Christ*. An ambassador does not speak his own words — he speaks the words of the one who sent him. And tonight, I am not speaking my own words. I am speaking the words of the King. And the King is saying, through me, through this message, through the stirring of His Holy Spirit in your heart right now: *"Be reconciled to God."*

That word *reconciled* means to have the relationship restored. It means the war is over. It means the distance is closed. It means the broken fellowship is repaired. God is not your enemy, my friend. He never wanted to be your enemy. *Sin* made Him your enemy. Sin put a wall between you and your Creator. But Christ tore that wall down at the cross. And now God says, "The way is open. Come back to Me. Be reconciled."

I have seen hardened criminals weep when they heard those words. I have seen wealthy executives who had everything the world could offer fall to their knees in surrender. I have seen teenagers, senior citizens, professors, farmers, doctors, homeless men, single mothers — people from every walk of life — melt under the tender plea of a loving God who says, "Come home."

What will you do with that plea tonight?

---

### II. NOW IS THE TIME — The Urgency of the Decision

Turn now to **2 Corinthians chapter 6, verse 2**. The Bible says:

> *"Behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation."*

Paul uses the word *"now"* twice. Not tomorrow. Not next week. Not next year. Not "when I get my life together." Not "when I am older." Not "when I have had a little more time to think about it." **NOW.**

I cannot tell you, my friends, how many people I have met who said, "I will think about it." And they left that meeting, and they never came back. I have met people who said, "Not tonight. Some other time." And that other time never came. I have met people who said, "I am not ready yet" — and they walked out of the meeting and were killed in an automobile accident on the way home and stood before God without Christ.

I do not say that to frighten you. I say it because it is true. The Bible says, *"You do not know what will happen tomorrow. For what is your life? It is even a vapor that appears for a little time and then vanishes away"* (James 4:14). Your life is a vapor. A mist. A breath on a cold morning that appears for a moment and then is gone.

The Bible says in Proverbs 27:1, *"Do not boast about tomorrow, for you do not know what a day may bring forth."* You do not *have* tomorrow. Tomorrow is not promised to you. The only moment you have — the only moment you are *guaranteed* — is this one. Right now.

And God, in His mercy, has arranged the circumstances of your life so that you are hearing this message right now. It is not an accident that you are here. It is not a coincidence. God drew you here. God has been working in your life — through circumstances, through people, through that quiet voice in the night that keeps asking, "When are you going to deal with Me?" — God has been pursuing you. And now, tonight, He says, *"Now is the accepted time."*

This is your moment, my friend. Do not let it pass.

---

### III. JESUS IS EVERYTHING — Wisdom, Righteousness, Sanctification, Redemption

But perhaps you are wondering, *What am I deciding about? Who exactly is this Jesus?* Let Paul tell you. Turn to **1 Corinthians chapter 1, verses 30 and 31**. The Bible says:

> *"But of Him you are in Christ Jesus, who became for us wisdom from God — and righteousness and sanctification and redemption — that, as it is written, 'He who glories, let him glory in the Lord.'"*

Listen to what Jesus is. Paul lists four things, and each one is staggering.

**First, He is your WISDOM.** In a world of confusion, where people do not know which way to turn, where philosophies clash and ideologies compete and everyone has an opinion but nobody has the truth — Jesus is wisdom. He is the answer to every question that matters. He is the truth in a world of lies. "In Him are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" (Colossians 2:3). You do not need to wander in the dark anymore. Jesus is the light.

**Second, He is your RIGHTEOUSNESS.** You cannot make yourself right with God. You have tried, and you have failed. Every religion in the world tells you to work harder, be better, do more. But the gospel says, "Christ *is* your righteousness." His perfect life is credited to your account. His obedience becomes your obedience. When God looks at you in Christ, He does not see your sin — He sees the righteousness of His Son. That is the glory of the gospel.

**Third, He is your SANCTIFICATION.** Not only does He save you, He *changes* you. He makes you holy. He sets you apart. He begins the work of transformation that we talked about from 2 Corinthians 3:18 — "from glory to glory." You are not left in your sin. You are not saved and then abandoned to fight on your own. Christ is your sanctification. He does the work of making you like Himself.

**Fourth, He is your REDEMPTION.** That word means He has bought you back. You were a slave to sin, a prisoner in chains, sold under the dominion of darkness. And Jesus paid the ransom. He bought you back with His own blood. You are *redeemed* — purchased, liberated, set free. The chains are broken. The prison door is open. You can walk out into freedom tonight.

And then Paul says this — and it is the capstone: *"He who glories, let him glory in the Lord."* In other words, after all this — after wisdom, righteousness, sanctification, and redemption — you have nothing to boast about in yourself. It is ALL Christ. ALL grace. ALL His work. The glory belongs to Him alone.

So what will you do with the One who is everything? What will you do with your wisdom, your righteousness, your sanctification, your redemption? Will you receive Him? Or will you walk away from the One who is the answer to everything your soul has ever longed for?

---

### IV. EXAMINE YOURSELF — Are You Really In the Faith?

Now I want to share with you a verse that may make some of you uncomfortable. But I share it in love, because I would rather you be uncomfortable for a few minutes tonight than unprepared for eternity. Turn to **2 Corinthians chapter 13, verse 5**. The Bible says:

> *"Examine yourselves as to whether you are in the faith. Test yourselves. Do you not know yourselves, that Jesus Christ is in you? — unless indeed you are disqualified."*

Paul is writing to a church. He is writing to people who call themselves Christians. And he says, "Examine yourselves." *Test* yourselves. Do not just *assume* you are in the faith. Do not just assume that because you walked an aisle, or prayed a prayer, or signed a card, or were baptized as a child, that everything is settled. Examine yourself.

My friends, this is one of the most important things I will say tonight: It is possible to be in the church and not be in Christ. It is possible to have a Christian label and not have the Christian life. It is possible to be going through the motions — attending, giving, serving — and yet Christ is not in you.

Jesus Himself warned about this. He said in Matthew 7:21-23, *"Not everyone who says to Me, 'Lord, Lord,' shall enter the kingdom of heaven, but he who does the will of My Father in heaven. Many will say to Me in that day, 'Lord, Lord, have we not prophesied in Your name, cast out demons in Your name, and done many wonders in Your name?' And then I will declare to them, 'I never knew you; depart from Me, you who practice lawlessness!'"*

Those are the most terrifying words in the Bible. "Many will say to Me in that day..." Many! Not a few. Many. People who prophesied. People who cast out demons. People who did great works in Christ's name. And Jesus says, "I never knew you."

So I ask you tonight, with the love of a friend and the urgency of a man who believes in eternity: Do you *know* Him? Not just *about* Him — do you *know* Him? Is Jesus Christ living in you by His Spirit? Is there evidence of the new birth in your life? Have you been changed? Are you being changed?

Do not be afraid to ask yourself these questions. It is far better to examine yourself now, while there is still time to come to Christ, than to discover the truth on that Day when it is too late.

---

### V. THE MOST SOLEMN WARNING — "If Anyone Does Not Love the Lord"

And now, my friends, I come to what may be the most solemn verse in all of Paul's letters. It is a verse that shakes me every time I read it. Turn to **1 Corinthians chapter 16, verse 22**. The Bible says:

> *"If anyone does not love the Lord Jesus Christ, let him be accursed. O Lord, come!"*

*If anyone does not love the Lord Jesus Christ, let him be accursed.*

That word *accursed* in the Greek is *anathema*. It means devoted to destruction. Set apart for judgment. Cut off.

Paul is not talking about people who have never heard of Jesus. He is talking about people who have heard — who have been in the church, who have been taught the truth, who have been given every opportunity — and yet do not *love* Him. Not merely intellectual assent. Not merely cultural identification. Not merely religious observance. *Love.* Do you *love* Jesus Christ?

That is the final test, my friends. Not "Do you believe the right doctrines?" The demons believe and tremble (James 2:19). Not "Do you attend the right church?" Sardis had a church. Not "Do you perform the right rituals?" The Pharisees performed every ritual perfectly. The final test is: Do you *love* the Lord Jesus Christ?

And then Paul adds two words that are not really English at all. He uses an Aramaic expression that was a common prayer in the early church: *"O Lord, come!"* — in Aramaic, *Maranatha!* It was the cry of the early believers. "Come, Lord Jesus! Come quickly!"

Do you see the contrast? On one hand, the solemn warning: if you do not love Christ, you face judgment. On the other hand, the joyful prayer: Lord, come! The same return of Christ that is *dread* for the unbeliever is *hope* for the believer. The same event that is *terror* for the one who does not love Him is *glory* for the one who does.

Which side are you on tonight? When you hear the words, "The Lord is coming," does your heart leap with joy — or sink with fear?

---

### VI. THE SEVEN CHURCHES AND THE FINAL INVITATION — "Behold, I Stand at the Door and Knock"

Now let me bring together everything we have studied in the seven churches of Revelation. We looked at Ephesus, who lost their first love. Smyrna, who suffered faithfully. Pergamos, who compromised with the world. Thyatira, who tolerated corruption. Sardis, who was dead while claiming to be alive. Philadelphia, who kept God's Word. And Laodicea, who was lukewarm and self-satisfied.

Seven churches. Seven conditions. Seven warnings. Seven promises to the overcomers.

But at the end of the letter to Laodicea — the very last of the seven letters — Jesus says something that I believe is meant for every person who has ever heard the gospel. It is in **Revelation chapter 3, verses 20 through 22**:

> *"Behold, I stand at the door and knock. If anyone hears My voice and opens the door, I will come in to him and dine with him, and he with Me. To him who overcomes I will grant to sit with Me on My throne, as I also overcame and sat down with My Father on His throne. He who has an ear, let him hear what the Spirit says to the churches."*

My friends, this is Jesus Christ — the risen, glorified, all-powerful Son of God — and He is standing at the door of your heart, and He is *knocking*. He is not breaking the door down. He is not forcing His way in. He is knocking. Gently. Persistently. Patiently.

Have you heard His knock? Maybe you heard it tonight in something I said. Maybe you have been hearing it for weeks, for months, for years. Maybe it is a quiet voice in the depths of your soul that will not go away, no matter how hard you try to silence it. That is Jesus, knocking.

And He says, *"If anyone hears My voice and opens the door, I will come in."* The door handle is on *your* side. God will not force salvation upon you. He will plead, He will persuade, He will knock — but He will not break the door down. You must open it. You must choose. You must decide.

And when you open the door, He does not just step in and stand in the hallway. He says, *"I will come in to him and dine with him, and he with Me."* In the ancient world, to dine with someone was the deepest expression of fellowship, of intimacy, of friendship. Jesus is saying, "I do not want to be a distant God. I want to sit at the table with you. I want *fellowship* with you. I want to know you, and I want you to know Me."

And then the promise to the overcomer — the final promise: *"I will grant to sit with Me on My throne."* My friends, do you grasp the magnitude of that? Jesus is saying, "Share My *throne*." The throne of the universe. The seat of all authority. He is inviting you — you, a sinner saved by grace — to share in His eternal reign. That is grace beyond all comprehension.

---

### VII. THE LAST INVITATION IN THE BIBLE — "Come!"

And now I want to take you to the very end of the Bible. The very last chapter. The very last invitation. Turn to **Revelation chapter 22, verse 17**. The Bible says:

> *"And the Spirit and the bride say, 'Come!' And let him who hears say, 'Come!' And let him who thirsts come. Whoever desires, let him take the water of life freely."*

Do you see it, my friends? The very last invitation in the Word of God is a single word: **COME.**

The Spirit says, "Come!" — that is God the Holy Spirit, drawing you, calling you, wooing you to Christ.

The bride says, "Come!" — that is the Church, the people of God, who have tasted the goodness of the Lord and want you to taste it too. They are saying, "Come and join us. Come and experience what we have found."

And then: "Let him who hears say, 'Come!'" — everyone who has received the invitation is now invited to pass it along. If you have heard the gospel, tell someone else. Say, "Come!"

And then the most beautiful, most inclusive, most wide-open invitation in all of Scripture: *"Let him who thirsts come. Whoever desires, let him take the water of life freely."*

**Whoever.** Not whoever is good enough. Not whoever is smart enough. Not whoever is religious enough. *Whoever desires.* If you have a thirst in your soul tonight — a thirst for forgiveness, for peace, for meaning, for God — you qualify. You are invited. Come.

And notice: *"let him take the water of life freely."* It is free. You cannot buy it. You cannot earn it. You cannot work for it. It is a gift. The greatest gift ever offered. Eternal life, freely given to whoever will take it.

The Bible begins with God creating a perfect world in Genesis. It ends with God creating a perfect world again in Revelation — a new heaven and a new earth where there is no more death, no more tears, no more pain, no more sin. And the very last word of the gospel, the final note on the final page, is not a command, not a threat, not a demand. It is an invitation.

*Come.*

---

### THE FINAL ALTAR CALL

My friends, we have journeyed together through eight sermons. We have walked through the cross, through grace, through faith, through the new birth, through the return of Christ, through the Word of God. And it all comes down to this one moment. This one question. This one decision.

**What will you do with Jesus?**

Pilate tried to wash his hands of the question. It did not work. You cannot wash your hands of Jesus. To ignore Him is to reject Him. To delay is to decide — against Him. There is no neutral ground.

Paul says in 2 Corinthians 5:20, God is *pleading* through me tonight: Be reconciled to God.

Paul says in 2 Corinthians 6:2, *NOW* is the accepted time. *NOW* is the day of salvation.

Paul says in 1 Corinthians 1:30, Jesus is your wisdom, your righteousness, your sanctification, your redemption — He is *everything*.

Paul says in 2 Corinthians 13:5, examine yourself. Are you truly in the faith?

Paul says in 1 Corinthians 16:22, if you do not love the Lord Jesus Christ — Maranatha. He is coming.

Jesus says in Revelation 3:20, "I stand at the door and knock." He is knocking at your heart right now. Can you hear it? That gentle, persistent, loving knock? He has been knocking your whole life. Tonight, will you open the door?

And the Spirit and the bride say, *"Come!"*

Come, you who are weary of carrying your burden of sin. Come and lay it at the foot of the cross.

Come, you who have tried everything else — success, pleasure, religion, philosophy — and found them all empty. Come to the One who is the water of life.

Come, you who have been running from God for years. Stop running. Turn around. He is right there, arms wide open, waiting for you.

Come, you who think you have gone too far, sinned too greatly, waited too long. The invitation says *whoever*. That means you. No matter what you have done. No matter where you have been. *Whoever desires, let him take the water of life freely.*

I am going to ask you to do something tonight. I am going to ask you to get up out of your seat, and come forward, and give your life to Jesus Christ. I am not asking you to join a church. I am not asking you to join a religion. I am asking you to come to a *Person* — the Person of Jesus Christ — and say, "Lord, I am a sinner. I need You. I believe You died for me and rose again. I open the door of my heart. Come in. Make me new. I am Yours."

Some of you have been Christians for years, but you know your heart has grown cold. You have become Ephesus — you have left your first love. Or you have become Laodicea — lukewarm, comfortable, self-sufficient. Come back tonight. Repent. Return to your first love.

And some of you have never made that decision. You have been in church, you have heard the gospel, you have been close — but you have never crossed the line. Tonight is your night. Now is the accepted time. The door is open. The invitation is given. The Spirit is calling. The bride is saying, "Come."

And the very last prayer in the Bible is this: *"Even so, come, Lord Jesus!"* (Revelation 22:20).

He is coming. He is coming for His own. Will you be among them?

What will you do with Jesus?

Come. Come now. While there is still time. While the door is still open. While the voice is still calling.

*Come.*

Let us pray.
