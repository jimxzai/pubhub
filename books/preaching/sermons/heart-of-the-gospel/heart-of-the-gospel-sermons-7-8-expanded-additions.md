# SERMON 7: "The Bible — God's Love Letter to You" — EXPANDED ADDITIONS

---

## A. Old Testament Typology — How the Whole Bible Points to Jesus

My friends, I want to show you something tonight that I believe will take your breath away. I want to show you that this Book — all sixty-six books of it, from the first verse of Genesis to the last verse of Revelation — is telling *one* story. And the hero of that story, on every single page, is Jesus Christ.

There is a scarlet thread that runs through the entire Bible. You pick it up in Genesis 3, when the blood of an animal was shed to cover the nakedness and shame of Adam and Eve. And you follow that thread through the blood of Abel's offering, through the blood on the doorposts in Egypt, through the blood of bulls and goats on the altar in the tabernacle, through the blood that the high priest carried once a year into the Holy of Holies — and that thread leads you, mile after mile, century after century, all the way to a hill outside Jerusalem called Calvary, where the Lamb of God shed His own precious blood for the sins of the whole world.

**The Bible says** in Luke 24:27: *"And beginning at Moses and all the prophets, He expounded to them in all the Scriptures the things concerning Himself."*

Did you hear that? After He rose from the dead, Jesus walked with two disciples on the road to Emmaus, and He opened up the Old Testament and said, "Let Me show you — it is ALL about Me." Every page. Every story. Every law. Every sacrifice. Every prophecy. Every psalm. It all points to Christ.

Let me take you on a walk through the Old Testament tonight, and let me show you Jesus hiding in plain sight on every page.

---

### Jesus in Every Book

**In Genesis**, Jesus is the Seed of the woman. God said to the serpent in Genesis 3:15, "I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your seed and her Seed; He shall bruise your head, and you shall bruise His heel." That is the first promise of a Savior — right there on the third page of the Bible. But He is also the Ark of safety. When the flood came and judgment swept across the earth, there was only one way to be saved — you had to get into the ark. My friend, Jesus is your Ark. The judgment is coming, and the only safety is in Him. And He is the Ram in the thicket — when Abraham lifted the knife over his son Isaac on Mount Moriah, God provided a ram, caught in the thicket by its horns, to die in Isaac's place. That was a picture of the Son of God, who would die in *your* place on that very same mountain range two thousand years later.

**In Exodus**, Jesus is the Passover Lamb. The death angel was coming through Egypt, and God said, "Take a lamb without blemish, kill it, put the blood on the doorpost, and when I see the blood, I will pass over you." My friend, that was not just a history lesson. That was a prophecy. John the Baptist looked at Jesus and said, "Behold! The Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world!" And Jesus is the Rock that was struck. In the wilderness, the people were dying of thirst, and God told Moses to strike the rock, and water gushed out to save the people. **The Bible says** in 1 Corinthians 10:4 that Rock was Christ. He was struck — struck on the cross — and from His wounded side flows the water of life for everyone who will drink.

**In Leviticus**, Jesus is the great High Priest who stands between a holy God and sinful humanity. He is also the Sacrifice itself. Every lamb, every bull, every goat that was offered on that altar was a shadow, a preview, a coming attraction of the one final Sacrifice that would take away sin once and for all. **The Bible says** in Hebrews 10:12: *"But this Man, after He had offered one sacrifice for sins forever, sat down at the right hand of God."* The Old Testament priests never sat down — their work was never finished. But Jesus sat down, because His sacrifice was complete.

**In Numbers**, Jesus is the Bronze Serpent lifted up on a pole. The people had sinned, and fiery serpents came among them, and many were dying. God told Moses, "Make a bronze serpent and put it on a pole, and whoever looks at it will live." And Jesus said in John 3:14-15, *"As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of Man be lifted up, that whoever believes in Him should not perish but have eternal life."* All you had to do was look and live. All you have to do tonight is look to Jesus and live.

**In Deuteronomy**, Jesus is the Prophet like Moses. God said in Deuteronomy 18:15, *"The LORD your God will raise up for you a Prophet like me from your midst, from your brethren. Him you shall hear."* Moses delivered God's people from bondage in Egypt. Jesus delivers God's people from bondage to sin. Moses was the mediator of the old covenant. Jesus is the mediator of the new and better covenant.

**In Joshua**, Jesus is the Captain of the Lord's host. When Joshua was standing before the walls of Jericho, a Man appeared with a drawn sword and said, "As Commander of the army of the LORD I have now come." That was the pre-incarnate Christ — the Son of God appearing in the Old Testament, leading His people into the promised land. My friend, Jesus is leading you into a promised land tonight — a land of forgiveness, a land of peace, a land of eternal life.

**In Ruth**, Jesus is the Kinsman-Redeemer. Under the old law, if a family member lost their land, a close relative — a kinsman — had the right to buy it back, to *redeem* it. Boaz redeemed Ruth's inheritance and took her as his bride. That is exactly what Jesus did for you. You had lost everything — your innocence, your fellowship with God, your eternal inheritance. And Jesus, your Kinsman-Redeemer, paid the price to buy it all back. He paid with His own blood. And He took you as His bride — the Church is called the bride of Christ.

**In the Psalms**, Jesus is the Good Shepherd of Psalm 23 — "The Lord is my shepherd; I shall not want." He is the Forsaken One of Psalm 22 — "My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?" — a psalm written a thousand years before the crucifixion that describes the scene at Calvary with such precise detail that you would think David was standing at the foot of the cross. The pierced hands and feet. The casting of lots for His garments. The mocking crowd. It is all there in Psalm 22. And He is the King of Psalm 2 — "I will declare the decree: The LORD has said to Me, 'You are My Son, today I have begotten You. Ask of Me, and I will give You the nations for Your inheritance.'"

**In Proverbs**, Jesus is Wisdom personified. Proverbs chapter 8 describes Wisdom as a person who was with God from the very beginning, who was "brought forth" before the world began, who was "daily His delight, rejoicing always before Him." That is Christ — the Wisdom of God. **The Bible says** in 1 Corinthians 1:24 that Christ is *"the power of God and the wisdom of God."*

**In Isaiah**, Jesus is the Suffering Servant of chapter 53 — "He was wounded for our transgressions, He was bruised for our iniquities" — and He is Immanuel in chapter 7 — "The virgin shall conceive and bear a Son, and shall call His name Immanuel," which means "God with us." God did not stay far away in heaven and shout instructions down. He came. He moved into the neighborhood. He became one of us. He is Immanuel — God *with us*.

**In Daniel**, Jesus is the Son of Man who comes with the clouds of heaven to receive an everlasting kingdom. He is also the Stone cut without hands — not carved by human effort, not manufactured by human religion — a Stone that crushes every earthly kingdom and fills the whole earth. That Stone is Christ, and His kingdom will never end.

**In Jonah**, Jesus is the One who spent three days and three nights in the heart of the earth. Jesus Himself said in Matthew 12:40, *"For as Jonah was three days and three nights in the belly of the great fish, so will the Son of Man be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth."* Jonah went into the belly of the fish and came out alive. Jesus went into the belly of the grave and came out alive. Death could not hold Him.

**In Micah**, Jesus is the One born in Bethlehem. Seven hundred years before the event, the prophet Micah wrote, *"But you, Bethlehem Ephrathah, though you are little among the thousands of Judah, yet out of you shall come forth to Me the One to be Ruler in Israel, whose goings forth are from of old, from everlasting."* Not just a ruler — an *eternal* ruler. One whose origins are from everlasting. God in human flesh, born in a manger in a tiny town, exactly as Micah predicted.

**In Zechariah**, Jesus is pierced — "They will look on Me whom they pierced" (Zechariah 12:10). He is betrayed for thirty pieces of silver — the exact price paid to Judas (Zechariah 11:12-13). He rides into Jerusalem on a donkey — "Behold, your King is coming to you; He is just and having salvation, lowly and riding on a donkey" (Zechariah 9:9). Every detail fulfilled. Every prophecy confirmed. Not one word fell to the ground.

**And in Malachi**, the last book of the Old Testament, Jesus is the Sun of Righteousness who will "arise with healing in His wings" (Malachi 4:2). The Old Testament closes with four hundred years of silence — four hundred years when God did not speak through a prophet. But the promise was there: the Sun is going to rise. And then, in the fullness of time, on a quiet night in Bethlehem, the Sun of Righteousness broke through the darkness, and the long night was over.

My friends, do you see it? One story. One scarlet thread. One Savior, from beginning to end. In Genesis, paradise is lost. In Revelation, paradise is restored. And in between, on every page, God is working out His plan to bring you home through the blood of His Son.

The Bible is not sixty-six separate books. It is one love letter — and every sentence is about Jesus.

## B. Additional Pauline Connections — The Bible as the Instrument of Faith, Hope, and Transformation

Now I want to show you from the letters of the Apostle Paul what this Book *does* — how it works in your life, what it accomplishes, why it matters so much that you read it, study it, and live by it.

---

### The Instrument of Faith

**The Bible says** in Romans 10:17: *"So then faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God."*

My friends, faith is not something you work up in your own heart. You cannot sit in a dark room and strain your brain and manufacture faith. Faith does not come from within you. Faith comes from *outside* you — it comes through the Word of God. When you hear God's Word — when you read it, when it is preached, when someone shares a verse with you — the Holy Spirit uses those words to create faith in your heart.

That is why reading the Bible is so important. That is why going to a Bible-preaching church is so important. That is why this Book must not sit on a shelf gathering dust. Because the Bible is not just information — it is the *instrument* by which God produces saving faith in the human soul. You cannot believe what you have never heard. And you cannot hear what has never been spoken. And the thing that must be spoken is the Word of God.

I have met people who say, "I wish I had more faith." My friend, the answer is simple. Get into the Word. Read it. Listen to it. Let it wash over your soul like rain on dry ground. And as you hear it, the Spirit will produce faith. Not your faith — *God's* faith, given as a gift through His Word.

---

### The Source of Hope

**The Bible says** in Romans 15:4: *"For whatever things were written before were written for our learning, that we through the patience and comfort of the Scriptures might have hope."*

The Bible is not just history. It is medicine. It is comfort. It is *hope*.

I have sat with people in hospital rooms who had nothing left but a Bible and a prayer. I have visited men on death row who told me the only thing keeping them alive — spiritually alive — was the Word of God. I have spoken with widows who lost their husbands, with parents who buried their children, with people who had every earthly reason to despair. And the ones who survived — the ones who did not give in to the darkness — were the ones who held on to the Scriptures.

Paul says the Bible gives us *patience* and *comfort*. Patience — the ability to endure when life is hard, to keep going when you want to quit, to trust God when nothing makes sense. And comfort — the tender assurance that God has not forgotten you, that He is still in control, that He is working all things together for good.

My friend, if you are hurting tonight, I am not going to give you a five-step plan for success. I am going to give you a Book. This Book. The Bible. Because the comfort of the Scriptures will outlast every self-help strategy the world has ever devised. The world says, "Time heals all wounds." But the Bible says God heals all wounds — and His Word is the balm.

---

### The Tutor That Leads You to Christ

**The Bible says** in Galatians 3:24: *"Therefore the law was our tutor to bring us to Christ, that we might be justified by faith."*

Now this is a fascinating picture. The word "tutor" in the Greek is *paidagogos*. In the ancient world, a paidagogos was not a teacher in the way we think of a teacher. He was the household servant whose job it was to walk the child to school. He took the child by the hand, led him through the streets, kept him out of trouble, and delivered him safely to the teacher.

Paul says that is what the law does. The law of God — the commandments, the rules, the "thou shalt" and "thou shalt not" — was never meant to save you. The law cannot save anyone. The law was designed to take you by the hand and walk you to the Savior. How? By showing you that you *cannot* keep it. By showing you your sin. By showing you that you need help. By showing you that you need a Savior.

Every commandment you have ever broken is the law doing its job. Every pang of guilt you have ever felt is the tutor pulling your hand and saying, "Come on. I am taking you to Jesus. He is the only one who can help you."

So do not resent the law. Do not resent the parts of the Bible that make you uncomfortable. They are there to lead you to Christ. Even the commandments are an act of love.

---

### The Sword of the Spirit

**The Bible says** in Ephesians 6:17: *"And take the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God."*

In the armor of God that Paul describes in Ephesians 6, there are six pieces. The belt of truth. The breastplate of righteousness. The shoes of the gospel of peace. The shield of faith. The helmet of salvation. And the sword of the Spirit. Five of those six are *defensive* — they protect you. But the sword is *offensive*. It is the one weapon God gives you to go on the attack against the enemy.

And what is that sword? The Word of God.

When Jesus was tempted by Satan in the wilderness, He did not rebuke the devil with His divine power. He did not call down fire from heaven. He did not argue or debate. He did one thing: He quoted Scripture. Three times Satan came at Him, and three times Jesus said, *"It is written..."* And the devil fled.

My friend, if the Son of God Himself used the Bible as His weapon against temptation, how much more do you and I need it? When the enemy whispers lies in your ear — "You are worthless. You are unforgiven. God does not love you. You might as well give up" — you fight back with the Word. "It is written: 'I am a new creation in Christ.' It is written: 'There is therefore now no condemnation.' It is written: 'Nothing can separate me from the love of God.' It is written: 'Greater is He who is in me than he who is in the world.'"

The devil is not afraid of your opinions. He is not afraid of your good intentions. He is not afraid of your New Year's resolutions. But he trembles at the Word of God. Because it is the sword of the Spirit, and it cuts deep.

---

### The Word That Must Dwell in You

**The Bible says** in Colossians 3:16: *"Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly in all wisdom, teaching and admonishing one another in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing with grace in your hearts to the Lord."*

Paul does not say, "Let the word of Christ *visit* you occasionally." He does not say, "Let the word of Christ stop by now and then when it is convenient." He says, "Let the word of Christ *dwell* in you." To dwell means to make your home. To move in. To settle down. To take up permanent residence.

My friend, the Bible should not be a guest in your life. It should be a *resident*. It should be at home in your heart, living in every room — in your thoughts, your decisions, your relationships, your work, your play, your rest. It should be so deeply embedded in your soul that when someone bumps into you, Scripture comes out.

And notice that word "richly." Not sparingly. Not in small doses. *Richly.* Abundantly. Lavishly. You cannot overdose on the Word of God. There is no such thing as too much Bible. Let it dwell in you richly — fill your mind with it, soak your heart in it, let it flow through your conversation and your conduct and your character.

---

### The Living Word That Reads You

**The Bible says** in Hebrews 4:12: *"For the word of God is living and powerful, and sharper than any two-edged sword, piercing even to the division of soul and spirit, and of joints and marrow, and is a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart."*

I said in the main message tonight that when you read Shakespeare, Shakespeare does not read you. But I want to go deeper with that thought.

This Book is *alive*. It is not dead ink on dead paper. It is living and powerful. The word "powerful" in the Greek is *energes* — we get our English word "energy" from it. The Bible is energized. It is charged with the power of the living God.

And it is sharper than a two-edged sword. A surgeon's scalpel can cut through flesh and bone, but it cannot touch the soul. The Word of God cuts deeper than any scalpel — it divides soul and spirit. It gets to the place no human instrument can reach. It exposes the thoughts and intentions of your heart — not just what you do, but *why* you do it. Not just your actions, but your motives.

I have had people come to me after a sermon and say, "Who told you about me? How did you know what I was going through?" My friend, I did not know. But the Word of God knew. Because it is alive, and it reads you. It sees you. It knows you. And it speaks directly to the deepest place in your being.

That is why some of you are uncomfortable right now. That is why some of you feel as if a spotlight has been turned on inside your chest. That is the Word of God doing what it was designed to do — exposing, revealing, convicting, and then healing.

---

### The Complete Equipment for Every Good Work

**The Bible says** in 2 Timothy 3:16-17: *"All Scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness, that the man of God may be complete, thoroughly equipped for every good work."*

Paul lists four things the Bible does:

It teaches you — *doctrine*. It tells you what is true. In a world of lies and confusion, the Bible gives you a foundation of truth that will never shift.

It rebukes you — *reproof*. It tells you where you are wrong. And sometimes we need that. We need someone to tell us, "You are heading in the wrong direction." The Bible loves you enough to tell you the truth, even when it hurts.

It corrects you — *correction*. It does not just point out the problem; it shows you how to fix it. The Bible is not just a mirror that shows you your dirty face — it is the water that washes you clean.

It trains you — *instruction in righteousness*. It teaches you how to live. Day by day, line upon line, it trains you in the ways of God so that you become the man or woman God created you to be.

And the result? You become *complete*. Not half-finished. Not partially equipped. Complete. Thoroughly equipped. Ready for every good work. There is nothing God will ever ask you to do that His Word has not already prepared you to do.

## C. Practical Application — "How to Read the Bible So It Changes Your Life"

Now, my friends, I have told you *about* the Bible tonight. But I want to close this section by telling you how to *read* it. Because owning a Bible is not the same as reading it. And reading it is not the same as letting it change your life.

Let me give you six simple principles that have guided my own Bible reading for more than fifty years.

---

**First, read it daily.** Make it a non-negotiable appointment. You eat every day, do you not? You would not think of going three or four days without food. Your body would grow weak and sick. Well, the Bible is food for your soul. Jesus said in Matthew 4:4, *"Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God."* If you feed your body but starve your soul, you are dying and you do not even know it. Read the Bible every single day, even if it is only a few verses. Let it be the first thing you do in the morning and the last thing you do at night.

**Second, read it prayerfully.** Before you open the Book, ask the Holy Spirit to open your eyes. Pray the prayer of Psalm 119:18: *"Open my eyes, that I may see wondrous things from Your law."* The Bible without the Spirit is like a light bulb without electricity — it has the capacity for light, but it is dark. But when the Spirit illuminates the Word, everything changes. You see things you never saw before. You understand things that once confused you. You hear the voice of God speaking directly to your heart.

**Third, read it obediently.** The Bible is not a book of suggestions. It is not a menu from which you pick and choose the things you like and ignore the rest. It is the Word of the living God, and it demands obedience. James 1:22 says, *"But be doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving yourselves."* When you read something in the Bible and you know God is telling you to do it — do it. Do not argue. Do not postpone. Do not make excuses. Obey.

**Fourth, read it personally.** God is speaking to *you*. Not just to ancient people in ancient times. When you read "For God so loved the world," put your name in there: "For God so loved *me*." When you read "Come unto Me, all you who are weary," hear Jesus saying that to *you* — right now, today, in whatever situation you are facing. The Bible is not a dusty old document. It is a living letter addressed to you by name.

**Fifth, memorize it.** The Bible says in Psalm 119:11, *"Your word I have hidden in my heart, that I might not sin against You."* When you memorize Scripture, you carry it with you everywhere — into the workplace, into the hospital room, into the moment of temptation, into the dark valley of grief. You may not always have a Bible in your hand, but you can always have the Bible in your heart.

I once heard a story about a missionary in a communist country who was thrown into prison for his faith. They took everything from him — his Bible, his books, his possessions, his freedom. But they could not take the hundreds of verses he had memorized over the years. He told me later, "I had a Bible in my heart that no guard could confiscate, no government could ban, and no prison could contain." My friend, memorize the Word. Put it in a place where no one can take it from you.

**Sixth, share it.** The Bible is not just for your own growth. It is for the world. When you find a verse that speaks to your heart, tell someone else about it. When a friend is hurting, open the Bible and read them a promise. When a neighbor is searching, hand them a Gospel of John and say, "Read this. It changed my life."

I once met a woman in our Charlotte crusade — a quiet, unassuming woman, nothing flashy about her — who told me she had led more than a hundred people to faith in Jesus Christ over the course of her life. I said, "How did you do it?" She said, "I just read them the Bible. I did not argue with them. I did not debate theology. I just opened the Book and let God's Word do the work." My friend, the Word of God is its own best argument. Just share it. Just open the Book and let the light shine.

## D. Broader Revelation Connections — The Final Word on the Word

Now let me take you to the last book of the Bible, the book of Revelation, and show you some things about the Word of God that I believe will mark your soul.

---

### A Special Blessing for Those Who Read

**The Bible says** in Revelation 1:3: *"Blessed is he who reads and those who hear the words of this prophecy, and keep those things which are written in it; for the time is near."*

My friends, did you know there is a special blessing — a promised blessing from God Himself — attached to reading Scripture? Revelation is the only book in the Bible that pronounces a specific blessing on its readers. "Blessed is he who reads." Blessed. *Happy.* Favored by God.

And notice the three parts: read, hear, and keep. It is not enough to read the words with your eyes — you must *hear* them with your heart. And it is not enough to hear them — you must *keep* them, which means to obey them, to guard them, to hold them fast.

In a world that is falling apart, in a time of uncertainty and fear, God says, "I will bless the one who reads My Word." My friend, if you want the blessing of God on your life, get into this Book.

---

### Sweet and Bitter — The Full Impact of Scripture

**The Bible says** in Revelation 10:9-10: *"So I went to the angel and said to him, 'Give me the little book.' And he said to me, 'Take and eat it; and it will make your stomach bitter, but it will be as sweet as honey in your mouth.' Then I took the little book out of the angel's hand and ate it, and it was as sweet as honey in my mouth. But when I had eaten it, my stomach became bitter."*

What a picture! John is told to *eat* the book. Not just read it — eat it. Take it into yourself. Let it become part of you.

And when he does, it is sweet in his mouth — oh, how sweet is the Word of God! The promises, the love, the grace, the hope — sweeter than honey. Psalm 119:103 says, *"How sweet are Your words to my taste, sweeter than honey to my mouth!"* There is nothing in this world as sweet as a promise from God when you need it most.

But then John says it became bitter in his stomach. Why? Because the Word of God does not only comfort — it also confronts. It does not only soothe — it also stings. It tells you things you need to hear that you may not want to hear. It calls sin what it is. It demands repentance. It requires change.

My friend, if you only want the sweet parts of the Bible, you do not really want the Bible at all. You want a religious candy bar. But God's Word is a full meal — it has sweetness AND substance. It comforts AND convicts. And you need both. A doctor who only tells you what you want to hear is not a good doctor. A book that only tells you pleasant things is not the Word of God.

Take the whole Word. Eat it. Let it be sweet in your mouth and, yes, sometimes bitter in your gut. Because it is all working together to make you more like Jesus.

---

### Jesus IS the Word

**The Bible says** in Revelation 19:13: *"He was clothed with a robe dipped in blood, and His name is called The Word of God."*

Now this is one of the most staggering verses in the entire Bible. When Jesus returns in glory — riding on a white horse, with the armies of heaven behind Him, His eyes like a flame of fire, and on His robe and on His thigh a name written, "King of Kings and Lord of Lords" — His name is called *The Word of God*.

Do you see what that means? Jesus is not just someone the Bible *talks about*. Jesus IS the Word. The Bible is Jesus in written form. When you hold this Book in your hands, you are holding a portrait of Christ. When you read its pages, you are hearing His voice. When you obey its commands, you are following His leadership.

**The Bible says** in John 1:1: *"In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God."* And verse 14: *"And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we beheld His glory."*

The Word was *with* God. The Word *was* God. And the Word became *flesh*. That is Jesus Christ. He is the eternal Word who became a human being, who lived among us, who died for us, who rose again, and who is coming back — and when He comes back, His name will be The Word of God.

So when you neglect the Bible, you are neglecting Christ. When you ignore the Scriptures, you are ignoring the One who loved you enough to die for you. And when you open this Book and read it with a believing heart, you are meeting with Jesus Himself.

---

### The Solemn Warning — Do Not Tamper With This Book

**The Bible says** in Revelation 22:18-19: *"For I testify to everyone who hears the words of the prophecy of this book: If anyone adds to these things, God will add to him the plagues that are written in this book; and if anyone takes away from the words of the book of this prophecy, God shall take away his part from the Book of Life, from the holy city, and from the things which are written in this book."*

My friends, these are among the most solemn words in all of Scripture. God takes His Word *seriously*. And He says, in no uncertain terms: Do not add to it. Do not take away from it. Do not tamper with it. Do not twist it to fit your preferences. Do not cut out the parts you do not like. Do not paste in ideas of your own invention.

We live in a day when people treat the Bible like a salad bar — they take what they like and leave the rest. They want the verses about love and grace but not the verses about sin and judgment. They want the promises but not the demands. They want heaven but they do not want to hear about hell.

God says: You do not have that right. This is MY Word. I spoke it. I preserved it. I intended every word, every verse, every chapter. And I will hold accountable anyone who dares to add to it or take from it.

My friend, handle this Book with reverence. Handle it with care. Handle it with the awe and trembling of a person who knows they are holding the very words of the living God. Because that is exactly what you are holding.

# SERMON 8: "What Will You Do With Jesus?" (Grand Finale) — EXPANDED ADDITIONS

---

## A. Old Testament Moments of Decision — Everyone Must Choose

My friends, I have shown you throughout this series that the Bible is one story. And tonight, in this final message, I want to show you that the Bible is also one long series of *decisions*. From the garden of Eden to the present moment, God has been putting a choice before the human race: *Will you follow Me, or will you go your own way?*

---

### Joshua's Challenge: "Choose This Day"

**The Bible says** in Joshua 24:15: *"And if it seems evil to you to serve the LORD, choose for yourselves this day whom you will serve, whether the gods which your fathers served that were on the other side of the River, or the gods of the Amorites, in whose land you dwell. But as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD."*

Joshua was an old man. He had fought the battles. He had led the people into the promised land. He had seen the walls of Jericho fall and the sun stand still at Gibeon. And now, with his life nearly over, he gathered the entire nation and said the most important thing a leader can ever say: "Choose. Right now. Today. Not tomorrow. Not next year. TODAY."

And then he told them his decision: "As for me and my house, we will serve the LORD." That is a man who has made up his mind. That is a man who is not wavering, not hesitating, not waiting to see which way the wind is blowing. He has planted his flag. He has drawn his line.

My friend, I am asking you to do the same thing tonight. Choose. Stop deliberating. Stop postponing. Stop saying, "Maybe someday." Joshua said, "This day." And I say to you — *this* night. What will you do with Jesus?

---

### Elijah on Mount Carmel: "How Long Will You Waver?"

**The Bible says** in 1 Kings 18:21: *"And Elijah came to all the people, and said, 'How long will you falter between two opinions? If the LORD is God, follow Him; but if Baal, follow him.' But the people answered him not a word."*

Here was a nation that wanted it both ways. They wanted to worship God on the Sabbath and worship Baal on Monday. They wanted the blessings of heaven without giving up the pleasures of the world. They were fence-sitters. They were straddling the line. They had one foot in the temple and one foot in the pagan grove.

And Elijah — that fiery, courageous, God-intoxicated prophet — stood before them and said, "How long? How long will you limp between two opinions?" The Hebrew word there means to *hobble*, to *stagger*, like a man with a crippled leg trying to walk in two directions at once. It is an ugly picture. It is a picture of spiritual indecision.

And do you know what the people said? Nothing. Not a word. Silence. They had no answer because they did not want to choose.

My friend, silence is an answer. Indecision is a decision. And the Bible makes clear that God does not respect fence-sitters. Jesus said in Revelation 3:15-16 that lukewarmness makes Him sick to His stomach. If you are going to follow God, follow Him. If you are going to follow the world, at least be honest about it. But stop pretending you can have both.

The fire fell on Mount Carmel that day, and the people fell on their faces and cried, "The LORD, He is God! The LORD, He is God!" The fire of God settled the question. And I believe the fire of the Holy Spirit wants to settle the question in your heart tonight.

---

### Ruth's Declaration: Total Commitment

**The Bible says** in Ruth 1:16: *"But Ruth said: 'Entreat me not to leave you, or to turn back from following after you; for wherever you go, I will go; and wherever you lodge, I will lodge; your people shall be my people, and your God, my God.'"*

Here was a young Moabite woman — a foreigner, a pagan, a woman with no standing in Israel — and she made a choice that would change her destiny forever. Naomi, her mother-in-law, told her to go back to her own people, to her own gods, to her own life. And Ruth said, "No. I am going with you. Your people will be my people. And your God will be my God."

No reservations. No conditions. No escape clause. No "Let me try it for a while and see how it goes." Total commitment. All in. Wherever you go, I go. Period.

And because of that one decision — that single, wholehearted, unreserved act of commitment — Ruth ended up in the lineage of Jesus Christ. She is in the genealogy of the Son of God in Matthew chapter 1. A pagan woman from Moab became the great-grandmother of King David and an ancestor of the Messiah — all because she chose to follow God with her whole heart.

My friend, God is not looking for half-hearted followers tonight. He is looking for people like Ruth who will say, "I am ALL in. No looking back. Your people, my people. Your God, my God. Wherever You go, I go."

---

### Moses' Choice: Suffering Over Sin

**The Bible says** in Hebrews 11:24-26: *"By faith Moses, when he became of age, refused to be called the son of Pharaoh's daughter, choosing rather to suffer affliction with the people of God than to enjoy the passing pleasures of sin, esteeming the reproach of Christ greater riches than the treasures in Egypt; for he looked to the reward."*

Moses had everything the world could offer. He was raised in the palace of Pharaoh. He had wealth, power, education, prestige. He was a prince of Egypt — the most powerful empire on earth. He could have had it all.

And he walked away from it. He *chose* — that is the word the Bible uses — he chose to suffer with God's people rather than enjoy the passing pleasures of sin. He chose affliction over affluence. He chose reproach over riches. He chose Christ over comfort.

How could he do that? Because he was looking at something the world could not see. He was looking at the *reward*. He was looking beyond the temporary to the eternal. He was measuring things not by the standards of Pharaoh's court but by the standards of heaven.

My friend, I am not telling you that following Jesus is easy. I am not telling you it will make you rich and popular. Moses was not rich or popular after his decision. He spent forty years in the desert tending sheep. But I am telling you that it is *worth it*. Every sacrifice you make for Christ will be repaid a thousand times over in eternity. Moses looked to the reward. And so should you.

---

### The Rich Young Ruler: The Saddest Decision

Now let me show you a man who made the *wrong* decision. Turn to Matthew 19:16-22.

A young man came running to Jesus. He was young. He was rich. He was moral. He was earnest. He fell at the feet of Jesus and said, "Good Teacher, what good thing shall I do that I may have eternal life?" He asked the right question. He came to the right Person. He heard the right answer.

Jesus said, "If you want to be perfect, go, sell what you have and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow Me."

Now I want you to notice — Jesus was not saying that salvation is earned by giving away money. Jesus was putting His finger on the one thing that stood between this young man and God. For this man, it was his wealth. His stuff. His possessions. Jesus was saying, "I want first place. I want the throne of your heart. And right now, your money is sitting on that throne. Will you move it and let Me sit there instead?"

And the Bible records what I believe is one of the saddest verses ever written: *"But when the young man heard that saying, he went away sorrowful, for he had great possessions."*

He went away. He walked away from Jesus. He walked away from eternal life. He walked away from the Son of God. And the Bible says he was *sorrowful*. He was not happy about his decision. He did not skip away whistling a tune. He was miserable. Because deep in his heart, he knew he was making the wrong choice. But his stuff meant more to him than his Savior.

My friend, what is sitting on the throne of your heart tonight? What is the one thing you are not willing to give up for Jesus? Is it money? Is it a relationship? Is it your pride? Is it a habit? Is it your reputation? Whatever it is — is it worth more to you than eternal life?

The rich young ruler chose his stuff over his Savior. And as far as we know, he never came back.

Do not be that person tonight.

### Barabbas: The Crowd's Terrible Choice

And then there is the most shocking decision of all. In Matthew 27:15-26, Pilate offered the crowd a choice. It was the custom to release one prisoner during the feast of Passover. And Pilate gave them two options: Jesus, the Son of God — or Barabbas, a robber and a murderer.

And the crowd screamed, "Give us Barabbas!"

They chose a murderer over the Messiah. They chose a criminal over Christ. They chose darkness over light, death over life, a man of violence over the Prince of Peace.

And my friend, I want you to understand something. Every person who rejects Jesus Christ is choosing Barabbas. You may not think of it that way. You may think you are simply choosing to live your own life, make your own decisions, follow your own path. But when you reject the Savior, you are choosing the alternative. And the alternative is sin, darkness, and death. You are choosing Barabbas.

The crowd chose Barabbas that Friday morning. And by Sunday, Jesus was risen and Barabbas was forgotten. The world's choice was wrong. Do not make the same mistake.

## B. Additional Pauline Connections — The Simplicity and Power of the Gospel Response

---

### The Simplest Door in the Universe

**The Bible says** in Romans 10:9-10: *"If you confess with your mouth the Lord Jesus and believe in your heart that God has raised Him from the dead, you will be saved. For with the heart one believes unto righteousness, and with the mouth confession is made unto salvation."*

My friends, the gospel is not complicated. Theologians have written millions of pages about it, and I thank God for good theology. But the essence of it — the core of it — the part that saves your soul — can be said by a child.

Confess with your mouth: "Jesus is Lord." Believe in your heart: God raised Him from the dead. And you *will be saved*. Not you might be. Not you could be. Not you have a decent chance. You WILL be saved. That is a promise from Almighty God, and He cannot lie.

Confession and belief. Mouth and heart. Public declaration and private trust. That is the door to eternal life. And it is the widest, most accessible door in the universe. You do not need a seminary degree to walk through it. You do not need to be a scholar, a saint, or a superhero. You just need to believe and confess.

---

### The Widest Word in the Universe

**The Bible says** in Romans 10:13: *"For whoever calls on the name of the Lord shall be saved."*

WHOEVER. My friend, I want you to sit with that word for a moment. Whoever. It is the widest word in the universe. It stretches from east to west and north to south. It takes in every race, every language, every nation, every class, every gender, every age, every background.

Whoever means the drug addict can come. Whoever means the prostitute can come. Whoever means the prisoner can come. Whoever means the businessman can come. Whoever means the churchgoer who has been faking it for years can come. Whoever means the atheist who is starting to wonder can come. Whoever means YOU can come.

I have been to the slums of Calcutta and preached to people who owned nothing in the world except the rags on their backs — and I told them, "Whoever." I have been in the finest cathedrals in Europe and preached to people in suits and fine clothes — and I told them, "Whoever." The ground is level at the foot of the cross. Nobody is too low to reach up. Nobody is too high to bow down. Whoever. That means you.

---

### The Only Gospel — No Counterfeits Accepted

**The Bible says** in Galatians 1:6-9: *"I marvel that you are turning away so soon from Him who called you in the grace of Christ, to a different gospel, which is not another; but there are some who trouble you and want to pervert the gospel of Christ. But even if we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel to you than what we have preached to you, let him be accursed."*

Paul says something here that takes my breath away. He says that even if an *angel from heaven* — a glowing, magnificent, supernatural being — were to come down and preach a different gospel, you should reject that angel. Even if Paul himself were to change his message, you should reject Paul. Because there is only ONE gospel, and it is the gospel of Jesus Christ, crucified, buried, and risen again.

My friend, the world is full of counterfeits tonight. There are people who will tell you that all roads lead to heaven. There are people who will tell you that you just need to be a good person. There are people who will tell you that there are many ways to God. But the Bible says there is one name, one way, one truth, one life — and His name is Jesus.

Do not be deceived. Do not be led astray by smooth words and eloquent speeches and popular opinions. There is one gospel. And this is it: Christ died for your sins according to the Scriptures, He was buried, and He rose again on the third day according to the Scriptures. Accept no substitutes.

---

### Grace Alone — You Cannot Earn It

**The Bible says** in Ephesians 2:8-9: *"For by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God, not of works, lest anyone should boast."*

My friend, I need to make sure you understand this, because it is the heart of the gospel: You cannot earn your way to heaven. You cannot be good enough. You cannot work hard enough. You cannot give enough money, attend enough church services, pray enough prayers, or live a clean enough life to *earn* your salvation.

Salvation is a GIFT. And a gift, by definition, is something you do not deserve and cannot pay for. If you could pay for it, it would not be a gift — it would be a purchase. If you could earn it, it would not be grace — it would be wages.

God says, "I am not selling salvation. I am GIVING it. Through My Son. By grace. Through faith. Not of works — so that nobody can stand before Me and boast about how they earned their way in." The only boast in heaven will be the boast of the cross. The only credit will go to Christ. The only song will be "Amazing grace, how sweet the sound, that saved a wretch like me."

You are a wretch. I am a wretch. We are all wretches. And grace — undeserved, unearned, unmerited, free grace — is the only thing that can save us. And it is yours for the asking tonight.

---

### Every Knee Will Bow — The Question Is When

**The Bible says** in Philippians 2:9-11: *"Therefore God also has highly exalted Him and given Him the name which is above every name, that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of those in heaven, and of those on earth, and of those under the earth, and that every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father."*

My friends, listen carefully. There is coming a day when EVERY person who has ever lived will bow before Jesus Christ. Every knee. Every tongue. No exceptions. The atheist who said, "There is no God" — his knee will bow. The skeptic who said, "I do not believe" — his tongue will confess. The powerful ruler who shook his fist at heaven — he will fall on his face before the King of Kings.

The question is not WHETHER you will bow. The question is WHEN. And the question is HOW.

Will you bow now — willingly, lovingly, gratefully — and receive Him as your Lord and Savior? Or will you bow then — forced by the sheer overwhelming glory of His presence — when it is too late to be saved?

My friend, it is far better to bow at the cross than at the throne of judgment. It is far better to confess Him as Lord tonight, in this room, with tears of repentance and joy, than to confess Him on that Day, with tears of regret and terror.

Bow now. While grace is still offered. While the door is still open. While His arms are still outstretched. Bow now.

### Brand New — The Promise of Transformation

**The Bible says** in 2 Corinthians 5:17: *"Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation; old things have passed away; behold, all things have become new."*

This is the promise I leave with you tonight: If you come to Jesus — right now, just as you are, with all your baggage, all your failure, all your shame, all your brokenness — EVERYTHING changes. You become brand new. Not repaired. Not renovated. Not improved. *New.*

The old guilt? Gone. The old bondage? Broken. The old emptiness? Filled. The old fear? Replaced with peace. The old death? Swallowed up in life.

You become a new creation. God does not patch up the old you — He makes a new you. And this new you is alive with the life of Christ, filled with the Spirit of God, and destined for eternity in the presence of the King.

That is what is waiting for you on the other side of "yes." All you have to do is say it.

## C. Practical Application — "How to Come to Christ Right Now"

My friends, some of you have been listening to eight sermons. You have heard about the cross. You have heard about the empty tomb. You have heard about grace and sin and the new birth and the King's return and the Word of God. And tonight it all comes down to this: What will you DO about it?

I want to make this as simple and as clear as I possibly can. If you want to give your life to Jesus Christ tonight, there are four simple steps. A child can understand them. And a dying man on his deathbed can take them.

---

**Step 1: ADMIT.** Admit that you are a sinner. Do not pretend. Do not excuse yourself. Do not compare yourself to someone worse. Just be honest with God and say, "Lord, I am a sinner. I have broken Your law. I have lived for myself. I cannot save myself."

**The Bible says** in Romans 3:23: *"For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God."* ALL. That includes you. That includes me. The first step is honesty.

---

**Step 2: BELIEVE.** Believe that Jesus Christ died for your sins and rose again from the dead. This is not just intellectual agreement — this is *heart trust*. You are putting the full weight of your eternal destiny on what Jesus did on that cross and what He proved at that tomb.

**The Bible says** in Romans 10:9: *"If you confess with your mouth the Lord Jesus and believe in your heart that God has raised Him from the dead, you will be saved."* Believe it. Trust it. Stake your life on it.

---

**Step 3: CONFESS.** Confess Jesus as your Lord. Not just your Savior — your LORD. This means you are handing over the keys. You are stepping off the throne of your life and letting Jesus sit there. You are saying, "From this moment forward, You are in charge. Not me. You."

**The Bible says** in Romans 10:10: *"For with the heart one believes unto righteousness, and with the mouth confession is made unto salvation."* Say it out loud. Tell someone. Make it public. "Jesus Christ is my Lord."

---

**Step 4: RECEIVE.** Open the door of your heart and receive Christ as your personal Savior. He has been knocking. He has been waiting. And now you are opening the door and inviting Him in.

**The Bible says** in Revelation 3:20: *"Behold, I stand at the door and knock. If anyone hears My voice and opens the door, I will come in to him and dine with him, and he with Me."*

**The Bible says** in John 1:12: *"But as many as received Him, to them He gave the right to become children of God, to those who believe in His name."* Receive Him. And in that moment, you become a child of God. Not by your works. Not by your effort. By His grace, through your faith, as His gift.

---

### A Prayer of Salvation

Now I want to lead you in a prayer. I want you to pray this prayer from your heart. It is not the words that save you — it is the sincerity behind them. God is not listening to the eloquence of your vocabulary. He is listening to the cry of your soul. If you mean these words, pray them with me now:

*"Dear God, I come to You tonight just as I am. I admit that I am a sinner. I have lived my life my own way, and it has brought me to a dead end. I cannot save myself. I cannot fix myself. I need a Savior.*

*I believe that Jesus Christ is Your Son. I believe He died on the cross for my sins — MY sins, not just the world's sins, but mine. I believe He was buried and on the third day He rose from the dead. I believe He is alive right now, and He is in this room tonight.*

*I confess Jesus Christ as my Lord. I step down from the throne of my life, and I give it to Him. He is my King. He is my Master. He is my Lord.*

*And right now, I open the door of my heart, and I receive Jesus Christ as my personal Savior. Come in, Lord Jesus. Come in and make me new. Forgive my sins. Wash me clean. Write my name in the Lamb's Book of Life. Give me Your Holy Spirit. And help me to follow You every day for the rest of my life.*

*I am Yours, Lord. All of me. Forever.*

*In the name of Jesus I pray. Amen."*

## D. The Grand Finale — Revelation's Final Picture

My friends, if you prayed that prayer tonight and meant it, I want to show you what is waiting for you. I want to pull back the curtain on eternity and let you see what your "yes" to Jesus has purchased for you — not through your merit, but through His blood.

### The New Heaven and the New Earth

**The Bible says** in Revelation 21:1-4: *"Now I saw a new heaven and a new earth, for the first heaven and the first earth had passed away. Also there was no more sea. Then I, John, saw the holy city, New Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband. And I heard a loud voice from heaven saying, 'Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and He will dwell with them, and they shall be His people. God Himself will be with them and be their God. And God will wipe away every tear from their eyes; there shall be no more death, nor sorrow, nor crying. There shall be no more pain, for the former things have passed away.'"*

Did you hear that? No more tears. No more death. No more sorrow. No more crying. No more pain. The former things — the cancer, the heartbreak, the loneliness, the addiction, the abuse, the grief, the fear — the former things have PASSED AWAY.

And God Himself — not an angel, not a representative, not a hologram on a screen — God HIMSELF will be with you. He will dwell with you. He will be your God, and you will be His people. Face to face. Forever. No more separation. No more distance. No more wondering if He is there. He is THERE. He is with you. And He is wiping away every tear with His own hand.

**The Bible says** in Revelation 21:5: *"Then He who sat on the throne said, 'Behold, I make all things new.'"*

All things new. Not some things. ALL things. Your broken heart — new. Your wounded soul — new. Your scarred past — new. Your crushed dreams — new. He makes ALL things new. And He says in verse 6, *"It is done! I am the Alpha and the Omega, the Beginning and the End. I will give of the fountain of the water of life freely to him who thirsts."*

Freely. There is that word again. Free. No charge. No admission fee. No entrance exam. The water of life — freely — to everyone who thirsts.

---

### The Lamb Is the Light

**The Bible says** in Revelation 21:22-23: *"But I saw no temple in it, for the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are its temple. The city had no need of the sun or of the moon to shine in it, for the glory of God illuminated it. The Lamb is its light."*

No temple! Do you know what that means? In the Old Testament, you had to go to the temple to meet with God. There was a barrier. There was a veil. There was distance. But in the new heaven and the new earth, there is no temple — because God is EVERYWHERE. His presence fills everything. You do not need a special building to meet with God because you are standing in the middle of His glory.

And no sun. No moon. No streetlights. No lamps. Because the Lamb — Jesus Christ — IS the light. The same Jesus who said, "I am the light of the world," will illuminate the entire city of God with His glory. Imagine living in a world where the light never fades, where there is no darkness, no shadow, no night. That is what awaits you.

**The Bible says** in Revelation 21:27: *"But there shall by no means enter it anything that defiles, or causes an abomination or a lie, but only those who are written in the Lamb's Book of Life."*

Only those who are written in the Lamb's Book of Life. My friend, is your name in that Book? The only way to get your name written in that Book is through the blood of the Lamb — through faith in Jesus Christ. And if you prayed that prayer tonight, your name is being written there right now. The angels are recording it. The ink is the blood of Jesus. And no power in heaven or earth or hell can erase it.

### The River of Life and the Tree of Life

**The Bible says** in Revelation 22:1-5: *"And he showed me a pure river of water of life, clear as crystal, proceeding from the throne of God and of the Lamb. In the middle of its street, and on either side of the river, was the tree of life, which bore twelve fruits, each tree yielding its fruit every month. The leaves of the tree were for the healing of the nations. And there shall be no more curse, but the throne of God and of the Lamb shall be in it, and His servants shall serve Him. They shall see His face, and His name shall be on their foreheads. There shall be no night there: They need no lamp nor light of the sun, for the Lord God gives them light. And they shall reign forever and ever."*

Do you see it? The river of life — flowing from the very throne of God. Crystal clear. Never polluted. Never dry. Always flowing with the life of God.

And the tree of life! Remember that tree? It was in the Garden of Eden in Genesis chapter 2. And when Adam and Eve sinned, God drove them out of the garden and placed angels with flaming swords to guard the way to the tree of life. For thousands of years, humanity has been barred from that tree. But now — in the new Jerusalem — the tree of life is BACK. And it is bearing twelve kinds of fruit, one for every month of the year, and its leaves are for the healing of the nations. Everything that sin broke, this tree will heal.

No more curse. The curse that fell on the ground in Genesis 3 — "Cursed is the ground for your sake" — that curse is GONE. Lifted. Forever removed. The creation that has been groaning under the weight of sin for thousands of years will finally be free.

And then the promise that takes my breath away every time I read it: *"They shall see His face."*

My friend, they shall see His FACE. Not a reflection. Not a shadow. Not a glimpse through a glass darkly. His FACE. You will look into the eyes of Jesus Christ. You will see the nail prints in His hands. You will hear His voice — not in a sermon, not through a book, not through a preacher — you will hear it DIRECTLY, face to face. And His name will be on your forehead, which means you belong to Him, forever and ever and ever.

And you shall reign with Him. Not as servants groveling in the dust. As KINGS and QUEENS reigning with Christ forever and ever.

This is what you are saying "yes" to tonight.

### The Final Invitation: "COME!"

And now, my friends, we have come to the last page of the Bible. We have come to the final words of Holy Scripture. We have come to the very end of God's love letter to the human race. And do you know what the last invitation in the Bible is?

**The Bible says** in Revelation 22:17: *"And the Spirit and the bride say, 'Come!' And let him who hears say, 'Come!' And let him who thirsts come. Whoever desires, let him take the water of life freely."*

COME.

That is it. That is the last word of the gospel. That is the final cry of the Spirit of God to the human race. Not "work harder." Not "try again." Not "clean up your act." Not "come back when you are good enough." Just one simple, glorious, wide-open, all-embracing word: COME.

The Spirit says COME. That is God the Holy Spirit — the same Spirit who has been tugging at your heart all through this series, the same Spirit who woke you up in the middle of the night with a longing you could not explain, the same Spirit who brought you to this place tonight. He is saying to you right now: COME.

The bride says COME. That is the Church — every believer who has ever lived, everyone who has tasted the goodness of God, everyone who has been saved by grace. They are cheering you on. They are calling out to you from across the ages: "We found Him! He is real! He is everything He promised! COME!"

And then let him who hears say COME. If you have heard the gospel tonight — if it has penetrated your heart even a little bit — then you are qualified to pass along the invitation. You do not have to understand everything. You do not have to have all the answers. You just have to say one word to the person next to you, to the friend who is struggling, to the family member who is lost: COME.

And whoever desires — WHOEVER desires — let him take the water of life freely.

There is no velvet rope. There is no VIP section. There is no background check, no credit score, no entrance requirement. If you desire Him — if there is even the faintest spark of longing in your heart tonight — you qualify. Come and drink. The water is free. The grace is free. The gift is free.

### The Last Words of the Bible — Grace, Jesus, and Hope

**The Bible says** in Revelation 22:20-21: *"He who testifies to these things says, 'Surely I am coming quickly.' Amen. Even so, come, Lord Jesus! The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen."*

The very last words of the Bible. And they are breathtaking.

Jesus speaks: "Surely I am coming quickly." He is coming. Do not doubt it. Do not dismiss it. Do not forget it. He is COMING. And He is coming QUICKLY. Sooner than you think. Sooner than the world expects. The King is on His way.

And then the response of every believing heart: "Even so, come, Lord Jesus!" Yes! Come! We are ready! We are waiting! Come, Lord Jesus!

And the last sentence: "The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen."

The Bible ends with GRACE. Not with a threat. Not with a command. Not with a warning. With grace. The undeserved, unearned, unmerited favor of God poured out through Jesus Christ. That is the last word. That is the final note. That is the song that echoes into eternity: GRACE.

### The Closing Illustration — Coming Home

My friends, let me close with this.

I heard a story many years ago about a boy who ran away from home. He was a stubborn, headstrong boy — sixteen years old — and he got into an argument with his father one night, and he packed a bag and walked out the door and said, "I am never coming back."

His father stood on the porch and watched him walk away into the darkness. And the father's heart broke. But he did not chase the boy, because he knew the boy would have to make his own choice.

Days passed. Weeks. Months. The boy drifted from city to city, working odd jobs, sleeping in shelters, making bad decisions, falling deeper and deeper into a life of misery. And every night, no matter how far he was from home, he thought of his father standing on that porch.

After two years, the boy could not take it anymore. He was broken. He was sick. He was ashamed. He wanted to go home, but he was afraid. What would his father say? Would he be angry? Would he turn him away? Would he say, "You made your choice — now live with it"?

The boy wrote a letter. Just a few lines. "Dear Dad, I have been a fool. I am sorry for what I did. I want to come home, but I do not know if you will have me. I will be on the train that passes by the house on Thursday afternoon. If you want me to come home, tie a white ribbon on the old oak tree in the front yard. If I see the ribbon, I will get off at the station. If there is no ribbon, I will stay on the train and you will never hear from me again."

Thursday came. The boy sat on that train with his heart pounding so hard he thought it would burst through his chest. Mile after mile. Getting closer. Getting closer. And then the train rounded the last curve before the house came into view, and the boy could not look. He turned to the man sitting next to him and said, "Mister, I cannot look. Will you look for me? Will you tell me if there is a white ribbon on the oak tree?"

The train came around the curve. And the stranger looked out the window. And his eyes went wide. And he turned to the boy and said, "Son... it is not just one ribbon. Every branch of that tree is covered. There must be a hundred ribbons. And your father is standing under the tree... waiting for you."

My friend, that is your Father tonight. God is not angry at you. God is not holding a grudge. God is not keeping score. God is standing under the tree with a hundred ribbons, waiting for you to come home. He has been waiting your whole life.

The cross of Calvary is God's ribbon on the tree. It is God shouting across the universe: "COME HOME! I LOVE YOU! I WANT YOU! COME HOME!"

What will you do with Jesus?

Everything in the Bible has been leading to this moment. Every sermon in this series has been building to this invitation. Every verse, every story, every promise, every warning — it all points here. To this night. To this choice. To this one word that changes everything:

**COME.**

Come to the cross. Come to the empty tomb. Come into the grace that never runs out. Come away from the sin that has been destroying you. Come out of the darkness and into the light. Come as you are — broken, tired, ashamed, afraid — come just as you are, and let the Savior make you new.

The Spirit says COME.
The bride says COME.
And Jesus says COME.

This is your moment. Do not let it pass. Do not walk out of this place the way you walked in. Open the door of your heart. Say yes to the One who has been knocking your whole life.

**The Bible says** in 2 Corinthians 6:2: *"Behold, NOW is the accepted time; behold, NOW is the day of salvation."*

Now. Not tomorrow. Not next week. Not someday. NOW.

Come. Come home. Come home to the Father who has been waiting for you with open arms and a hundred white ribbons on the tree.

Come, and let the heart of the gospel become the heartbeat of your life — forever.

*"Even so, come, Lord Jesus! The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen."*
