# SERMON 1: "The Cross — Where God Met Man" — EXPANDED ADDITIONS

---

## A. Old Testament Typology of the Cross

### I. The Protoevangelium — The First Gospel Promise (Genesis 3:15)

My friends, I want to take you back tonight — not just two thousand years to Calvary, but all the way back to the very beginning. Back to the Garden of Eden. Back to the darkest hour the human race had ever known.

Adam and Eve had sinned. They had disobeyed God. They had eaten the forbidden fruit. And in that moment, everything changed. The fellowship was broken. Shame entered the world. Fear entered the world. Death entered the world. And Adam and Eve stood there in the garden, hiding behind fig leaves, trembling before the God who had made them.

And what did God do? Did He destroy them? He would have been just to do so. Did He turn His back and walk away? He would have been right to do so. No! The Bible says God came walking in the garden in the cool of the day, calling out, "Adam, where are you?" God came looking for the sinner. God came seeking the lost. And right there, in the wreckage of paradise, before the sweat had dried on Adam's brow, God made a promise.

The Bible says in Genesis 3:15 — and I want you to listen carefully because this is the first gospel sermon ever preached, and it was preached by God Himself — He said to the serpent: *"I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your seed and her Seed; He shall bruise your head, and you shall bruise His heel."*

Now, theologians call this the *protoevangelium* — the first gospel. The first announcement of the good news. And it was spoken before there was a Bible. Before there was a church. Before there was a single hymn or a single sermon. In the ashes of humanity's first failure, God planted the seed of humanity's ultimate hope.

"He shall bruise your head, and you shall bruise His heel." Do you see what God is saying? He is saying: One is coming. A descendant of the woman. And there will be a battle. The serpent — Satan — will strike His heel. That is the cross. That is Calvary. That is the suffering and the agony and the nails and the thorns. Satan bruised the heel of Jesus Christ on that old rugged cross.

But — and here is the glory of it — He shall bruise YOUR HEAD. A heel wound hurts, my friends, but it is not fatal. A crushed head is fatal. At the cross, it looked like Satan won. It looked like darkness had triumphed. But in reality, Jesus was crushing the serpent's skull. The cross was not Christ's defeat — it was Satan's death blow!

And here is what staggers me: God planned this before the foundation of the world. Before the first star was flung into space. Before the first atom was formed. Before the mountains rose and the oceans filled, God already had a plan to save you. The cross was not an emergency. It was not a reaction. It was not Plan B. It was the eternal purpose of Almighty God, set in motion before time itself began.

My friend, you are not an afterthought to God. Your salvation was in His heart before the universe existed.

### II. Abraham and Isaac on Mount Moriah (Genesis 22)

Now turn with me to one of the most powerful pictures of the cross in all of the Old Testament. Genesis chapter 22.

The Bible says that God tested Abraham. He said, "Abraham, take your son, your only son Isaac, whom you love, and go to the land of Moriah, and offer him there as a burnt offering on one of the mountains of which I shall tell you."

Now, I want you to feel the weight of those words. "Your son." "Your only son." "Whom you love." Does that language sound familiar? *"For God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son."* Two thousand years before Calvary, God was painting a picture.

And Abraham obeyed. He got up early the next morning. He saddled his donkey. He cut the wood for the burnt offering. And he took his son, and they began the journey to Moriah.

And then we come to one of the most heartbreaking details in all of Scripture. The Bible says in Genesis 22:6: *"So Abraham took the wood of the burnt offering and laid it on Isaac his son."*

Isaac carried the wood up the mountain. The wood on which he was to be sacrificed — he carried it on his own back up the hill. My friends, does that not take your breath away? A thousand years later, another Son would carry wood up a hill. Jesus Christ carried His own cross up the slopes of Calvary. The wood on which He would die — He bore it on His own shoulders.

And as they walked together, Isaac turned to his father and asked the question that echoes through the ages: "My father, look — the fire and the wood, but where is the lamb for a burnt offering?"

And Abraham spoke words that he may not have fully understood, but that the Holy Spirit breathed through him as a prophecy: *"My son, God will provide Himself a lamb."*

God will provide HIMSELF a lamb. Not just God will provide a lamb. God will provide HIMSELF — as the lamb. And that is exactly what He did. On that same mountain ridge — for Mount Moriah is the very ridge where Calvary stands! — God provided Himself as the Lamb. Jesus Christ, the Son of God, the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world.

And at the last moment, as Abraham raised the knife, God stopped his hand. And the Bible says Abraham looked up and saw a ram caught in a thicket by its horns. A substitute. An animal died in Isaac's place.

That is the cross, my friends. That is the gospel in miniature. You and I were on the altar. We were under the knife of God's righteous judgment. But God provided a substitute. Jesus Christ took our place. He died so that we might live.

### III. The Passover Lamb (Exodus 12)

Now I want to take you to Egypt. The children of Israel had been slaves for four hundred years. Four hundred years of bondage. Four hundred years of whips and chains and brick pits and broken backs. And God said, "Enough. I am going to deliver My people."

And God sent ten plagues upon Egypt. But the final plague — the tenth plague — is the one I want you to see tonight. The Bible says in Exodus 12 that the angel of death was going to pass through the land of Egypt, and the firstborn in every household would die.

But God made a way of escape. He told Moses: Tell every household to take a lamb — a lamb without blemish, without spot, without defect — and kill it, and take the blood and put it on the two doorposts and on the lintel of the house.

And then God said these words — and I want you to hear them, because they are the gospel in the Old Testament: *"When I see the blood, I will pass over you."*

When I see the BLOOD. Not when I see your good works. Not when I see your education. Not when I see how many times you went to the synagogue. Not when I see how hard you tried. When I see the BLOOD.

The angel of death came that night. And everywhere there was blood on the doorpost, death passed over. Everywhere there was no blood, death came in. It did not matter how wealthy the family was. It did not matter how moral they were. It did not matter how sincere they were. The only thing that mattered was the blood.

And the Bible tells us something remarkable about that Passover lamb. No bone of it was to be broken. The Bible says in Exodus 12:46: *"Nor shall you break one of its bones."*

Now turn to John 19:36, a thousand years later. When Jesus hung on the cross, the soldiers came to break His legs to hasten death. But when they came to Jesus, He was already dead. And the Bible says: *"For these things were done that the Scripture should be fulfilled, 'Not one of His bones shall be broken.'"*

Not one bone! A thousand years before Calvary, God was painting the picture. Detail by detail. Brushstroke by brushstroke. The Passover lamb was always pointing to Jesus.

My friends, the blood of Jesus Christ is on the doorpost of heaven tonight. And God says to you what He said to Israel: "When I see the blood, I will pass over you." The judgment that you deserve — the death that you have earned by your sin — it will pass over you. Not because you are good enough. Because the blood is enough.

### IV. The Suffering Servant (Isaiah 53)

Now I want to take you to what many scholars consider the most remarkable chapter in all of the Old Testament. Isaiah chapter 53. Written seven hundred years before Jesus Christ was born. Seven hundred years! And yet it reads like an eyewitness account of Calvary.

The Bible says in Isaiah 53:3: *"He is despised and rejected by men, a Man of sorrows and acquainted with grief."*

Was that not Jesus? Despised. Rejected. The religious leaders hated Him. His own people cried out, "Crucify Him!" A Man of sorrows. In the Garden of Gethsemane, His sweat fell like great drops of blood. Acquainted with grief. He wept at the tomb of Lazarus. He grieved over Jerusalem.

Verse 4: *"Surely He has borne our griefs and carried our sorrows."*

OUR griefs. OUR sorrows. Not His own — He had no sin to grieve over. He carried OURS.

Verse 5 — and this is the heart of the gospel, my friends, so I want you to listen as if you have never heard it before: *"But He was wounded for OUR transgressions, He was bruised for OUR iniquities; the chastisement for OUR peace was upon Him, and by His stripes WE are healed."*

Did you hear those pronouns? OUR. OUR. OUR. WE. This is personal. This is not abstract theology. This is God saying: He took what was YOURS. Your transgressions. Your iniquities. Your punishment. He took it all, and He put it on His Son.

Verse 6: *"All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned, every one, to his own way; and the LORD has laid on Him the iniquity of us all."*

All of it. Every lie you ever told. Every lustful thought. Every act of cruelty. Every moment of selfishness. Every time you shook your fist at heaven. The Lord laid it ALL on Him.

Verse 7: *"He was oppressed and He was afflicted, yet He opened not His mouth; He was led as a lamb to the slaughter, and as a sheep before its shearers is silent, so He opened not His mouth."*

When they spat in His face, He said nothing. When they slapped Him, He said nothing. When they drove the nails, He said nothing — nothing in His own defense. He opened not His mouth. Why? Because He was willing. He went to that cross voluntarily, silently, deliberately — for you.

And verse 10: *"Yet it pleased the LORD to bruise Him."*

Now that is a hard verse, my friends. It pleased the Lord to bruise Him? How could a Father take pleasure in the suffering of His Son? Because the Father was not taking pleasure in the pain — He was taking pleasure in what the pain would accomplish. He was looking beyond the cross to the empty tomb. He was looking beyond the nails to the nail-scarred hands reaching out to save a lost world. He was looking beyond Good Friday to Easter Sunday. It pleased the Lord because the cross would bring YOU home.

Seven hundred years before it happened, God told the world exactly what He was going to do. And He did it — down to the last detail.

### V. David's Prophetic Psalm (Psalm 22)

And now let me take you to the Psalms. Psalm 22, written by King David approximately one thousand years before the birth of Jesus Christ.

And I want you to understand something remarkable about this psalm. It describes crucifixion. It describes the piercing of hands and feet, the mocking of the crowd, the dividing of garments. But here is what will make the hair stand up on the back of your neck: when David wrote this psalm, crucifixion had not been invented yet. The Romans would not invent crucifixion for another eight hundred years. David described something that did not yet exist in the world!

The psalm begins with these words: *"My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?"*

Where have you heard those words before? The Bible says in Matthew 27:46 that at the ninth hour, Jesus cried out with a loud voice: *"Eli, Eli, lama sabachthani?"* — which is to say, "My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?"

The very words. The exact words. Jesus, hanging on the cross, opened His mouth and spoke the opening line of Psalm 22. He was telling the world: This is what David wrote about. This is the fulfillment. This is the moment the psalm was pointing to all along.

Verse 7-8: *"All those who see Me ridicule Me; they shoot out the lip, they shake the head, saying, 'He trusted in the LORD, let Him rescue Him; let Him deliver Him, since He delights in Him!'"*

And the Bible says in Matthew 27:39-43 that the people who passed by the cross wagged their heads and said, "If You are the Son of God, come down from the cross." The chief priests mocked Him, saying, "He trusted in God; let Him deliver Him now."

Word for word. Detail for detail. One thousand years in advance.

Verse 16: *"They pierced My hands and My feet."*

A thousand years before crucifixion existed! David had never seen a crucifixion. He did not know what it was. But the Holy Spirit moved through him and he wrote: "They pierced My hands and My feet."

Verse 18: *"They divide My garments among them, and for My clothing they cast lots."*

And the Bible says in John 19:23-24 that the soldiers took the garments of Jesus and divided them into four parts, and for His tunic they cast lots.

My friends, how do you explain this? How does a man describe an event a thousand years before it happens, with details that match perfectly? There is only one explanation. The God who knows the end from the beginning — the God who holds all of history in His hands — He breathed these words through David as a witness to all generations that the cross was no accident. It was planned. It was prophesied. It was purposed. And it was for YOU.

## B. Additional Pauline Connections

### I. While We Were Still Sinners (Romans 5:6-8)

The Bible says in Romans 5:6-8: *"For when we were still without strength, in due time Christ died for the ungodly. For scarcely for a righteous man will one die; yet perhaps for a good man someone would even dare to die. But God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us."*

My friends, I want you to notice the timing of the cross. WHEN did Christ die for us? While we were STILL sinners. Not after we cleaned ourselves up. Not after we started going to church. Not after we turned over a new leaf. While we were enemies. While we were rebels. While we were shaking our fists at heaven.

Paul says you might find someone willing to die for a good person. A soldier might jump on a grenade to save his buddies. A mother might run into a burning building to save her child. That kind of love, we understand. But Christ died for the ungodly. He died for people who hated Him. He died for people who cursed His name. He died for people who would spit in His face if they had the chance.

That is what makes the love of the cross so staggering. It is not a love that responds to worthiness. It is a love that creates worthiness. God did not look at you and say, "There is someone worth dying for." He looked at you and said, "There is someone I love" — and then He died.

I remember a woman who came to one of our crusades in tears. She said, "Reverend Graham, you don't understand. If you knew what I've done, you would not invite me forward." I said, "Ma'am, if I knew what you've done, I would invite you forward twice. Because Jesus didn't come for perfect people. He came for sinners. And the worse the sinner, the greater the glory of His grace."

### II. Crucified With Christ (Galatians 2:20)

The Bible says in Galatians 2:20: *"I have been crucified with Christ; it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me; and the life which I now live in the flesh I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave Himself for me."*

Now this is a verse that goes beyond theology into personal experience. Paul is not just saying that Jesus died for him. He is saying that he died WITH Jesus. When Christ went to the cross, Paul went with Him. The old Paul — the proud Pharisee, the persecutor of the church, the self-righteous religious man — that Paul was nailed to the cross and killed.

And something new was born. "It is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me." The old life is dead. A new life has begun. And this new life is not lived by willpower or self-effort. It is lived by FAITH — faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave Himself for me.

My friends, this is what happens when you truly come to the cross. You do not just get forgiveness — you get a new identity. You do not just get a clean record — you get a new life. The old you dies, and Christ begins to live His life through you. That is not religion. That is transformation.

### III. Boasting Only in the Cross (Galatians 6:14)

The Bible says in Galatians 6:14: *"But God forbid that I should boast except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, by whom the world has been crucified to me, and I to the world."*

In Paul's day, people boasted about their achievements, their pedigree, their religious credentials. Paul had plenty to boast about. He was a Hebrew of Hebrews. A Pharisee. Educated at the finest schools. Blameless under the law. But Paul said, "I throw it all away. I boast in one thing and one thing only — the cross."

The cross, he says, has crucified the world to him. The things that used to dazzle him — the applause of men, the pursuit of power, the glitter of the world's approval — it is all dead to him now. And he is dead to it. He is a dead man walking through a dead world, and the only thing alive in his heart is the cross of Jesus Christ.

My friends, what do you boast about? What are you proud of? Your career? Your house? Your bank account? Your children's accomplishments? There is nothing wrong with those things. But if any of them takes the place of the cross in your heart, you have made an idol. Paul says: "God forbid that I should boast in anything except the cross." When the cross becomes the biggest thing in your life, everything else falls into its proper place.

### IV. The Cross Broke Down the Wall (Ephesians 2:13-16)

The Bible says in Ephesians 2:13-16: *"But now in Christ Jesus you who once were far off have been brought near by the blood of Christ. For He Himself is our peace, who has made both one, and has broken down the middle wall of separation... so as to create in Himself one new man from the two, thus making peace, and that He might reconcile them both to God in one body through the cross."*

In the temple in Jerusalem, there was a wall. A literal, physical wall. It was called the dividing wall of separation. It separated the Court of the Gentiles from the inner courts where only Jews could go. There was an inscription on that wall that said, in effect: "If you are a Gentile, do not pass this point. If you do, you will have only yourself to blame for the death that will follow."

That wall was a picture of the division that sin creates. It separated Jew from Gentile. It separated man from God. It said: "You are not welcome here. You do not belong."

But Paul says that at the cross, Jesus broke down that wall. He demolished it. He took the separation and the hostility and the division, and He nailed it to the cross. And now, through His blood, everyone — Jew, Gentile, rich, poor, slave, free, man, woman — everyone can come near to God.

The cross is the great unifier, my friends. It does not matter what race you are. It does not matter what background you come from. At the foot of the cross, the ground is level. Everyone stands as a sinner in need of grace. And everyone is offered the same grace.

### V. The Cross — A Triumph Over Satan (Colossians 2:13-15)

The Bible says in Colossians 2:13-15: *"And you, being dead in your trespasses... He has made alive together with Him, having forgiven you all trespasses, having wiped out the handwriting of requirements that was against us, which was contrary to us. And He has taken it out of the way, having nailed it to the cross. Having disarmed principalities and powers, He made a public spectacle of them, triumphing over them in it."*

Now I want you to see this picture. In the Roman world, when a general won a great military victory, he would return to Rome in what was called a "triumph." He would march through the streets in a chariot, and behind him, in chains, he would parade the defeated enemies for all the world to see. It was a public spectacle. A display of total, absolute victory.

Paul says THAT is what happened at the cross. When Jesus Christ hung on that cross and breathed His last, it looked to the world like defeat. It looked like Satan had won. The principalities and powers of darkness must have been celebrating. They thought they had destroyed the Son of God.

But the Bible says Jesus was not being defeated — He was disarming them. He was stripping them of their weapons. He was making a public spectacle of them. He was leading them in a triumphal procession. The cross was not the devil's victory parade — it was GOD'S victory parade!

Every accusation Satan had against you — nailed to the cross. Every debt of sin you owed — nailed to the cross. Every claim the enemy had on your soul — nailed to the cross and cancelled forever.

The next time the devil comes to you and says, "You are guilty. You are condemned. You will never be free" — you point him to the cross and you say, "That debt has been nailed to the cross. It has been paid in full. And you, Satan, have been disarmed, defeated, and put to open shame by my Lord Jesus Christ."

### VI. The Humbling and Exaltation (Philippians 2:5-11)

The Bible says in Philippians 2:5-11: *"Let this mind be in you which was also in Christ Jesus, who, being in the form of God, did not consider it robbery to be equal with God, but made Himself of no reputation, taking the form of a bondservant, and coming in the likeness of men. And being found in appearance as a man, He humbled Himself and became obedient to the point of death, even the death of the cross. Therefore God also has highly exalted Him and given Him the name which is above every name, that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow."*

My friends, I want you to see the staircase that Jesus walked down to get to you.

He was in the form of God. He was equal with the Father. He dwelt in unapproachable light. Angels covered their faces in His presence and sang, "Holy, holy, holy!" He was the Creator of the universe, the Alpha and the Omega, the beginning and the end.

And He made Himself of no reputation. The King of Glory emptied Himself. The theologians call it the *kenosis* — the self-emptying. He did not stop being God. But He laid aside the privileges of deity. He set aside His crown. He stepped down from the throne.

He took the form of a bondservant. Not a king. Not a nobleman. A servant. The God who made the stars picked up a towel and washed the dirty feet of fishermen.

He came in the likeness of men. The infinite became an infant. The Creator became a creature. The One who sustains the universe with the word of His power had to be sustained by a teenage girl's milk.

He humbled Himself. Down, down, down He came. From the throne to the manger. From the manger to the carpenter's shop. From the carpenter's shop to the dusty roads of Palestine. From the roads to the upper room. From the upper room to the garden. From the garden to the trial. From the trial to the scourging post. From the scourging post to the cross.

He became obedient to the point of death — even the death of the cross. Even! That word tells you that the cross was the lowest possible point. It was not just death — it was the most shameful, most agonizing, most cursed form of death the world had ever conceived. And the Son of God willingly went there. For you.

BUT — and here is where the story turns — THEREFORE God has highly exalted Him. Because He went all the way down, God raised Him all the way up. Because He humbled Himself, God gave Him the name above every name. And the Bible says that one day — one glorious, coming day — every knee will bow and every tongue will confess that Jesus Christ is Lord.

Every knee. The atheist's knee. The skeptic's knee. The mocker's knee. The devil's knee. One day, every knee in heaven and on earth and under the earth will bow before the One who died on the cross.

## C. Practical Application — "What Does the Cross Mean for Your Monday Morning?"

My friends, everything I have told you tonight is glorious theology. But if it does not change the way you live on Monday morning, it has not done its work in your heart. So let me bring this home. Let me make it practical. Let me tell you what the cross means for your everyday life.

### The Cross Means You Are Forgiven — Live Like It

Some of you are walking around under a cloud of guilt that is crushing you. You did something years ago that you have never forgiven yourself for. Every time you close your eyes, you see it. Every time the house is quiet, you hear the accusing voice: "You are guilty. You are a fraud. If people knew the real you, they would be disgusted."

My friend, the cross says: YOU ARE FORGIVEN. The Bible says, *"As far as the east is from the west, so far has He removed our transgressions from us"* (Psalm 103:12). God does not hold it against you anymore. He has cast it into the depths of the sea. He has put up a sign that says "No Fishing."

Stop punishing yourself for something that Jesus already paid for. When you refuse to accept God's forgiveness, you are saying that the cross was not enough. But it WAS enough. Live like it.

### The Cross Means You Are Free — Stop Living in Chains

The Bible says, *"If the Son makes you free, you shall be free indeed"* (John 8:36). Some of you are in bondage tonight. Bondage to alcohol. Bondage to drugs. Bondage to pornography. Bondage to anger. Bondage to fear. You have tried to break free a hundred times, and a hundred times you have failed.

The cross says: The chains have been broken. You do not have to live like a prisoner anymore. The door of the cell is wide open. Satan wants you to believe that you are still locked up, but the cross broke every chain. Walk out. Walk out in the power of the risen Christ. You are free.

### The Cross Means You Are Loved — Stop Looking for Love in the Wrong Places

I have met people all over this world — in the richest countries and the poorest countries — who are desperate for love. They look for it in relationships that use them. They look for it in substances that destroy them. They look for it in achievements that never satisfy them. They jump from one thing to the next, always searching, never finding.

The Bible says, *"God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us"* (Romans 5:8). You want to know if you are loved? Look at the cross. That is how much you are worth to God. He did not send an angel. He did not send a message. He sent His Son. And His Son stretched out His arms on that cross as if to say, "I love you THIS much."

You do not need to earn anyone's approval. You do not need to perform for anyone's affection. The God of the universe already loves you with an everlasting love. The cross is the proof.

### The Cross Means You Have Purpose — Your Life Is Not an Accident

Some of you feel like your life does not matter. You feel invisible. You feel like a number, a cog in a machine, a face in the crowd that nobody would miss. The devil whispers, "You are worthless. You are an accident. Nobody cares whether you live or die."

But the cross says something different. The cross says that Almighty God thought you were worth dying for. If your life did not matter, He would not have paid such a price. The Bible says you were *"bought at a price"* (1 Corinthians 6:20). Not with silver and gold, but with the precious blood of Jesus Christ. You have value because God assigned you value at the cross.

### Illustration — A Life Changed by the Cross

I remember hearing about a young woman — I will call her Sarah — who grew up in a broken home. Her father left when she was six. Her mother turned to alcohol. By the time Sarah was sixteen, she was living on the streets. She got into drugs. She got into trouble with the law. By twenty-two, she had been arrested three times and had a child she could barely care for.

One night, cold and hungry, she wandered into a church because the doors were open and the lights were on. She sat in the back row. She did not know the songs. She did not know the prayers. She did not even own a Bible.

But the preacher that night talked about the cross. He said that Jesus Christ died for sinners — not for good people, but for broken, messed-up, hopeless sinners. He said that no one was too far gone. He said that the blood of Jesus could wash away every stain.

And something broke inside of Sarah. She went forward. She knelt at that altar. She wept until there were no more tears left. And she said, "Jesus, if You can do anything with a mess like me, I'm Yours."

That was twelve years ago. Today, Sarah runs a shelter for homeless women in her city. She has reunited with her daughter. She leads a Bible study every Tuesday night. And when you ask her what changed, she does not give a complicated answer. She simply says, "I met Jesus at the cross. And He gave me a life I never knew was possible."

That is what the cross does, my friends. It takes the broken and makes them whole. It takes the guilty and makes them clean. It takes the hopeless and gives them a future.

## D. Broader Revelation Connections

### I. The Lamb Slain from the Foundation of the World (Revelation 13:8)

The Bible says in Revelation 13:8: *"The Lamb slain from the foundation of the world."*

Now I want you to grasp the weight of that phrase. FROM THE FOUNDATION OF THE WORLD. Before God said, "Let there be light." Before the oceans filled their basins. Before the first bird sang its first song. Before time itself began its march — in the councils of eternity, in the fellowship of the Godhead, the cross was already determined.

God did not wake up on a Friday morning in Jerusalem and say, "Oh no, they are crucifying My Son — I had better make something of this." No! The cross was the plan from the beginning. Jesus was the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world. Before you were born, before you sinned, before you needed saving — the cross was already in place, waiting for you.

That means God was never caught off guard by your failure. He was never surprised by your sin. He already had the answer before you had the problem. The cross was ready before you were born.

### II. Robes Washed White in Blood (Revelation 7:14)

The Bible says in Revelation 7:14: *"These are the ones who come out of the great tribulation, and washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb."*

John looks into heaven and sees a great multitude that no one can number — from every nation, tribe, people, and tongue — standing before the throne, dressed in white robes. And when he asks who they are, he is told: They washed their robes in the blood of the Lamb.

Now, that is strange language, isn't it? You wash something in blood and it comes out WHITE? That does not make sense in the natural world. But in the spiritual world, it is the only thing that makes sense. The blood of Jesus Christ does not stain — it cleanses. The blood of Jesus does not make you dirty — it makes you pure.

Every single person you will see in heaven — every saint from every century, every redeemed sinner from every continent — they are all there for one reason and one reason only: the blood of the Lamb. Not their good works. Not their religion. Not their moral effort. The blood. The cross. That is the only ticket to heaven.

### III. They Overcame by the Blood (Revelation 12:11)

The Bible says in Revelation 12:11: *"And they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony, and they did not love their lives to the death."*

This is a picture of spiritual warfare, my friends. The "him" is Satan — the accuser of the brethren. And the Bible says the saints overcame him. How? By the blood of the Lamb.

The blood of Jesus is not just something that saves you — it is something that fights for you. When Satan accuses you, when he brings up your past, when he whispers that you are a failure and a fake — you overcome him by the blood. You say, "Yes, I am a sinner. But the Lamb was slain for me. His blood covers me. His cross speaks for me. And you, devil, have no claim on someone who is under the blood."

The cross is your weapon, my friend. It is your shield. It is your victory. Do not ever let the enemy make you forget what happened at Calvary. The blood of the Lamb is the most powerful force in the universe, and it is on your side.

# SERMON 2: "The Empty Tomb Changed Everything" — EXPANDED ADDITIONS

## A. Old Testament Typology of the Resurrection

### I. Jonah and the Whale — Three Days in the Deep (Jonah 1-2; Matthew 12:40)

My friends, I want to show you something tonight that Jesus Himself pointed to as a picture of His resurrection.

The Bible says in Matthew 12:40 that Jesus told the Pharisees: *"For as Jonah was three days and three nights in the belly of the great fish, so will the Son of Man be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth."*

Jesus said that! He pointed back to the story of Jonah and said, "That is a picture of what is going to happen to Me."

Now think about Jonah. God told him to go to Nineveh. Jonah ran the other way. He got on a ship. God sent a storm. The sailors threw Jonah overboard. And a great fish swallowed him whole.

For three days and three nights, Jonah was in the belly of that fish. He was in the dark. He was surrounded by death. By every human reckoning, Jonah was finished. A man in the belly of a great fish at the bottom of the sea — what hope could there possibly be?

But on the third day, the fish vomited Jonah out onto dry land. The deep could not hold him. The belly of the beast could not digest him. God reached into the darkness and brought His servant out alive.

And that is exactly what happened to Jesus Christ. For three days and three nights, the Son of God was in the heart of the earth. He was in the tomb. He was in the realm of the dead. The stone was sealed. The guards were posted. By every human reckoning, it was over.

But on the third day, the grave could not hold Him. Death could not digest Him. The tomb could not keep Him. God reached into the darkness and brought His Son out alive. And just as Jonah went to Nineveh after his "resurrection" and a whole city repented, Jesus rose from the dead and the whole world was offered salvation.

The belly of the fish could not hold Jonah. The belly of the earth could not hold Jesus. That is the power of God.

### II. Joseph in Genesis — From the Pit to the Throne

Now I want to show you another Old Testament picture that will take your breath away. The story of Joseph, the son of Jacob, in the book of Genesis.

Joseph was the beloved son of his father. His father gave him a coat of many colors — a robe of honor. But his brothers hated him. They were jealous of him. They conspired against him.

And what did they do? They threw him into a pit. A dark, empty pit. They left him for dead. Then they sold him into slavery. He was taken to Egypt — a foreign land, far from his father. He was falsely accused by Potiphar's wife. He was thrown into prison. For years, he languished in the dungeon.

But God was not finished with Joseph. In one day — in one remarkable, supernatural, God-ordained day — Joseph went from the prison to the palace. He was brought before Pharaoh. He was given authority over all of Egypt. He was seated at the right hand of the most powerful ruler on earth. The man who had been left for dead, sold by his own brothers, buried in a dungeon — ROSE to the highest position in the land.

My friends, do you see the picture? Joseph is a type of Christ.

Jesus was the beloved Son of His Father. He was betrayed by His own people. He was sold — Judas sold Him for thirty pieces of silver. He descended into the pit of death. He was buried in a tomb. But God raised Him up! And He ascended — not to the right hand of Pharaoh, but to the right hand of Almighty God!

And just as Joseph, from his exalted position, forgave his brothers and saved them from famine, Jesus, from His exalted position at the Father's right hand, forgives sinners and saves them from eternal death.

Joseph said to his brothers, "You meant it for evil, but God meant it for good, to save many people alive" (Genesis 50:20). And that is exactly what God says about the cross and the resurrection. Satan meant it for evil. But God meant it for good — to save many people alive. And the resurrection is the proof that God's purpose triumphed over Satan's plot.

### III. The Dry Bones of Ezekiel 37

The Bible says in Ezekiel 37:1-3: *"The hand of the LORD came upon me and brought me out in the Spirit of the LORD, and set me down in the midst of the valley; and it was full of bones. Then He caused me to pass by them all around, and behold, there were very many in the open valley; and indeed they were very dry. And He said to me, 'Son of man, can these bones live?'"*

Can these bones live? What a question! Ezekiel looked out over that valley and saw nothing but death. Skeletons. Dry, bleached, hopeless bones. There was no breath in them. There was no life in them. There was no hope for them. By every law of nature, by every measure of human possibility, those bones were finished.

And God asked: "Can these bones live?"

Ezekiel gave the only honest answer a man could give: "O Lord GOD, You know."

And then God told Ezekiel to prophesy to the bones. To speak to the dead. To preach to corpses. And as Ezekiel prophesied, the bones began to rattle. They began to come together. Bone to bone. Sinew and flesh covered them. And then the breath of God came into them, and they stood up — a vast army, alive and breathing and strong.

My friends, that is what God does. He takes the dead and makes them alive. He takes the hopeless and gives them hope. He takes what is finished and says, "I am not finished."

Some of you feel tonight like you are nothing but dry bones. Your marriage is dead. Your dreams are dead. Your faith is dead. Your hope is dead. And the devil is standing over you saying, "It's over. You will never come back from this."

But God looks at your situation and asks the question He asked Ezekiel: "Can these bones live?" And the answer is: YES! The God who raised Jesus Christ from the dead can raise ANYTHING from the dead. He specializes in resurrection. He specializes in bringing life out of death, beauty out of ashes, hope out of despair.

### IV. The Red Sea — Walking Through Death to Life (Exodus 14)

The children of Israel were trapped. The Red Sea was in front of them. The mountains were on either side. And Pharaoh's army was behind them, charging fast. There was no way out. They were hemmed in on every side. It was certain death.

And what did God do? He parted the waters. He opened a path straight through the sea — straight through what should have been their grave. And the children of Israel walked through on dry ground. They walked through death and came out the other side alive.

The Bible uses this image as a picture of resurrection. They went DOWN into the sea — just as Jesus went down into the grave. The waters stood as walls on their right and left — just as the stone sealed the tomb. And they came UP on the other side — just as Jesus came up out of the grave on the third day.

And the enemy who pursued them? Pharaoh's army was swallowed up. The waters closed over them. They were destroyed. Just as death itself was swallowed up in the resurrection of Jesus Christ.

My friends, you may feel trapped tonight. You may feel like there is no way out. The sea is in front of you and the enemy is behind you and there is no escape. But the God who parted the Red Sea is the God who opened the tomb. He makes a way where there is no way. He brings life where there is only death. Trust Him. Walk forward. The same power that brought Israel through the sea and brought Jesus through the grave will bring you through whatever you are facing tonight.

### V. Isaac — Received Back from the Dead (Hebrews 11:17-19)

I mentioned Isaac earlier in connection with the cross, but the writer of Hebrews shows us something else about that story — something that points directly to the resurrection.

The Bible says in Hebrews 11:17-19: *"By faith Abraham, when he was tested, offered up Isaac, and he who had received the promises offered up his only begotten son, of whom it was said, 'In Isaac your seed shall be called,' concluding that God was able to raise him up, even from the dead, from which he also received him in a figurative sense."*

Did you catch that? Abraham believed that even if he killed Isaac, God could raise him from the dead. Abraham had such confidence in the power and faithfulness of God that he believed in resurrection — before any resurrection had ever occurred.

And the Bible says he received Isaac back "in a figurative sense" from the dead. Isaac went up that mountain as good as dead. But he came back down alive. It was the first "resurrection" in the Bible. A son laid on the wood. A death averted. A life given back.

This is the great pattern of Scripture. God takes what is dead and brings it to life. He did it with Isaac in figure. He did it with Jesus in fact. And He offers to do it with you tonight.

## B. Additional Pauline Connections

### I. Declared to Be the Son of God (Romans 1:4)

The Bible says in Romans 1:4 that Jesus was *"declared to be the Son of God with power according to the Spirit of holiness, by the resurrection from the dead."*

My friends, there were many who claimed to be special in the ancient world. There were many who claimed to be prophets, teachers, even divine. But there is one test that separates Jesus from every other person who ever made such claims: the resurrection.

Muhammad is in his grave. Buddha is in his grave. Confucius is in his grave. Every founder of every religion, every philosopher, every great leader in human history — they are all still dead.

But Jesus Christ is alive. And the resurrection is God's declaration — His public, visible, undeniable declaration — that says: THIS IS MY SON. This is not just a good man. This is not just a great teacher. This is My Son, with power, risen from the dead. The case is closed. The verdict is in. The resurrection settles the question of who Jesus is, once and for all.

### II. Raised Because of Our Justification (Romans 4:25)

The Bible says in Romans 4:25: *"Who was delivered up because of our offenses, and was raised because of our justification."*

Now this is one of the most important verses in the Bible, and many people overlook it. It says Jesus was delivered to death because of our sins — we understand that. That is the cross. But then it says He was RAISED because of our justification.

What does that mean? It means the resurrection is the PROOF that our justification is complete. If Jesus had stayed in that grave, it would have meant the payment was not accepted. It would have meant we were still guilty. It would have meant sin still had a claim on us.

But God raised Him up! And by raising Him, God was saying to the entire universe: "The debt is paid. The sentence is served. The price is accepted. My people are JUSTIFIED — declared righteous, acquitted, free."

I heard about a man who was accused of a crime he did not commit. He went to prison. He spent years behind bars. But his lawyer kept fighting. And one day, the court reversed the verdict. Not guilty. The prison doors swung open.

My friends, the resurrection is the opening of the prison doors. It is God's court reversing the verdict against you. Not guilty! Justified! Free! And the proof is the empty tomb.

### III. Walk in Newness of Life (Romans 6:4-5)

The Bible says in Romans 6:4-5: *"Therefore we were buried with Him through baptism into death, that just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life. For if we have been united together in the likeness of His death, certainly we also shall be in the likeness of His resurrection."*

Paul is saying that when you are baptized, you are acting out the resurrection. You go down into the water — that is the burial. You come up out of the water — that is the resurrection. Every baptism is a sermon without words. It says: "The old life is dead and buried. A new life has begun."

But it is not just a picture. It is a reality. The same power that raised Jesus from the dead is at work in you, right now, raising you to walk in newness of life. New desires. New directions. New strength. New purpose. You are not just forgiven — you are made NEW.

### IV. Resurrection Power Living in You (Romans 8:11)

The Bible says in Romans 8:11: *"But if the Spirit of Him who raised Jesus from the dead dwells in you, He who raised Christ from the dead will also give life to your mortal bodies through His Spirit who dwells in you."*

I want you to hear this, my friends, because this is one of the most staggering promises in all of Scripture. The same Spirit — the SAME Spirit — that raised Jesus Christ from the dead LIVES IN YOU if you are a believer. The resurrection power of Almighty God is not just a historical fact — it is a present reality dwelling inside your body right now.

Think about that. The power that rolled the stone away. The power that raised a dead man to life after three days in the grave. The power that defeated death and hell and Satan — that power lives in you.

You say, "I can't overcome this addiction." The resurrection power says, "Yes, you can." You say, "I can't forgive that person." The resurrection power says, "Yes, you can." You say, "I can't go on." The resurrection power says, "Yes, you can." Because the same power that raised Jesus from the dead is available to you — not in theory, but in reality, through the Holy Spirit who lives inside you.

### V. The Exceeding Greatness of His Power (Ephesians 1:19-21)

The Bible says in Ephesians 1:19-21: *"And what is the exceeding greatness of His power toward us who believe, according to the working of His mighty power which He worked in Christ when He raised Him from the dead and seated Him at His right hand in the heavenly places, far above all principality and power and might and dominion."*

Paul is praying that the Ephesians would understand something. He wants them to know the POWER that is available to them. And what is the measure of that power? What is the yardstick? The resurrection.

Paul says: The same power God used when He raised Jesus from the dead — that incomprehensible, death-defeating, grave-shattering power — is directed TOWARD US WHO BELIEVE. Toward you. Toward me. It is not a power we have to go looking for. It is a power directed at us, flowing toward us, available to us every moment of every day.

My friends, you are not living on your own strength. If you are a believer in Jesus Christ, you have access to resurrection power. The same power that conquered the greatest enemy the human race has ever known — death itself — is working in you and for you tonight.

### VI. To Know the Power of His Resurrection (Philippians 3:10)

The Bible says in Philippians 3:10: *"That I may know Him and the power of His resurrection, and the fellowship of His sufferings, being conformed to His death."*

This was the cry of Paul's heart. Not just to know ABOUT Jesus, but to KNOW Him. Not just to believe in the resurrection, but to EXPERIENCE its power.

Paul had seen miracles. Paul had planted churches. Paul had written letters that would change the world. But his deepest desire, his burning passion, was simply this: "I want to know Him. I want to know the power of His resurrection."

My friends, you can attend church your whole life and never know the power of the resurrection. You can read your Bible every day and never experience it. You can believe all the right things and still miss it. The resurrection is not just a doctrine to be believed — it is a power to be experienced. And Paul says, "I want it. More than comfort, more than safety, more than success — I want to know HIM and the power of His resurrection."

Is that your desire tonight? Not just religion. Not just information. But to KNOW the risen Christ — personally, powerfully, transformatively?

## C. Practical Application — "Living in Resurrection Power"

My friends, I have given you theology tonight. I have given you history. I have given you prophecy. But now I want to bring it down to where you live. Right where you are. Right in the middle of your struggles, your fears, your daily life. What does the resurrection mean for YOU — practically, today?

### The Resurrection Means Death Is Not the End — Grieve with Hope

Some of you have lost someone you love. A husband. A wife. A parent. A child. And the grief is so heavy you do not know how you can carry it one more day. I understand that grief. I have stood at many gravesides. I have wept with many families.

But the Bible says in 1 Thessalonians 4:13: *"I do not want you to be ignorant, brethren, concerning those who have fallen asleep, lest you sorrow as others who have no hope."*

Paul does not say "do not sorrow." He says do not sorrow AS THOSE WHO HAVE NO HOPE. Christians grieve — but we grieve differently. We grieve with hope. Because the tomb is empty. Because Jesus promised, "Because I live, you will live also." Because this separation is not permanent.

If your loved one was in Christ, they are not gone. They are more alive than you are. They are in the presence of the risen Savior. And one day — one great and glorious day — you will see them again. Not as a wish. Not as a fantasy. As a certainty, guaranteed by the resurrection of Jesus Christ.

### The Resurrection Means Your Past Does Not Define You — You Can Start Over

Some of you are haunted by your past. You have made terrible mistakes. You have burned bridges. You have destroyed relationships. You have wasted years. And the voice of the enemy says, "You can never come back from this. You are defined by what you did."

But the resurrection says otherwise. The resurrection is the ultimate "starting over." Jesus went into the grave with the weight of the whole world's sin on His shoulders. And He came out clean, alive, victorious. The past was buried. The new had come.

And the Bible says that same resurrection power is available to you. *"If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation; old things have passed away; behold, all things have become new"* (2 Corinthians 5:17). Your past is not your future. Your failure is not your identity. The God of the resurrection specializes in new beginnings.

### The Resurrection Means Impossible Situations Have Answers

You may be facing something tonight that looks absolutely impossible. A marriage on the rocks. A diagnosis the doctors say is terminal. A financial disaster that has no solution. A child who has wandered far from God.

My friends, the resurrection is God's announcement to the world that He specializes in the impossible. The disciples looked at that sealed, guarded tomb and said, "It's over. There's nothing we can do." And God said, "Watch this."

If God can raise the dead, He can handle your situation. If God can open a tomb, He can open a door. Nothing — NOTHING — is too hard for the God who raised Jesus Christ from the dead.

### The Resurrection Means Your Labor Is Not in Vain

The Bible says in 1 Corinthians 15:58: *"Therefore, my beloved brethren, be steadfast, immovable, always abounding in the work of the Lord, knowing that your labor is not in vain in the Lord."*

Notice when Paul says this. He says it RIGHT AFTER the great resurrection chapter. After fifty-seven verses about the resurrection, Paul says: THEREFORE — because of the resurrection — your labor is not in vain.

Some of you are faithfully serving God and seeing no results. You are teaching that Sunday school class and the kids do not seem to care. You are loving your unbelieving spouse and nothing is changing. You are praying for your wayward son and he is still running. You are giving and serving and sacrificing, and you wonder, "Does any of it matter?"

Because of the resurrection, YES. Every prayer matters. Every act of kindness matters. Every sacrificial gift matters. Every tear you shed for someone else matters. Nothing done for the Lord is wasted. The God who raised Jesus from the dead will raise your labor to fruitfulness — maybe not in this life, maybe not in your timing, but certainly and absolutely in His.

### Illustration — Hope in the Face of Death

I want to close this section with a story.

Some years ago, I heard about a young father — thirty-four years old — who was diagnosed with an aggressive cancer. The doctors gave him months to live. He had a wife and two small children, a boy of five and a girl of three.

As the disease progressed and his body wasted away, his wife said the hardest part was the children. They did not understand why Daddy could not play with them anymore. They did not understand why Daddy was so thin, so tired, so weak.

One night, near the end, the little boy climbed into bed beside his father. He looked up with those big eyes and said, "Daddy, are you going to die?"

And that father — that dying father, that man with barely enough strength to speak — looked at his son and said, "Yes, buddy, I am. But I want you to know something. I am going to see Jesus. And Jesus beat death. He came back from the dead. And because He did, one day I'm coming back too. And we're going to play catch again. And we're never going to have to say goodbye."

That little boy did not understand all the theology. He did not understand atonement and justification and eschatology. But he understood this: his daddy knew Someone who was stronger than death.

My friends, that is the hope of the resurrection. It is not a theory. It is not a wish. It is the unshakeable, rock-solid, grave-defeating promise of the risen Son of God: "Because I live, you will live also."

## D. Broader Revelation Connections

### I. The Firstborn from the Dead (Revelation 1:5)

The Bible says in Revelation 1:5: *"And from Jesus Christ, the faithful witness, the firstborn from the dead, and the ruler over the kings of the earth."*

Jesus Christ is called the FIRSTBORN from the dead. Now that word "firstborn" does not mean He was the first person ever brought back to life — Lazarus was raised, the widow of Nain's son was raised, Jairus's daughter was raised. But all of those people died again. They were resuscitated, brought back to the same mortal life, and eventually they died.

Jesus is different. He is the firstborn from the dead in the sense that He is the FIRST to rise in a glorified, immortal, never-dying-again body. He is the pioneer. He is the trailblazer. He went first, and we will follow.

The word "firstborn" also means "preeminent." He holds the highest rank among all who will ever be raised. He is the firstborn — the most honored, the most exalted, the head of a whole new race of resurrected people.

And here is the promise: He is the FIRST. Not the only. The first. Where He has gone, we will go. What He has become, we will become. His resurrection body is the pattern for ours. His victory over death is the guarantee of ours.

He is the firstborn. We are the family that follows.

### II. The First Resurrection (Revelation 20:4-6)

The Bible says in Revelation 20:5-6: *"This is the first resurrection. Blessed and holy is he who has part in the first resurrection. Over such the second death has no power, but they shall be priests of God and of Christ, and shall reign with Him a thousand years."*

My friends, the Bible speaks of two resurrections. The first resurrection is for those who belong to Jesus Christ. It is a resurrection to LIFE, to glory, to honor, to reigning with Christ forever. And the Bible says that over those who take part in the first resurrection, the second death has NO POWER.

No power! The second death — which is the lake of fire, eternal separation from God — has no authority, no jurisdiction, no claim whatsoever on those who are in Christ. The resurrection does not just give you life — it makes you untouchable by eternal death. The second death looks at you and says, "I cannot touch this one. This one belongs to the risen Lamb."

Blessed and holy! That is what the Bible calls those who have part in the first resurrection. Not just saved — blessed. Not just forgiven — holy. Set apart. Honored. Royalty in the kingdom of God.

My friend, which resurrection will you be part of? The first resurrection, to life and glory? Or the second resurrection, to judgment? The choice is yours. And the choice must be made in this life. There are no second chances in the grave.

### III. No More Death — The Resurrection's Ultimate Promise (Revelation 21:4)

The Bible says in Revelation 21:4: *"And God will wipe away every tear from their eyes; there shall be no more death, nor sorrow, nor crying. There shall be no more pain, for the former things have passed away."*

This is the final word. This is where the resurrection leads. This is the destination of the great story that began at an empty tomb in Jerusalem two thousand years ago.

No more death. Do you understand what that means? No more hospitals. No more funerals. No more cancer wards. No more phone calls in the middle of the night. No more standing at gravesides in the rain. No more saying goodbye to the people you love. Death itself will be dead.

No more sorrow. No more depression. No more heartbreak. No more loneliness that eats at your soul in the dark hours of the night.

No more crying. God Himself — with His own hand — will wipe away every tear from your eyes. The God who spoke the galaxies into existence will tenderly, personally, lovingly dry your tears. And He will dry them for the last time. Because there will be nothing left to cry about.

No more pain. No more migraines. No more arthritis. No more chronic illness that steals your strength day by day. No more pain — forever.

My friends, this is what the resurrection promises. This is where it all leads. Jesus Christ walked out of that tomb so that one day, death itself would walk into its tomb. And it will never come out again.

The resurrection of Jesus Christ is not just an event in history. It is a promise about the future. It is God's guarantee that everything broken will be made whole, everything sad will come untrue, and everything dead will come alive.

Because He lives, you can face tomorrow. Because He lives, all fear is gone. Because He lives — because that tomb is empty, because death is defeated, because the grave has lost its grip — you have a hope that will never, ever, EVER disappoint you.

The tomb is empty, my friends. And that changes everything.

Will you put your trust in the risen Christ tonight?
