## SERMON 3: "The Age of Grace — The Clock Is Ticking" — EXPANDED ADDITIONS

---

### A. Old Testament Typology of the Age of Grace

Friends, I want to take you back tonight into the Old Testament. Some people think the Old Testament is just ancient history -- dusty stories about people who lived thousands of years ago. But the Bible says that "these things were written for our instruction" (Romans 15:4). Every one of these Old Testament stories is a picture -- a shadow -- of the grace of God that we are living in right now. And every single one of them carries the same warning: grace has a deadline.

**Noah's Ark — The Door That God Shut**

Turn with me to Genesis, chapters 6 and 7. The Bible says that in the days of Noah, the wickedness of man was great on the earth. Every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually. Does that sound familiar? Turn on the news tonight. Scroll through your phone. The world we are living in looks an awful lot like the world Noah was living in.

But God, in His mercy, gave the world a warning. He told Noah to build an ark. And the Bible tells us that Noah was "a preacher of righteousness" (2 Peter 2:5). For one hundred and twenty years, Noah preached. One hundred and twenty years! Can you imagine that? He hammered nails and he hammered the truth. He built that ark plank by plank, and with every plank he was saying to his neighbors, "Judgment is coming. Get on the boat."

And what did they do? They laughed. They mocked. They went on eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage. They threw parties. They made jokes about the old fool building a boat on dry land. "Where's the rain, Noah?" they said. "Where's the flood?"

I can almost hear them. It sounds like the voices I hear today. "Where is this Second Coming you keep talking about? The world has been going on just fine for thousands of years. Stop being so dramatic."

But the Bible says the day came when God told Noah, "Come into the ark, you and all your household" (Genesis 7:1). And then -- and I want you to hear this -- Genesis 7:16 says, "The Lord shut him in." GOD shut the door. Not Noah. God.

And when God shut that door, it did not matter how hard you knocked. It did not matter how loud you screamed. It did not matter if you suddenly believed that Noah had been right all along. The door was shut. Grace was over.

Jesus Himself pointed back to this moment. In Matthew 24:37-39, He said, "As the days of Noah were, so also will the coming of the Son of Man be. For as in the days before the flood, they were eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage, until the day that Noah entered the ark, and did not know until the flood came and took them all away, so also will the coming of the Son of Man be."

My friend, we are living in the hundred and twenty years right now. The ark is being built. The invitation is going out. The door is still open. But a day is coming -- and it may be closer than any of us think -- when God Himself will shut the door. And when He does, no amount of pleading will open it again.

**The Cities of Refuge — You Must Run**

In the book of Numbers, chapter 35, and again in Joshua chapter 20, God established six Cities of Refuge throughout the land of Israel. Here was the arrangement: if a man accidentally killed another person -- not murder, but an accidental death -- the family of the dead man had a right to seek vengeance. They would send an "avenger of blood" to pursue the man who had caused the death. And that man had one hope, and one hope only: he had to flee to a City of Refuge.

If he made it inside the gates of that city, he was safe. The avenger could not touch him. He was protected. He was covered.

But here is what I want you to notice: he had to RUN. He could not stroll. He could not take his time. He could not say, "I'll get there eventually." The avenger of blood was coming, and if that man was caught outside the city, he would die.

The Bible says that Christ is our City of Refuge. Hebrews 6:18 says that we "have fled for refuge to lay hold of the hope set before us." That word "fled" is urgent. It is a running word. It is a desperate word.

And I want to tell you tonight: the avenger is coming. The wages of sin is death, and judgment is pursuing every man, woman, and child who has not found shelter in Jesus Christ. But the gates of the City are open! Christ is standing with His arms wide, saying, "Come to Me!" But you must run. You must flee. You must come NOW, while the gates are still open, because a day is coming when they will close.

I have talked to people who say, "I'll get right with God someday." Someday! My friend, someday may be too late. The avenger does not wait for your schedule. Flee to Christ tonight. Run to Him. Do not walk. Do not hesitate. Run!

**The Year of Jubilee — The Great Cancellation**

In Leviticus chapter 25, God instituted something beautiful called the Year of Jubilee. Every fiftieth year, trumpets would sound throughout the land of Israel, and three magnificent things would happen. First, all debts were cancelled. If you owed money, the slate was wiped clean. Second, all slaves were freed. If you had sold yourself into servitude because of poverty, you walked out a free man. Third, all property was restored. If your family had lost its inheritance, it came back to you.

Can you imagine the joy of that day? The trumpet sounds and the man crushed under debt suddenly owes nothing. The woman bound in slavery suddenly walks free. The family who lost everything suddenly gets it all back.

My friend, THAT is a picture of the Age of Grace. Jesus came and He announced it Himself. In Luke 4:18-19, He stood up in the synagogue in Nazareth and read from the prophet Isaiah: "The Spirit of the Lord is upon Me, because He has anointed Me to preach the gospel to the poor; He has sent Me to heal the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives... to proclaim the acceptable year of the Lord."

The acceptable year of the Lord! The great Jubilee! That is what we are living in. Your debts of sin -- cancelled at the cross. Your slavery to addiction, to fear, to guilt -- broken by the power of Christ. Your lost inheritance -- restored through the resurrection.

But here is what you must understand: Jubilee only lasted for a season. It was one year out of fifty. And when that year ended, the regular rules came back. The window closed.

We are living in the great Jubilee of God's grace. The trumpet has sounded. The debts are being cancelled. The captives are being set free. But this Jubilee will not last forever. The year will end. The window will close. And if you have not claimed your freedom, if you have not had your debts cancelled, if you have not received your inheritance -- you will have missed it.

Do not miss the Jubilee. Come to Christ while the trumpet is still sounding.

**Rahab and the Scarlet Cord — The Blood That Saves**

In the book of Joshua, chapters 2 and 6, we read one of the most remarkable stories in all the Bible. The city of Jericho was about to be destroyed. God had pronounced judgment. The Israelites were coming, and nothing could stop them.

But inside that doomed city lived a woman named Rahab. Now, the Bible does not hide the fact that Rahab was a harlot -- a prostitute. She was not a good woman by anyone's standard. She had lived a sinful life in a sinful city. And yet, when the spies of Israel came to her home, something happened in her heart. She believed. She said, "I know that the Lord has given you the land" (Joshua 2:9). She had heard about the God of Israel, and she believed.

And because she believed, the spies made her a promise. They said, "Tie this scarlet cord in the window. When we come back to destroy the city, everyone inside your house -- behind that scarlet cord -- will be saved."

And that is exactly what happened. The walls of Jericho came crashing down. The entire city was destroyed. But Rahab's house stood. And everyone inside -- her father, her mother, her brothers, her sisters -- was saved. Protected by the scarlet cord.

Now, what was that scarlet cord? It was red. The color of blood. It is a picture of the blood of Jesus Christ that was shed on Calvary. When judgment falls -- and it will fall -- those who are covered by the blood will be saved. Those who are not will perish.

But I want you to notice several things. First, Rahab had to CHOOSE. She had to believe the promise and act on it. She had to hang that cord in the window. Second, she had to STAY INSIDE. It would do no good to hang the cord and then go wandering through the streets of Jericho. She had to remain under the protection of the blood. And third, she had to do it BEFORE the walls fell. Once the trumpets sounded and the walls came down, it was too late to go looking for scarlet cord.

My friend, the blood of Jesus Christ has been shed. The scarlet cord is hanging. The door is open. But you must choose. You must come inside. And you must do it now -- before the walls come down.

And let me tell you something else that thrills my soul: Rahab the harlot is listed in the genealogy of Jesus Christ (Matthew 1:5). She is in the hall of faith in Hebrews 11:31. A prostitute from a pagan city, saved by grace through faith, and woven into the very bloodline of the Savior. If God can save Rahab, He can save you. If God can use Rahab, He can use you. No past is too dirty for the blood to clean.

**The Day of Atonement — The Veil Is Torn**

In Leviticus chapter 16, God prescribed the most solemn day on the Jewish calendar: Yom Kippur, the Day of Atonement. Once a year -- and ONLY once a year -- the High Priest would enter the Holy of Holies, the innermost chamber of the Tabernacle, where the very presence of God dwelt above the Ark of the Covenant.

He would take the blood of a sacrificial animal and sprinkle it on the Mercy Seat. And on that one day, through that blood, atonement was made for the sins of the nation. The rest of the year, the way was blocked. A thick veil hung between man and God. But on that one day, access was granted. Through blood.

Now, when Jesus died on the cross, the Bible tells us something staggering. Matthew 27:51 says, "The veil of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom." Not from bottom to top -- which is how man would tear it. From TOP to BOTTOM -- because God tore it. God Himself ripped open the barrier between Himself and sinful humanity.

And the book of Hebrews tells us that Jesus is our great High Priest who has entered the heavenly Holy of Holies once for all, not with the blood of animals, but with His own blood, having obtained eternal redemption (Hebrews 9:11-12).

We are living in the great Day of Atonement. The veil has been torn. The way into the presence of God is wide open. You do not need a priest. You do not need a ritual. You do not need to wait for a special day on the calendar. RIGHT NOW, through the blood of Jesus Christ, you can come boldly to the throne of grace (Hebrews 4:16).

But the Day of Atonement was one day. It had a beginning and it had an end. And this great spiritual Day of Atonement we are living in -- this Age of Grace -- will also come to an end. The access that is open now will not be open forever. The veil that has been torn will not remain torn for all eternity for those who refuse to walk through it.

Come through the veil tonight. The way is open. The blood has been shed. The High Priest is waiting. Come now.

### B. Additional Pauline Connections

The Apostle Paul understood grace better than perhaps any man who ever lived, because Paul experienced it more dramatically than almost anyone. He was a murderer of Christians, a persecutor of the church, a man who held the coats of those who stoned Stephen. And yet grace found him on the road to Damascus. So when Paul writes about grace, he is not writing theory. He is writing testimony.

**Romans 2:4 — Patience Is Not Permission**

The Bible says in Romans 2:4, "Do you despise the riches of His goodness, forbearance, and longsuffering, not knowing that the goodness of God leads you to repentance?"

I want you to hear that carefully. The goodness of God LEADS you to repentance. Every morning that you wake up breathing is not God's way of saying, "I approve of how you're living." It is God's way of saying, "I am giving you another chance to repent."

Every paycheck you cash, every meal you eat, every sunset you watch, every child's laughter you hear -- that is the goodness of God pursuing you, wooing you, drawing you toward repentance. And when you take that goodness and use it as an excuse to keep sinning -- when you say, "Well, God hasn't struck me with lightning yet, so I must be okay" -- you are despising the riches of His goodness.

My friend, God's patience is not His permission. It is His invitation. And invitations have an RSVP date.

**Romans 9:22-23 — Grace Has a Purpose**

Paul writes something profound in Romans 9:22-23. He says that God "endured with much longsuffering the vessels of wrath prepared for destruction, and that He might make known the riches of His glory on the vessels of mercy, which He had prepared beforehand for glory."

Why does God wait? Why does He not bring judgment right now? Because He is still calling vessels of mercy. He is still saving souls. He is still drawing men and women and boys and girls out of darkness and into His marvelous light. Every day that grace continues, another person somewhere in the world is hearing the gospel for the first time. Another prodigal is coming home. Another Rahab is hanging a scarlet cord.

God's patience has a purpose. And that purpose is YOU. He is waiting for you.

But notice that the verse says He endured with "much longsuffering." That word "longsuffering" means that it costs God something to wait. He is suffering long. His holiness demands justice, and He is holding it back -- but He will not hold it back forever.

**Galatians 4:4 — God's Timetable**

"When the fullness of the time had come, God sent forth His Son" (Galatians 4:4). I love that phrase -- "the fullness of the time." God operates on a timetable. He is never early. He is never late. He is always exactly on time.

The first coming of Christ was predicted, planned, and executed on a precise divine schedule. The Roman Empire had built roads so the gospel could spread. The Greek language had become universal so the Scriptures could be understood. The Pax Romana had brought a period of relative peace so missionaries could travel safely. Everything was ready. And at exactly the right moment, a baby was born in Bethlehem.

If the first coming was on schedule, you can be absolutely certain that the second coming will be on schedule. God has a day marked on His calendar. He has a moment appointed. And when that moment arrives -- not one second before and not one second after -- the trumpet will sound, the Lord will descend from heaven with a shout, and the Age of Grace will be over.

You do not know that date. I do not know that date. No preacher, no prophet, no angel in heaven knows that date. But God knows it. And it is fixed. It is certain. It is coming.

**Ephesians 5:15-16 — Redeeming the Time**

Paul writes to the Ephesians, "See then that you walk circumspectly, not as fools but as wise, redeeming the time, because the days are evil" (Ephesians 5:15-16).

That word "redeeming" is a marketplace word. It means to buy up, to purchase, to snap up a bargain before it is gone. Paul is saying: time is a commodity, and it is running out. Buy it up! Use every minute! Make every conversation count! Do not waste a single day, because the days are evil and they are numbered.

I think of a man I heard about years ago who was given six months to live by his doctor. And in those six months, he shared the gospel with more people than he had in the previous sixty years of his life. He wrote letters. He made phone calls. He grabbed people by the arm and said, "I need to tell you something before I go." Why? Because when you know the clock is ticking, you stop wasting time.

My friend, your clock IS ticking. My clock is ticking. The clock of this age is ticking. We do not know how much time is left. So redeem it. Buy it up. Make it count.

**1 Thessalonians 5:1-3 — The Thief in the Night**

"The day of the Lord so comes as a thief in the night. For when they say, 'Peace and safety!' then sudden destruction comes upon them, as labor pains upon a pregnant woman. And they shall not escape" (1 Thessalonians 5:1-3).

A thief does not send you a letter saying, "I will be breaking into your house on Tuesday at 9 PM." A thief comes when you are not expecting him. And that is exactly how the end of grace will come. It will come when the world is saying, "Everything is fine. Peace and safety. The economy is good. Technology is advancing. We have never had it better."

And in the middle of that false security -- sudden destruction. Like a woman who goes from comfort to contractions in an instant. No warning. No preparation time. No second chances.

Paul says, "They shall NOT escape." That is a hard word. But it is a true word. And it is in your Bible for a reason -- to wake you up. To shake you out of your complacency. To make you understand that this thing is REAL and it is COMING.

**2 Thessalonians 2:10-12 — The Terrifying Point of No Return**

This may be the most sobering passage Paul ever wrote. He speaks of those who "did not receive the love of the truth, that they might be saved. And for this reason God will send them strong delusion, that they should believe the lie, that they all may be condemned who did not believe the truth but had pleasure in unrighteousness" (2 Thessalonians 2:10-12).

Read that again carefully. There comes a point -- and God alone knows when that point is -- when a person has rejected the truth so many times, has hardened their heart so thoroughly, has said "no" to the Holy Spirit so persistently, that God gives them over. He sends a strong delusion. He lets them believe the lie. And from that point on, they CANNOT believe, because they WOULD NOT believe.

This is not God being cruel. This is God honoring the choice that a person has made over and over and over again. You said, "I don't want the truth." God says, "Very well. You will have the lie." You said, "I don't want grace." God says, "Very well. You will have judgment."

My friend, I do not know how many times you have heard the gospel. I do not know how many times the Holy Spirit has tugged at your heart. But I beg you: do not assume there will always be a next time. There may come a hearing beyond which you cannot hear. There may come a hardening beyond which you cannot soften. Tonight may be your last opportunity. Do not let it pass.

### C. Practical Application — "How to Live in the Age of Grace"

If you are a believer tonight, I want to speak to you directly. You are saved by grace. Wonderful. But how are you LIVING in the Age of Grace? Are you living like someone who knows the clock is ticking, or are you living like someone who thinks they have forever?

**Live with Urgency**

Every conversation you have could be someone's last chance to hear the gospel. I mean that literally. The person sitting next to you on the bus, the cashier at the grocery store, your neighbor who waves from the driveway -- you do not know how much time they have left. You do not know if anyone else will ever tell them about Jesus.

I heard about a woman in Charlotte, North Carolina, many years ago who felt a strong urging from the Holy Spirit to speak to a young man at a gas station. She argued with God. She said, "Lord, he'll think I'm crazy. I'm just getting gas. This isn't the time." She drove away. And three days later, that young man was killed in an automobile accident. She went to his funeral and wept -- not just for him, but because she knew that God had given her a moment and she had let it pass.

Now, I cannot tell you whether that young man knew the Lord or not. That is between him and God. But I can tell you this: that woman lived the rest of her life with a sense of urgency she never had before. She never again let an opportunity pass without at least trying to share the love of Christ. Because she understood what it means when the Bible says, "Now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation" (2 Corinthians 6:2).

Do not wait for the perfect moment. The perfect moment is NOW.

**Live with Gratitude**

Grace is not something you earned. Grace is not something you deserve. Grace is not something that was owed to you. Grace is a gift -- pure, unmerited, extravagant, costly gift. It cost the Son of God His life. It cost the Father His Son. It cost the Spirit the agony of watching humanity crucify the Creator.

And what do we do with it? Too often, we take it for granted. We treat grace like the air we breathe -- we forget it is even there until it is taken away.

Every morning when you open your eyes, your first thought should be: "Thank You, Lord, for another day of grace." Every night when you lay your head on the pillow, your last thought should be: "Thank You, Lord, that You did not come back today, and my loved ones still have a chance to be saved."

Gratitude changes everything. It changes how you treat people. It changes how you handle suffering. It changes how you view your money, your time, your abilities. When you understand that EVERYTHING you have is a product of grace, you hold it loosely and you give it freely.

**Live with Purpose**

The Bible says in 2 Corinthians 5:20, "We are ambassadors for Christ." An ambassador is not a tourist. A tourist takes pictures, buys souvenirs, and goes home. An ambassador represents a King. An ambassador carries a message. An ambassador has authority and responsibility.

You are not on vacation in this world. You are on assignment. You have been placed in your neighborhood, your workplace, your school, your family for a PURPOSE -- to represent the King of Kings and to deliver His message of reconciliation to a dying world.

Every day you live in the Age of Grace is a day you have been commissioned to be an ambassador. Do not waste it sightseeing. Do not waste it accumulating things that moths will eat and rust will destroy. Use it. Use every day, every hour, every conversation for the purpose for which you were saved: to make Christ known.

**Live with Expectancy**

The Bible says Jesus is coming back. And He could come back at any moment. The early church lived with this expectancy. They greeted each other with the word "Maranatha" -- "the Lord is coming!" It was on their lips constantly. It shaped their decisions, their priorities, their courage.

Do you live with that expectancy? When you wake up in the morning, do you think, "This could be the day"? When you go to bed at night, do you think, "He could come before dawn"?

Living with expectancy does not mean sitting on a hillside staring at the sky. It means living every moment as if it could be your last moment of ministry. It means keeping short accounts with God and with people. It means not putting off until tomorrow what the Spirit is prompting you to do today. It means living ready -- bags packed, lamp trimmed, oil in the vessel, watching and waiting for the Bridegroom.

**An Illustration — The Man Who Almost Waited Too Long**

I remember a crusade many years ago -- I will not say the city, to protect the family -- where a man came forward on the last night. He was weeping so hard he could barely stand. One of our counselors put an arm around him and asked what was wrong.

He said, "I have been coming to these meetings every single night this week. Every night I have felt God pulling at my heart. Every night I have gripped the seat in front of me and told myself, 'Tomorrow night. I'll go tomorrow night.' And tonight, as I was driving here, something inside me said, 'This is your last chance. If you don't go tonight, you never will.'"

He gave his life to Christ that night. And I thank God for that. But what haunts me is this: what if he had waited one more night? There WAS no more night. The crusade was over. And I wonder how many people in that same auditorium gripped their seats every single night and never came forward. And the crusade ended. And the moment passed.

My friend, I do not know how many "nights" you have left. I do not know how many more times you will hear the gospel preached. But I know this: tonight, right now, the Holy Spirit is speaking to you. Do not grip the seat. Let go. Come forward. Come to Christ. The clock is ticking, and one day -- one day very soon -- the alarm will sound and the Age of Grace will be over.

### D. Broader Revelation Connections

The book of Revelation gives us a terrifying glimpse of what happens when grace ends. And I want to take you there tonight -- not to scare you, but to sober you. Not to make you afraid, but to make you WISE.

**Revelation 6:15-17 — When Grace Becomes Wrath**

In Revelation 6:15-17, John describes the reaction of humanity when they realize that the Age of Grace is over and the time of judgment has come. Listen to what the Bible says:

"And the kings of the earth, the great men, the rich men, the commanders, the mighty men, every slave and every free man, hid themselves in the caves and in the rocks of the mountains, and said to the mountains and rocks, 'Fall on us and hide us from the face of Him who sits on the throne and from the wrath of the Lamb! For the great day of His wrath has come, and who is able to stand?'"

Do you see who is hiding? Kings. Great men. Rich men. Commanders. Mighty men. The powerful, the wealthy, the influential -- the very people who thought they did not need God, who thought their money and their power and their prestige would protect them. And where are they? Crawling into caves. Begging rocks to crush them. Preferring DEATH to facing the wrath of the Lamb.

And notice that phrase: "the wrath of the LAMB." A lamb is gentle. A lamb is meek. A lamb is soft and quiet. The Lamb of God has been gentle with you. He has been patient. He has been tender. He has been standing at the door of your heart, KNOCKING -- not breaking it down, not forcing His way in, but knocking gently, saying, "If anyone hears My voice and opens the door, I will come in" (Revelation 3:20).

But the same Lamb who knocks gently in grace will roar in judgment. And on that day, there will be no door to open. There will be no invitation to accept. There will only be wrath -- the stored-up, righteous, holy wrath of a God who waited and waited and waited, and was rejected and rejected and rejected.

Do not wait for the wrath. Come to the Lamb now, while He is still knocking.

**Revelation 10:6 — "There Should Be Delay No Longer"**

In Revelation 10:5-6, John sees a mighty angel standing with one foot on the sea and one foot on the land, raising his hand to heaven and swearing by Him who lives forever and ever "that there should be delay no longer."

That phrase should send a chill through every person who is putting off their decision for Christ. "Delay no longer." God has been delaying judgment. He has been holding back the flood. He has been restraining the full outpouring of His wrath. But a day is coming when the angel will make this declaration, and the delays will be over. The patience will end. The clock will stop.

We do not know when that moment is. But we know it is coming. And we know it is closer today than it was yesterday.

**Revelation 14:6-7 — The Last Gospel Call**

Even in the midst of the Tribulation -- even after the Age of Grace as we know it has ended -- God is STILL reaching out. Revelation 14:6-7 shows us an angel flying in the midst of heaven, "having the everlasting gospel to preach to those who dwell on the earth -- to every nation, tribe, tongue, and people -- saying with a loud voice, 'Fear God and give glory to Him, for the hour of His judgment has come; and worship Him who made heaven and earth, the sea and springs of water.'"

Even then, God is sending the gospel. Even then, His mercy is reaching. But notice: the angel says, "the hour of His judgment HAS COME." It is no longer future. It is present. It is happening. And receiving Christ in the Tribulation will cost you your head (Revelation 20:4). How much better -- how infinitely, unspeakably better -- to come to Christ NOW, in the Age of Grace, when the cost is simply your pride, your self-will, your rebellion.

Why would you wait for the hard road when the easy road is open right now?

**Revelation 22:10-12 — "Behold, I Am Coming Quickly"**

The last chapter of the last book of the Bible carries this urgent message: "Do not seal the words of the prophecy of this book, for the time is at hand. He who is unjust, let him be unjust still; he who is filthy, let him be filthy still; he who is righteous, let him be righteous still; he who is holy, let him be holy still. And behold, I am coming quickly, and My reward is with Me, to give to every one according to his work" (Revelation 22:10-12).

"Do not seal the words." In other words, do not hide this message. Do not keep it for some future generation. The time is NOW. And then that haunting statement: "He who is unjust, let him be unjust still; he who is filthy, let him be filthy still." There will come a moment when every person's state is FIXED. Whatever you are at that moment, you will be forever. If you are righteous, you will be righteous forever. If you are filthy, you will be filthy forever. No more chances. No more changes. Fixed for eternity.

And then the words of Christ Himself: "Behold, I am coming quickly." Not slowly. Not eventually. QUICKLY. And when He comes, He brings rewards and He brings judgment. What He brings to YOU depends on what you do with Him tonight.

The Bible ends, in the very last invitation of Scripture, with these words: "The Spirit and the bride say, 'Come!' And let him who hears say, 'Come!' And let him who thirsts come. Whoever desires, let him take the water of life freely" (Revelation 22:17).

Come. That is the last word of invitation in all of Scripture. Come. The door is open. The water is free. The grace is available. Come.

But come NOW. Because the next verse after the invitation is the warning: "I am coming quickly."

Will you come tonight? Will you come before the door shuts? Will you come before the clock runs out? The Age of Grace is still here -- but the clock is ticking. And it will not tick forever.

Let us bow our heads.

## SERMON 4: "Sin — The Disease Nobody Wants to Talk About" — EXPANDED ADDITIONS

---

### A. Old Testament Typology of Sin and Its Remedy

Friends, tonight we are going to talk about a word that the modern world has tried to eliminate from its vocabulary. Psychologists call it "dysfunction." Sociologists call it "deviant behavior." Politicians call it "a mistake in judgment." But the Bible calls it SIN. And until you call it what God calls it, you cannot be cured of it.

The Old Testament is filled with pictures of sin -- what it is, what it does, how it works, and how God provides the remedy. Let us look at some of them.

**Genesis 3 — The First Sin and the Three-Fold Temptation**

Go back to the very beginning. Genesis chapter 3. The Garden of Eden. Everything was perfect. Man walked with God. There was no death, no disease, no divorce, no depression, no despair. Paradise.

And then the serpent came. And he whispered the oldest lie in the universe: "You will be like God" (Genesis 3:5). That is the root of every sin ever committed -- the desire to be your own god. To sit on your own throne. To call your own shots. To determine for yourself what is right and what is wrong.

And look at how the temptation worked. Genesis 3:6 says that Eve "saw that the tree was good for food" -- there is the lust of the flesh. She saw that it was "pleasant to the eyes" -- there is the lust of the eyes. And she saw it was "desirable to make one wise" -- there is the pride of life.

The Apostle John identifies these exact three categories in 1 John 2:16: "For all that is in the world -- the lust of the flesh, the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life -- is not of the Father but is of the world."

Every sin you have ever committed falls into one of these three categories. Every temptation you will ever face is a variation on one of these three themes. The lust of the flesh -- I WANT it. The lust of the eyes -- I SEE it. The pride of life -- I DESERVE it.

And do you know what is remarkable? When Satan tempted Jesus in the wilderness (Matthew 4:1-11), he used the exact same three strategies. "Turn stones to bread" -- lust of the flesh. "I will give you all the kingdoms of the world" -- lust of the eyes. "Throw yourself down from the temple" -- pride of life. The same three hooks. But where Eve took the bait, Jesus refused it. Where Adam fell, Christ stood. And because He stood, we can stand in Him.

**Genesis 4 — Sin Crouching at the Door**

Just one chapter later, we see what sin does when it enters the world. In Genesis 4, we have the story of Cain and Abel. Two brothers. Two offerings. Abel brought the best of his flock -- a blood sacrifice. Cain brought the fruit of the ground -- the work of his own hands. God accepted Abel's offering and rejected Cain's.

Why? Because without the shedding of blood, there is no remission of sin (Hebrews 9:22). Abel came God's way. Cain came his own way. And that is the fundamental divide of all humanity: those who come to God on His terms, through blood, through sacrifice, through Christ -- and those who try to come on their own terms, through their own works, their own goodness, their own religion.

But here is what I want you to see. When Cain became angry, God said something to him that should make every one of us sit up straight. Genesis 4:7: "Sin lies at the door. And its desire is for you."

Sin is personified here as a wild animal. A predator. Crouching at the door. Waiting. Watching. Hungry. And its desire is for YOU. Sin wants to devour you. Sin wants to consume you. Sin wants to destroy everything you love, everything you have built, everything you are.

And what happened? Cain did not master it. He let it in. And in one generation, humanity went from disobedience (eating a piece of fruit) to MURDER (killing your own brother). That is what sin does. It escalates. It always escalates. The little lie becomes the big lie. The wandering eye becomes the affair. The casual drink becomes the addiction. The angry thought becomes the violent act. Sin NEVER stays small. It is a cancer, and cancer always grows.

**Numbers 21 — The Bronze Serpent — Look and Live**

In Numbers chapter 21, the people of Israel were in the wilderness, and they sinned against God by complaining and grumbling. And God sent fiery serpents among them, and the serpents bit the people, and many of them died.

Now, I want you to feel the horror of that scene. Imagine a camp of hundreds of thousands of people, and suddenly venomous snakes are everywhere -- slithering into tents, coiling around ankles, striking without warning. People screaming. People dying. Children bitten. Mothers weeping.

And what did God tell Moses to do? Something that makes no sense to the natural mind. He said, "Make a fiery serpent, and set it on a pole; and it shall be that everyone who is bitten, when he looks at it, shall live" (Numbers 21:8).

A bronze serpent on a pole. You are dying of snakebite, and God says, "Look at the serpent on the pole and you will live." It makes no medical sense. It makes no logical sense. But it makes GOSPEL sense.

Because Jesus Himself pointed to this moment and said, "As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of Man be lifted up, that whoever believes in Him should not perish but have eternal life" (John 3:14-15). And the very next verse -- the most famous verse in all the Bible -- John 3:16: "For God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son, that whoever believes in Him should not perish but have everlasting life."

The bronze serpent on the pole is a picture of Jesus on the cross. He was "made sin for us" (2 Corinthians 5:21) -- the sinless One took the form of the very thing that was killing us. And all you have to do is LOOK. Look to the cross. Look to Jesus. Believe. And you will live.

The people who were bitten by the snakes did not have to perform a ritual. They did not have to go on a pilgrimage. They did not have to earn their healing. They simply had to LOOK. And looking was an act of faith. It was believing that God's remedy was sufficient, even when it did not make sense to the human mind.

My friend, the cross does not make sense to the human mind either. The idea that a carpenter from Nazareth dying on a Roman cross two thousand years ago could solve YOUR sin problem -- that sounds foolish to the world. But the Bible says, "the message of the cross is foolishness to those who are perishing, but to us who are being saved it is the power of God" (1 Corinthians 1:18).

Look and live. It is that simple. And it is that profound.

**2 Samuel 11-12 — David's Fall — How Sin Blinds, Deceives, and Escalates**

If you want to see how sin works in a human heart -- how it blinds, how it deceives, how it escalates -- look at David. Here was a man the Bible calls "a man after God's own heart" (Acts 13:22). A man who killed Goliath. A man who wrote the Psalms. A man who danced before the Ark of the Covenant. A man who loved God with everything he had.

And one evening, he went for a walk on his rooftop, and he saw a woman bathing. And instead of turning away, he looked. And instead of walking away, he inquired. And instead of stopping, he sent for her. And instead of repenting, he took her. And when she became pregnant, instead of confessing, he tried to cover it up. And when the cover-up failed, he committed murder -- arranging for her husband Uriah to be killed in battle.

Do you see the progression? A look became a lust. A lust became an act. An act became a cover-up. A cover-up became a murder. Step by step by step, each sin leading to a worse sin, each compromise demanding a bigger compromise. That is ALWAYS how it works. Sin promises you a shortcut to pleasure and delivers a long road to destruction.

And the most frightening part? David did not see it. For perhaps a year, he went about his business as king, acting as if nothing had happened. He was BLIND. Sin had blinded him to his own condition. He could look at the nation and see injustice everywhere but could not see the injustice in his own bedroom. He could write psalms about the righteousness of God and not feel the conviction of his own unrighteousness.

Until Nathan came. The prophet Nathan told David a story about a rich man who stole a poor man's only lamb. And David was furious. "The man who has done this shall surely die!" he said (2 Samuel 12:5). And Nathan looked the king in the eye and said four of the most powerful words in all of Scripture: "You are the man" (2 Samuel 12:7).

Four words. And the blindness fell off like scales. And David saw himself as he truly was. Not a great king. Not a mighty warrior. Not a brilliant poet. A sinner. An adulterer. A murderer. A man in desperate need of the mercy of God.

My friend, some of you tonight are living in blindness. You have compartmentalized your sin. You have convinced yourself that because you are successful, because you are respected, because you go to church, because you give to charity -- you are fine. But God has sent a Nathan to you tonight. And the message is the same: "You are the man. You are the woman." You are the sinner who needs a Savior.

Will you hear it? Will you let the blindness fall away? Will you see yourself as God sees you?

**Psalm 51 — The Model of Repentance**

And look at what David did when his eyes were opened. He did not make excuses. He did not blame Bathsheba. He did not blame his circumstances. He did not say, "Well, I was under a lot of stress as king." He fell on his face before God and prayed the prayer recorded in Psalm 51 -- the greatest prayer of repentance ever written.

"Have mercy upon me, O God, according to Your lovingkindness; according to the multitude of Your tender mercies, blot out my transgressions. Wash me thoroughly from my iniquity, and cleanse me from my sin. For I acknowledge my transgressions, and my sin is always before me. Against You, You only, have I sinned, and done this evil in Your sight" (Psalm 51:1-4).

And then that great cry of verse 10: "Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a steadfast spirit within me."

David did not ask God to REPAIR his heart. He asked God to CREATE a new one. Because he understood that the old heart was beyond repair. The old heart was deceitful above all things and desperately wicked (Jeremiah 17:9). He needed something new. Something clean. Something that only God could create.

And that is what God offers you tonight. Not a patch on the old life. Not a renovation of the old heart. A NEW creation. "If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation; old things have passed away; behold, all things have become new" (2 Corinthians 5:17).

**Isaiah 1:18 — The Blood That Makes Scarlet White**

And here is the promise that covers it all. Isaiah 1:18: "Come now, and let us reason together," says the Lord. "Though your sins are like scarlet, they shall be as white as snow; though they are red like crimson, they shall be as wool."

Scarlet was the most permanent dye in the ancient world. You could not wash it out. You could not bleach it away. Once a garment was dyed scarlet, it was scarlet forever. And God says, "Your sin is like that. Deep. Permanent. Irremovable by any human effort."

But then He says, "I will make it white as snow." The God who cannot lie says He can take the deepest, most permanent stain of sin and make it utterly, completely, brilliantly white. How? Through the blood of Jesus Christ. "The blood of Jesus Christ His Son cleanses us from ALL sin" (1 John 1:7). Not some sin. Not small sin. Not respectable sin. ALL sin.

I do not care what you have done. I do not care how deep the stain goes. I do not care if every person in this auditorium would be horrified if they knew your secret. God already knows it. And He says, "Come. Let us reason together. I can make that scarlet white."

### B. Additional Pauline Connections

The Apostle Paul was a theologian, but he was also a doctor of the soul. He diagnosed the human condition with surgical precision, and then he prescribed the only cure. Let us look at his diagnosis.

**Romans 3:23 — The Universal Diagnosis**

"For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God" (Romans 3:23).

All. Not "some." Not "most." Not "the really bad ones." ALL. The prostitute on the street corner and the pastor in the pulpit. The prisoner in the cell and the judge on the bench. The thief and the philanthropist. The atheist and the churchgoer. ALL have sinned.

You say, "But I'm a good person." Compared to whom? Compared to your neighbor? Maybe. Compared to the glory of God? You fall short. We ALL fall short. The standard is not the person sitting next to you. The standard is the perfect, holy, infinite glory of Almighty God. And when measured against THAT standard, the best person who ever lived apart from Christ is a sinner who falls short.

It is like trying to jump across the Grand Canyon. One person might jump six feet. Another might jump ten. Another might jump twenty. But nobody is jumping across. Everybody falls short. And the difference between six feet and twenty feet is meaningless when the canyon is a mile wide.

You might be a "better" sinner than your neighbor. But you are still a sinner. And sinners cannot enter the presence of a holy God without a Savior.

**Romans 5:12 — The Inherited Disease**

"Therefore, just as through one man sin entered the world, and death through sin, and thus death spread to all men, because all sinned" (Romans 5:12).

This is what theologians call "original sin," and it is one of the hardest truths for the modern mind to accept. You were born a sinner. You did not become a sinner the first time you told a lie or stole a cookie. You were a sinner from the moment of conception. David said, "In sin my mother conceived me" (Psalm 51:5). Not that the act of conception was sinful, but that sin was part of his nature from the very beginning.

You do not have to teach a child to be selfish. You do not have to teach a toddler to say "Mine!" You do not have to teach a teenager to rebel. It comes naturally. It is in the blood. It is in the DNA of the soul. We inherited it from Adam.

This is why being "a good person" is not enough. The problem is not just what you DO -- it is what you ARE. You can clean up the outside of the cup, but the inside is still full of corruption (Matthew 23:25-26). You can put a fresh coat of paint on a condemned building, but the foundation is still crumbling.

You do not need improvement. You need a new birth. Jesus said, "You must be born again" (John 3:7). Not reformed. Not educated. Not medicated. BORN AGAIN. A completely new life from a completely new source.

**Romans 6:23 — Wages and Gifts**

"For the wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord" (Romans 6:23).

I want you to see the contrast in this verse. On one side: wages. On the other side: a gift. Wages are something you EARN. You work, and you get paid. If you work for sin, you will be paid -- and the payment is death. Not just physical death, but spiritual death. Eternal separation from God. Hell.

Nobody in hell can say, "This is unfair." It is the wage they earned. They worked for sin, and they received their paycheck.

But on the other side is a gift. A gift is something you do NOT earn. You do not work for a gift. You do not deserve a gift. Someone simply gives it to you out of love. And the gift God offers is eternal life -- not just living forever, but living forever in the presence and joy and glory of God Himself.

Now, here is what amazes me. Every person in this auditorium will receive one or the other. Wages or a gift. Death or life. And the only difference is whether you try to earn your way or whether you receive what God is offering freely. You can have the wages you deserve, or you can have the gift you do not deserve. Which will it be?

**Romans 7:15-24 — The Apostle's Honest Confession**

One of the most honest passages in all the Bible is Romans 7:15-24, where Paul opens his heart and reveals the war that rages inside every believer. "For what I will to do, that I do not practice; but what I hate, that I do... O wretched man that I am! Who will deliver me from this body of death?" (Romans 7:15, 24).

If the Apostle Paul -- the man who wrote half the New Testament, who planted churches across the Roman world, who was caught up to the third heaven -- if PAUL struggled with sin, then you and I should not be surprised that we struggle too.

This is one of the most comforting passages in the Bible for the honest Christian. Because every believer knows this war. You love God, but you still sin. You want to do right, but you find yourself doing wrong. You set your mind on holiness, and then the flesh rises up and drags you down. And you cry out with Paul, "O wretched man that I am!"

But Paul does not stop there. The very next verse -- Romans 7:25 -- says, "I thank God -- through Jesus Christ our Lord!" There IS a deliverer. His name is Jesus. And the answer to the war within is not trying harder, not gritting your teeth, not white-knuckling your way to righteousness. The answer is CHRIST IN YOU, the hope of glory (Colossians 1:27).

**Galatians 5:19-21 and 5:24 — The Works of the Flesh and the Cross**

Paul gives us a comprehensive catalog of sin in Galatians 5:19-21: "adultery, fornication, uncleanness, lewdness, idolatry, sorcery, hatred, contentions, jealousies, outbursts of wrath, selfish ambitions, dissensions, heresies, envy, murders, drunkenness, revelries, and the like."

Read that list slowly. It covers every category of sin. Sexual sin. Spiritual sin. Relational sin. Self-destructive sin. And Paul adds those devastating words: "and the like" -- meaning, this list is not exhaustive. It is just a sample. The flesh is capable of things so dark that Paul does not even bother to list them all.

But then he gives the answer in Galatians 5:24: "Those who are Christ's have crucified the flesh with its passions and desires."

Crucified. Not managed. Not controlled. Not put on a diet. CRUCIFIED. Put to death on the cross. The answer to the works of the flesh is not a self-help program. It is an execution. You take the old nature to the cross and you nail it there, and you leave it there, and you walk away in the power of the resurrection.

This is a daily act. Every morning, you must take up your cross (Luke 9:23). Every day, you must reckon yourself dead to sin and alive to God in Christ Jesus (Romans 6:11). It is not a one-time event. It is a daily crucifixion. And it is the only thing that works.

**Ephesians 2:1-5 — Dead, But God**

"And you He made alive, who were dead in trespasses and sins, in which you once walked according to the course of this world, according to the prince of the power of the air, the spirit who now works in the sons of disobedience, among whom also we all once conducted ourselves in the lusts of our flesh, fulfilling the desires of the flesh and of the mind, and were by nature children of wrath, just as the others. But God, who is rich in mercy, because of His great love with which He loved us, even when we were dead in trespasses, made us alive together with Christ" (Ephesians 2:1-5).

Dead. That is the word Paul uses. Not sick. Not weak. Not struggling. DEAD. You were not drowning and in need of a lifeguard. You were at the bottom of the ocean, dead, and in need of a resurrection.

A dead man cannot help himself. A dead man cannot pull himself up by his bootstraps. A dead man cannot earn salvation or work his way to heaven or improve his moral condition. A dead man needs LIFE. And life can only come from the Life-giver.

And then those two most beautiful words in the English language: "But God." Dead in sin -- BUT GOD. Children of wrath -- BUT GOD. Without hope, without help, without a chance -- BUT GOD. Rich in mercy. Great in love. Powerful in resurrection. He made us alive. Not because we deserved it. Not because we asked for it. Not because we were good enough. But because He is rich in mercy and great in love.

If you are dead in sin tonight, you need the "But God" of Ephesians 2. You need the resurrection power of Jesus Christ to raise you out of spiritual death and into spiritual life. And that power is available right now, in this moment, if you will simply receive it by faith.

### C. Practical Application — "Breaking Free from Sin's Grip"

I want to get practical now. Many of you know you are in the grip of sin. You have known it for a long time. Maybe you have tried to break free on your own and failed. Maybe you have given up trying. Let me give you a biblical path to freedom.

**Identify It — Call It What God Calls It**

The first step to breaking free from sin is to stop using soft language. Stop calling your sin a "mistake." A mistake is when you dial the wrong number. Stop calling it an "issue." An issue is something you discuss in a committee meeting. Stop calling it a "struggle." Stop calling it a "lifestyle choice." Stop calling it "my truth."

Call it what God calls it: SIN. Rebellion against a holy God. Transgression of His law. An offense against His character.

I know that sounds harsh. But a doctor who tells you "you have a slight irregularity" when you actually have cancer is not being kind -- he is being CRUEL. You need the truth to be free. And the truth is: sin is sin. It is deadly. It is damnable. And it must be dealt with honestly before it can be dealt with effectively.

**Confess It — Bring It into the Light**

The Bible says, "If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness" (1 John 1:9).

Confession means bringing your sin into the light. It means saying to God, with specific honesty, "I did this. It was wrong. I have no excuse."

What you hide controls you. That is one of the great principles of the spiritual life. The secret sin, the hidden addiction, the private shame -- as long as it stays in the dark, it has power over you. It grows in the dark. It feeds on secrecy. It thrives in silence.

But when you confess it -- when you bring it into the light -- something remarkable happens. The power breaks. The chains loosen. The weight lifts. Not because confession is a magic formula, but because confession connects you to the faithfulness and justice of God. HE is faithful. HE is just. HE forgives. HE cleanses. You do the confessing; He does the forgiving.

I knew a man who carried a secret sin for thirty-seven years. Thirty-seven years of guilt. Thirty-seven years of shame. Thirty-seven years of looking over his shoulder. And when he finally confessed it -- first to God, then to a trusted friend -- he told me, "It was like a thousand pounds lifted off my chest. I could breathe again. I could sleep again. I could look people in the eye again."

Thirty-seven years of bondage. And freedom came in one moment of honest confession. What are you carrying tonight? How long have you been carrying it? Bring it to God. Bring it to the cross. He already knows about it anyway. And He is waiting to forgive.

**Forsake It — Turn and Walk the Other Way**

Confession without forsaking is not repentance -- it is just ventilating. The Bible says, "Let the wicked forsake his way, and the unrighteous man his thoughts; let him return to the Lord, and He will have mercy on him; and to our God, for He will abundantly pardon" (Isaiah 55:7).

Repentance is not just feeling sorry. Judas felt sorry. Esau felt sorry. Being sorry is not enough. Repentance means turning around. It means walking in the opposite direction. It means not only saying, "I was wrong," but also saying, "I am done. By the grace of God, I am walking away from this sin and I am not going back."

Now, does that mean you will never stumble? No. Sanctification is a process. But there is a difference between a man who falls on his face while walking toward God and a man who is running full speed toward the cliff. The direction of your life matters. When you stumble, you get up, you confess, and you keep walking toward Christ. But the direction must change.

**Replace It — Fill the House**

Jesus told a parable in Matthew 12:43-45 about a man from whom an unclean spirit was cast out. The spirit wandered in dry places, then returned and found the house "empty, swept, and put in order." So it went and brought seven spirits more wicked than itself, and the last state of that man was worse than the first.

Here is the lesson: you cannot just empty the house. You must FILL it. If you remove the sin but do not replace it with the Spirit of God, you have just created a vacuum -- and sin will rush back in, worse than before.

This is why so many people fail in their attempts to break free from sin. They try to "quit" the sin without "starting" the new life. They give up the old habits but do not develop new ones. They leave the old friends but do not find new fellowship. They stop going to the bar but do not start going to church. They create an empty house. And the enemy always fills an empty house.

Fill your life with the Word of God. Fill your time with prayer. Fill your relationships with godly people. Fill your mind with things that are true, noble, just, pure, lovely, and of good report (Philippians 4:8). A full house has no room for the old tenants.

**Accountability — You Need People Who Will Tell You the Truth**

Proverbs 27:17 says, "As iron sharpens iron, so a man sharpens the countenance of his friend." James 5:16 says, "Confess your trespasses to one another, and pray for one another, that you may be healed."

You were not designed to fight sin alone. You need brothers and sisters who know your weaknesses, who have permission to ask hard questions, who will love you enough to confront you when you are straying.

I know a man who overcame a devastating addiction, and when I asked him how, he said, "I gave three men permission to ask me anything, anytime, and I promised them I would never lie." That is accountability. That is iron sharpening iron. That is the body of Christ functioning as God designed it.

Find someone you trust. Open your life to them. Give them permission to speak truth to you, even when it hurts. Especially when it hurts. Because the wounds of a friend are faithful, but the kisses of an enemy are deceitful (Proverbs 27:6).

**An Illustration — The Destructive Power of Hidden Sin and the Freedom of Confession**

I want to tell you about a man I met many years ago at one of our crusades. He was a prominent businessman. Well-respected in his community. Deacon in his church. Everyone thought he had it all together.

But he came to me privately one evening, and he was trembling. He said, "Mr. Graham, I have been living a double life for fifteen years. On the outside, I look like a Christian. On the inside, I am rotting. There is a sin in my life that I have never told anyone about. Not my wife. Not my pastor. Not my best friend. And it is killing me."

He told me what it was, and I will not share those details -- they are between him and God. But I can tell you that the weight of it had given him ulcers, migraines, sleepless nights, and thoughts of ending his own life. The hidden sin was literally destroying his body and his mind.

I opened my Bible and read him 1 John 1:9: "If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness." And then I read him Psalm 32:3-5, where David wrote: "When I kept silent, my bones grew old through my groaning all the day long. For day and night Your hand was heavy upon me; my vitality was turned into the drought of summer. I acknowledged my sin to You, and my iniquity I have not hidden. I said, 'I will confess my transgressions to the Lord,' and You forgave the iniquity of my sin."

That man knelt down right there and confessed everything to God. And when he stood up, his face was different. The lines of tension were gone. The haunted look in his eyes was replaced by tears -- not tears of sorrow, but tears of relief. He said, "I feel like I have been holding my breath for fifteen years and I finally exhaled."

He went home and told his wife. It was hard. There were consequences. But he told me later, "The worst day of honesty was better than the best day of hiding."

My friend, what are you hiding? What secret sin is eating you alive? Bring it to the cross. Bring it to Jesus. He already knows. And He is not waiting to condemn you -- He is waiting to forgive you. "There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus" (Romans 8:1).

### D. Broader Revelation Connections

Let me take you to the end of the Bible now, to the book of Revelation, and show you where unforgiven sin ultimately leads. This is not easy to preach. It is not easy to hear. But it is in the Word of God, and I would not be faithful to my calling if I did not tell you the truth.

**Revelation 20:11-15 — The Great White Throne**

In Revelation 20:11-15, John describes the most terrifying courtroom scene in all of history. He sees a great white throne, and on it sits God Himself. And the dead -- all the dead who died without Christ -- are standing before that throne.

"And the books were opened. And another book was opened, which is the Book of Life. And the dead were judged according to their works, by the things which were written in the books" (Revelation 20:12).

Books. Plural. Every sin you have ever committed is recorded. Every lie. Every lustful thought. Every act of cruelty. Every moment of rebellion. Every time you took God's name in vain. Every time you chose yourself over Him. Written down. Stored up. Preserved as evidence.

And the verdict? "Anyone not found written in the Book of Life was cast into the lake of fire" (Revelation 20:15).

This is where unforgiven sin leads. Not to annihilation. Not to a slap on the wrist. Not to a second chance. To the lake of fire. Eternal separation from God.

I do not enjoy preaching about hell. No true preacher does. But I would rather make you uncomfortable for thirty minutes tonight than see you lost for eternity. The doctor who refuses to tell you about cancer because it might upset you is not your friend. And the preacher who refuses to tell you about hell because it might offend you is not your friend either.

But here is the good news -- and there IS good news. The Book of Life is still open. Your name can be written in it tonight. Not by your works. Not by your goodness. By the blood of Jesus Christ. "They overcame him by the blood of the Lamb" (Revelation 12:11). When the books are opened and your sins are laid out for all to see, the blood of Jesus covers every page. "Not guilty" is written across every entry. And your name -- YOUR name -- is found in the Book of Life. And instead of the lake of fire, you enter the presence of God forever.

**Revelation 21:8 — Sin's Eternal Consequences**

"But the cowardly, unbelieving, abominable, murderers, sexually immoral, sorcerers, idolaters, and all liars shall have their part in the lake which burns with fire and brimstone, which is the second death" (Revelation 21:8).

I want you to notice that list. It does not start with murderers and sorcerers. It starts with the COWARDLY and the UNBELIEVING. The first two on the list are not the spectacular sinners -- they are the quiet ones. The ones who were too afraid to stand for Christ. The ones who simply did not believe.

You do not have to be a murderer to go to hell. You just have to be an unbeliever. You do not have to commit some heinous crime. You just have to refuse the gift. The cowardly person who knew the truth but was too afraid of what people would think to step forward and receive Christ -- that person is on this list. The unbelieving person who heard the gospel a hundred times but never surrendered -- that person is on this list.

And notice that "all liars" are on the list too. ALL liars. Have you ever told a lie? Then without Christ's forgiveness, you are on the list. This is not about being "bad enough" for hell. It is about being human enough to sin and proud enough to reject the only cure.

**Revelation 21:27 — Nothing Unclean Enters**

"But there shall by no means enter it anything that defiles, or causes an abomination or a lie, but only those who are written in the Lamb's Book of Life" (Revelation 21:27).

Heaven is a pure place. A holy place. A clean place. And nothing dirty gets in. Not one sin. Not one stain. Not one speck of defilement. The door of heaven has a sign over it, and the sign reads: "CLEAN ONLY."

And that is your problem. Because you are NOT clean. I am not clean. None of us, in ourselves, are clean. We are stained with sin from the crown of our heads to the soles of our feet.

So how do we get in? Only one way. "The blood of Jesus Christ His Son cleanses us from all sin" (1 John 1:7). The blood of Jesus is the only detergent in the universe strong enough to remove the stain of sin and make you clean enough to enter the presence of a holy God.

You can try religion. You can try good works. You can try moral reform. You can try meditation, education, medication, or self-improvement. But at the gates of heaven, the only thing that matters is: are you washed in the blood?

**Revelation 22:14-15 — Inside or Outside, Washed or Unwashed**

"Blessed are those who wash their robes, that they may have the right to the tree of life, and may enter through the gates into the city. But outside are dogs and sorcerers and sexually immoral and murderers and idolaters, and whoever loves and practices a lie" (Revelation 22:14-15).

Two groups. Inside and outside. Washed and unwashed. Blessed and cursed. Those who have the right to the tree of life and those who are shut out forever.

And the dividing line is not how much money you gave. Not how many church services you attended. Not how many good deeds you performed. The dividing line is the washing. "Blessed are those who WASH THEIR ROBES." In the blood of the Lamb.

Tonight, you are in one group or the other. You are either washed or unwashed. You are either inside or outside. And when this life is over -- and it WILL be over sooner than any of us expect -- your group is fixed for eternity.

But right now, in this moment, you can move from one group to the other. You can come from outside to inside. You can be washed. You can be cleansed. You can be forgiven. All it takes is coming to Jesus Christ and saying, "Lord, I am a sinner. I cannot save myself. I believe You died for me. I believe Your blood can wash me clean. I receive You as my Lord and my Savior."

That is all. That is everything.

---

**A Final Word**

Sin is the disease. Christ is the cure. You have heard the diagnosis tonight, and you have heard the remedy. The question is: what will you do with it?

Will you be like the people bitten by serpents in the wilderness who refused to look at the bronze serpent on the pole -- and died? Or will you look and live?

Will you be like Cain, who brought his own works to God and was rejected? Or will you come God's way, through blood, through sacrifice, through the cross?

Will you be like David, hiding his sin until it destroyed him from the inside out? Or will you confess it tonight and find the mercy that is waiting for you?

The Bible says, "Today, if you will hear His voice, do not harden your hearts" (Hebrews 3:15). Today. Not tomorrow. Not next week. Not when you have cleaned up your act. TODAY.

I am going to ask you to do something right now. I am going to ask you to bow your head and close your eyes. And in the quiet of this moment, I am going to ask you to make a decision. The most important decision you will ever make. More important than who you will marry. More important than what career you will choose. More important than where you will live.

The decision is this: will you receive Jesus Christ as your Lord and Savior? Will you confess your sin and trust in His blood to make you clean? Will you step out of the group of the unwashed and into the group of the washed?

If that is the desire of your heart tonight, I want you to come. Just as you are. Do not wait until you are better. Do not wait until you feel worthy. Come with your sin, come with your shame, come with your brokenness, and lay it all at the foot of the cross. He is waiting. He will not turn you away. The Bible says, "The one who comes to Me I will by no means cast out" (John 6:37).

Come now. While the Spirit is moving. While the door is open. While grace is still being offered.

Come.
