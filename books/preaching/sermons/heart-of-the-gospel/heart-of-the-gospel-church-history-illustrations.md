# CHURCH HISTORY ILLUSTRATIONS FOR "THE HEART OF THE GOSPEL" SERMON SERIES

## Sermon 1: "The Cross — Where God Met Man" — Church History Illustrations

### Athanasius Contra Mundum — One Man Against the World
Friends, let me tell you about a man named Athanasius. He lived in the fourth century, in the city of Alexandria, Egypt. And he stood for the truth of the cross when the WHOLE WORLD seemed to turn against him.

You see, a man named Arius began teaching that Jesus was not truly God — that He was a created being, the first and greatest of God's creation, but NOT God Himself. And this teaching spread like wildfire. Emperors embraced it. Bishops caved to the pressure. It seemed like the whole church was going to abandon the truth that the Son of God — GOD in human flesh — died on that cross.

But Athanasius would not bend. They exiled him five times. Five times! He spent 17 years of his life in exile because he would not deny that Jesus Christ was fully God. They said, "Athanasius, the whole world is against you!" And he replied, "Then Athanasius is against the whole world."

Why did it matter so much? Because if Jesus was not God, then the cross is meaningless! If a mere creature died on Calvary, then the price wasn't enough. Only GOD could pay the infinite debt of human sin. Athanasius understood that. The deity of Christ and the power of the cross stand or fall TOGETHER.

The Bible says in Colossians 2:9, "In Him dwells all the fullness of the Godhead bodily." It was GOD on that cross. And Athanasius was willing to stand alone to defend that truth. My friend, are you willing to stand for the cross even when the whole world is against you?

### The Moravian Missionaries — "May the Lamb Receive His Reward!"
In 1732, in a small community called Herrnhut in Germany, a group of believers known as the Moravians heard about slaves in the Danish West Indies who had no one to tell them about Jesus. These slaves worked on sugar plantations under brutal conditions, and no missionary could get access to them. The plantation owners would not allow outsiders in.

So two young Moravian men — Leonard Dober and David Nitschmann — made an extraordinary decision. They would SELL THEMSELVES INTO SLAVERY to reach those slaves with the gospel. They would become slaves themselves so they could work alongside the other slaves and tell them about the cross of Jesus Christ.

As the ship pulled away from the harbor, their families and friends wept on the dock. And Dober and Nitschmann lifted their voices and shouted across the water words that echo through history: "MAY THE LAMB THAT WAS SLAIN RECEIVE THE REWARD OF HIS SUFFERING!"

The Lamb that was slain — that is Jesus. The reward of His suffering — that is YOU. That is every soul saved by the blood of the cross. These men voluntarily gave up their freedom because they understood the cross. The cross so transformed them that they would become slaves so that slaves could be set free in Christ.

I wonder tonight: Has the cross so gripped your heart that you would give everything? Has the love of Calvary so overwhelmed you that no sacrifice is too great?

### Martin Luther's Tower Experience
Martin Luther was a monk. He fasted. He prayed. He crawled up the Scala Sancta — the holy stairs in Rome — on his bloody knees, saying a prayer on every step, trying to earn God's favor. He went to confession so often that his priest told him to go away and come back when he had a REAL sin to confess!

Luther was terrified of God. Terrified of judgment. Terrified of the cross — because the cross told him that sin was so serious that GOD HAD TO DIE. And Luther knew he was a sinner.

Then one day, in the tower of the monastery in Wittenberg, Luther was studying the book of Romans. And he came to Romans 1:17 — "The just shall live by FAITH." And suddenly, as if a light from heaven broke into that dark tower, Luther understood. The righteousness of God is not something we EARN. It is something we RECEIVE — by faith! The cross was not God's condemnation of Luther. It was God's GIFT to Luther!

Luther wrote later: "I felt myself to be reborn and to have gone through open doors into paradise. The whole of Scripture took on a new meaning. This passage of Paul became to me a gate to heaven."

That understanding — that the cross is grace, not works; gift, not payment; faith, not effort — sparked the Protestant Reformation. It changed the world. And it started when one man finally understood what happened at Calvary.

### Hudson Taylor — "Christ Is Lord of All, or He Is Not Lord at All"
Hudson Taylor was a young Englishman who heard the call of God to go to China — the interior of China, where no Western missionary had ever gone. People told him he was crazy. China was dangerous. The culture was foreign. The language was impossible. Missionaries who went to the coast barely survived; Taylor wanted to go INLAND.

But Taylor had been to the cross. He had seen the love of God poured out in blood. And he said, "Christ is either Lord of ALL, or He is not Lord at all." If Jesus died for the WHOLE world, then someone must go to the WHOLE world.

Taylor founded the China Inland Mission. He adopted Chinese dress. He learned the language. He endured incredible hardships — the deaths of his wife and several children, riots, disease, poverty. But he pressed on. By the time of his death, the China Inland Mission had 825 missionaries and 125,000 Chinese Christians.

When asked the secret of his perseverance, Taylor said simply, "I have found that there are three stages in every great work of God: first it is impossible, then it is difficult, then it is done."

The cross gave him that confidence. If God could accomplish the impossible at Calvary, He could accomplish the impossible in China. And He did.

## Sermon 2: "The Empty Tomb Changed Everything" — Church History Illustrations

### Polycarp's Martyrdom — "How Can I Blaspheme My King?"
Polycarp was the bishop of Smyrna. He was a disciple of the apostle John — a man who knew someone who knew Jesus personally. And when Polycarp was 86 years old, the Roman authorities arrested him and brought him into the arena before a howling mob.

The proconsul said, "Have respect for your old age. Swear by the fortune of Caesar. Say, 'Away with the atheists'" — meaning the Christians.

Polycarp looked at the pagan crowd, swept his hand toward THEM, and said, "Away with the atheists!"

The proconsul pressed him: "Swear, and I will release you. Curse Christ!"

And this 86-year-old man — who had walked with those who walked with Jesus, who knew the resurrection was REAL because he knew the witnesses — said these immortal words: "Eighty-six years have I served Him, and He has done me no wrong. HOW CAN I BLASPHEME MY KING AND MY SAVIOR?"

They threatened him with fire. Polycarp replied, "You threaten me with fire that burns for an hour and then goes out. But you do not know the fire of the coming judgment and eternal punishment. Why do you delay? Bring what you wish."

They burned him at the stake. But witnesses reported that the flames formed an arch around him like a sail, and his body glowed like gold. A fragrance like incense filled the arena.

What gave an 86-year-old man the courage to face the flames? The resurrection. He KNEW Jesus was alive. He KNEW death was not the end. He KNEW that to be absent from the body was to be present with the Lord. The empty tomb changed everything for Polycarp — and it can change everything for you.

### The Catacombs of Rome — Worshipping Among the Dead
Beneath the streets of Rome lie over 600 miles of underground tunnels called the catacombs. For nearly three centuries, during periods of intense persecution, Christians buried their dead and often gathered for worship in these dark, narrow passageways.

What did they carve on the walls? Not symbols of death and despair. They carved the FISH (ichthys — the Greek acrostic for "Jesus Christ, God's Son, Savior"). They carved the ANCHOR (the hope of the soul — Hebrews 6:19). They carved the PHOENIX (the mythical bird that rises from its own ashes — a symbol of resurrection). They carved PALM BRANCHES (victory!). They carved the good SHEPHERD carrying a lamb on His shoulders.

These people worshipped AMONG TOMBS. They gathered in the place of death. And yet their symbols were all about LIFE, HOPE, VICTORY, and RESURRECTION. Why? Because they believed — with every fiber of their being — that the tomb was not the end. Jesus had walked out of His tomb, and one day they would walk out of theirs.

If you ever doubt the resurrection, go to the catacombs of Rome. Stand in those dark tunnels and look at what Christians carved when they had nothing left in this world. They carved hope. They carved life. They carved resurrection. The empty tomb changed everything.

### The Transformation of the Disciples
Consider the disciples before and after the resurrection:

Before: Peter denied Jesus three times. All the disciples "forsook Him and fled" (Matthew 26:56). They hid behind locked doors "for fear of the Jews" (John 20:19). Thomas said he would not believe unless he could put his fingers in the nail holes.

After: Peter preached at Pentecost and 3,000 were saved. Every single disciple (except John, who was exiled) was martyred for preaching the resurrection. Peter was crucified upside down — asking to be inverted because he was not worthy to die the same way as his Lord. James was beheaded. Andrew was crucified on an X-shaped cross. Thomas took the gospel all the way to India and was run through with a spear.

What changed? What turned cowards into conquerors? What transformed terrified men hiding behind locked doors into bold preachers who turned the world upside down?

They SAW Him. They ate with Him. They touched Him. They talked with Him. For 40 days after His resurrection, Jesus appeared to them — individually, in small groups, and once to over 500 people at the same time (1 Corinthians 15:6).

People will die for what they BELIEVE to be true. But no one dies for what they KNOW to be a lie. The disciples were in a unique position — they KNEW whether the resurrection was true or false. And every single one of them chose death rather than deny what they had seen with their own eyes.

### Dietrich Bonhoeffer — "This Is the End; For Me, the Beginning of Life"
Dietrich Bonhoeffer was a German pastor and theologian who stood against Adolf Hitler and the Nazi regime. While many German churches compromised and supported the Nazis, Bonhoeffer founded the Confessing Church and spoke out boldly against the evil of the Third Reich.

He was arrested and imprisoned in 1943. For two years he endured the hardships of Nazi prisons. He ministered to fellow prisoners. He wrote letters that would become some of the most profound theological writings of the 20th century.

On April 9, 1945 — just weeks before the war ended — Bonhoeffer was executed by hanging at Flossenbürg concentration camp. The camp doctor who witnessed the execution wrote: "I saw Pastor Bonhoeffer kneeling on the floor praying fervently to his God. I was most deeply moved by the way this lovable man prayed, so devout and so certain that God heard his prayer. At the place of execution, he again said a short prayer and then climbed the steps to the gallows, brave and composed."

Bonhoeffer's last words to a fellow prisoner: "This is the end — for me, the beginning of life."

The beginning of LIFE. Not the end. The BEGINNING. Because of the resurrection, death is not a wall — it is a DOOR. Bonhoeffer walked through that door with confidence because he knew that on the other side, the risen Christ was waiting.

## Sermon 3: "The Age of Grace — The Clock Is Ticking" — Church History Illustrations

### John Wesley's Heart "Strangely Warmed"
John Wesley was born into a Christian home. His father was a minister. His mother Susanna was one of the godliest women in English history. Wesley went to Oxford University. He became an ordained minister. He sailed across the Atlantic as a missionary to Georgia. He fasted twice a week. He visited prisoners. He taught Sunday School.

And he was LOST.

Wesley himself admitted it later. He had religion without regeneration. He had the form of godliness without the power. He was a minister who did not truly know the Lord he preached about.

On May 24, 1738, Wesley reluctantly went to a meeting on Aldersgate Street in London. Someone was reading Luther's preface to the book of Romans. And Wesley wrote in his journal:

"About a quarter before nine, while the reader was describing the change which God works in the heart through faith in Christ, I felt my heart strangely warmed. I felt I did trust in Christ, Christ alone, for salvation; and an assurance was given me that He had taken away MY sins, even mine, and saved ME from the law of sin and death."

Even MINE. Even ME. Grace became personal. After years of religion, years of trying to earn God's favor, years of missionary work and good deeds — grace finally reached John Wesley's heart. And from that moment, Wesley became a flame of revival. He rode 250,000 miles on horseback. He preached 40,000 sermons. He changed England without firing a shot.

My friend, you can be religious without being saved. You can be a churchgoer without being a Christian. Grace must be personal. Has grace touched YOUR heart? Not your head — your HEART.

### D.L. Moody and the Chicago Fire
In October 1871, Dwight L. Moody was preaching to a large crowd in Chicago. He preached powerfully about what to do with Jesus. But at the end of his message, instead of pressing for a decision, he said something he would regret for the rest of his life: "Go home and think about what you'll do with Christ. Come back next Sunday and give me your answer."

That night, the Great Chicago Fire broke out. The flames consumed 17,000 buildings. 300 people died. 100,000 were left homeless. The very church where Moody had just preached burned to the ground.

Moody was devastated. He never knew how many people from that audience perished in the fire — people he had told to "come back next Sunday." People who never got a next Sunday.

From that night forward, D.L. Moody NEVER again told an audience to go home and "think about it." He always pressed for an immediate decision. He always said NOW. Because he learned — at a terrible cost — that tomorrow is promised to no one.

The Bible says, "Behold, NOW is the accepted time; behold, NOW is the day of salvation" (2 Corinthians 6:2). Not next Sunday. Not tomorrow. NOW. The clock is ticking. Grace has an expiration date. Don't walk out of this place tonight without settling the question of your soul.

### The Welsh Revival (1904-1905)
In 1904, a young coal miner named Evan Roberts was touched by the Spirit of God. He went back to his small hometown of Loughor, Wales, and began to hold prayer meetings. He had a simple message with four points:
1. Confess all known sin
2. Remove anything doubtful from your life
3. Be ready to obey the Holy Spirit instantly
4. Confess Christ publicly

What happened next was nothing short of supernatural. Within five months, 100,000 people in Wales came to Christ. One hundred THOUSAND! In a country of less than 2 million!

The police reported that crime dropped so drastically they had nothing to do. Some formed gospel quartets to pass the time! Bars closed for lack of customers. Courts had empty dockets. The coal mines were disrupted — not by strikes, but because the miners stopped cursing, and the pit ponies didn't understand the new, clean language and wouldn't respond to commands!

Debts were repaid. Families were restored. Stolen goods were returned. The revival spread from Wales to the rest of the United Kingdom, to Scandinavia, to India, to Korea, to Africa.

That is what grace does when it breaks out in power. It doesn't just save individuals — it transforms COMMUNITIES. It doesn't just change hearts — it changes CULTURES. We need that grace today. And it is available. The same Spirit that moved in Wales is moving still. But the clock is ticking. Will you respond while the Spirit is moving?

### William Booth — One Word: "OTHERS"
William Booth founded the Salvation Army in 1865 to reach the poorest of the poor in London's East End. For decades, Booth and his followers went into the darkest, most dangerous parts of the city — the gin shops, the brothels, the workhouses — with the gospel of Jesus Christ.

As Booth grew old and his health failed, he was asked to send a one-word telegram to be read at the Salvation Army's annual convention. He thought about it carefully. And the telegram he sent contained just one word:

"OTHERS."

Grace received must become grace extended. If God has been gracious to you, you owe it to the world to be gracious to others. If someone told YOU the good news, you must tell someone else. The clock is ticking — not just for YOUR soul, but for the souls of those around you who have never heard.

Booth once said: "I want to stand on a street corner in hell and point people to heaven." He could not bear the thought of anyone perishing without hearing the gospel. Can you?

## Sermon 4: "Sin — The Disease Nobody Wants to Talk About" — Church History Illustrations

### Augustine's Confessions — "Make Me Chaste, But Not Yet"
Augustine of Hippo was one of the most brilliant minds in human history. He was also one of the most tormented by sin. As a young man in North Africa and Rome, he lived a wild, immoral life. He had a mistress. He had a son out of wedlock. He dabbled in pagan philosophy. He chased pleasure, fame, and intellectual stimulation.

And yet he could not escape the pull of God. His mother, Monica, prayed for his salvation for 30 YEARS. Thirty years of tears, prayers, and pleading with God for her son's soul.

Augustine later wrote his famous prayer of that period: "Lord, make me chaste — but not yet!" He WANTED to be free from sin. He just didn't want it TODAY. He wanted one more day, one more indulgence, one more taste of the pleasures that were destroying him.

Then one day, sitting in a garden in Milan, weeping over his sin, Augustine heard a child's voice from a neighboring house singing: "Tolle lege, tolle lege" — "Take up and read, take up and read." He grabbed the scroll of Paul's epistle to the Romans that lay nearby and his eyes fell on Romans 13:13-14: "Not in revelry and drunkenness, not in lewdness and lust... But put on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make no provision for the flesh."

In that moment, the chains broke. The darkness fled. Augustine was FREE. He became one of the greatest theologians in church history. His book "Confessions" has been read for 1,600 years. And it all started with a mother's prayers and a moment of honest surrender.

Are you praying Augustine's prayer tonight? "Lord, save me — but not yet"? How long will you keep God waiting? Your mother may be praying for you right now. God's Spirit may be pulling on your heart THIS VERY MOMENT. Don't wait another day. The disease of sin only gets worse with time.

### John Newton — "Amazing Grace"
John Newton was a slave trader. He transported human beings across the Atlantic in chains. He was vulgar, profane, and cruel. By his own account, he was the worst of sinners.

On March 10, 1748, Newton's ship was caught in a violent storm. The vessel was breaking apart. Water was pouring in. Newton was sure he was going to die. And in that moment of terror, he cried out, "Lord, have mercy on us!"

Later he was amazed that he had said those words. He — the blasphemer, the slave trader, the man who had made a mockery of everything sacred — had called on the Lord. And the storm began to subside.

That night marked the beginning of Newton's conversion. It was not instantaneous — it took years for the full reality of his sin and God's grace to work its way through his life. But eventually, Newton left the slave trade, became a minister, and wrote the most beloved hymn in the English language:

"Amazing grace, how sweet the sound, that saved a wretch like me! I once was lost but now am found, was blind but now I see."

Newton never forgot what he was. When he was old and his memory was failing, a friend tried to comfort him. Newton said, "My memory is nearly gone. But I remember two things: I am a great sinner, and Christ is a great Savior."

That is the bottom line, my friends. You are a great sinner. Christ is a great Savior. Sin is the disease. Jesus is the cure. And no one is too far gone for His grace.

### Charles Colson — From the White House to the Prison House
Charles Colson was one of the most powerful men in America. He was Special Counsel to President Richard Nixon. He was known as Nixon's "hatchet man" — the one who would do whatever it took to destroy political enemies. He once said he would "walk over my own grandmother" to get Nixon re-elected.

Then Watergate happened. The cover-up unraveled. And Colson — the tough, ruthless political warrior — found himself facing criminal charges and public disgrace.

A friend gave him a copy of C.S. Lewis's "Mere Christianity." Colson read it and was confronted with the reality of his own pride and sin. One night, sitting in his car in his friend's driveway, Colson broke down weeping. He gave his life to Jesus Christ.

The media mocked him. "Colson's found religion!" they sneered. They said it was a ploy to get a lighter sentence. But Colson pled guilty and went to prison. And there, among inmates and outcasts, he found his calling.

After prison, Colson founded Prison Fellowship — the largest prison ministry in the world. For nearly 40 years until his death in 2012, Colson brought the gospel to prisoners in every state and in countries around the world. The man who had been the instrument of political power became the servant of the powerless.

Paul wrote in 1 Corinthians 6:11 — "And such WERE some of you. But you were washed, but you were sanctified, but you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus." WERE. Past tense. What you WERE is not what you ARE in Christ. Colson was a corrupt politician. But Christ made him a servant of the gospel. That is the power of God to transform a sinner.

### The Thief on the Cross — Saved in the Last Moment
This is not a church history story — it's the greatest story ever told about a sinner finding grace.

Three crosses on a hill. The middle cross held the Son of God. On one side, a criminal who mocked Jesus to the end. On the other side, a criminal who mocked Jesus at first — Matthew 27:44 says BOTH criminals reviled Him — but then something changed.

This man watched Jesus suffer without complaining. He heard Jesus say, "Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do." He saw something in Jesus that was different from any man he had ever known.

And he said, "Lord, remember me when You come into Your kingdom."

No church attendance. No baptism. No good works. No Bible study. No tithe. He couldn't even bow his head or fold his hands — they were nailed to a cross. All he had was a prayer of faith: "Lord, remember me."

And Jesus said, "Today you will be with Me in Paradise."

TODAY. Not "after you've proven yourself." Not "after you've cleaned up your life." Not "if you can pass the entrance exam." TODAY.

Sin is the disease. Grace is the cure. And it is available to anyone — even a dying criminal — in an instant, by faith.

## Sermon 5: "Born Again — What Jesus Really Meant" — Church History Illustrations

### George Whitefield — "You Must Be Born Again" 300 Times
George Whitefield was perhaps the greatest preacher who ever lived. He preached to crowds of 30,000 people in the open air — without a microphone! Benjamin Franklin, no friend of organized religion, was so fascinated by Whitefield that he calculated the preacher's voice could be heard by 30,000 people based on pure vocal power alone.

Whitefield was once asked why he preached so often on John 3:7 — "You must be born again." He replied simply: "Because you MUST be born again!"

He preached that sermon over 300 times. And each time, something remarkable happened. In the coal mining regions of England, Whitefield preached to miners coming up from the pits — their faces black with coal dust. As the Holy Spirit convicted them and the new birth took hold, tears streamed down their blackened faces, making white tracks through the grime.

Those white tracks were the outward sign of an inward change. The coal dust of sin being washed away by the tears of repentance. The new birth visible on their very faces.

Whitefield said, "Other men may preach the gospel better than I can, but no man can preach a better gospel." It is the same message that must be preached in every generation: You must be born again.

### The Conversion of C.S. Lewis — The Reluctant Convert
C.S. Lewis was an atheist. A brilliant Oxford scholar, a veteran of World War I, a man who prided himself on his intellect and his rejection of what he called "the Christian mythology."

But God pursued him. Through friends like J.R.R. Tolkien (author of "The Lord of the Rings"). Through the books of George MacDonald. Through the relentless logic of Christian philosophy that kept cornering his brilliant mind.

Lewis wrote later about his conversion: "In the Trinity Term of 1929, I gave in, and admitted that God was God, and knelt and prayed: perhaps, that night, the most dejected and reluctant convert in all England."

He didn't come to Christ on a wave of emotion. He came kicking and fighting. He came because he could no longer honestly deny the truth. He came because God would not leave him alone.

And then the joy came. Lewis went on to write "Mere Christianity," "The Screwtape Letters," "The Chronicles of Narnia," and dozens of other works that have brought millions to faith. The reluctant convert became the most influential Christian writer of the 20th century.

The new birth is not always dramatic. Sometimes it comes with tears and shaking. Sometimes it comes quietly, like dawn breaking slowly over the horizon. What matters is not HOW it happens but THAT it happens. Have you been born again?

### Nicky Cruz — "Every Piece Would Love You"
In the 1950s, Nicky Cruz was one of the most feared gang leaders in New York City. President of the Mau Maus gang in Brooklyn. Violent. Savage. He had been told by his parents as a child that he was the "son of Satan." He had been in and out of jail. He carried a knife and was ready to use it on anyone who crossed him.

Then a skinny preacher from Pennsylvania named David Wilkerson walked into his territory. Cruz threatened him: "You come near me, and I'll kill you."

Wilkerson looked at him and said, "Yeah, you could do that. You could cut me in a thousand pieces and lay them out in the street. But every piece would still love you."

Every piece would LOVE you. No one had ever said anything like that to Nicky Cruz. The words pierced through the armor of rage and violence. Not immediately — but eventually, at a rally Wilkerson organized, the Holy Spirit broke through, and Nicky Cruz was born again.

The knife-wielding gang leader became an evangelist who has preached to millions around the world. His story, told in "The Cross and the Switchblade" and his own autobiography "Run Baby Run," has inspired countless people to believe that no one — NO ONE — is beyond the reach of the new birth.

If God can be born again Nicky Cruz, He can reach YOU. If the new birth can transform a violent gang leader into a preacher of love, it can transform anyone. Including you. Tonight.

### Corrie ten Boom — The Power to Forgive
Corrie ten Boom and her family hid Jews in their home in Haarlem, Holland, during World War II. They were eventually betrayed, arrested, and sent to the Ravensbrück concentration camp. Corrie's father died 10 days after imprisonment. Her beloved sister Betsie died in the camp, weakened by cruelty and starvation.

After the war, Corrie traveled the world preaching God's love and forgiveness. One night, in a church in Germany, a man approached her after her message. He extended his hand. And Corrie recognized him — he had been one of the SS guards at Ravensbrück. One of the cruelest.

He said, "You mentioned Ravensbrück. I was a guard there. But since then, I have become a Christian. I know that God has forgiven me, but I would like to hear it from you as well. Will you forgive me?"

Corrie stood frozen. Her sister's face flashed before her eyes. The cold, the hunger, the degradation. And this man — this man — was asking for forgiveness?

She wrote later: "I stood there with the coldness clutching my heart. 'Jesus, help me!' I prayed silently. 'I can lift my hand. I can do that much. You supply the feeling.' And so woodenly, mechanically, I thrust my hand into the one stretched out to me. And as I did, an incredible thing took place. The current started in my shoulder, raced down my arm, sprang into our joined hands. And then this healing warmth seemed to flood my whole being, bringing tears to my eyes. 'I forgive you, brother!' I cried. 'With all my heart!'"

Only the power of the new birth can produce such forgiveness. Human nature says REVENGE. The born-again nature says FORGIVENESS. That is the miracle of the new birth — it gives you power to do what you could NEVER do on your own.

## Sermon 6: "Revelation — The King Is Coming Back" — Church History Illustrations

### The Early Church — "Maranatha!"
In the first century, Christians had a greeting. It was not "Hello" or "Good morning." It was one word: "MARANATHA!" It is an Aramaic word that means "The Lord is coming!" or "Come, Lord!"

They said it when they met each other on the street. They said it when they gathered for worship. They said it at the breaking of bread. They wrote it in their letters. It was found in the Didache — one of the earliest Christian documents outside the New Testament — which ends with the cry: "Maranatha! Come, Lord Jesus!"

These early believers lived every single day as if Jesus could return at any moment. And it changed EVERYTHING about how they lived. They were bold in persecution because the Lord was coming. They were generous with their possessions because the Lord was coming. They were holy in their conduct because the Lord was coming. They were urgent in their evangelism because the Lord was coming.

We have lost that urgency. We have grown comfortable. We have settled in. We act as if this world is our permanent home. But it is NOT. The early church knew better. Maranatha! The Lord IS coming. Live like it.

### William Miller and the Great Disappointment (1844)
In the 1830s and 1840s, a Baptist preacher named William Miller studied the prophecies of Daniel and concluded that Jesus would return on October 22, 1844. He gained a huge following — thousands sold their homes, quit their jobs, and gathered on hilltops to wait for the Lord's return.

October 22 came. And nothing happened. It became known as "The Great Disappointment." People who had given up everything were devastated. Some left the faith entirely. Others were mocked mercilessly.

There is a powerful lesson here: We do NOT know the day or the hour. Jesus Himself said, "But of that day and hour no one knows, not even the angels of heaven, but My Father only" (Matthew 24:36). Anyone who sets a date for Christ's return is going beyond what Scripture says.

BUT — and this is crucial — the fact that Miller was wrong about the DATE does not mean he was wrong about the EVENT. Jesus IS coming back. The Bible promises it over 300 times. We just don't know WHEN. And that's the point. You must be ready EVERY day. Not just October 22. Every single day.

The lesson of the Great Disappointment is not "He's not coming." The lesson is: "He IS coming — so always be ready."

### Jim Elliot — "He Is No Fool"
Jim Elliot was a young missionary who felt called to reach the Auca (Huaorani) Indians in the jungles of Ecuador — one of the most violent and isolated tribes on earth. They had killed every outsider who had ever approached them.

On January 8, 1956, Elliot and four other young missionaries — Pete Fleming, Ed McCully, Nate Saint, and Roger Youderian — made contact with the Aucas on a small beach along the Curaray River. The Aucas attacked. All five missionaries were killed. They were speared to death. Jim Elliot was 28 years old.

The news shocked the world. Five young men, five widows, five families shattered. What a waste, the world said. What a tragedy.

But Jim Elliot had written in his journal years before: "He is no fool who gives what he cannot keep to gain what he cannot lose."

He could not keep his life — we all die. But he gained what he cannot lose — eternity with Christ. He lived in light of the King's return. He invested in what lasts forever.

And the story doesn't end on that beach. Elliot's wife, Elisabeth, and Nate Saint's sister, Rachel, went BACK to the Aucas. They lived among them. They loved them. They shared the gospel. And the very men who killed the missionaries came to Christ. The tribe was transformed.

The King IS coming back. And every sacrifice made for His kingdom will be rewarded. "Well done, good and faithful servant." Those are the words Jim Elliot heard when he stepped from that bloody beach into the presence of his King.

### Billy Graham's Last Crusade — "The King Is Coming"
At his final major crusade in 2005 in New York City, Billy Graham was old, frail, and suffering from multiple health problems. He had to be helped to the pulpit. His voice was weaker than it had been in decades. Over 230,000 people attended.

And what did the aging evangelist preach about? The return of Christ. "I may not be here much longer," he told the crowd. "But I want you to know something: Jesus Christ is coming back. He's coming back!"

There was an urgency in his voice — the urgency of a man who knew he was near the end of his earthly race and wanted to make sure everyone heard the most important truth one more time: The King is coming.

Graham went home to be with the Lord on February 21, 2018, at the age of 99. His body lay in repose in the Capitol Rotunda — only the fourth private citizen in history to receive that honor. But the message inscribed near his casket was simple: "Preacher of the Gospel of the Lord Jesus Christ."

The King he preached about for 70 years — that King IS coming back. And Billy Graham will be there to welcome Him. Will you?

## Sermon 7: "The Bible — God's Love Letter to You" — Church History Illustrations

### William Tyndale — Burned for Translating the Bible
In the 1520s, the Bible existed only in Latin — a language common people could not read. The Catholic Church kept Scripture locked away from ordinary believers. One English scholar dared to change that.

William Tyndale had a confrontation with a churchman who said, "We had better be without God's laws than the Pope's." Tyndale fired back: "I defy the Pope and all his laws! If God spare my life, I will cause a boy that drives the plough to know more of the Scripture than you do!"

And that is exactly what he did. Tyndale translated the New Testament from the original Greek into English — the first printed English New Testament in history. He smuggled copies into England in bales of cloth and barrels of flour.

The authorities hunted him. For years he lived on the run across Europe. Finally, he was betrayed by a friend, arrested, and imprisoned for over a year. On October 6, 1536, Tyndale was strangled and burned at the stake.

His last words, crying out from the flames: "Lord, open the King of England's eyes!"

Within four years of Tyndale's death, King Henry VIII authorized the Great Bible — based largely on Tyndale's translation — to be placed in every church in England. God answered Tyndale's dying prayer.

You hold a Bible in your hand today because men like William Tyndale were willing to DIE for it. Ninety percent of the King James Version of the New Testament comes directly from Tyndale's translation. Every time you read the Bible in English, you are reading Tyndale's legacy — purchased with his blood.

Do you value that Book? Do you read it? Do you treasure it? A man DIED so you could have it. The least you can do is OPEN it.

### Mary Jones and Her Bible — 26 Miles Barefoot
In 1794, in a small village in Wales, a 10-year-old girl named Mary Jones began saving her pennies to buy a Bible. For SIX YEARS she saved — doing odd jobs, gathering eggs, sewing, cleaning — every spare penny went toward her Bible fund.

When she was 16, Mary had finally saved enough. But the nearest place to buy a Welsh Bible was in the town of Bala — 26 miles away. Mary set out on foot. Barefoot. Over rough mountain roads. Twenty-six miles.

When she arrived, exhausted and footsore, she found the Reverend Thomas Charles. She asked for a Bible. He told her he had only a few copies, and they were all promised to others.

Mary broke down weeping. She had walked 26 miles. She had saved for 6 years. And there was no Bible for her.

Thomas Charles was so moved that he gave her one of the reserved copies. And then, inspired by her passion, he went to London and helped found the British and Foreign Bible Society in 1804 — an organization dedicated to making the Bible available to EVERYONE, in EVERY language.

Today, the Bible has been translated into over 3,500 languages. And it started with a 16-year-old girl who wanted God's Word so badly she walked 26 miles barefoot to get it.

How far would YOU walk for a Bible? Some of us have five Bibles sitting on a shelf, gathering dust. Mary Jones walked 26 miles for ONE. Do we love God's Word as much as she did?

### George Müller — The Bible 200 Times Through
George Müller of Bristol, England, cared for over 10,000 orphans during his lifetime. He built five large orphan houses. He never once asked anyone for money. He never went into debt. He simply PRAYED — and God provided millions of pounds over his lifetime.

What was the secret of his extraordinary faith? Müller himself revealed it: the Bible.

Müller read the Bible cover to cover more than 200 times. Not skimming. READING. He spent hours every day in the Word of God. He said: "The vigor of our spiritual life will be in exact proportion to the place held by the Bible in our life and thoughts."

He also said: "I have had the most amazing answers to prayer... but the most wonderful of all is that I have been able to read the Bible on my knees and understand it."

Müller's faith was built on the Bible. His prayers were shaped by the Bible. His orphan houses were sustained by the God of the Bible. The Word of God was not just a book to him — it was the living voice of the living God, and he ordered his entire life by it.

If you want extraordinary faith, start with the ordinary discipline of daily Bible reading. Müller didn't have a special gift. He had a special BOOK. And that same Book is available to you.

### Brother Andrew — "God's Smuggler"
In the 1950s and 60s, a Dutchman known as "Brother Andrew" began one of the most daring ministries in modern history: smuggling Bibles behind the Iron Curtain into communist countries where the Bible was banned.

He would load his Volkswagen Beetle with Bibles and drive across heavily guarded borders into the Soviet Union, China, Cuba, and other closed countries. At each checkpoint, he would pray: "Lord, You made blind eyes see. Now make seeing eyes blind." And border guards would look directly at boxes of Bibles and somehow not see them.

Once, in Romania, Brother Andrew delivered Bibles to underground churches where believers had been sharing a single Bible among hundreds of people — tearing out individual books and passing them around. One woman had memorized the entire book of Romans because she was afraid her torn-out pages would be confiscated before she could finish reading them.

Brother Andrew once said, "One day Bibles will be freely available in Russia." People laughed at him. The Soviet Union seemed permanent and invincible. But in 1991, the Berlin Wall fell and the Soviet Union collapsed. Today, millions of Bibles are freely distributed throughout Russia and Eastern Europe.

The Bible CANNOT be stopped. Empires have tried to destroy it. Dictators have tried to ban it. Philosophers have tried to disprove it. And it is still the #1 bestselling book in the world, every single year. Voltaire, the famous atheist, predicted that within 100 years of his death, the Bible would be a forgotten relic. Within 50 years of his death, the Geneva Bible Society was using HIS HOUSE to print and distribute Bibles!

"The grass withers, the flower fades, but the word of our God stands forever" (Isaiah 40:8).

## Sermon 8: "What Will You Do With Jesus?" (Grand Finale) — Church History Illustrations

### Pontius Pilate — History's Greatest Wrong Decision
Pontius Pilate was the Roman governor of Judea. He held the power of life and death. And one morning, they brought a prisoner into his courtroom — a carpenter from Galilee named Jesus of Nazareth.

Pilate examined Him. He interrogated Him. He asked Him questions. And Pilate came to a conclusion — three times he said publicly: "I find no fault in Him" (John 18:38, 19:4, 19:6). NO FAULT. Pilate knew Jesus was innocent.

But the crowd was howling. The religious leaders were threatening. Pilate's political career was at stake. And so Pilate called for a bowl of water and washed his hands before the crowd, saying, "I am innocent of the blood of this just Person" (Matthew 27:24).

He washed his hands of Jesus. He handed Him over to be crucified. History's greatest wrong decision, made by a man who KNEW the truth but didn't have the courage to act on it.

What happened to Pilate? Ancient historians tell us he was eventually recalled to Rome in disgrace. Tradition says he was exiled to Gaul (modern France), where he died a broken, tormented man. Some traditions say he took his own life, haunted by nightmares of the Man he condemned.

You cannot wash your hands of Jesus. You cannot remain neutral. You must decide. And refusing to decide IS a decision — it is a decision to reject Him. Pilate tried to avoid the question, and history has judged him for it ever since. What will YOU do with Jesus? Don't be a Pilate. Don't wash your hands and walk away from the most important decision of your life.

### The Conversion of Saul of Tarsus
Saul of Tarsus was the greatest enemy Christianity ever faced. He was present at the stoning of Stephen — the first Christian martyr. He "made havoc of the church, entering every house, and dragging off men and women, committing them to prison" (Acts 8:3). He breathed "threats and murder" against the followers of Jesus (Acts 9:1).

If anyone in history seemed LEAST likely to become a Christian, it was Saul of Tarsus. He hated everything about Jesus and His followers. He was on his way to Damascus with arrest warrants to drag more believers to prison.

And then — a light from heaven. Brighter than the noonday sun. Saul fell to the ground. And a voice: "Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting Me?"

"Who are You, Lord?"

"I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting."

In that moment, the greatest enemy of Christianity became its greatest champion. Saul became Paul. The persecutor became the apostle. The man who dragged Christians to prison would himself be imprisoned for Christ. The man who consented to Stephen's death would himself die a martyr's death.

All because of one encounter with the risen Jesus on a dusty road outside Damascus. One encounter changed everything.

"What will you do with Jesus?" changed Saul's answer from "Destroy Him" to "Serve Him." And that change has echoed through 2,000 years of history. What will YOUR encounter with Jesus produce? Will you fight Him, or follow Him?

### Billy Graham's Conversion — A 16-Year-Old Boy Says "Yes"
In 1934, in Charlotte, North Carolina, a traveling evangelist named Mordecai Ham set up a tent for revival meetings. A local farmer brought his teenage son. The boy didn't want to go. He sat in the back and tried to avoid the evangelist's gaze.

But the Spirit of God was at work. Night after night, the boy came back. The Word of God pierced his heart. The Holy Spirit convicted him of sin. And one evening, William Franklin Graham Jr. — Billy Frank to his friends — walked forward and gave his life to Jesus Christ. He was 16 years old.

No one in that tent could have imagined what God would do with that lanky farm boy. Billy Graham went on to preach the gospel to more people in person than anyone in the history of the world — over 215 million people in 185 countries. He was the personal spiritual advisor to every U.S. president from Truman to Obama. He was consistently ranked as one of the most admired people on earth.

And it all started with a 16-year-old boy who said "yes" to Jesus at a tent revival in North Carolina.

What are you waiting for? Billy Graham was just a teenager. He didn't have a seminary degree. He didn't have a plan. He just said YES. And God did the rest.

### The Final Scene — A Pine Coffin and an Eternal Message
On February 21, 2018, Billy Graham passed into the presence of the Lord at the age of 99. His funeral was held on March 2 in a tent — just like the tent where he first heard the gospel — on the grounds of the Billy Graham Library in Charlotte, North Carolina.

His coffin was made of pine plywood — built by inmates at Angola Prison in Louisiana, one of the toughest prisons in America. Men whose lives had been changed by the gospel built the final resting place for the man who had preached that gospel for 70 years.

Written on the coffin, by Billy Graham's own request, were just four words: "Preacher of the Gospel."

Not "world-famous evangelist." Not "advisor to presidents." Not "bestselling author." Just: "Preacher of the Gospel."

Four U.S. presidents attended. Heads of state sent condolences. The world mourned. But the message at the funeral was the same message Billy Graham preached at his very first sermon: Jesus Christ, and Him crucified and risen.

And even in death, the question remained — the same question he had asked at every crusade, on every continent, to every audience: "What will you do with Jesus?"

Tonight, I ask you that same question. Not because Billy Graham asks it. Not because a preacher asks it. But because GOD asks it. Jesus stands before you just as He stood before Pilate. You cannot wash your hands. You cannot avoid the question. You must decide.

What will YOU do with Jesus?
