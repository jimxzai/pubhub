# Evangelistic Mode

The default mode: an ordinary gospel setting with no recent, irreversible loss in the room.

If there *is* such a loss, stop and read `grief-mode.md` instead.

---

## Reusable skeleton

Scales from about 20 to 45 minutes. Radio and television versions compress the same shape into 13–25.

```
① Ice-break / self-deprecation ...................... 60–90 sec
   A short story with the laugh landing on yourself.
   Purpose: lower defenses, establish that a person is speaking.

② Entry point ....................................... 2–4 min
   EITHER a live cultural question everyone is arguing about
   OR the text read cold — but then an image within two sentences.
   Cite outside authority so the concern isn't only yours.

③ Nail the question ................................. 30 sec
   State plainly the one question this message answers.

④ The text .......................................... 
   One core passage, quoted directly, no academic excursion.
   (In the era-first variant this is where Scripture first appears.
    In the text-first variant this has already happened at ②.)

⑤ Body: 3–4 points ..................................
   Each = one proposition + one image + one "you."
   Transitions are questions: Why? What did He mean? What about you?
   ~150–250 words per point, then turn.

⑥ Narrow ............................................ ← the hinge
   Reduce to two or three options and seal the middle.
   "You can ridicule. You can reject. You can put it off.
    Or you can receive Him."

⑦ Testimony ......................................... 30–60 sec
   Not the climax — a credibility anchor. Keep the register low.
   Admit what you cannot prove or explain.

⑧ Invitation ........................................ (below)
```

**One long narrative maximum, and it belongs in the middle.** Never open with it and never close with it. His youth message carried the Joseph account as its only extended story, sitting squarely in the body.

## Invitation anatomy

### Seed it in the opening

The most-missed move in his entire method. The invitation is not appended at the end; it is installed in the first minute.

His 1971 opening prayer announced that this would be an hour of decision and that *even refusing* would leave them changed. Refusal was permitted. Neutrality was not. From that sentence forward every listener was already on the scale.

**Practically:** in your first ninety seconds, say something that makes "hearing this and doing nothing" an impossible category.

### Name the cost

He told new believers they were "in for it" — that opposition begins at conversion, that a Christian who drops his guard for a day is in trouble, that the road runs against the traffic. In 1982 he pressed harder still: count the cost, the gate is narrow, the way is *hard*.

Promising ease sounds like a sales pitch. Naming cost sounds like someone telling the truth. **The second one converts better, which is a fact about human beings, not a technique.**

### Number the actions

His three steps ran unchanged across at least eleven years:

1. Repent of sin — turn, change your mind, change direction
2. By faith receive Christ as Savior, Lord, and Master
3. Be willing to obey Him in the small things He will teach you

Numbering matters because it converts an emotional state into something a person can execute. A listener who feels moved but doesn't know what to *do* will do nothing.

### Explain why it must be public

Don't merely ask — give the reason. His two arguments:

- Every person Jesus called in the Gospels, He called publicly
- A commitment made before witnesses holds, the way a marriage does

He was also careful to say the walk forward doesn't save anyone; it's the outward sign of an inward decision. That distinction protects people from confusing an action with a conversion.

### Remove the excuses

He would say out loud that their friends, their bus, and their group would wait. Small logistical obstacles stop more people than intellectual objections do — so name and dissolve the small ones.

### End on a verb

"Rise and come." "Get up out of your seat." "Come now."

Never end on reflection. No "may we go home and consider this."

## Adapting the invitation to the setting

The call always exists; its form changes with what the room allows.

| Setting | Form it takes |
|---|---|
| Crusade / large gathering | Come forward, publicly, now |
| Television | Phone number on screen, counselors waiting, repeated |
| Secular venue where an altar call is impossible | "You can make that move today in your own soul" |
| Small group / one-to-one | A specific next action, named and agreed |
| Chinese congregational setting | Raise a hand, stay after, fill a card — match your church's culture |

The 1955 address to a business club had no altar call — the venue forbade it — so he closed by telling them there was one move left on the board and they could make it inside themselves that day. **The form bent. The call didn't disappear.**

## Era variants

Both are authentic; choose deliberately.

| | Mid-career (1950s–70s) | Late (1980s–) |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | Cultural moment, text later | Text read cold, first sentence |
| Structure | Flowing; elimination then construction | Numbered points ("fourthly… fifthly…") |
| Pace | Suspense every few minutes | Trusts the listener's patience |
| On cost | Present | **Heavier** — narrow gate, count the cost |
| Testimony | ~30 sec, credibility anchor | Longer, and markedly less flattering |
| Local application | General | **Localized** — "you here have heard more gospel than almost anyone, so more is required" |

The common assumption that he softened with age is wrong on this axis. On cost and accountability he got harder.

**Localized conviction is worth stealing.** Not "the world is sinful" but "this city, this campus, this congregation, specifically, has this specific advantage and therefore this specific responsibility." Generalized guilt slides off. Named responsibility doesn't.

## What he deliberately refused to do

Useful because omissions are as characteristic as habits:

- **No theological argument.** Preaching on the Holy Spirit amid the charismatic controversy, he said the disagreements would take too long and moved on. Protecting the through-line beat winning the argument.
- **No self-promotion.** Introduced with a long recitation of his statistics, he defused it with a joke in his first sentence.
- **No rare vocabulary.** He said "a legal standing with God," never "forensic imputation." Across the whole corpus there is almost nothing requiring a dictionary.
- **No dwelling.** One point, ~200 words, turn.
- **No "let's think it over."** Always today, tonight, now.
