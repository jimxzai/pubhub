# Craft Checklist

Sentence-level technique, and the checklist to run over a finished draft.

---

## The imagery rule

**One abstract noun = one image + one turn. Never let a doctrine stand naked for more than two sentences.**

This is the single highest-yield rule in the method. Most flat sermons fail here and nowhere else.

How he handled the hardest abstractions:

| Abstraction | What he did instead of defining it |
|---|---|
| The meaning of the cross | Four real crosses in four sentences — one on a cathedral standing in bombed rubble, one lowered onto a rebuilt cathedral by helicopter, one raised over a concentration camp's mass grave, one left in the snow at the summit of Everest |
| Faith | Five ordinary acts of trust — the chair you sat in without inspecting, the bank you expect to honor your check, the currency you accept at face value, the hilltop you drive over trusting the other lane, the coffee you drank without testing it |
| Sin's direction | One preposition. Jonah went *down* to Joppa, *down* into the ship, *down* into the hold. He simply made you notice the direction |
| Christ's authority over nature | The storm acted out — lightning stopped, thunder stopped, rain stopped, wind stopped, sea stopped — then a pilot in a typhoon, sweat running down his face, saying there is something up there more powerful than this airplane |
| The world's predicament | A painting of the devil playing chess against a boy, the boy in tears, the painting titled *Checkmate* — and a chess master studying it who suddenly shouts that there is a move |
| Surrendering control | A cockpit. Without Christ flying it, danger; with Him, someone who knows the route, the instruments, and every hazard |

Notice: **none of these are explanations.** They are things you can see. The doctrine arrives inside the picture, not before it.

When you catch yourself writing a third consecutive sentence of abstraction, stop and find the picture.

## Cadence

### One clause per line; the full stop is a drumbeat

Three or four parallel short sentences, each landing slightly heavier, the last breaking the pattern:

```
He wasn't just another revolutionary.
He wasn't just another great man.
He was God — in the flesh.
```

```
He didn't squirm.
He didn't yell.
He didn't scream.
He just took it.
```

Chinese suits this better than English does — the language is already short. Resist the pull toward long subordinated sentences.

### Ask, answer, ask again

Advance by self-questioning rather than stacked declaratives. *What did He mean? — Why? — And what about you?*

Questions create micro-suspense; the answer releases it. About one of these every three to five sentences. A paragraph of pure declaration is a paragraph the listener stops tracking.

### Concede the objection before it's raised

Say the listener's counter-argument out loud, in their voice, before they can: *you may say this sounds too simple — I know it does, it may even sound illogical, it may not sound very academic.* Or simply: you may reject this, and that is your right.

Voicing the objection dismantles the defense. Pretending it doesn't exist hardens it.

### Say "you," not "people"

Count them. **Under thirty second-person pronouns in a full message means you wrote a lecture.**

Then go further and name the listener's present interior state: *some of you have been resisting this all evening — you know you need this and you haven't moved.* That move is what turns a hall of thousands into one conversation.

## The two emotional mechanics

He almost never used sentiment. The feeling comes from structure.

**A — Cosmic scale collapsed to personal scale.** Go as wide as possible: world history, the universe, armies of angels with drawn swords ready to wipe out the planet. Then, in one sentence, collapse it onto a single person: *and on that cross, He had the capacity to think of you.*

**The drop is the emotion.** No adjectives needed. Add adjectives and it dies.

**B — Slow motion at the peak.** Decelerate to one action per sentence. They took off His clothes. They beat Him until He could barely stand. They pressed thorns onto His brow. They laughed and spat.

Describe; don't editorialize. Then let a single line of commentary fall after — *anyone who can see that and not be moved has a heart of stone.*

**C — Humor as a pressure valve, placed just before the heavy blow.** Loosen the room, then hit. And the laugh must point at yourself — his ran to his own credentials, his own team, his own marriage. Humor aimed at the audience costs you the room. Aimed at yourself, it buys permission to say something hard next.

## Final checklist

Run this over the finished draft.

### Mode
- [ ] Is there recent, irreversible loss in the room? If yes — is this in grief mode? (see `grief-mode.md`)

### Structure
- [ ] Does something other than Scripture grab attention in the first 90 seconds? (Or, if text-first: does an image land within two sentences of the verse?)
- [ ] Can the whole message be stated as **one question**? Does it appear at least three times?
- [ ] Are there 3–4 points, not more?
- [ ] Does the ending narrow to two or three options? *(evangelistic mode only)*
- [ ] Is the one long narrative in the middle — not the opening, not the close?

### Language
- [ ] Any stretch of three-plus sentences with no image? That's the failure point — fix it.
- [ ] Longest sentence: over ~40 characters in Chinese, or two breaths in English? Break it.
- [ ] Any word requiring a dictionary? Cut it.
- [ ] Count "你" / "you." Under 30 means teaching, not gospel preaching.

### Emotion
- [ ] At least one cosmic-to-personal collapse?
- [ ] At least one slow-motion passage?
- [ ] Any humor at all — and does the laugh land on you?

### Invitation *(evangelistic mode only)*
- [ ] Seeded in the opening, not bolted on at the end?
- [ ] Is the cost named honestly?
- [ ] Are the actions numbered and executable?
- [ ] Is the reason for a public response explained, not just demanded?
- [ ] Are the small excuses dissolved in advance?
- [ ] Does the last sentence contain a verb?

### Contemporary fit
- [ ] Is the entry point something people are *actually* anxious about now — not a borrowed 1950s crisis? (see `contemporary-entry.md`)
- [ ] Is the tension one the listener already carried in, rather than one you installed?
- [ ] If judgment appears, is it after they're willing to hear — not in the first three minutes?
- [ ] If the response is public, does its form fit this congregation's culture?

### Testimony (see `testimony.md`)
- [ ] Is there at least one real life in this message — yours, a Scripture figure, a missionary, or someone in the congregation?
- [ ] Is your own testimony about weakness rather than victory? Placed *after* the argument?
- [ ] Does the Scripture figure keep their failures and their cost?
- [ ] Any missionary story — can you trace it to a primary source? If not, cut it or say you couldn't verify it.
- [ ] Any congregation member's story — consent obtained, and identifying details cleared?

### Integrity
- [ ] Every statistic, quotation, and anecdote verifiable?
- [ ] No Chinese-character gospel etymologies? (来, 義, 船, 禁 — all folk etymology)
- [ ] No claimed private conversations with important people?
- [ ] No implied promise that obedience produces the same outcome?
- [ ] Is this your own writing rather than reproduced sermon text?

### The one that matters most
- [ ] Would the specific person you're picturing feel **cared for** by this — or handled?
