# Patristic use of Lamentations: index

All files are public-domain English translations (ANF, NPNF series 1 and 2) fetched from newadvent.org and saved as page text (HTML tags stripped; the site's inline Scripture cross-reference tags, such as "Lamentations 4:20", are left in place and are the site's, not the father's). Each file starts with a 4-line header: AUTHOR, WORK, TRANSLATOR, SOURCE_URL. The page saved is the newadvent page that holds the passage (often a whole book or long chapter of the work); the quotable sentence is a short excerpt.

Quotable sentences are copied programmatically from the saved file (checked as exact substrings, 60 words or fewer).

## Honest scope (read before citing)

- No continuous patristic commentary on Lamentations exists in the ANF/NPNF collections on newadvent. Every row below is a father citing or alluding to a verse inside another work (apologetics, letters, polemic, homily on another book). None is "the fathers' interpretation of Lamentations". Label them as such in the book.
- Method: all 420 work pages and 3,285 chapter pages linked from newadvent.org/fathers/ were downloaded and searched for the site's Lamentations cross-reference tags (bible/lam001-005.htm) and for the word "Lamentations". Passages the site did not tag, and works not on newadvent (Jerome's and Origen's own Lamentations commentaries, Theodoret, Ephrem, etc.), are not covered.
- Leads that were checked and did NOT hold: Tertullian, Against Praxeas (no Lam 4:20 there; the page contains no "Lamentations"); Irenaeus, Against Heresies, Cyprian, Clement Stromata (no tagged Lamentations citation found). Lam 1:12 "Is it nothing to you, all you who pass by": no occurrence of the phrase in any father page searched. Chrysostom on 3:30 (cheek): not found; the turn-the-cheek link is in Origen, Contra Celsum VII and Augustine, Contra Faustum XIX.
- Lam 4:20 ("The Spirit/breath before our face, Christ the Lord, was taken in their destructions") is the verse the fathers cite most (Origen, Eusebius, Cyril of Jerusalem, Ambrose, Augustine, Apostolic Constitutions). They read it of Christ, from a text that differs from the Hebrew-based English Bible; Aphrahat reads it of the king (Zedekiah). Use only as "the fathers' use", not as the verse's meaning.
- Dates, church-order authorship and the Apostolic Constitutions' date are not stated on the pages; do not give them without another source.
