TITLE: O Sacred Head, Now Wounded
AUTHOR: Attributed to Bernard of Clairvaux (Salve caput cruentatum); German by Paul Gerhardt, 1656; English translation by James W. Alexander
YEAR: 1830 (English translation, as stated on source page)
SOURCE_URL: http://www.hymntime.com/tch/htm/o/s/a/c/osacredh.htm
NOTE: Not on Wikisource; text from the Cyber Hymnal page, which says the version is from Alexander's posthumous The Breaking Crucible, 1861, pp. 7-10 (other hymnals print altered versions).

O sacred head, now wounded,
With grief and shame weighed down,
Now scornfully surrounded
With thorns, Thine only crown;
O sacred head, what glory!
What bliss, till now was Thine!
Yet, though despised and gory,
I joy to call Thee mine.

O noblest brow, and dearest!
In other days the world
All feared, when Thou appeared’st,
What shame on Thee is hurled!
How art Thou pale with anguish,
With sore abuse and scorn;
How does that visage languish,
When once was bright as morn.
