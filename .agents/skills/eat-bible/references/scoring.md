# Evidence-based publisher score and improvement plan

A score is editorial judgment supported by evidence, not a build result.
Keep the same ten dimensions between reviews; cover design is part of layout,
not a new eleventh dimension added to inflate an overall score.

| Dimension | Evidence to inspect |
|---|---|
| Scripture accuracy | Declared editions; verified verses/excerpts, discrepancies and source coverage |
| Format consistency | Established section order, headings, numbering and bilingual conventions |
| Layout and production | Front/back cover quality; typography, tables, page balance, logs and renders |
| Logical structure | Argument flow, cross-references, explanation versus assertion |
| Chapter-specific insights | Text-grounded, substantive observations rather than repeated generalities |
| Commentary verifiability | Original sources and locators, exact quotes versus labeled summaries |
| Bilingual completeness | Declared scope met in both languages; intentional excerpts clearly labeled |
| Citation format | Consistent credits, edition notices, references and ledger alignment |
| Usability | Contents/bookmarks, navigation, indices, reading order, legibility and reader orientation |
| Spiritual spine | Whole-book progression, part transitions, chapter coordinates and substantive return to Christ |

For each row record score out of 10 (or unverified), evidence, coverage/sample,
and main remaining defect. Use explicit statuses: checked now, carried forward
with date/artifact, or unverified. A prior score is not fresh verification.
Distinguish genuine `not applicable` from a failed/skipped check.

## Calculation and confidence

Default to equal weights: overall = sum of ten scores / 10. Use a small
calculation to verify the arithmetic, retain the unrounded mean, and display
one decimal. If established weights exist, state and preserve them; explain
any rubric change. Do not present a partial-row mean as a whole-book score.
With unknown rows, give evaluated coverage and a provisional assessment instead.
If carrying prior rows forward, label the composite provisional and cite them.

Broad anchors: 6 = substantial unfinished work; 7 = usable draft with material
issues; 8 = polished but meaningful gaps; 9 = strong work with specific residual
defects; near 10 = exceptionally consistent, comprehensively evidenced work.
These are judgment anchors, not a formula mapping lint counts to scores.
A numeric 10 cannot coexist with a known material defect in the scored scope.
An arithmetic error in an old report should be corrected transparently, not
explained away by changing the dimensions or weights after the fact.

Report separately: content/design assessment, automated production checks,
and release readiness. Rights/permission verification, physical printer proof,
accessibility conformance, and independent Scripture/content review require
their own evidence where relevant. Do not imply legal or accessibility
certification from a score, an attribution line, embedded fonts, or PDF tagging.

## Score report and improvement priorities

When an updated report is authorized, follow existing
`docs/scores/SLUG-YYYY-MM-DD.md` naming. Preserve prior evidence and avoid
duplicate final versions. Include the exact PDF path, SHA-256, date, page count,
source/builder/template identity, reviewed pages/passages, commands/results,
ten rows, reproducible calculation, limitations, and prioritized next actions.
Only compare before/after numbers when rubric and evidence scope are comparable.

For a plan, give each item: observed defect, proposed fix, affected scope, and
acceptance check. Prioritize inaccurate text and lost/clipped content first,
then navigation/layout defects, then typography/design polish. For a cover-only
brief stay within covers and report unrelated defects without silently fixing
them. “Closer to 10” means closing evidenced gaps, not promising a target score.
